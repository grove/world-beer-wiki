# Maltose-negative yeasts in low-alcohol brewing

Many brewing worts contain abundant maltose. Some yeasts selected for lower-alcohol beer cannot efficiently use maltose and/or maltotriose, restricting ethanol formation while still metabolizing other available sugars. A 2026 review describes experimental work with non-*Saccharomyces* species and selected *Saccharomyces* variants. **Maltose-negative is a functional description of a tested strain or organism in conditions, not a single brewing style.**[^v16-nolo-review]

## Flavor cannot be inferred from low ABV alone

Studies report different effects on esters, aldehydes, sweetness, clarity and perceived wort-like character. A yeast that produces little ethanol may still leave sensory challenges; a named species may behave differently under different wort or aeration conditions.[^v16-nolo-review]

## The experimental comparison matters

A 2026 primary study combining wort design, yeast selection and enzymatic treatment observed selected samples below 0.5% alcohol with changing residual sweetness and sensory response. This is evidence for tested production designs; it is not a universal regulatory rule or proof that every bottle made with those strains will meet an alcohol threshold.[^v16-nolo-primary]

## Safety and labeling limits

Low ethanol and residual sugars alter spoilage constraints. This reference does not recommend spontaneous inoculation for alcohol-free products, provide microbiological controls or make jurisdiction-specific product-label claims.
