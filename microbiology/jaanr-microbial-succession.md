# Jaanr microbial succession: molds, yeasts and LAB over time

A mixed traditional starter does not remain microbiologically fixed from preparation to service. Thapa and Tamang's 2006 study of *kodo ko jaanr*, a finger-millet fermentation in the Darjeeling hills and Sikkim, reported distinct trajectories for filamentous fungi, yeasts and lactic-acid bacteria. Mould counts fell as fermentation progressed, yeast counts rose earlier, and measured acidity increased.[^v18-jaanr]

## A changing community does several jobs

The authors associated starch breakdown with *Saccharomycopsis fibuligera* and *Rhizopus* organisms. They measured a shifting balance between starch conversion, reducing sugars and alcoholic fermentation. This warns against inferring an all-purpose “jaanr microbe” from a single isolate or final sample. Detection establishes presence under the method; demonstrating a specific role requires further evidence.[^v18-jaanr]

## No strain list is a cultural recipe

The same study reports a laboratory reconstruction using chosen strains and sensory scoring. This is useful as an experiment, but it should not overwrite local preparation knowledge or replace a traditional starter with an alleged universally authentic two-organism formula.
