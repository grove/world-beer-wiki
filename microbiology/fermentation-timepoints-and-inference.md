# Fermentation timepoints change what a sample can establish

## The same batch is not the same sample forever

Yeast metabolism changes wort during fermentation, and the published laboratory guide by Thesseling and colleagues treats monitoring as a sequence of observations rather than a single static product profile. One measured sample represents a particular stage and set of conditions. It cannot be used to reconstruct every organism present at every other stage.[^v116-lab]

Acidification adds another temporal dimension. ASBC's pH method concerns a reading of a particular liquid sample with a calibrated meter; the titratable-acidity method measures the response of that sampled matrix to an endpoint. Neither measurement alone reveals the full history of microbial succession or which species contributed which compounds.[^v116-ph][^v116-ta]

## A stage is not a culture rule

Traditions that use changing starters or repeated vessel use require local descriptions and appropriate provenance. A laboratory fermentation model cannot be assumed to capture all community practices or environmental exposures. Likewise, a two-timepoint plot does not show how a microbiome changed continuously between the observations.

For reproducible comparisons, report sampling time, ingredient substrate, mixing and preparation, measurement method, analytical units and the exact source context. When species identification comes from sequencing or culture isolation, state it as that specific form of evidence, not as proven metabolic function.

Read the [microbial succession guide](microbial-succession.md), [gravity is not identity](fermentation-endpoints-are-not-identities.md) and [the distinction between brewing and ceremonial time](../calendar/brewing-time-versus-ceremonial-time.md). Nothing here establishes a universal safe fermentation duration.
