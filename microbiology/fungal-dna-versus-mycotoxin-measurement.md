# Fungal DNA, toxin detection and beer quality answer different questions

## Detection methods are not interchangeable

Quantitative PCR can detect a specified fungal DNA target, while liquid chromatography and mass spectrometry can measure particular chemical molecules in a sample. Biehl and colleagues' 2026 barley-to-beer paper used methods from both families to compare fungal presence indicators with toxic metabolites. The very fact that the experiments require distinct assays is a warning against turning a fungal DNA number into a toxin concentration or a direct consumer safety score.[^v117-fusarium-trial]

Okechukwu and colleagues' 2026 method review examines established chromatography-based analyses alongside emerging sensor approaches for food and feed. A promising sensor on one matrix does not establish equivalent sensitivity, selectivity, sample recovery or field deployment across different beers, malts and grains.[^v117-food-mycotoxins]

## Sampling determines what can be concluded

A positive molecular result does not necessarily mean an organism is alive and growing at the later processing stage. A non-detection on a particular analytical panel means the analyte was not detected under the method's limits; it does not prove zero contamination, absence of all modified compounds or compliance with a regulation for another food category.

For a coherent wiki page, store what was sampled, method, DNA target or chemical analyte, date, units, limit of detection and source-specific bias. Avoid conflating a field plant disease, a laboratory spoilage experiment and a starter organism intentionally used in living fermentation.
