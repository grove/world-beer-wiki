# Borde: organisms changing during one sampled fermentation

What looks like “the microbes in borde” is actually a time-dependent series of samples. A 2024 investigation of Ethiopian **borde** examined microbiological, rheological and chemical properties in preparations collected from three locations. The article reports bacterial groups prominent early and yeast and lactic-acid bacteria dominating measured later stages.[^v18-borde]

## Why a final count hides the process

A laboratory colony count describes organisms recovered by a particular method at a particular stage. It does not identify the function of every detected taxon or establish that the detected organism caused an observed flavor. Similarly, a late-stage sample need not describe the earlier environmental or ingredient sources of microorganisms.

## The same beverage is also a food matrix

The study measured flow behavior and described shear-thinning properties. That physical structure can matter when comparing borde with clarified beer. Sensory similarity, microbial composition and grain use are different axes, so a wiki page should not collapse them into one “African sour beer” category.[^v18-borde]

## An important laboratory limit

The authors reported antioxidant and enzyme-inhibition measures, but in-vitro assays cannot prove a benefit from consuming the drink. Nor do observed changes establish uniform pathogen safety.
