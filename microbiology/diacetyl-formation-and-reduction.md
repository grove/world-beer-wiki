# Diacetyl: formation and removal during fermentation

Diacetyl is a vicinal diketone often associated with buttery aromas. In brewing it can arise when yeast-produced alpha-acetolactate is converted outside the cell and then later reduced by yeast as fermentation matures.[^v14-diacetyl]

## The important pathway

The review connects precursor production with valine metabolism, spontaneous precursor breakdown, and enzymatic reduction. Its central lesson is that a diacetyl measurement at one moment does not describe the later beer without knowing yeast activity and process state.[^v14-diacetyl]

## Edition 1.7: newly sourced connections

A 2013 amino-acid study connects alpha-acetohydroxy acid precursors with fermentation VDK levels and emphasizes variation in sensory thresholds. A 2018 adaptation experiment reported lower diacetyl for one selected lager-yeast isolate in pilot fermentation. Neither result establishes a universal maturation schedule or a yeast strain that guarantees a particular beer’s final aroma.[^v17-vdk-2013][^v17-vdk-2018]

The [VDK sensory guide](../sensory/vicinal-diketones-as-moving-target.md) distinguishes precursor formation, yeast-mediated reduction and panel perception. Measured concentration, tasting notes and fermentation stage are not interchangeable.
