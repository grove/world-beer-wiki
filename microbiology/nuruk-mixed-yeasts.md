# Nuruk-associated yeasts and the limits of laboratory strain screening

## Starters are a collection, not a single yeast

Korea's heritage announcement describes **nuruk** as a traditional fermentation starter that contributes starch-converting enzymes and fermentation organisms to makgeolli production. A mixed grain starter is not identical to the commercial yeast a brewery might choose after isolating one organism.[^v13-korea-heritage]

## What a new experiment measured

A 2026 study compared **63 *S. cerevisiae* and 23 non-*Saccharomyces* isolates** recovered from Korean starters and fermented products. It analyzed volatiles, organic acids and amino-acid-related compounds in laboratory culture, finding differences across the selected groups. Those are screenable metabolic traits—not a sensory census of village makgeolli served across Korea.[^v13-korea-yeast26]

## Why the whole environment matters

Read [Makgeolli making and sharing](../culture/makgeolli-making-and-sharing.md), [Makgeolli](../styles/makgeolli.md) and [Mould and mixed starters](../ingredients/mould-and-mixed-starters.md). In real production, temperature, grain, starter preparation and interactions between organisms matter as much as isolate names.

## Evidence boundaries

YPD laboratory cultures are not finished rice wine. No identified isolate is claimed as required by every maker. All dated observations are tied to the source and location described above. This is a research draft.
