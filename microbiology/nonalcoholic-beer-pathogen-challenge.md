# Challenge tests and the safety boundaries of alcohol-free beer

Alcohol, acidity, hop compounds and processing can interact to suppress some microorganisms in conventional beer. Removing most ethanol changes that combination, so a label such as 'beer' alone cannot stand in for microbiological validation. Çobo and colleagues experimentally challenged selected low- and nonalcoholic beer matrices with several foodborne pathogens under controlled pH, alcohol and storage conditions.[^v110-nab-hurdle]

## What was measured

The 2023 researchers inoculated prepared matrices with defined microbial cocktails and monitored them during storage at two temperatures for up to 63 days. In particular tested nonalcoholic conditions, *E. coli* O157:H7 and *Salmonella* showed growth at the warmer tested temperature, while the low-alcohol conditions behaved differently. Such results identify possible vulnerabilities in tested systems, not prevalence of pathogens in retail beer.[^v110-nab-hurdle]

## Why there is no single protective threshold here

A later 2026 review contrasts biological alcohol limitation and post-fermentation ethanol removal, describing their sensory and microbial consequences. Product formulation, package, processing controls and verified commercial safety systems all matter. It would be unsafe to use a single pH, ABV or temperature number from one challenge study as a home or industrial safety prescription.[^v110-nab-review]

## Evidence and limitations

No consumer safety instruction or process-control specification is offered. Challenge inoculation is a controlled laboratory method, not a description of ordinary commercial contamination. These limitations should be retained when quoting the article or extracting relationships.
