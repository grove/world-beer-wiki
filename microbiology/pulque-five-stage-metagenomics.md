# Pulque microbial succession in a five-stage metagenomic study

A 2020 shotgun-metagenomic paper sampled **five stages** of pulque fermentation and described changing bacterial and fungal communities. Its abstract reports species belonging to *Acinetobacter*, *Lactobacillus*, *Lactococcus*, *Leuconostoc*, *Saccharomyces* and *Zymomonas* that passed the study's abundance threshold in at least one stage. These are measurements within that design, not a six-genus list all makers must maintain.[^v111-pulque-stages]

## Chemistry and organisms change together

The authors report declining sucrose accompanied by increases in ethanol and lactic acid and changing relative organism abundances. The review of pulque production gives separate biochemical routes by which yeasts, bacteria and *Zymomonas* may contribute alcohol, acidity or viscosity. Together the sources explain plausible functions, but the sequencing alone cannot assign every metabolite to one organism or culture.[^v111-pulque-stages][^v111-pulque-review]

## What a five-stage study cannot answer

A relative read fraction is not a cell count; detection is not evidence of metabolic activity, and a metagenomic pathway prediction is not proof that a food confers health benefits. Reuse this article to ask precise questions about fermentation stage and species, rather than to prescribe an 'authentic' pulque starter or imply safety from diversity. Actual producer and local-language accounts remain essential for the cultural picture.[^v111-pulque-stages]
