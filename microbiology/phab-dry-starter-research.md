# Phab and neighboring Himalayan dry starters: 35 samples

A 2019 study examined **35 samples** of traditionally prepared Eastern Himalayan starters, including Bhutanese *phab*, *marcha*, *paa*, *pee* and *phut*. Researchers obtained and identified 201 bacterial isolates using phenotypic assays and 16S ribosomal sequencing. These are valuable data about bacteria cultured from sampled starter materials; they do **not** constitute the complete fungal and yeast inventory of every household starter.[^v19-phab]

## More than one kind of starter

The published account distinguishes a flattened, maize-based *phab* associated with Bhutanese ara production from rice-flour starters in several neighboring communities. Techniques, plants and their cultural names differ; “Himalayan starter” is therefore a navigation label, not a single conserved recipe or universal ethnic identity.[^v19-phab]

## Separating culture and laboratory evidence

The article’s isolations are biological observations. A Bhutan-based fieldwork abstract separately studies ara’s role in sociability and ritual. Neither source alone proves what live culture every producer uses, how a community interprets bacteria, or whether a finished alcoholic beverage is safe. Do not use the detection of lactic-acid bacteria as an endorsement of unknown starter preparation or medicinal effect.[^v19-bhutan-ara][^v19-phab]
