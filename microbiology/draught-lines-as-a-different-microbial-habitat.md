# Draught lines are not fermentation tanks

The word “microbes” does not automatically mean a planned fermentation. Brewers Association materials describe how finished beer passes through lines, couplers and faucets that require maintenance. Deposits in the service system are an operational quality concern, unlike intentionally selected yeast or bacteria in a well-described fermentation method.[^v114-draught]

A retailer-focused manual excerpt discusses organic soils and microbial biofilms accumulating in draught equipment. It distinguishes a sanitary service environment from the biological changes that produced a sour beer or farmhouse beer upstream. One cannot infer which microorganisms are present in a particular bar line, or that every organism detected would be pathogenic, simply from the existence of a general service guideline.[^v114-draught-retail]

Materials, cleaning records, time between service and beverage composition can all affect a system. The professional guidance discusses line care, equipment design, gas balance and dispensing rather than suggesting that a pub deliberately inoculate a new culture. It is a best-practice framework, not data from a systematic census of all venues.[^v114-draught]

These links prevent a common category error: treating post-packaging contamination as a tradition-specific ingredient.[^v114-draught-retail]
