# Kveik population history: new genomic evidence in 2026

Dondrup and colleagues published a **2026 genomic analysis of 64 isolates from 25 western Norwegian farmhouse brewing cultures**, sampled with explicit intergenerational cultural criteria. They describe kveik as an early-diverging, geographically structured group among domesticated *S. cerevisiae*, and discuss substantial uncertainty in older admixture interpretations.[^v16-kveik-2026]

## Two recent studies, different samples

A separate 2026 study examined **1,760 isolates from 44 European farmhouse cultures** spanning Norway, Latvia, Lithuania and Russia. It reported both nearly homogeneous household cultures and mixed populations with many variants. The two studies have different objectives, geography and methods. Their counts must not be added together as a census of all brewers, or interpreted as evidence of an exact invention date.[^v16-farmhouse-2026]

## What phylogeny cannot provide

Genetic divergence estimates are model-dependent and do not date the first time a particular named village beer was brewed. Genomes do not record a full ceremony, brewer identity, flavor or handling practice. The cultural sampling criteria and actual knowledge transmission deserve their own study alongside the phylogeny.[^v16-kveik-2026]

## Why this matters

Household-maintained cultures may be repositories of distinct genetic variation. Supporting continued local practice and preserving lawful, consent-aware scientific records are related but not identical goals.
