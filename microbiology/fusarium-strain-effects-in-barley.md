# Fusarium field trials: fungal strain, cultivar and year are separate variables

## A particular field experiment, not a universal species rule

Jayathissa and colleagues' 2024 Plant Pathology article reported work on two barley cultivars, **Newdale and AAC Goldman**, under disease-conducive field conditions across 2019–2021. Seven Fusarium graminearum isolates and control plots allowed the researchers to compare fungal strain and host variation. The severity and fungal density patterns differed by year, and the authors examined impacts during subsequent malting. The observations are not a map of every Canadian crop or a probability that a particular bottle will gush.[^v117-fungal-strains]

The broader 2022 Fusarium review establishes that different fungal metabolites can occur in cereal material and that grain-to-malt processing can modify their amounts. It does not allow the detected amount of fungal DNA in one field trial to stand for a specific toxin concentration or for viable growth in a finished beer.[^v117-fusarium-review]

## A scientific comparison needs its denominator

Disease severity, DNA abundance, grain yield, hydrolytic performance and chemically quantified mycotoxins have different units and study endpoints. A higher value in one cannot be substituted for another. Experimental inoculation is intentionally not representative of a randomly selected commercial crop lot.

For the wiki, retain **isolate identity, cultivar, field year, measured stage and assay** whenever discussing strain effects. Do not infer that the fungus is a maker-chosen fermenter, or that laboratory findings establish a recipe or human exposure level.
