# Where mahewu microorganisms came from in a 2019 study

A 2019 investigation by Pswarayi and Gänzle asked a deceptively simple question: **where do mahewu's fermenting organisms come from?** Millet malt was a plausible inoculum because cooked cereal porridge undergoes heating, whereas malt can enter after that step. The researchers combined culture-based methods, 16S sequencing and strain-specific quantitative PCR to track selected organisms in malt and fermented drink.[^v112-mahewu-2019]

## An ingredient can carry a community

The study detected selected *Lactobacillus fermentum* strains in millet malt and mahewu, supporting malt as a major microbial source in this system. Mahewu did not simply contain all malt-associated species in equal proportion: lactic acid bacteria became prominent while other groups were suppressed or did not persist. A raw-material community and a fermented-food community are not identical.[^v112-mahewu-2019]

## Temperature, sample and method caveats

The authors reported seasonal differences in sampled organisms; an organism present in summer but not winter at a particular site is not a universal Zimbabwean summer microbe. Their model inoculations and field measurements answer separate questions. DNA detection is not equivalent to proof of living cells, and decreasing one group is not a product safety certificate. Compare the newer 2026 starter design as experimental intervention rather than as the missing 'authentic' original recipe.[^v112-mahewu-2019]
