# Low-alcohol microbiological hurdles are not labeling thresholds

## A label reports a category, not a safety trial

US TTB guidance defines wording such as “non-alcoholic,” “alcohol free” and “low alcohol” for regulated malt-beverage labels. Its thresholds are legal descriptors; they are not laboratory measures of pathogen survival, product shelf stability or the quality of a manufacturing food-safety system.[^v115-alcohol]

Çobo and colleagues examined inoculated model low- and nonalcoholic beers under selected pH, ethanol and storage-temperature combinations over a defined observation period. Their results showed that survival or growth could occur under some of the tested conditions. The experiment supports the importance of validated process controls, not a prediction that every product sharing a nominal ABV will behave the same way.[^v115-challenge]

## Study setting versus product claim

The study adjusted matrices and deliberately introduced test organisms. It did not sample all packaged beers sold at retail or provide a compliance test for a particular brand. Conversely, a product can meet a labeling definition while its manufacturer still has to establish controls appropriate to its formulation, processing, packaging and distribution.

The scientific interpretation requires knowing the exact **test organism, matrix, treatment, environment, time and endpoint**. The legal interpretation requires a **jurisdiction, coverage, definition and date**. Conflating these evidence types could encourage unsafe generalizations.

This article provides no microbial culturing, preservation or safety bypass instructions; actual products require competent food-safety review.
