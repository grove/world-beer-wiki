# A Taiwanese millet ferment: 147 lactic-acid-bacterial isolates

Chao and colleagues examined a traditional Taiwanese millet or glutinous-rice alcoholic beverage in a 2013 study using cultivation, DNA fingerprinting and other molecular identification methods. They reported **147 lactic acid bacterial isolates** in **38 genetic fingerprint groups**. The work investigated both starter preparation and a later brewing stage rather than treating fermentation as one static microbe list.[^v112-taiwan-lab-2013]

## Stage affects what is found

The reported results connected *Pediococcus pentosaceus* with both sampled phases, while other lactic acid bacteria were more prominent during the beverage fermentation. An isolate count is not a cell count in every household, and a sequencing signature does not automatically establish metabolic responsibility for a sensory outcome. Taxonomy and method dates should accompany the reported organism names.[^v112-taiwan-lab-2013]

## The study's cultural limit

The accessible abstract characterizes its source generally as an Indigenous Taiwanese beverage but does **not** identify the makers as Paiwan. A separate Indigenous-affairs page describes Paiwan millet-wine offerings, yet it cannot supply missing provenance for this sample. This distinction guards against an especially misleading kind of wiki cross-link: making a community's ritual depend on a laboratory bottle with no documented community connection.[^v112-taiwan-lab-2013]
