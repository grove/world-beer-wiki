# Lactiplantibacillus plantarum in selected souring cultures

Lallemand identifies its WildBrew Sour Pitch preparation as **Lactiplantibacillus plantarum**, a lactic-acid bacterial culture selected for beer souring. The manufacturer lists kettle souring among its applications. This is direct evidence about that product and intended use—not a specification for every strain of the species.[^atlas-sour-pitch]

## Why the distinction matters

A species name identifies a biological grouping. A commercial preparation adds selection, manufacturing and a stated application. Published temperature, hop-tolerance or performance values belong to the material and conditions described; copying them into a species article as universal limits would erase those qualifications.[^atlas-sour-pitch]

For comparison, record the producer or isolate, substrate, hop exposure, stage of inoculation and measured outcome. A name alone cannot tell the reader when acidification occurred or which other organisms subsequently participated.

## What it does not identify

The use of a selected acidifying culture does not make a beer spontaneously fermented. Nor does the manufacturer's list of possible beer applications replace the identities of traditions such as lambic. See [Kettle souring](../processes/kettle-souring.md) for the process distinction and [Brewing bacteria](../ingredients/brewing-bacteria.md) for the broader context.

Compare [Lachancea](lachancea.md), where the acidifying organism is a yeast rather than a bacterium. Similar sourness is not a reliable organism-identification test.

## Evidence note

No health benefit, universal safety assurance, dosing schedule or home-isolation method is implied. The entry deliberately avoids extrapolating one marketed preparation's tolerance figures across a species.
