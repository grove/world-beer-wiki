# Lager yeast viability in a large-tank experiment

Yeast performance cannot be deduced from species alone. A 2025 industrial-brewery study investigated yeast multiplication and viability under **high-gravity lager fermentation** conditions, varying pitching, oxygen supply, filling time and temperature. The experiment was conducted at production scale but in a specified process window, not across every lager brewery or strain.[^v114-yeastplant]

The researchers used response-surface methods to separate the effects of their chosen factors. Their abstract reports statistically detectable effects of several inputs on biomass and viability and a more qualified result for temperature within the study's narrow range. That should not be inverted into a universal claim that temperature never matters to yeast: temperature may matter for other strains, sensory compounds, process scales and ranges.[^v114-yeastplant]

This is a useful counterpart to studies of traditional mixed starters. Industrial yeast counts, pitch rates and viability tests ask whether a managed population behaves reproducibly. A household-maintained mixed starter can raise different questions about species coexistence, sourcing and transmission. Those two kinds of evidence are complementary but not equivalent.[^v114-yeastplant]
