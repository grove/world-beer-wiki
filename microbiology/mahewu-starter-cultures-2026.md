# Mahewu starter cultures in a 2026 controlled study

A 2026 open-access study sought starter combinations for mahewu production in settings without reliable frozen supply chains. It sequenced **six finger-millet malt samples** and constructed mixed-community fermentations to compare spontaneous conditions with selected lactic acid bacterial additions. The methods deliberately created laboratory systems; results are about those systems, not every rural household.[^v112-mahewu-2026]

## Fermentation at two temperatures behaves differently

The study compared **15°C and 25°C** fermentations. In the no-starter models, Enterobacteriaceae remained prominent considerably longer at the lower temperature. In supplemented models, the dominance and performance of different lactic acid bacteria also varied with temperature; *Lactiplantibacillus plantarum*, *Limosilactobacillus fermentum* and *Weissella* occupy different niches in different conditions. This is an important mechanism, not a recommendation to incubate homemade drinks at a specified temperature.[^v112-mahewu-2026]

## Drying and safety questions remain

Researchers assessed air-dried and freeze-dried starter storage. Stored cultures showed changing acidification activity; the paper does not supply a blanket safety validation for arbitrary raw materials, real households, or community practices. Amplicon identification of potentially concerning organisms is evidence of DNA, not confirmation that viable pathogens were present. The earlier strain-tracking work is a valuable comparator, not a licence to prescribe a community's starter.[^v112-mahewu-2026][^v112-mahewu-2019]
