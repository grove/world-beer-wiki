# Kunun-zaki microbiology during steeping and fermentation

Efiuvwevwere and Akona followed microbiota associated with a millet-based kunun-zaki process. The PubMed abstract describes an initially broad microflora and shifts following steeping, eventually with *Lactobacillus* species prominent. It also reports yeast, including *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*, in the acidic ferment. The observations support a dynamic process, not a universal inoculation list.[^v111-kunun-cultures]

## Why method changes interpretation

Steeping, wet milling, heating and fermentation create changing habitats. A separate 2015 food study used a defined fortified millet protocol and documented partial gelatinization and short fermentation. Those are not identical datasets. Combining the 1995 culture trajectory with the 2015 ingredients to invent a single necessary Nigerian procedure would mistake comparison for reproduction.[^v111-kunun-cultures][^v111-kunun-study]

## Health and category limits

A drop in pH or disappearance of culturable organisms in one protocol does not prove absence of every hazard; laboratory culture methods also cannot detect all species. Kunun-zaki is often represented as non-alcoholic, but a named beverage's real alcohol content and microbial safety cannot be certified without relevant product measurements. The most valuable link is between grain processing, low-degree fermentation and everyday food-drink context.[^v111-kunun-cultures]
