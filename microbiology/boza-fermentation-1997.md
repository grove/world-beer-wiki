# What changed in a 24-hour Turkish boza fermentation

A 1997 Turkish study deliberately prepared boza from cooked maize, wheat and rice flours and followed fermentation for 24 hours. The authors tracked increasing yeast and lactic acid bacterial populations, decreasing pH, and increasing ethanol. Their reported endpoint **0.79% ethanol** is a measurement from that preparation, not an upper bound imposed by the tradition's name.[^v112-boza-1997]

## The same time course answers several questions

The measured organisms belonged to several culturable groups, including multiple lactic acid bacteria and yeasts identified under the taxonomy and methods used at the time. A mixed culture can produce sourness and alcohol concurrently, but a 24-hour growth curve is not evidence that each organism contributed equally to each measured metabolite. The food's starting substrate, inoculation and analytical methods remain part of the result.[^v112-boza-1997]

## Why this matters to readers

Many descriptions call boza 'nonalcoholic' while microbiologists identify low but nonzero ethanol in particular experiments. Neither statement should be silently upgraded to a regulatory definition across all markets. Compare this time course with Bulgarian isolate genetics: culture enumeration of a mixture and genome sequencing of three selected strains are different study designs, neither a census of all Balkan boza.[^v112-boza-1997]
