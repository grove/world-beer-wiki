# Lachancea and acid-producing brewing yeast

**A sour beer does not necessarily require a bacterial souring stage.** Lallemand describes WildBrew Philly Sour as a selected *Lachancea* culture capable of producing lactic acid alongside ethanol. The public page identifies it as *Lachancea spp.*; this reference does not silently replace that wording with an assumed species name.[^atlas-philly-sour]

## Similar sensory result, different biological route

Compare the selected bacterial preparation discussed under [Lactiplantibacillus plantarum](lactiplantibacillus-plantarum.md). Both entries concern acidifying brewing cultures, but one describes a yeast and the other a bacterium. Genus identity, commercial culture and production method should remain separate records.

The producer's aroma and performance descriptions also belong to its product. They are not evidence that every member of *Lachancea* behaves identically in every wort.[^atlas-philly-sour]

## A classification trap

A modern sour-beer description can emphasize the result without requiring one piece of equipment or one microbial pathway. Consult [Straight sour beer](../styles/straight-sour-beer.md) for the category and [Kettle souring](../processes/kettle-souring.md) for a particular process. The two should not be treated as synonyms.

This distinction enables a better retrieval question: “Which documented process produced the acidity?” rather than “Which sour style proves the beer contains bacteria?”

## Evidence note

This entry reports a manufacturer's technical description, not a controlled comparison performed for the wiki. It omits product dosage, universal temperature limits and health claims. The manufacturer's suggestions for style applications do not establish historical equivalence with those named traditions.
