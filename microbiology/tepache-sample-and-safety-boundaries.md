# Tepache microbiota and single-batch safety limits

A 2022 study reports a complex microbial ecosystem in sampled pineapple-based tepache, alongside lactic acid and other chemical measures. It also describes **predicted** metabolic functions. Such predictions guide hypotheses but do not demonstrate a clinical probiotic effect or a universal set of microorganisms in market and homemade tepache.[^v111-tepache-ecology]

## An instructive sample-size problem

A later 2025 teaching/research project analyzed one household batch using cultured counts, simulated gastric-fluid treatment and antibiotic-resistance assays. Its headline finding about resistant organisms deserves careful reporting, but one prepared batch cannot estimate community-level prevalence, prove all tepache unsafe, or show resistance genes will transfer after drinking. Laboratory conditions and consumed dose are separate questions.[^v111-tepache-one]

## Turn a warning into better questions

Proposed future work could compare sites and seasons, ingredient sanitation, handling, actual dose and culture dynamics. Those studies would require appropriate experts and participant consent. For now this page is a warning against converting a 'probiotic' slogan, a species label or one resistance assay into medical, food-safety or regulatory advice. See the broader wiki guide on why fermentation's presence does not imply safe water or food.[^v111-tepache-one]
