# Monascus-like microfossils and ancient fermentation inference

## The evidence is morphological

Researchers examining pottery from Shangshan compared recovered fungal structures with reference **Monascus** mold and yeast morphology. Alongside rice and other starch remains, their analysis supports a hypothesis of mold-assisted fermentation about 10,000–9,000 calibrated years before present. This is inference from structures and archaeological context, not a sequenced ancient culture revived in a laboratory.[^v13-shangshan]

## Why qu matters

The researchers interpret the coexistence of particular fungal forms and grain residues as consistent with a *qu*-type starter, which helps starch become fermentable. Calling it 'koji' without qualification would replace a Chinese archaeological interpretation with a later Japanese production term; direct continuity from the ancient site to a contemporary starter is unproven.[^v13-shangshan]

## Evidence boundaries

Morphological analogies are not a direct strain ID; prehistoric cultural continuity, a complete recipe and ritual details are not demonstrated by this study. All dated observations are tied to the source and location described above. This is a research draft.
