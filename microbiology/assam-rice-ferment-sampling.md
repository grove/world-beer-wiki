# Reading the 2024 southern Assam rice-ferment microbiome survey

Yumnam, Hazarika and Sharma's 2024 study used **Illumina MiSeq** to examine microbial communities in rice-fermented beverages collected from five groups in southern Assam. It explicitly states 15 collected samples, three per cohort, with market products excluded. Those details are the foundation for any responsible interpretation of its result tables.[^v16-assam]

## Detected DNA is not the same as causal activity

The published results discuss bacterial taxa such as Pediococcus, Lactobacillus, Bacillus, Leuconostoc and Acetobacter across samples. Detection or relative sequence abundance does **not** establish which species produced ethanol, which transformed starch, or whether a particular detected organism was alive and active in a particular preparation. Nor does it establish probiotic benefit.[^v16-assam]

## A later but different research question

A 2026 Assam investigation connects microbial composition with metabolites in rohi modh, sai mod and jou-bidwi. These named drinks and that design differ from the 2024 five-group sample; do not merge them into one synthetic dataset or universal recipe.[^v16-assam-2026]

## Read beside community history

Sampling names carry cultural information, but community descriptions should be verified through their own witnesses. Compare the situated study account and local drink pages before using the microbiome table as an explanation of a whole tradition.
