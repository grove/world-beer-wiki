# Nuruk in 42 samples: diversity, not a universal starter

A Korean study profiled **42 samples of traditional nuruk**, reporting many bacterial, fungal and yeast taxa. Its indexed abstract notes that the often-cited yeast *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* was isolated in only 18 of the 42, and that the familiar mold *Aspergillus oryzae* was found in 13. That finding is striking—but it does not mean makgeolli lacks alcoholic fermentation or that any one isolate is compulsory in a batch.[^v19-nuruk42]

## What did the samples measure?

A culture and microflora survey identifies organisms recoverable under the chosen methods and timing. It may miss low-abundance species, dormant components or organisms active earlier in grain fermentation. The authors also noted potentially concerning organisms in some samples, but screening results are not a safety certificate for another producer’s starter or finished drink.[^v19-nuruk42]

## Why fermentation studies can disagree

A separate sequencing study followed a makgeolli brewing process and described dynamic microbial changes, with *Saccharomyces* prominent in its monitored fermentation. Comparing that **process trajectory** with a **starter sample collection** is not a contradiction; the substrate, phase, sample selection and measurement differ. A starter’s initial species list cannot substitute for a time series of biochemical activity.[^v19-makgeolli-seq]
