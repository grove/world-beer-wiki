# Non-Saccharomyces isolates in Abidjan sorghum beer

A particular Côte d’Ivoire isolate study; no assumption that all African sorghum beers share these strains.

## Why look beyond Saccharomyces?

A microbiology study sampled traditional sorghum beer in the Abidjan district of Côte d’Ivoire and investigated non-*Saccharomyces* isolates. The researchers identified organisms reported under *Issatchenkia orientalis* and *Pichia kudriavzevii* naming, and examined their fermentation potential. Taxonomic synonyms and sequence methods require care when interpreting the names.[^v15-cotedivoire-2022]

## A candidate strain is not a tradition

The selected isolates showed particular performance under the study’s conditions. That is an experimental observation about samples—not evidence that one organism historically originated tchapalo, that all makers pitch it, or that the study establishes a preferred commercial starter.[^v15-cotedivoire-2022]

## Comparative relevance

The study makes a useful counterpart to Himalayan dry starters and European mixed fermentation: microbial communities have composition and function, but a community’s beverage has additional social and material identity. A presence/absence table is not a global beer classification.[^v15-cotedivoire-2022]
