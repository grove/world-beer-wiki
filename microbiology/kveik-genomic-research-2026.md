# What 2026 genomics revealed about Norwegian kveik

## Family history and genomic history are different

A 2026 study sequenced **64 isolates from 25 traditional western Norwegian cultures** selected by cultural-heritage criteria. It identified geographic structure and an early-diverging group of domesticated *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* strains. These are population-genetic inferences, not recovered centuries-old yeast specimens from named farms.[^v13-kveik26]

## What earlier experiments found

The 2018 study analyzed yeast from nine Norwegian sources and described traits including flocculation, POF-negative phenotypes in its samples, and warm-fermentation performance. The studies differ in sampling and methods. Their results do not make every commercially sold 'kveik' a faithful representation of an inherited household culture, nor predict every strain's flavor at every temperature.[^v13-kveik18]

## Evidence boundaries

The 2026 paper’s PubMed abstract supports sample sizes and genomic conclusions, not exact family timelines or a universal yeast temperature recommendation. All dated observations are tied to the source and location described above. This is a research draft.

## Edition 1.6: sourced research connections

An additional, independent **August 2026** paper (Dondrup and colleagues) evaluated 64 isolates derived from 25 culturally selected western Norwegian brewing cultures. It proposed early-diverging phylogenetic placement and argued that earlier apparent admixture signals require correction for population structure and selection. It does not resolve the birthplace or calendar date of kveik domestication.[^v16-kveik-2026]

See [the side-by-side study comparison](kveik-population-history-2026.md). Do not conflate this 25-culture study with the other 2026 multi-country 44-culture investigation.
