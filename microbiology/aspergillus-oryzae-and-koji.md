# Aspergillus oryzae and rice koji

In the sake process described by the Japan Sake and Shochu Makers Association, **rice koji** is steamed rice on which **Aspergillus oryzae** has been cultivated. The fungus, the rice carrying it, and the resulting enzyme activity are connected concepts, not interchangeable names.[^atlas-jss-koji]

## A division of labor

The association explains that koji contributes enzymes that break down rice starch and protein, while alcoholic yeast participates in the fermentation. This is why calling koji merely “the yeast” obscures what the brewing material does.[^atlas-jss-koji]

For a reader following [Sake](../styles/sake.md), the essential comparison is with [starch conversion](../processes/starch-conversion.md): where do the enzymes come from, what substrate do they act on, and when is the released sugar fermented?

## Do not flatten starter traditions

The broader [mould and mixed starter reference](../ingredients/mould-and-mixed-starters.md) keeps named preparations distinct. It is not justified to rename nuruk, marcha, ragi or ÉPOP “koji” merely because they participate in grain fermentation. Similar biological problems can be addressed by differently maintained materials and knowledge systems.

## Evidence note

This page is limited to a named producer association's sake explanation. It is not a guide to recognizing safe molds by appearance, a claim that every Aspergillus species is appropriate for food use, or instructions to cultivate unidentified material. The presence of fungal growth alone establishes none of those things.
