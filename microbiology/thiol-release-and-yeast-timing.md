# Thiol release is time-dependent and strain-dependent

A yeast strain can affect beer aroma even when the yeast itself is not described by a fruity flavor. In a 2026 pilot-scale study, researchers followed polyfunctional-thiol production and yeast gene expression through fermentation with two commercial strains. Most production in non-dry-hopped trials peaked early, but the timing, response to dry hopping, and finished amount depended on conditions and strain.[^v18-thiol-time]

## A transcript is not a molecule

The authors sequenced expression patterns of candidate enzymes and transporters while measuring thiol compounds. They explicitly found that transcript abundance alone did not fully explain which strain released which amount. That is a useful methodological warning: a gene name or an expression result cannot by itself predict the sensory character of a commercial beer.[^v18-thiol-time]

## Some transformation continues after fermentation

The paper also detected further increases in particular thiols during post-fermentation processing. Aroma generation is therefore not always confined to the period when vigorous alcoholic fermentation is visible. But it would be wrong to turn that observation into a recommended storage schedule or a guarantee of aromatic improvement.

## Chemistry before recipe

An earlier analytical paper demonstrated disulfide-bound thiol reservoirs in malt and hops under a different experimental approach. Read [precursors](../ingredients/polyfunctional-thiol-precursors.md) beside [dry-hop enzymes](../processes/dry-hopping-and-hop-creep.md) and [sensory thiols](../sensory/polyfunctional-thiols-and-aroma.md). Thiol release and *hop creep* are distinct biochemical phenomena; a similarly timed change does not equate them.[^v18-thiol-bound]
