# Lager yeast and hybrid ancestry

The wiki's existing yeast reference describes *Saccharomyces pastorianus* as having ancestry from *S. cerevisiae* and the cold-tolerant *S. eubayanus*, following Libkind and colleagues' 2011 research abstract. The hybrid is a biological subject; “lager” also names brewing practices and beer families.[^atlas-yeast-overview]

## What ancestry does—and does not—explain

Identifying parental ancestry does not by itself identify the historical person who first brewed with a hybrid, date a particular brewery's practice, or prove the route by which organisms moved. A genomic explanation and an archival explanation can support one another, but neither should be silently substituted for the other.

Likewise, organism identity and operating conditions are separate records. The [California Common](../styles/california-common.md) profile describes a modern style reference involving lager yeast at relatively warm fermentation conditions. That example warns against identifying yeast species from temperature alone.[^atlas-california-context]

## A better comparison

Compare [S. cerevisiae](saccharomyces-cerevisiae.md) at the species/lineage level, then compare beers at the level of process and sensory outcome. Ask which culture was used, under what conditions, and what the producer calls the beer. A familiar style label is not a laboratory identification.

## Edition 1.14: newly sourced connections

Hybrid ancestry establishes an organism's evolutionary relationships, not how every individual industrial pitch will perform. A 2025 lager fermentation study examined **pitching rate, wort aeration, filling duration and fermentation temperature** in a production-scale high-gravity setting. Its measured cell viability is a process-conditioned endpoint; it is not a taxonomic rule.[^v114-yeastplant]

Read [the industrial yeast study](industrial-lager-yeast-viability.md) and [the tank process](../processes/industrial-lager-aeration-and-filling.md) alongside genomic accounts. A cultivar, species, strain and managed yeast population operate at different descriptive levels.[^v114-yeastplant]
