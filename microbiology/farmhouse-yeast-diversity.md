# Farmhouse yeast is not always a single strain

## What a culture can contain

Researchers in 2026 analyzed **1,760 isolates from 44 farmhouse cultures** maintained in Norway, Latvia, Lithuania and Russia. Their abstract reports a range from near-monocultures to systems containing more than 30 genetically distinct *S. cerevisiae* variants. This makes 'the yeast' a potentially misleading singular noun for a family-maintained fermentation community.[^v13-farmhouse26]

## Why preservation matters

The team reports geographic structuring and features different from industrial-lineage patterns, including variation in stress response, genetic makeup and reproductive ability. Preservation of the actual culture and its custodianship can therefore conserve diversity that may disappear if a commercial isolate alone is retained.[^v13-farmhouse26]

## What not to conclude

The sample spans four countries, not every farmhouse tradition worldwide. It does not mean that every starter contains bacteria or mold, or that diversity is automatically better or safer. Compare [Kveik](../ingredients/kveik.md), [S. cerevisiae](../microbiology/saccharomyces-cerevisiae.md) and [culture storage](../objects/yeast-storage-tools.md).

## Evidence boundaries

Genomic research identifies sampled lineages and laboratory traits. It does not authenticate folk claims of fixed brewing dates or medical benefit. All dated observations are tied to the source and location described above. This is a research draft.
