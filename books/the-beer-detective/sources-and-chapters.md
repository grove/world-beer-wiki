### Claims requiring care and unresolved questions

- **Three Raqefet mortars produced a proposed cereal-brewing interpretation around 13,000 years ago.** Interpretation of residue, wear and experimental comparison, not surviving liquid; chronology alone does not establish motive for cultivation.

- **Malting and starch conversion help explain why grain possession alone does not establish brewing.** General mechanism is explanatory background, not an ancient operating recipe; no modern temperature schedule is projected backward.

- **Published objections and a 2019 response concern starch morphology, mortar use and brewing reconstruction.** Critic's position encountered through responding authors, not the complete original critique; dispute remains unresolved in this chapter.

- **A separate Raqefet study interprets flowering-plant impressions as grave linings.** Burial preparation supports context, not independent proof of brewing or a recoverable ceremony.

- **Cultivation and the gradual appearance of domesticated wheat traits need not coincide.** Abstract-only scope; study does not identify alcohol as a cause of agriculture.

- **Shubayqa 1 preserves bread-like plant foods around 14,400 years ago.** Earliest surviving evidence is not an invention date; no direct competition between beer and bread is inferred.

### Claims requiring care and unresolved questions

- **Isoamyl acetate can be produced by yeast without banana fruit; molecular resemblance does not establish ingredient provenance.** The compound is one contributor to banana-like aroma, not a universal complete description of it.

- **The 1985 banana-pulp study identified alcohol acetyltransferase activity producing isoamyl acetate and increasing during ripening.** Abstract-level access; no detailed kinetic or purification claim beyond that record.

- **Deleting both ATF1 and ATF2 eliminated detectable isoamyl acetate in the tested laboratory strain; other esters remained.
