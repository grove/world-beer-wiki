# Publishing materials

## Back-cover description

Why does a beer smell of bananas when no banana was added? What can an ancient drinking vessel tell us after the drink has vanished? And how did a city's complaint about bad beer become a famous creation story?

*The Beer Detective* follows twenty-four investigations through archaeology, chemistry, household enterprise and the politics of a good story. A hymn becomes a disputed recipe. Women take down the signs outside their businesses. A communal brewery sends its unfinished drink to many front doors. A sealed bottle continues to change.

Traveling from ancient Mesopotamia to present-day Ghana, Korea, Tanzania and Peru, the book asks what survives in an account, a microorganism, a customary practice or a laboratory result. Its clues lead beyond the familiar histories of celebrated brewers to the people who carry ingredients, maintain shared equipment and keep crops in use.

These are stories for anyone who enjoys finding out how we know what we think we know. Some mysteries resolve neatly. Others become more interesting when the easy answer falls apart. The evidence supplies the surprises.

## Promotional description — 50 words

Beer smells of fruit never added, carries stories older than its evidence, and depends on work easily overlooked. The Beer Detective follows twenty-four investigations across archaeology, chemistry and living traditions, uncovering how drinks acquire their flavors, histories and reputations, and what a careful look can reveal beyond the familiar glass.

## Project biography

*The Beer Detective* is a World Beer Wiki book project: an original narrative nonfiction manuscript developed from the repository's research routes and separately checked sources. Research, drafting, internal editing and production were assisted by AI. This complete review edition awaits independent human editorial, specialist and community review.
