# Fresh framing review: introduction, afterword and research note

Completed 28 October 2026 by the AI reviewer responsible for the Part IV cross-review, after reading both `front-matter.md` and `back-matter.md` in full. This is an internal editorial pass, not a human reader response or specialist endorsement.

## Purpose and outcome

The opening should invite the reader into the investigations, the afterword should leave a useful perspective and an ending, and the research note should hold the detailed account of method and limits. The original draft gave all three some of the same methodological work. The edit consolidates routine disclosure without hiding it.

The introduction retains its banana-aroma opening, the twenty-four-case itinerary and the final turn toward the stone in chapter 1. It now contains **973 words**, including its heading attributes under the manuscript counting convention, and excluding its two footnote definitions. This is within the requested 900–1,150-word range; no material was added solely to reach a length target.

The afterword retains the empty-glass frame, its movement between chemical and social scales, the value of specific questions, and the choice to enjoy or decline a drink. Its closing paragraphs were left intact. Its length is now **490 words** under the same convention, down from 572. The acknowledgments are 98 words and the research note 584.

## Direct changes

- Condensed the edition's AI and review-status paragraph while retaining the absence of independent human editorial, specialist and community review and the reliance on published work rather than new field visits or interviews.
- Replaced an introduction sentence defending the absence of invented dialogue with concrete previews already supported by chapters 13, 14 and 17: a sign taken down, wort moved between buildings and overlapping microbial work.
- Recast the living-tradition paragraph around continuing decisions, present-day enterprises and futures under discussion. It preserves the cultural point without repeating a defensive contrast between Europe and everyone else.
- Shortened the introduction's source-access discussion to a useful invitation to follow the notes, directing methodological detail to the research note.
- Removed the afterword's second limitations paragraph. The edition statement and research note already disclose the same absent fieldwork and human review.
- Removed the acknowledgments' repeated statement that no reviewers were engaged and its defensive statement about people who were not contacted. The surviving text acknowledges cited contributions and explicitly separates citation from endorsement.
- Corrected the research note's Raqefet account: objections are presented through the researchers' published response; the original commentary was not fully accessed. The previous wording could imply direct reading of both sides.
- Clarified the Pilsen conflict as later accounts assigning agency differently, rather than wording that could suggest an unexplained disagreement about the event date.
- Changed “repository draft” to “repository manuscript” for the climate source comparison, matching chapter 24's note without asserting a particular draft status beyond the record.
- Removed the unverified formatting promise that notes are numbered afresh within each chapter and link back in every edition. The text now accurately states their function without depending on a particular PDF/EPUB numbering configuration.

## Editorial judgment

The introduction now spends more of its space on what the reader will notice and less on how the manuscript defends itself. The afterword's ending remains connected to the field, organism, carrier and brewer; it no longer interrupts that movement with a duplicated review-status statement. The detailed research note continues to state restricted access, AI involvement, absent human consultation and the most important needs for further review.

These edits improve division of purpose among the framing sections. They do not claim that the remaining human editorial, scientific, historical, source-language or community-informed work has been completed.
