Reviewed 28 October 2026. This is an internal AI developmental, source, prose and cultural-scope review of chapters 13–18. It is not independent human scholarship, scientific peer review, brewer consultation, legal advice or community endorsement.

## Developmental pass

All six chapters were read against their chosen investigation. Openings and conclusions were checked for an identifiable question and a resolved or properly bounded consequence. The sequence moves from documentary visibility through cooperative infrastructure, livelihood inputs and household exchange to mixed starters and complementary preparations. The chapters are complete narrative essays rather than outlines.

The strongest new turn is in chapter 17: the makgeolli experiment included a separate commercial yeast starter. The apparent conflict with a dry-nuruk survey therefore cannot be presented as a natural succession puzzle solved by speculation. That methodological correction now drives the chapter's opening and its argument about categories of evidence.

Chapter 18 was strengthened beyond the wiki's single-paper account by following the field study's references to independent enzyme experiments and Bupigu brewing research, then using landscape and anthropological abstracts for precisely bounded context. Its opening centers the warm liquid reserved during mso preparation rather than describing an invented arrival in a village.

## Prose pass

The chapters were checked for invented scenery, cinematic weather, implied travel, composite people and dialogue. None is supplied. Named historical and living people appear only where published sources identify them. Short hypotheticals about inspection archives, distant beer imitation, ingredient tradeoffs and preservation are explicitly framed as reasoning.

## Cultural and hostile-reading pass

The following objections were applied to the draft, with the resulting boundaries retained:

- **“Communal” sounds automatically open and equal.** Chapter 14 explicitly distinguishes shared facilities from unrestricted rights and does not infer internal equality.
- **Women are being turned into universal custodians of tradition.** Chapters 13, 15 and 16 describe documented work, economic constraints and distinct situations. Neither historical exclusion nor contemporary persistence makes every woman a representative hero.
- **Present makers are being used as fossils for ancient people.** Chapter 16 treats analogy as a source of testable possibilities. Electric milling makes change part of the evidence.
- **Labor is being praised while its material constraints disappear.** Chapter 15 keeps water, capital, time and supply relationships central. It does not present resilience as an adequate substitute for infrastructure.
- **Scientific naming replaces maker knowledge.** Chapters 17 and 18 distinguish measurements from the skills and judgments needed to prepare and use materials. The analysis does not claim that laboratory recognition created the knowledge.
- **Local terms have become generic cultural decoration.** Nyalu/mso and nyambo/kikonde remain attached to Kilema and Bupigu respectively; Rombo's anthropological argument remains separate. Himalayan starter names do not establish common genealogy.
- **Heritage recognition proves universal agreement.** Neither national designation nor the Piura ceremony is claimed to certify every practice, settle distribution of income or endorse the book.
- **A biological or food-safety claim sneaks in through a source's rhetoric.** Unsupported health assertions and claims that acidity/fermentation guarantees safety were not adopted. Msesewe is discussed as a research observation, not a plant-use recommendation.
