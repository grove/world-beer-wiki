## Selection principles

Each investigation begins with a manageable discrepancy rather than a catalogue of myths. Yeast appears in an earlier legal record despite its absence from the famous list. Two accounts supply different actors for the same beer disposal. A striking field comparison cannot identify which preparation stage caused the difference. A visual expectation does not necessarily persist when the drink is tasted.

The chapters then change scale. Legal ingredients lead to regulation and identity; a dated batch leads to rights, retailing and transport; a test plate leads to preparation and collaboration; a rating leads to different purposes for sensory judgment. Those changes make a complete narrative from the evidence without inventing a visit, scene or private motive.

The chapter 20 selection preserves variation among source accounts instead of constructing a synthetic riot. Chapter 21 gives a living drink its own setting rather than requiring it to satisfy a European beer definition. Chapter 22 includes a convergence result so that perception does not become a one-directional story of helpless consumers. Chapter 19 separates the value of a chosen brewing constraint from extravagant claims made in its name.

## Source coverage and access provenance

The accompanying register has thirty-three substantive claims: eight for chapter 19, nine for 20, eight for 21 and eight for 22. Its source objects distinguish the chapter drafter's recorded access from sources independently reopened by the fresh reviewer. A source repeated in several claim records remains one source; repetition is not independent corroboration.

The fresh reviewer independently consulted the Library of Congress legal-history article, Rupprecht's historical entry, Rail's complete extracted PDF, the complete masato article, the pathogen study's PubMed abstract, the CDC page, the 2017 sensory article and indexed 2022 sensory excerpts. Other access remains exactly as recorded in the drafter's chapter notes. In particular, there is no newly claimed full reading of the 1987 judgment, original Czech founding documents, original 1893 case report, or restricted 2021/2022 sensory papers.

Primary and institutional sources are used within their strengths. A brewer's chronology records the producer's historical account; it does not prove unchanging taste. A historian's translation provides access to an earlier document; it is not a new archival inspection. A challenge experiment informs a defined microbial question; it is not a field result for another beverage. A judging guide makes practical concerns visible without becoming a controlled experiment.

## Deliberate exclusions

These chapters do not supply current labeling advice, live opening information, an operating recipe, a water-treatment procedure or a safety test. They do not infer an accident from a surprising product, universal sensory equivalence from a nonsignificant test, or population-wide health outcomes from a small exploratory sample. These boundaries follow the actual questions and evidence, rather than substituting a warning for a narrative.

The book has not interviewed the named participants or sought community approval. The Shawi project's participatory work belongs to the original researchers and community; it cannot be transferred to this manuscript as an endorsement. Human editorial, specialist and community-informed review remains future work.
