# Part II cross-part editorial review

Completed 28 October 2026 by the Part I drafting agent, after the Part II drafting agent explicitly relinquished chapters 07–12 for this pass. This was a fresh, complete reading of those six narratives and their notes, followed by targeted direct editing and source checks. It is internal AI review by an agent other than the original drafter, not independent human review or specialist peer review.

## Overall assessment

The six chapters sustain distinct scientific questions and generally preserve the strongest necessary limitations: the genetic experiment is strain-specific; IBU is an analytical proxy rather than a complete sensory report; photochemical models are not ordinary bottle trials; particular cysteinylated aldehydes failed as the proposed staling source; the barley field trial did not find a significant fungal-density/gushing relationship; nanobubble identity is inferred; and lager's historical itinerary remains unresolved.

The principal developmental weakness was repetition of a study summary followed by a generic caution and an analogy. In several places the narration explained why the preceding paragraph deserved inclusion rather than advancing the investigation. Selective edits replace those pauses with a next question, a concrete contrast, or a tighter causal account. Existing access limits remain in the notes. No source was promoted from abstract to full-text access without a corresponding actual retrieval.

## Targeted factual rechecks

These were independent of the original drafting agent's access. They were targeted checks, not fresh complete literature reviews of every chapter:

- Kishimoto et al., [IBU contribution study](https://doi.org/10.1080/03610470.2021.1878684): publisher-indexed abstract confirmed 47.5/49.9 IBU, 39.9/2.2 ppm iso-alpha acid, and lower perceived bitterness in the deteriorated-hop comparison at equal IBU. Full text remained inaccessible for this pass.
- Bustillo Trueba et al., [free and cysteinylated aldehydes](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodres.2020.110049): publisher-indexed abstract, highlights, and introduction confirmed the specific negative finding. The chapter's distinction between the general bound-state idea and the failed specific source was preserved.
- Deckers et al., [DLS and primary gushing](https://doi.org/10.1094/ASBCJ-2011-0524-01): publisher abstract explicitly distinguishes approximately 100-nanometer particles from the proposed hydrophobin-stabilized nanobubble identity. It also supports the carbonated/still-water contrast and describes the proteins as amphiphilic.
- Jayathissa et al., [field and malting study](https://doi.org/10.1111/ppa.13918): extensive publisher-indexed methods, results and discussion were inspected, including §2.6, §§3.7–3.8, and the discussion. These confirm the common-beer-batch assay and the non-significant fungal-density relationship. The alternative role of other fungi remains a hypothesis.
- Bergin et al., [European isolates](https://doi.org/10.1093/femsyr/foac053): publisher and PMC abstracts confirmed the two Irish strains, Holarctic placement, and comparison with Tibetan ancestry. This supports the narrower modern-distribution wording, not a medieval route.
- Hutzler et al., [lager-origin hypothesis](https://doi.org/10.1093/femsyr/foad023): PubMed and the authors' institutional abstract confirmed the proposed Munich interval of 1602–1615 and the hypothesized introduction of *S. cerevisiae* into an environment containing *S. eubayanus*. The chapter already framed this correctly as a hypothesis, so no stronger wording was introduced.

## Cultural and hostile-reader assessment

Retained Tonto as a named Ugandan beverage with its own ingredients and practices, rather than treating a shared aroma molecule as evidence that it is interchangeable with European wheat beer. Preserved the distinction between using a culture skillfully and identifying its genome. The nanobubble and gene-deletion cases retain what the experiments actually establish without becoming diagnoses of every bottle or every fruity aroma.

The main hostile-reader questions were: could a single description be mistaken for an ingredient audit; could equal IBU be mistaken for equal sensation; could light damage be confused with all sulfur aromas; could a failed bound-aldehyde candidate be presented as confirmed; could a negative field correlation negate purified-protein causation; and could modern geography be mistaken for an early migration itinerary? The revised text maintains those boundaries. No invented visits, conversations, experiments by the book's narrator, or sensory experiences were found.

## Checks and remaining limits

After editing, all chapter footnote references still matched their definitions, no Part II note IDs were duplicated, and all wiki routes existed. No new external source was required for a new narrative claim: the added assay detail belongs to the already cited field-study methods. No raw analytical data, genomes, images, or statistical models were reanalysed. Human sensory-science, brewing-chemistry, plant-pathology, yeast-genetics, and cultural review remains outstanding.

This cross-pass improves the prose but does not remove the book's deliberately investigative character. A final whole-book editor should watch for the same pattern recurring across parts: a compelling material case can lose energy when its broader methodological lesson is stated more than once. Chapter 09 is somewhat shorter after warranted cuts; no filler was added to preserve its earlier count.
