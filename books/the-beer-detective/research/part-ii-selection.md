# Part II: selection and scope

Research baseline: `grove/world-beer-wiki` commit `3b985c19a9dcccef8d334be54b5405e42093e812`. Original-source checks performed on 28 October 2026. This is the research/editorial record for chapters 7–12, not a claim that every paper linked by the wiki was read in full.

The part asks what ordinary sensory evidence can establish, and what further experiment changes the answer. Each chapter starts with a different kind of evidence: a familiar smell, conflicting measurements, a short-lived radical, a sealed package, a failed field correlation, and two soil samples. The progression goes from a mistaken inference about an ingredient to the reconstruction of an organism's ancestry.

## Cases considered and bounded

Clove is a short comparison inside chapter 7, not a second full ester chapter: its distinct precursor pathway keeps the ingredient inference honest without duplicating the aroma argument. Kveik was excluded from chapter 12 because its population history would require a separate narrative and would displace the lager-parent investigation. Thiols beyond lightstruck chemistry, diacetyl, dimethyl sulfide, general brewing recipes and sensory training manuals were excluded to preserve six complete investigations rather than an encyclopedia of defects.

The narrative does not depend on interviews or first-hand travel. Named experiments and sampling settings come from published records. No dialogue, private thought, laboratory atmosphere or participant emotion has been invented. Analogies are explanatory constructions rather than reported events.

## Evidence handling

The accompanying claim register records the actual access level for every cited original source: full public method summary, full-text page with relevant sections, indexed full-text excerpts, publisher summary, or abstract. A paywall or verification page was not treated as full-text access. When only an abstract was available, the chapter stays within its findings; source notes say so. Publisher migration dates and online-versus-issue dates were checked where they could confuse the narrative. The wiki supplied leads and a topical map; it did not independently verify its own sources.

Sources published after the wiki baseline were used where available and relevant, notably the 2026 Tonto study, while identifying the actual 2023 sample-collection date. The Irish discovery and Munich hypothesis were added to avoid ending lager ancestry at the wiki's 2011 source. The source register does not assert that these searches were an exhaustive systematic review.
