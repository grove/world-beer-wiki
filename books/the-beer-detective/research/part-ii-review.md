# Part II editorial and evidence review

Scope: chapters 7–12. Review performed during drafting and again against the completed text on 28 October 2026. These are actual AI drafting, verification and editing passes. No human specialist, independent laboratory, cultural consultant or external peer reviewer was engaged. Publication-level expert review remains outstanding.

This record describes the drafting pass. Subsequent edits and their rationale appear in [the cross-part review](cross-review-part-ii.md), which supersedes descriptions here wherever noted. In particular, the Tonto collection date remains in the source register rather than the narrative, and chapter 8's access qualification now appears in its source note.

## Developmental pass

The six chapters were read together for progression and duplication. Their central questions are deliberately different: ingredient provenance, analytical meaning, a photochemical route, storage reactions, gas-release mechanisms, and microbial ancestry. The shared theme is the difference between a suggestive observation and an explanatory test. The first chapter begins with an accusation about ingredients; the last returns to physical samples that can redraw a family tree. Chapters 9 and 10 explicitly separate light damage from ordinary aging so the reader is not given the same bottle mystery twice.

Changes made during drafting and this pass:

- Kept clove as a brief contrast within chapter 7 and removed the plan for a separate kveik history from chapter 12. Both choices preserve a complete narrative rather than turning chapters into catalogues.
- Made the negative Canadian field result the opening of chapter 11. A mechanism-only opening would have encouraged the mistaken conclusion that detecting a particular fungus automatically predicts gushing.
- Used the Irish campus samples to frame chapter 12, taking the historical arc beyond the initial Patagonian discovery. The ending retains an open research question without pretending the missing historical individual has been recovered.
- Added the visible-light distinction to chapter 9 after returning to the original 2006 PDF. This closes an explanatory gap left by the earlier description of a UV-laser experiment.
- Added the official total-package-oxygen definition to chapter 10. This gives the space above the beer a concrete analytical role instead of leaving the closed-bottle explanation as a metaphor.

## Prose pass

The completed chapters were read for unexplained terms, transitions and repeated endings. Ester, radical, nucleation, bound/free aldehyde and hybrid are explained at their first substantial use. Technical names remain where they distinguish mechanisms; exact experimental concentrations and procedural steps are omitted when they would not advance the narrative. No quotation or imagined laboratory dialogue is used. Study summaries are brief and supported by multiple sources per chapter, with original explanatory reasoning between them.

The argument has some recurring questions because all six chapters concern inference. The opening evidence and final image differ, and chapters do not reuse a fixed set of headings or conclude with a checklist. The final source notes carry access qualifications that would otherwise crowd the narrative; chapter 8 retains one short abstract-access qualification because the opening numerical comparison depends on it.

## Cultural and representational pass

Tonto is described by its local name and production context, with the sampled Ugandan districts identified. The text avoids treating it as a primitive counterpart to European beer or reducing it to a shared chemical adjective. The brewing skill of historical makers is distinguished from later genetic identification; the existence of modern instruments is not portrayed as the beginning of effective fermentation knowledge. Geographic discoveries are not national ownership claims. Researchers are named for actual contributions without assigning invented motives or heroic solitary discoveries.

## Hostile-reader pass

The most useful objections were tested against the prose: “Does equal IBU really never matter?”, “Did anyone taste this particular molecule?”, “Did they see a nanobubble or infer one?”, “If the cysteine experiment was negative, why mention bound forms?”, “Does Ireland prove an Irish origin?”, and “Is a brewery record a genome?” The manuscript now supplies the relevant distinctions. It makes no claim that analytical instruments are inferior to sensory experience, or that uncertainty about a specific historical route makes the broad lager hybrid relationship uncertain.

Remaining limitations are substantive: some original studies were accessible only as abstracts; no raw experimental datasets were independently reanalyzed; no laboratory replication, fresh sensory test or human specialist review was performed. Those limitations do not permit invented certainty. The text is a researched narrative manuscript ready for editorial review, not a substitute for the original studies or a technical operating manual.
