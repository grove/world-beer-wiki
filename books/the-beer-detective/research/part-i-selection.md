# Part I selection and chapter design

Prepared 28 October 2026 against wiki baseline `3b985c19a9dcccef8d334be54b5405e42093e812`. This records the six commissioned Part I subjects and the choices made within them. It is not a claim that every possible historical topic in the repository was independently assessed.

| Chapter | Central question | Evidence carrying the account | Narrative and scope decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 01. Before the Field | What would establish brewing before agriculture, and what would still be missing from a claim that beer caused agriculture? | Raqefet mortar study and published response to criticism; floral-burial study; domestication research; Shubayqa bread remains | Begin with a hollow in stone. Separate recognition of brewing, chronology, burial context, and agricultural causation. Treat the disputed identification as an interpretation throughout. Do not stage a Natufian feast or make a global first-invention claim. |
| 02. A Goddess in the Brewhouse | What can a brewing hymn tell someone who lacks its audience's practical knowledge? | Oxford translation; Civil's complete scholarly edition and commentary; Damerow; Paulette's firsthand experimental account | Begin with the shovel, move through translation and bappir to experimental choices, and return to the poem's ending. A poem can preserve recipe-like knowledge; the target is the stronger claim of a complete, unambiguous formula. |
| 03. The Accountant's Beer | Why can a tablet tell us the day of an allocation more precisely than it tells us the beer's composition? | Several separately identified museum tablets; CDLI compensation note; Damerow on barley equivalents; modern catalog translation | Start from the exact Ibbi-Sin date and small object. Follow communication, provisions, quality categories, values, and modern custody. Avoid universal beer wages, invented tasting notes, and conflation of distinct objects or periods. |
| 04. What the Pottery Remembered | How can chemical ingredients and microfossils answer complementary questions about a vanished drink? | Jiahu 2004 chemistry and 2026 microfossils; contemporaneous Penn announcement; Mijiaya and Qiaotou comparisons | Contrast materials with transformations. State explicitly that the newer work did not chemically reanalyse the original samples. Neighboring studies explain methods, not an asserted route of invention or descent. |
| 05. The Last Service | What does a brewery's deliberate ending reveal about its work and its political setting? | Cerro Baúl excavation report; Nash interview; complete 2019 archaeometric study; 2024 dregs abstract; modern museum collaboration | Begin with broken vessels and fire, then reconstruct labor, material supply, hospitality, and the interpretation of closure. Preserve Wari/Tiwanaku/Inca distinctions, women's inferred expertise, and the agency of contemporary collaborators. Avoid an eyewitness feast, fixed ancient recipe, or claim that beer alone held an empire together. |
| 06. The Inventor Arrives Late | How did a changing commercial drink acquire a precise, convenient inventor story? | Sumner's complete study; Ellis's 1736 primary manual; Cornell's historical investigation; Powerhouse engine and drawing records | Open with steam power and a retained horse wheel, then test the late Harwood story against process, storage, and source transmission. The engine provides a surviving material counterpoint without pretending it caused porter. Do not replace one sole inventor with another. |

## Selection reasons and boundaries

The six chapters offer distinct evidence problems: an archaeological brewing identification, a translated literary composition, administrative writing, complementary laboratory approaches, a political production installation, and a retrospective invention narrative. Their order does not assert an evolutionary ladder from simple to advanced brewing. Jiahu and Cerro Baúl are treated as technologies and institutions in their own settings, not as incomplete versions of European beer.

The accessible sources changed the treatments. Raqefet's published controversy required adding the dispute to the wiki's initial route. Reading Civil's commentary required qualifying the categorical claim that the Ninkasi hymn is not a recipe. The Jiahu paper required replacing the literal suggestion of two tests on one identical pot with a more precise description of a shared research problem. Full access to Sumner and Ellis made the porter chapter more than a repetition of a debunking summary. Retrieving the 2019 Wari PDF improved the distinction between a residue result and the political interpretation built around it.

The commissioned subjects were retained. Subjects or digressions deliberately not developed include a global earliest-alcohol competition, ancient beer health benefits, a speculative universal labor-ration system, a complete history of Chinese starters, all Andean brewing traditions, and a survey of modern porter styles. Each would require a different evidence base and would pull attention from the chapter's question.

The chapter notes disclose actual access. The companion `part-i-sources.json` registers 39 consequential claims and 31 distinct scholarly, primary, or institutional sources. Where full access failed, the narrative is confined to the accessible material and the limitation remains visible. Public announcements are evidence of institutional presentation or a modern collaboration, not extra independent experiments.
