## Developmental finding

The part has a useful movement from records of women's work through shared facilities and household economies to the technical knowledge embodied in starters and brewing sequences. Its strongest details are specific actions: signs removed in Exeter, kettle money collected in the Upper Palatinate, a Nagbo brewer's curtailed working week, different sales flags in different Peruvian settings, commercial yeast added in an experiment, and warm liquid reserved during mso preparation. Those details should carry more of the argument than repeated explanations of the writer's caution.

The complete fresh reading found several paragraphs that restated source-access limitations already precisely disclosed in the notes. It also found a cluster of three explanations of Zoigl authenticity where one thought experiment and the actual household variation were sufficient. These were edited without adding material merely to restore length. The local setting, named people, study limitations and closing images remain.

## Cultural and hostile-reader checks

The complete read asked whether unusual resistance had become typical behavior, whether an institutional designation implied consensus, whether one local study was being generalized nationally, whether a modern implement invalidated a tradition, whether microbial isolation established absence, and whether a technical resemblance supplied a historical transmission route. The three factual refinements above answer real overreading risks. The retained local names, places and actual operations do more useful work than a repeated assurance that the author is being respectful.

The chapters contain no invented interview, private thought, witnessed brewing session or reconstructed dialogue. Named researchers and publicly documented participants retain credit. Anonymous testimony remains anonymous. The comparison of living practices does not imply that a laboratory can replace makers' judgments or that all makers share one standard of success.

This pass supplies no endorsement from brewers, historians, Ghanaian participants, Andean communities, Korean or Himalayan starter makers, or Chagga-speaking readers. Questions about local terminology, economic priorities, divisions of labor and the weight placed on specific studies remain appropriate for future human review.
