# Part IV-A: documented review of chapters 19–22

Completed 28 October 2026. This record consolidates the chapter drafter's stated source work and a separate AI agent's complete fresh reading and targeted source checks. The distinction matters: the drafter's chapter notes are a record of that agent's access, while the fresh reviewer claims only the additional work actually performed. This is internal AI editing, not human peer review, clinical or legal review, or community endorsement.

A more detailed account of the independent checks and direct changes appears in [cross-review-part-iv.md](cross-review-part-iv.md). The accompanying [part-iva-sources.json](part-iva-sources.json) contains thirty-three claim records with explicit access provenance and uncertainties.

## 1. Developmental review

All four chapters were read in full against their opening questions, narrative turns and conclusions. Each contains a complete investigation: a legal list's limits, a dated brewery foundation's institutional causes, an exploratory microbiological result's implications, and changing sensory expectations. The endings return to the governing evidence rather than abandoning the reader in a list of unresolved concerns.

The fresh pass strengthened chapter 20 with a named participant, Václav Mirwald, whose tavern and brewing rights make the commercial arrangements concrete. It strengthened chapter 21 with observed procedural details instead of adding more general warnings. Chapter 22's revision names the actual expected attributes, giving its convergence result something definite to resolve. Chapter 19's structure required no text change.

## 2. Factual and statistical review

The drafter's notes document separate consultation of sources beyond the wiki, including improved access to Rail's full article, the distinction between corporate dates and original records, the exact masato sample groups, the 2017 sensory null/convergence result, and restricted-access labels for the judgment and later sensory papers.

The fresh review independently checked the following consequential points:

- **Chapter 19:** The reproduced 1516 ingredient rule; the earlier Bamberg yeast provision; Rupprecht's 1909 term and 1602 wheat-beer history; and the Library of Congress account of the 1893 court dispute. The source's erroneous yeast-not-used-before-1906 statement remains excluded. The original oath and court volume were not independently read.
- **Chapter 20:** Rail's complete extracted PDF supports the contrast between later anniversary explanation and the earlier appeal, pre-Pilsen bottom fermentation, the thirty-six barrels, and Mirwald's interests. The remaining retrospective corporate/institutional claims retain the drafter's access labels, without a new verification claim.
- **Chapter 21:** The full masato article confirms nineteen household water samples and seven outdoor-source samples, two positive masato samples among eleven, unknown individual fermentation durations, dilution of fermented mash, July 2017 photography and January 2018 validation. The pathogen study's complete abstract and the CDC page were independently read. Source claims of instant pathogen killing and speculative health comparisons are not adopted.
- **Chapter 22:** Full 2017 methods confirm that both beers were color-adjusted from one base, and that a twenty-five-person preliminary triangle test preceded the 136-person main study. The reported expected attributes and post-tasting results were checked. The 2022 indexed design and result excerpts support the distinction between attribute-specific effects and liking.

No claim has been promoted from an abstract into unexamined full methods. A failure to demonstrate discrimination is not treated as proof of identity. A post-tasting nonsignificant comparison is not rewritten as an exactly zero effect. Coliform-negative samples do not become all-hazard clearance, and the sample comparison does not become a causal estimate of fermentation's treatment effect.

## 3. Prose and repetition review

The opening objects are concrete and varied: an ingredient list, condemned barrels, a car battery and two colored beers. The reviewer retained these and avoided adding scenery unsupported by the evidence.

Direct edits were focused. Chapter 20 received one substantive named example and lost an unnecessary occupational label. Chapter 21 replaced two general passages with the actual missing timing information and dilution step, and dated the community validation. Chapter 22's opening and result description were made more specific and statistically accurate. No new section of boilerplate was added.

All four chapters were checked for invented dialogue, inferred private reactions and rhetorical certainty beyond the source. The Pilsen scene does not acquire a composite crowd. The Bavarian court anecdote does not acquire a motive. The masato researchers' collaboration does not become this book's fieldwork. Sensory participants are not represented as uniformly deceived individuals.

## 4. Cultural and safety review

Chapter 19 uses the preceding living-tradition chapters to keep one region's rule from becoming a world definition. Chapter 20 distinguishes brewing-right holders from every citizen and institutional success from a single genius. Chapter 21 retains Shawi participation, the drink's cultural specificity and the separation between original community validation and endorsement of this book. It avoids treating masato as a defective industrial beer or presenting it as universally safe. Chapter 22 distinguishes context-sensitive enjoyment from chemical composition, without ridiculing drinkers.

The discussion contains no recipe, plant-use instruction, dosing advice, homemade safety test or current legal prescription. The challenge study is neither extrapolated to masato nor used to declare all commercial low/no-alcohol beer unsafe. This restraint follows the evidence and task; the chapter remains an investigation rather than a compliance checklist.

## 5. Hostile-reader review

The reviewer tested plausible objections:

| Objection | Result |
| --- | --- |
| The book makes a medieval law foolish because it is not modern science. | The chapter explicitly distinguishes the document's purpose from the modern question and retains the rule's historical substance. |
| The Pilsen story merely replaces one hero with another. | Mirwald is one documented stakeholder; his addition illuminates rights and retailing without claiming he alone caused the brewery. |
| A small study is being stretched into a safety promise. | Exact sample units, unknown duration, indicator limits and the absence of a controlled preparation sequence remain explicit. |
| Concern about safety erases Indigenous priorities. | The participatory methods and local uses remain central; measurements and community priorities are treated as related questions. |
| Nonsignificant tasting results are being sold as proof that color never matters. | Both the revised statistical wording and later positive studies prevent that conclusion. |
| Professional judging advice is being treated as experimental proof. | The educational and partly superseded status of the guide remains disclosed. |
| The notes imply that every cited original was fully read. | Source objects and chapter notes distinguish excerpts, summaries, original texts and drafter-recorded versus reviewer-confirmed access. |
