The chapter correctly distinguishes the text of a restriction from a complete recipe and practical recognition of yeast from later microbiological explanation. The argument does not treat Bavaria's ingredient convention as a universal test for other brewing traditions. Its 1987 section is historical rather than advice on current labeling rules.

The Library of Congress article was independently opened, including its original-text section and 1893 court-case account. The manuscript appropriately excludes that article's erroneous statement that yeast was not used before 1906. Klaus Rupprecht's full public historical entry was independently opened and checked for the 1489 Bamberg yeast provision, the 1909 term and the 1602 wheat-beer monopoly. These checks support the chapter's main corrective sequence.

The manuscript attributes the cat episode to Gesley's account and does not pretend to have inspected the original Reichsgericht volume. Its neutral account of the cat entering the mash avoids inventing intent. No sensational detail was added. The original 1987 judgment was not independently retrieved in this cross-review; the drafter's indexed-excerpt disclosure remains.

## Chapter 21: The Water in the Bowl

All three major sources were independently checked:

- [Bussalleu and colleagues, Cultural Values and the Coliform Bacterial Load of Masato](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7719114/): full open article, methods, Tables 1–2, photographic-study account, results and discussion.
- [Çobo and colleagues, Survival of Foodborne Pathogens in Low and Nonalcoholic Craft Beer](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37865164/): complete PubMed abstract, not full methods.
- [CDC, About Water Disinfection with Chlorine and Chloramine](https://www.cdc.gov/drinking-water/about/about-water-disinfection-with-chlorine-and-chloramine.html): full public page, dated 14 February 2024.

**Verified numerical scope:** The masato paper records nineteen household water samples and seven outdoor-source samples, all coliform-positive, and eleven masato samples of which two were positive. The manuscript avoids the source abstract's misleading compression of the twenty-six samples into “communal water sources.” It does not convert 9/11 negatives into a percentage of hazards removed or imply a randomized before-and-after experiment.

**Focused edits:** Replaced generic caution with the study's concrete inability to confirm fermentation duration for each masato sample. Replaced a wholly hypothetical dilution example with the preparation account's actual mixing of fermented mash and water. Added the January 2018 date of community validation workshops to the photographic account and note. These changes make the limits part of the investigation instead of a separate warning repeated in general terms.

**Safety reasoning retained:** Coliform testing is distinguished from all-hazard testing; survival is distinguished from growth; a challenge study is distinguished from field sampling; beer experiments are not treated as results for masato. No recipe, safe operating threshold, ingestion advice or homemade safety test was added. The source paper's unsupported statement that alcohol-containing drinks kill pathogens on contact is not adopted. Its speculative comparison of disease rates in different child populations is also not adopted. The low/no-alcohol study's differing outcomes are scoped to the tested organisms and conditions, avoiding a claim that all commercially available products are unsafe.

**Cultural scope retained:** The Shawi project's actual participatory methods remain visible. The original community's validation of project photographs is explicitly different from review of this book. Preparation practices are attached to the reported setting. The narrative does not require masato to become a European beer category and does not imply that hazard assessment overrides participants' priorities.

## Chapter 22: The Beer the Eyes Ordered

The 2017 full article was independently opened, including stimulus preparation, the preliminary triangle test, participant count and post-tasting results. The 2022 publisher-indexed abstract, highlights and design excerpt were also independently checked. The draft's 2021 abstract-limited and superseded judging-guide disclosures remain accurate as statements of the drafter's access; these were not expanded into claims of new full-text review.

**Focused edits:** Clarified that both pale and dark beers were color-adjusted from a common base, rather than implying one was an untouched control. Named the observed expectations—greater bitterness, strength and body for the dark beer, greater expected liking for the pale beer. Recast the subsequent finding as narrowing gaps and no statistically significant post-tasting differences on those ratings, avoiding a proof-of-no-effect reading. The preliminary test is described as failing to demonstrate discrimination, not proving chemical identity.

The paper did report interactions and convergence, so the chapter's narrative turn is supported. No universal conclusion about all color effects follows. The 2022 distinction between attribute-specific effects and liking remains important and is retained. A contextual change in enjoyment is not misrepresented as a chemical change in the beer.
