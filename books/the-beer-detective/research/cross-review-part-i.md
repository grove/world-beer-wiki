# Fresh cross-part review: chapters 1–6

Date: 28 October 2026. Reviewer: a collaborating AI agent that did not draft Part I. The agent read all six complete chapter bodies and their source notes, then consulted the drafting selection/review records and the claim register. This is a fresh editorial pass within the AI-assisted workflow, not independent human peer review, an archaeological evaluation or an independent translation.

## Overall finding

The six cases have clear identities: mortars, a hymn, administrative tablets, pottery residues, a brewery's closure and a retrospective invention story. Their ordering works without asserting a single evolutionary ladder. The principal editorial issue was cumulative explanation of how evidence should be treated. Specific cautions earned their place, but several surrounding paragraphs repeated lessons already established by the objects and experiments. The pass removed about 550 narrative words relative to the drafting handoff while retaining each case's essential dispute, access boundary and cultural distinction.

No chapter was replaced or restructured into a template. All six were edited directly. Their particular openings, principal evidence and endings remain intact.

## Concrete source recheck

One technical concern warranted new retrieval: the first explanation of *qu* assumed the reader already knew what the starter was, and its wording could suggest that yeast fermentation always followed completed starch conversion. The reviewer checked the publisher-indexed process descriptions in Liu and colleagues' 2026 Jiahu paper and Liu and colleagues' 2024 primary study, “Revealing the 2300-Year-Old Fermented Beverage in a Bronze Bottle from Shaanxi, China,” *Fermentation* 10, 365, [doi:10.3390/fermentation10070365](https://doi.org/10.3390/fermentation10070365).

The indexed introduction and discussion describe cereal-based starters containing molds, yeasts and bacteria, their addition to prepared starchy ingredients, and simultaneous saccharification and fermentation. Both direct publisher page requests returned 429 during this pass. Access is therefore recorded as publisher-indexed full-text sections, not a successful direct full-paper reading. The new source supports the explanation of the mechanism; the book does not import its Qin-vessel results into the much older Jiahu case or claim a direct historical connection. A new note, `c04-7`, and one claim-register entry record the addition.

No new source retrieval was needed for the other focused prose cuts. The pass does not claim to have repeated the drafting agent's source reading, inspected original artifacts, evaluated spectra or micrographs, translated Sumerian, or resolved the Raqefet controversy. The existing access qualifications remain in place.

## Factual and cultural boundaries checked in the complete read

The Raqefet find remains an interpretation under dispute, not proof that beer caused agriculture. The hymn remains both a literary composition and a possible carrier of practical knowledge, without being promoted to an unambiguous modern operating formula. Administrative evidence remains local to named objects rather than a universal system of beer wages. Jiahu's new analysis remains complementary rather than a chemical retest of identical samples. Cerro Baúl remains Wari, and its destructive ending remains the excavators' contextual interpretation. The Harwood story remains a late historical claim rather than a fictional scene performed as fact.

The review found no invented dialogue, private thoughts or claimed firsthand visits. The text does not treat Chinese or Andean production as incomplete versions of barley-and-hop brewing. Contemporary craft knowledge is credited as expertise rather than presented as a timeless relic. The edits remove repeated cautions without relaxing those substantive limits.

## Remaining editorial and research limits

Part I necessarily contains more discussion of inference than a conventional chronology because its central subjects are disputed identifications and misleading origin stories. The cuts reduce the accumulation; they do not remove method from cases where method supplies the plot. The account still requires human subject-specialist and appropriate cultural review before it could claim that level of validation. Specific outstanding access limits remain as described in the chapter notes and the drafting record. This fresh pass is an additional editorial reading, not a substitute for those reviews.
