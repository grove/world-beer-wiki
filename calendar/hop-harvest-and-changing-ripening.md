# European hop ripening and changing seasonal timing

The hop harvest belongs to agricultural calendars, not to one worldwide beer-festival date. A 2023 climate analysis found changes in development and ripening dates among studied European hop regions.[^v14-hops-climate]

## Shifts recorded by study

The authors compare observations before and after 1994 and model future scenarios. They report that critical hop-ripening phases shifted in studied locations, with alpha-acid content also affected. This is not evidence that every hop variety, farm or hemisphere shares one harvest month.[^v14-hops-climate]

## Edition 1.14: newly sourced connections

Historic England's account of English oasts shows why harvest timing is linked to **urgent drying**, not just to cone maturity. Moist hops needed rapid transfer to nearby kilns before the crop deteriorated; drying, cooling and packing formed a closely connected seasonal work cycle.[^v114-oasts]

This is documented English infrastructure history, not a fixed date for modern German, New Zealand or British hop harvests. Read [the agricultural calendar](english-hop-harvest-and-drying-season.md) and [hop-farm work](../culture/kent-hop-farms-and-work.md) to keep season, place and processing form separate.[^v114-oasts]
