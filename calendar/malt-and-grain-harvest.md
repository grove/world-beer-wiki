# Harvest timing is not a world beer calendar

## The same word hides different calendars

The Sarawak Gawai Dayak public festival is associated with rice-harvest completion in June, while the Mising Ali-Aye-Ligang listing describes the beginning of an agricultural cycle in a local month associated with February. Treating both simply as 'autumn harvest festivals' would be inaccurate. Harvest is a regional agricultural event, not a fixed global season.[^v13-gawai-tourism][^v13-assam-festival]

## Why grain availability matters

Sorghum beer research from West Africa describes processes and supply chains connecting grain production, malting, commercial brewing and short-lived finished beverage. Different processing and storage arrangements separate harvesting from preparation. One harvest does not necessarily correspond to only one annual brew.[^v13-dolo-review]

## Model the local claim

The [Gawai](../calendar/gawai-dayak.md), [Ali-Aye-Ligang](../calendar/ali-aye-ligang.md) and [Sorghum malt](../ingredients/sorghum-malt.md) entries allow calendar comparisons without imposing Northern European seasons as a baseline.

## Evidence boundaries

This comparison is not a separate festival or claim that all brews were seasonal. It has no universally applicable month metadata. All dated observations are tied to the source and location described above. This is a research draft.
