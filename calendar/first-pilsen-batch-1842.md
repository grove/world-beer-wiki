# 5 October 1842: first batch at the Pilsen Burghers Brewery

A modern beer calendar can contain an **archival date** without claiming that a community has celebrated it every year. Plzeňský Prazdroj's official history places Josef Groll's first batch at the new Burghers' Brewery on **5 October 1842**. It is an event in a company's founding chronology, not evidence that bottom fermentation was first discovered that morning.[^v114-plzen]

The date is useful precisely because it can be checked as a date rather than guessed from a season. We should not transform it into a “first Pilsner day” festival in every year, attribute an official annual public event to a local community without a schedule, or pretend the entire style was already uniform across later imitations.[^v114-plzen]

The chronology gives this batch a context of pooled brewing rights and improved quality expectations in a town that had brewed beer before 1842. It also describes how trade, transport and branding extended the product's reach. The turning point was institutional and commercial as much as sensory. The historical entry can be linked to a modern [Czech premium pale lager](../styles/czech-premium-pale-lager.md) without treating a contemporary competition guideline as a transcript of the 1842 recipe.[^v114-plzen]
