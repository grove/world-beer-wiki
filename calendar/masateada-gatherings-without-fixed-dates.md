# Masateadas are gatherings, not a universal calendar holiday

A social event can be strongly associated with a fermented drink without having a fixed date on a national holiday calendar. The Yaminahua *masateadas* in an ethnographic study are occasions of visiting, sharing masato and dancing. The source's abstract does not establish that they happen every year on the same Gregorian day—or even as a single annually recurring festival.[^v110-yaminahua]

## Why the calendar needs more than months

The archive should distinguish a gathering called by relatives or neighbors from agricultural production seasons, officially scheduled festivals, and dated historical episodes. These have different evidentiary structures. Adding a fictitious 'Masateada Day' would create an attractive but misleading calendar entry. This page is instead a social-time reference attached to a documented community interpretation.[^v110-yaminahua]

## A local practice, not a universal cassava rule

Researchers also describe different practices among other Amazonian communities. A shared cassava staple or beverage name is not evidence for the Yaminahua social gathering schedule. Following the linked cultural case provides the specific relational interpretation; following the masato profile provides the broader beverage context. Keep those two analytical questions separate.[^v110-yaminahua]

## Evidence and limitations

No upcoming event dates, Gregorian months or tourism itinerary are inferred from an ethnographic abstract. These limitations should be retained when quoting the article or extracting relationships.
