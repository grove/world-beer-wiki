# Maibock and spring service

The name *Maibock* offers a calendar clue, not a complete timetable. BJCP's 2021 Helles Bock entry connects Maibock service with spring and particularly May, while acknowledging a wider range of color and hopping in seasonal examples than exported products may suggest.[^atlas-maibock]

## Serving time is not brewing time

A seasonal association identifies when a beer is offered or anticipated. It does not, by itself, establish the date of mashing, fermentation, maturation or packaging. Those belong to the particular brewer's production record.

The [Helles Bock profile](../styles/helles-bock.md) explains the pale, strong lager reference. [Doppelbock](../styles/doppelbock.md) is a neighboring strength-and-malt comparison, not another name for every spring beer. The guideline's comparison helps distinguish sensory categories; it is not a genealogy of all beers with a calendar name.[^atlas-maibock]

## How to use this calendar entry

Treat May as a useful browsing month for the stated northern-European context. Do not turn it into a global law that a beer cannot be sold outside that month, or transplant “spring” unchanged into the southern hemisphere.

Compare [Oktoberfest](oktoberfest.md), where a named event has edition-specific dates. The contrast is between a **seasonal association** and an **organized event schedule**, not simply between two months.

## Evidence note

This entry uses a modern classification reference's seasonal description. A full history of Maibock release customs would need dated advertisements, brewery records or other period evidence. No exact ancient origin or universal May celebration is claimed.
