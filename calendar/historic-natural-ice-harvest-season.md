# Natural ice harvesting for historic brewing was seasonal, not one beer holiday

## Winter ice was collected when conditions allowed

The Swiss National Museum describes nineteenth-century ice ponds and ice-harvesting networks that served breweries. Lakes could be cut when they froze sufficiently, and ice was then insulated, stored or shipped for later use. Cold winter conditions, site geography and labor determined when that could occur; the article does not document one universally recurring Gregorian day for all Swiss brewers.[^v119-swissice26]

The University of Chicago Library's brewing exhibit records natural ice and ice houses as an important predecessor to mechanical refrigeration. It does not identify a global public festival celebrating ice collection or an annual date when the practice began or ended.[^v119-chicagoice]

## Calendar metadata should preserve season rather than invent precision

An ice harvest from a named site in one archival winter could be dated if actual archival records supported it. General evidence that work occurred in winters is a **seasonal pattern**, not the same as a locally scheduled ceremony or modern public event. Nor can a record of ice cutting establish that a beer was brewed or served on the same day.

The entry is therefore marked as a non-fixed **seasonal** reference, with no recurring date or made-up event edition.
