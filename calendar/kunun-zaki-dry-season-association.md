# Kunun-zaki and the dry-season context

A 2015 Nigerian study describes kunun-zaki as a refreshing and inexpensive drink and notes its use in dry-season conditions while also acknowledging year-round consumption. This is a useful **seasonal association**, but the study was an experiment on a fortified beverage rather than a longitudinal survey of regional drinking calendars.[^v111-kunun-study]

## Season is not ceremony

The dry season has different timing and intensity across Nigeria, and the cited statement does not identify a single festival, day or universal production window. The wiki therefore records only a local/regional climate-linked observation. It does not generate an October, December or lunar calendar date, let alone automatically list a modern public event.[^v111-kunun-study]

## Better future research

A seasonal article could grow through longitudinal household accounts, producer interviews and region-specific weather and grain supply data. Such evidence must distinguish when ingredients are harvested, when the beverage is prepared and when it is served. For now the reliable lesson is that food-drink use may respond to climate without requiring a ritual calendar; this article remains a bounded, draft starting point.[^v111-kunun-study]
