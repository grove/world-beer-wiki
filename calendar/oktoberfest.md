# Oktoberfest as an event rather than a beer recipe

The City of Munich traces Oktoberfest to the October 1810 wedding celebrations of Ludwig and Therese and the horse race that concluded them. Its historical account distinguishes those festivities from the later development of beer stalls and large halls. Today's familiar form was not present fully formed at the beginning.[^atlas-oktoberfest-history]

## Three histories, not one

Separate the **festival's organization**, the **beers served at a particular time**, and the **international use of “Oktoberfest” as a beer label**. The wiki's [Festbier](../styles/festbier.md) profile distinguishes the modern golden reference from amber [Märzen](../styles/marzen.md); that is a style distinction, not a complete chronology of the event.[^atlas-festival-style-context]

A festival's first year therefore cannot establish the age of a present beer recipe. Likewise, a beer brewed outside Munich can use a festival-inspired description without being evidence about what was served at the original celebration.

## A dated edition, not an evergreen schedule

The official page lists **19 September–4 October 2026** for the Munich edition. These dates are already past relative to this entry's consultation date, **27 October 2026**. The stored record is an organizer-announced date range, not a live countdown, a confirmation of every scheduled activity, or a prediction for 2027.[^atlas-oktoberfest-2026]

This is why calendar exports preserve the year and consultation date. An old page's future tense must not make a past edition “upcoming.”

## Evidence note

The organizer's historical narrative is useful for the event's self-described history. Detailed disputes about invention, attendance or a specific brewer's recipe would require additional sources. No current booking, pricing or trademark advice is offered here.
