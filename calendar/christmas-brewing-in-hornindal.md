# Christmas brewing in a Hornindal field account

Christmas can explain **why a batch is brewed** without identifying a separate universal beer style. Garshol's account, published in December 2015, follows Terje Raftevold brewing in the Hornindal area. The observed mid-December preparation was Christmas beer, with somewhat more malt than his usual brew rather than an entirely different production system.[^atlas-hornindal]

## Occasion and method are different dimensions

The account describes a locally learned brewing practice and a connection between making beer and social participation. Its Christmas setting supplies an occasion; the [Kornøl profile](../styles/kornol.md) and [raw-ale process](../processes/raw-ale.md) explain the brewing method. One dimension should not replace the other.[^atlas-hornindal]

A useful record therefore separates the occasion, actual brew date, intended serving date, maker and process. “Christmas beer” alone cannot answer all five questions. Nor does the evidence justify supplying a mandatory spice mixture or alcohol range.

## What this page deliberately does not claim

It does not reconstruct medieval Norwegian brewing obligations, prescribe particular December brewing days, or describe all modern commercial juleøl. Those are worthwhile but separate historical and contemporary research topics. A single field visit cannot establish a nationwide recurring schedule.

The [calendar directory](index.md) places this local case beside festival calendars and production seasons. That comparison makes room for many ways that time matters to beer, rather than compressing them into a generic list of seasonal styles.
