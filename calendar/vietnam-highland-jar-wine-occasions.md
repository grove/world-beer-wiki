# Jar-wine occasions in Vietnam’s Central Highlands

The official tourism account of Ma *rượu cần* in Lam Dong associates jars brought out for harvest celebrations, weddings, funerals and worship occasions. The Vietnam News Agency reports S’Tieng jar-wine service during village festivals and family weddings in Binh Phuoc. These sources name **types of occasion**, not a single annual calendar fixed by day and month.[^v19-ma-tourism][^v19-stieng]

## Why an event needs more than a month

A harvest celebration may follow local cultivation and crop conditions, while a marriage or funeral is connected to a life event. A drink linked to several occasions may be prepared differently depending on family and community. No supplied source lets us calculate the next ceremony in an individual village, or describe attendance and drinking as compulsory. The most useful future metadata would name community, location, occasion and the testimony or institution that documented it.

## Heritage versus event marketing

The S’Tieng report explicitly mentions a local tradition recognized in Vietnam’s national heritage framework. That is not a UNESCO-wide inscription, and it does not give a calendar of public performances. The Ma promotional narrative also contains ceremonial claims which should remain attributed to the institution until community review can supply nuance.[^v19-stieng][^v19-ma-tourism]

For drinking practice see [Ma jar culture](../culture/ma-ruou-can-in-lam-dong.md) and [S’Tieng craft knowledge](../culture/stieng-ruou-can-and-transmission.md). Compare [brewing time vs ceremonial time](brewing-time-versus-ceremonial-time.md) and [Gawai Dayak rice tuak](gawai-dayak.md) without treating the festivals as interchangeable.
