# Barley harvest windows are weather and crop events, not fixed beer holidays

## Harvest timing is local and year dependent

A barley grain becomes ripe as plant and seed conditions change. A 2026 review of pre-harvest sprouting emphasizes that the interaction of dormancy, humidity, rainfall and temperature creates different risks near harvest. It cannot give one annual date when all barley is safely ready for malting; even nearby cultivars may behave differently.[^v117-sprouting-review]

Martin and colleagues' review of Bere discusses crop suitability to northern Scottish-island conditions and the importance of local cropping contexts. It does not justify turning Orkney planting or harvest into a uniform day for every Scottish, European or global brewer.[^v117-bere-review]

## A date can describe several different things

A recorded harvest day belongs to a particular farm and season. A historical grain tax date, a village ceremony and a modern brewery's annual release may involve barley, but they are distinct kinds of events. Neither a scientific growth-stage model nor a crop-history narrative gives the current public event schedule for another community.

A responsible crop calendar should keep the evidence type, locale, crop season, named variety when available, study period and the possibility of year-to-year change. The fact that a grain was harvested does not establish that brewing or drinking happened on the same date.
