# Ali-Aye-Ligang and the Mising agricultural calendar

## When a calendar begins with the field

India's Ministry of Tourism describes Ali-Aye-Ligang as a Mising festival marking the opening of a farming cycle, with communal celebration, feasting and particular work restrictions. It identifies the first Wednesday of *Gimur Polo*, described in the listing in relation to February. A local calendar name matters more than a simplified evergreen date; no future edition is calculated from this text.[^v13-assam-festival]

## Why rice beer belongs in the account

Field research into **ÉPOP**—the plant-and-rice starter used in documented Mising Apong production—shows that brewing materials and knowledge are maintained through household and community practices. That is evidence for an ingredient and knowledge system; the festival listing alone does not establish which starter recipe every household uses during the festival.[^v13-assam-study]
