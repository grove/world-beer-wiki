# Lambic's cool-season brewing window

Not every beer season is a festival. In their 2014 account of traditional lambic production, Spitaels and colleagues describe brewing during colder months, approximately **October to March**, because overnight cooling depends on cold nights. The season follows a production constraint rather than a celebratory date.[^atlas-lambic-2014]

## Follow the operation

Boiled wort is cooled in a shallow open vessel, or [coolship](../objects/coolship.md), before transfer for fermentation and maturation. The study's description links outdoor conditions, equipment and timing. It does not establish that any particular night within those months is automatically suitable.[^atlas-lambic-2014]

The [Lambic profile](../styles/lambic.md) provides the beverage context. The important calendar distinction is between **brewing**, **maturation** and **serving**: a seasonal production window does not mean the beer can only be consumed during that same window.

## What would support a climate-history claim?

A future article could compare dated brewing logs, weather records and producers' accounts of changed windows. This page does not infer a measured climate trend from one process description, or assume that refrigeration and contemporary brewery choices leave every window unchanged.

## Evidence note

The months are a source-specific description of Belgian practice, not a globally transferable recipe or a safety threshold. Similar open-cooling equipment elsewhere requires local evidence. Mere exposure to night air also does not establish the identity, quality or safety of the eventual fermentation.
