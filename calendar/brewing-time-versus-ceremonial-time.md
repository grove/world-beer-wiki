# Brewing time and ceremonial time are not the same

Comparison of a Wari archaeological closure event with seasonal and starter preparation described in ethnographic reviews; no recurring date inferred.

## A ceremony can demand a prepared drink

Excavation at Cerro Baúl identifies feasting and ritual closure of a brewery during the abandonment of a Wari colony. The evidence suggests production and consumption played an organized role in the sequence, but does not identify an annually recurring date in a Gregorian calendar.[^v15-wari-2005]

## A season can change process length

Himalayan fermentation reviews note differences in preparation time between warm and cold conditions. Environmental season can influence the practical interval between preparing starter, fermenting grain and serving a drink, without defining a religious festival day.[^v15-himalaya-review]

## A calendar data rule

For each documented practice, record separately when material was harvested, when brewing took place, when a beverage was served and when an associated ceremony happened. An archaeological event with uncertain exact day must not be turned into an annual reminder; neither should a regional seasonal observation become a global rule.[^v15-wari-2005][^v15-himalaya-review]

## Edition 1.16: newly sourced connections

The time printed beside a fermentation measurement and the date of a community gathering describe different events. A laboratory brewing protocol may record gravity at several fermentation stages, but that sequence is bounded by one batch and a defined experimental setup. It cannot supply the day of a local ceremony or the schedule of another producer.[^v116-lab]

In this atlas, a reported **sampling time** belongs to technical research metadata; a dated historical document belongs to history; a recurring public event requires its own verified temporal and local evidence. Nor should a day of fermentation be converted to a universal “ready by day N” food-safety claim.

Compare [fermentation timepoint interpretation](../microbiology/fermentation-timepoints-and-inference.md) with [historical extract research](../history/richardson-saccharometry-and-extract.md). The second reading does not establish a modern observance. The distinction protects both the original scientific study and a living cultural calendar.
