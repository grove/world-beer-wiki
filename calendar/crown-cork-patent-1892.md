# 2 February 1892: William Painter's crown-cork patent

## The evidence supports a specific day

United States Patent 468,226 for William Painter's bottle-sealing device is dated **2 February 1892**. The specification describes a combination of a metal cap and compressible sealing material for a specially shaped bottle mouth. That is a precise grant/publication day in a patent record, not the first day beer was bottled, sold, carbonated or consumed.[^v118-patent1892]

An ASME engineering landmark account places Painter's closure within the development of bottling equipment for carbonated beverages. The landmark describes related machinery and later technological improvements; it does not document how every independent brewery adopted the invention or whether every bottle had an identical liner.[^v118-asme-crown]

## Do not invent a recurring observance

A beer atlas calendar can represent an archival industrial date because it helps readers locate documentary evidence in time. Nothing in these two sources establishes that a public festival, a national bottle-cap holiday or an annually scheduled event took place on 2 February. Nor does the patent date reveal the preferred beer style of people who used the closure.

The entry therefore uses **historical-event** calendar metadata with exactly one dated edition. It is not generated as a recurring event and is not a legal opinion about the patent's present status.
