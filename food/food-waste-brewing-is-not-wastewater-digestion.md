# Brewing with surplus food and digesting brewery wastes are different circularity choices

## A new ingredient and a waste-treatment substrate are not equivalent

Končar's 2025 European craft-brewing study examines barriers and enablers to using food-waste ingredients in beer, drawing on **seven practitioners in four countries**. This is a small qualitative study of organizations and business conditions, not an experimental food-safety test, market census or proof that consumers prefer any particular surplus ingredient beer.[^v119-foodwaste25]

A 2026 circularity review instead surveys spent grain and surplus yeast after brewing for possible food, feed, material and energy uses. Whether a material can safely become a food ingredient is a separate regulatory and laboratory question from whether it has potential as a biogas feedstock.[^v119-spent26]

The 2026 review of brewery byproduct bioenergy discusses anaerobic digestion and microbial transformations used for gas generation from waste streams. Those conditions and microorganisms should never be substituted for organisms or methods intended to make edible beverages.[^v119-bioenergy26]

## Words such as circular need a documented boundary

A producer can avoid purchasing a new ingredient, divert waste from disposal, or recover fuel—but environmental outcomes still depend on sourcing, processing energy, competing uses, transport and disposal. A shared term “valorization” cannot replace a lifecycle comparison or product-specific safety evaluation.

This guide does not authorize consuming unidentified waste materials or give a procedure for preparing them.
