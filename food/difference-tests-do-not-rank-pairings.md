# A blinded difference test cannot tell you which beer pairs best with food

## First decide what a tasting asks

Imagine two beers alongside one meal. A blinded triangle test could ask whether assessors can discriminate the beer samples, but that is not the same as asking whether one improves the eating experience. ISO 4120:2021's official abstract defines a forced-choice discrimination or similarity method and explicitly says it does not identify the responsible sensory attribute or direction of difference.[^v116-triangle]

If consumers are instead asked to rank combinations or describe enjoyment, that is a preference question with different controls and participants. The two designs cannot be combined into a single unqualified statement that “this style pairs best.”

## Context changes the observation

The official ISO 8589 test-room description addresses controlling evaluation conditions; it is general, not proof that a café or social meal was conducted under those conditions.[^v116-room] A 2022 beer-expectation study reports that labels and colour can affect anticipated or reported attributes in the tested population. That finding also makes the label presented before tasting part of a food-pairing study's design, not merely decorative copy.[^v116-expect]

## Read the sample, not a universal rule

Pairing results need the exact food formulation, beer and serving temperature, panel composition, order, blinding and statistical uncertainty. For a different cuisine or product there may be no direct evidence. A consumer preference in one tasting is also not nutritional guidance, a medically appropriate recommendation or cultural ownership.
