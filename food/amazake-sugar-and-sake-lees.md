# Amazake at the food-drink boundary

Amazake is a useful case of how food terminology travels poorly across languages. The 2021 technical review distinguishes a drink sweetened through koji-enabled rice-starch conversion from an amazake variant made with *sakekasu*, the residue of sake production. The word 'sweet' does not tell a reader which base was used, and the word 'fermented' does not prove both had the same alcoholic history.[^v112-amazake-2021]

## Why an ingredient list matters

Koji-derived enzymes can create fermentable sugars without requiring yeast to transform those sugars into much ethanol. Sake lees already come from an alcoholic brewing system, and the form and composition of a product may differ depending on how those materials are handled. The wiki does not assume an identical alcohol content or flavor for the two types.[^v112-amazake-2021]

## No functional-food endorsement

Because the reviewed source is authored by an employee of a sake producer and discusses proposed health benefits, it should be read with attention to the type of evidence. This entry makes a process and food-identity distinction, not a dietary prescription, health claim or retail label assessment. Compare the separate sake and rice-mold pages to see why cultural names should remain specific.[^v112-amazake-2021]
