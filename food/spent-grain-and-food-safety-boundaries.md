# Brewer's spent grain as food or feed is not automatically safety-cleared

## One valuable by-product, several separate questions

Brewer's spent grain is the solid material remaining after wort is separated from mashed grain. It can be discussed as a resource for food, feed or other uses, but a circular-economy idea is not a test for chemical contamination, microbial safety, allergens or fitness for a particular market. A 2026 review of the barley–malt–beer chain addresses the transfer and redistribution of certain cereal-associated contaminants among process fractions. It does not certify any untested spent-grain batch.[^v117-chain]

The European Union's 2023/915 regulation, consolidated as of August 2026, sets contaminant maxima by **specific food categories** and associated exceptions. Whether and how rules apply to a particular grain-derived product requires accurate classification and current law, not a borrowed limit for a different food.[^v117-eu-contaminants]

An amendment in 2024 changed deoxynivalenol provisions from July that year. This is a clear example of why an undated blog's historic grain threshold must not be quoted as a permanent or worldwide safety standard.[^v117-eu-don-amendment]

## Evidence before valorization claims

A food ingredient and a livestock feed are not governed by identical processing needs, risk assessments or regulations. Even chemical laboratory results depend on what was sampled and the methods used. A sustainability outcome also requires a functional unit and process assessment.

The wiki offers neither reuse recipes nor a disposal decision for suspect materials. Compare [spent-grain safety and circularity](../environment/spent-grain-food-safety-and-circularity.md), [material reuse](../environment/spent-grain-and-circular-use.md), and [the original ingredient reference](../ingredients/brewers-spent-grain.md).

## Edition 1.19: newly sourced connections

Food waste entering a new beer recipe and brewery spent grain leaving production are different material and regulatory pathways. Končar's 2025 qualitative study interviewed seven practitioners from four European countries about barriers and enablers to using food waste in beer. Its interview findings are not safety analyses of a named ingredient, nutrition results or a representative consumer tasting panel.[^v119-foodwaste25]

A 2026 review of spent grain and surplus yeast surveys possible uses in food, feed, biotechnology, materials and energy. A potential application does not certify a byproduct lot for consumption, and using waste to produce methane is not itself a food process.[^v119-spent26]

Preserve intended use, ingredient origin, testing regime and relevant legal category before comparing material reuse. Read [food waste in beer versus wastewater digestion](food-waste-brewing-is-not-wastewater-digestion.md) and [energy feedstocks](../ingredients/spent-grain-and-yeast-as-bioenergy-feedstocks.md). These are research paths, not food-preservation or waste-handling recipes.
