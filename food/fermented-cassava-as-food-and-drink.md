# Fermented cassava as food, drink and social practice

Some fermented cassava preparations occupy a space that a Western food-versus-beverage menu cannot explain fully. The Tupinambá of Olivença ethnography treats giroba as fermented food involved in everyday relationships, while a Tapirapé cauim study characterizes a preparation consumed as part of household nourishment. These are **two distinct communities and source genres**, not interchangeable traditions.[^v111-giroba][^v111-cauim-bacteria]

## Chemistry cannot choose a cultural label

Lactic acid, ethanol and the organisms found in a sampled batch may tell a scientist something about the process. They do not by themselves determine whether participants regard the result as a meal, refreshment, sharing food, or a ceremonial drink. Translating the entire category as 'beer' can be analytically useful but culturally incomplete. Respect regional terminology and the voices behind it.[^v111-cauim-bacteria]

## No health claim is implied

Ethnographic discussion of nourishment and a microbiologist's account of acidification do not establish nutritional adequacy or product safety. Sources should be cited for what they actually observed. This entry invites comparisons with grain food-drinks and masato without prescribing how to prepare or consume an untested traditional ferment.[^v111-giroba]
