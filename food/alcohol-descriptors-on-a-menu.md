# Reading alcohol descriptors on a beer menu

## A meal has more than one kind of description

Restaurant menus may list a beer style, a brand, a claimed ABV, a “non-alcoholic” descriptor and suggested food pairings. Those are different statements. US TTB guidance specifies how particular low-alcohol terms may be applied on federally regulated malt-beverage labels; it should not silently become the definition used for a beer listed in every country.[^v115-alcohol]

A 2023 UK consultation asked whether descriptor thresholds and wording should change, partly because production methods had expanded. This remains a record of a consultation, not conclusive evidence of a 2026 UK rule. Venue descriptions should be checked against the relevant currently applicable market rules and the actual product label.[^v115-uk]

## A pairing is a separate question

A 2026 consumer experiment considered alcoholic and non-alcoholic versions of three beer styles alongside one Swedish shrimp dish. Its observed responses are tied to that tasting design; they do not establish that all beers with a similar alcohol label pair equally well with every meal.[^v115-food]

A useful menu or reference could record **manufacturer-declared strength, product name, serving size, source and date**, then separately describe a pairing study or diner preference. It should not infer nutritional values, absence of allergens, medical suitability or food safety from “0.0.”

Use [alcohol-free beer with food](alcohol-free-beer-with-food.md) for the experiment and [cross-border descriptors](../categories/no-low-alcohol-descriptors-across-borders.md) for vocabulary. This page is a reading aid, not a recommendation for people with medical or legal restrictions.
