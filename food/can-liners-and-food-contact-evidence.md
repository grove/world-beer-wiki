# A beer can's food-contact layer needs evidence separate from taste

## A regulated chemical category is not a sensory descriptor

Food-contact material requirements may apply to components that are not obvious when someone looks at the outside of a can. EU Regulation 2024/3190 addresses BPA and related bisphenol substances in certain materials and articles, including coatings. The legal text includes material-specific rules, transitional periods and exceptions. It does not prove what a particular beer can's inner coating contains or whether every imported product has been independently tested.[^v118-bpa-law]

The European Commission's official food-contact legislation overview identifies the act and a correction made in 2026. Current legal application requires the correct consolidated version and actual food-contact article. The legal concept of a permitted material is also not a guarantee about every beverage's microbial or chemical quality.[^v118-bpa-explainer]

## A tasting problem needs a different study

A 2025 experiment on storage of a selected unpasteurized beer examined flavor and stability outcomes under filtration, yeast and temperature treatments. Those tests did not analyze regulated bisphenol migration from can linings. A stale aroma is therefore not a valid substitute for a migration measurement, and a “metallic” tasting note cannot identify a specific coating compound.[^v118-storage25]

A food reference should keep contact-layer composition, legal market, specific analytical test and product-level claims apart from tasting observations. Diet, allergy and individual health advice cannot be inferred from the words “can” or “BPA-free.” Read [can-liner construction](../objects/metal-can-linings-and-food-contact.md), [compliance versus beer flavor](../categories/food-contact-law-is-not-beer-flavor.md) and [label interpretation](alcohol-descriptors-on-a-menu.md).
