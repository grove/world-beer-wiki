# Can alcohol-free beer work with food?

A 2026 consumer study compared alcoholic and non-alcoholic versions of a pilsner, wheat beer and IPA, both alone and with *skagenröra*, a Swedish shrimp dish. In this experiment the non-alcoholic versions were less liked alone but did not perform significantly differently in the tested pairing.[^v14-food-na]

## The contextual finding

The result reminds researchers that beverage preference can change when food is present. It does not imply that every alcohol-free beer works equally well with every dish, nor that a food pairing eliminates the health risks of alcohol in the alcoholic comparison products.[^v14-food-na]

## Edition 1.15: newly sourced connections

The 2026 pairing experiment evaluated particular alcoholic and non-alcoholic versions of three beer styles in one food context. Its consumer responses do not support a universal claim that any beer marked “0.0” will pair equally well with any meal, nor that a tasting score predicts individual dietary needs.[^v115-food]

For restaurant lists, product strength and sensory style must also be distinguished: US TTB gives specific requirements for the “non-alcoholic,” “alcohol free” and “low alcohol” labels on covered malt beverages. Those words cannot be transferred automatically to another country or treated as independent evidence of a product's manufacturing route.[^v115-alcohol]
