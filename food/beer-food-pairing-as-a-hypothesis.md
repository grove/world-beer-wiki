# Beer and food pairing as a testable hypothesis

Food and beer pairing advice is often expressed as a universal rule. A 2019 gastronomy study interviewed ten beer experts and ten sommeliers to ask how experts explained matches and mismatches, producing fifteen proposed principles.[^v14-food]

## An expert explanation is not a taste vote

The study identifies strategies for discussing sensory interactions. Interview-derived principles are useful hypotheses, not proven laws of preference. A separate consumer study of beer and cheese found highly heterogeneous liking, reinforcing that “correct pairings” depend on the people and examples studied.[^v14-cheese]

## A better wiki question

Instead of prescribing “stout goes with chocolate,” ask what bitterness, acidity, roast, texture, serving temperature and food fat or salt might do in a specific pairing. Read [sensory chemistry](../sensory/aroma-is-not-an-ingredient.md) and [beer categories](../categories/beer-classification.md) as useful scaffolding.
