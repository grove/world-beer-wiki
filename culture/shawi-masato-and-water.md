# Shawi masato: daily life, photography and water evidence

A community-partnered investigation of masato in a Shawi community of the Peruvian Amazon shows why a fermented cassava drink belongs in the wiki as more than an ingredient list. Participatory photographs document activities around planting, cooking, mashing, carrying and serving cassava. The published account credits participating community members and describes how the beverage fits daily hydration and household work.[^v18-shawi]

## Making and drinking belong to different moments

The photographs present a substantial sequence: cultivating and washing cassava, cooking and mashing it, preparing material for fermentation, and later diluting a portion for drinking. The preparation described includes chewing some cooked cassava, but that is **not** a universal property of every drink called masato. Other Peruvian samples documented in the [canonical masato profile](../styles/masato-de-yuca.md) involve different techniques. Do not substitute a single household practice for every nation or community.[^v18-shawi]

## What the sample comparison can—and cannot—show

The team tested 26 water-source samples and 11 masato samples. All 26 water samples showed coliform contamination; two of eleven masato samples tested positive. Those specific results motivate further research but **do not certify masato as potable**, prove clinical protection, or imply that fermentation makes unsafe water reliably safe. The published study explicitly called for additional microbiological-safety work.[^v18-shawi]

## Research ethics are part of the story

Community photographic participation and consent matter; so does who gets to interpret the results. The wiki should not reproduce personal photos, intimate knowledge or private routines just because a paper exists. Compare the [cassava preparation method](../processes/shawi-masato-preparation.md) and the [safety-claim caution](../stories/fermentation-is-not-a-water-treatment.md) without generalizing this community's choices.
