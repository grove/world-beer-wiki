# Makgeolli: making, sharing and national heritage

## A beverage and a practice

In June 2021 South Korea's Cultural Heritage Administration announced the **national** intangible-cultural-heritage designation of makgeolli making *and sharing*. The announcement describes rice, water and nuruk, the grain starter that makes starch sugars available during fermentation, but deliberately recognizes the surrounding social knowledge as well as the liquid.[^v13-korea-heritage]

## Changing conditions

The government account discusses the movement from household brewing toward commercial breweries during the twentieth century, regulatory restrictions in the colonial period, and renewed interest in home production. It is therefore misleading to frame makgeolli either as an unchanged ancient recipe or as simply a modern supermarket drink. The Korea Tourism Organization also places regional varieties in relation to agricultural work, food and leisure.[^v13-korea-heritage][^v13-korea-tourism]

## Which name is useful?

The [Makgeolli profile](../styles/makgeolli.md) covers a finished beverage and its measured commercial variation; [Koji](../microbiology/aspergillus-oryzae-and-koji.md) and [mixed starters](../ingredients/mould-and-mixed-starters.md) introduce related but nonidentical saccharification systems. Nuruk is not a synonym for Japanese rice koji, and neither starter defines the whole social practice.

## Evidence boundaries

This is a government heritage rationale and tourism overview. They do not demonstrate that every family, temple or region uses the same starter or celebration. All dated observations are tied to the source and location described above. This is a research draft.
