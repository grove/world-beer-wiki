# Georgian highland beer, blessings and village gatherings

Georgia's public tourism documentation identifies *aludi* as part of Georgian highland community life, with brewing preparation before celebrations and beer offered at summer gatherings. It describes elders offering blessings and relates beer to occasions that can include festive and mourning contexts. This makes aludi an excellent entry point into **beer as hospitality and ceremonial exchange**, not merely a flavor category.[^v112-aludi-georgia]

## Names and geographic specificity

The account names several mountain areas and reports different Georgian terms used for preparing brewing pots. Those words belong to specific regional reports; they should not be silently turned into instructions used in every Pshavi or Tusheti village. A historian or local brewer could clarify pronunciation, variation, and which practices are still followed.[^v112-aludi-georgia]

## Evidence boundary

A government tourism page is strong evidence of what an institution publicly presents as heritage. It is not a substitute for an interview with an individual maker or direct historical measurement. In particular, the official national intangible-heritage description must not be upgraded to a UNESCO listing. Compare Norwegian juleøl or German Zoigl to investigate different modes of communal brewing, not a single trans-European ritual ancestry.[^v112-aludi-georgia]
