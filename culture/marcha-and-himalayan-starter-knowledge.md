# Marcha and named Himalayan starter traditions

Eastern Himalayan studies of family/community starter knowledge; names and regional practice are not interchangeable across mountain communities.

## A starter is a material practice

A 2019 microbiology study describes marcha as a dried, rice-based mixed starter used in the Darjeeling hills and Sikkim, eastern Nepal and parts of Bhutan. Community accounts cited by the researchers describe grain flour, herbs and the perpetuation of prior starter. The culturally transmitted object is not a laboratory vial containing a single microbe.[^v15-himalaya-2019]

## Many names, not one timeless recipe

The study also names phab in Bhutan and several starters from communities of Northeast India. Similar functions—converting starch and supporting alcoholic fermentation—do not prove identical ingredient ratios or a shared origin. Their local terms and makers should be recorded rather than replaced by a generic “Asian rice yeast” label.[^v15-himalaya-2019]

## Season changes what happens

A 2024 review discusses variation in fermentation timing and preparation, including differences between warmer and colder periods. Treat these as reported examples, not a fixed Himalayan calendar; specific households and materials matter.[^v15-himalaya-review]

## Care with research claims

An isolate survey can characterize organisms in tested starter samples; it cannot establish that every family maintains the same taxa, nor that every therapeutic or nutritional claim in ethnographic writing has been clinically demonstrated.[^v15-himalaya-2019][^v15-himalaya-review]
