# Gawai Dayak and Sarawak rice tuak

## Harvest, visiting and preparations

Tourism Malaysia describes Gawai Dayak as a Sarawak festival, publicly observed on **1 June**, connected with the end of rice harvesting and visits among relatives and friends. In its account, preparations begin with *tuak*, a rice wine, and other foods. The public holiday was formalized in the 1960s; this modern administrative history should not be confused with the much wider history of Dayak harvest observances.[^v13-gawai-tourism]

## Who does the collective label describe?

The tourism account explicitly uses Dayak as a collective term for several peoples. It is useful evidence for a public celebration, but not a universal script for each Iban, Bidayuh, Kayan or other community. Likewise, the presence of rice tuak in a festival description is not proof that every participant drinks it or that one recipe applies throughout Sarawak.[^v13-gawai-tourism]
