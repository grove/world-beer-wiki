# American brewing voices preserved in oral history

Smithsonian American Brewing History Initiative collection-level evidence; interviews remain attributed rather than presented as independently read transcripts.

## A different sort of beer archive

From 2017 to 2023 the Smithsonian National Museum of American History collected 70 oral histories about brewing and beer culture in the United States. The finding aid describes brewery operators, homebrewers, and other voices speaking about work, taste, markets and personal identity. This is primary testimony preserved with catalog context, not a collection of style specifications.[^v15-smithsonian-oral]

## Whose story is selected?

The finding aid identifies subjects including Asian American, Black American, Latino and LGBTQ+ experiences. These categories matter because a history told only through the largest producers can miss entrepreneurs and consumers who shaped culture at smaller scales. A catalog is still a selected collection, not a representative demographic survey.[^v15-smithsonian-oral]

## One interview description

The catalog entry for brewer Marcus Baskerville describes his experience of underrepresentation at beer events and his founding of the Black Is Beautiful collaboration. Those are topics listed by the archivist; this page does not pretend to quote Baskerville’s full narration, which was not accessed here.[^v15-smithsonian-baskerville]

## Rights and ethics

The Smithsonian finding aid warns about reproduction conditions and remarks made by non-narrating participants. The wiki can link to records and summarize authorized catalog descriptions without republishing unlicensed transcripts or attributing casual remarks to an interviewee.[^v15-smithsonian-oral]
