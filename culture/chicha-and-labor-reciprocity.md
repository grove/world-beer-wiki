# Chicha and labor reciprocity on Peru’s north coast

Chicha in Andean history can be a form of hospitality and political obligation as well as a drink. Jerry Moore’s archaeological study of Manchan examines maize-beer production in a non-elite setting of the Chimu Empire.[^v14-chimu]

## Provisioning a labor gathering

Moore’s abstract discusses *hospitable repayment*: political leaders were expected to provision corvée workers with food and chicha. That is evidence for a social institution, not for a particular sensory profile or for the idea that workers had one uniform relationship with rulers.[^v14-chimu]

## What archaeology cannot supply

An archaeological production area helps establish scale, equipment and location. It cannot by itself recover the words, motives or taste impressions of every participant. The [Chicha de Jora](../styles/chicha-de-jora.md) entry covers related ferment traditions; [Manchan](../history/chimu-chicha-at-manchan.md) provides the dated historical frame.

## Research extension 1.5: Avoid confusing Wari feasting with later labor systems

The Cerro Baúl brewery provides archaeological evidence of Wari organized production and ranked access to feasting, while other studies describe later and living systems of reciprocal work. These are comparisons of beer’s social roles, not evidence that one ritual descended directly from another.[^v15-wari-2005]
