# Tesgüino, invitation and reciprocal work in Norogachi

A recipe tells only part of this story. In a 2023 publication, narrator **Alberta Espino Romero** describes batáli, or tesgüino, in Norogachi. **Regina Espino Moreno** provides translation, while **Jesús Villalpando Quiñonez** presents linguistic analysis. Keeping those roles visible prevents a community account from becoming anonymous “folklore.”[^atlas-tesguino]

## An invitation can organize work

The publication includes preparation, drinking occasions and **bajuréa**, an invitation associated with tesgüino and reciprocal work. In the narrative, a prepared batch accompanies inviting others to help in the fields. The analysis distinguishes this from assuming every such gathering is a compulsory institutional work obligation.[^atlas-tesguino]

It also distinguishes **kórima**, reciprocal assistance that need not involve tesgüino. Thus the reasonable conclusion is not “cooperation requires beer,” but that a particular account shows how brewing, invitation and cooperation can be connected.[^atlas-tesguino]

## Read the social relationship, not just the vessel

For comparison, ask who issues an invitation, which work is involved, how people describe obligations, and whose words the account preserves. These questions are more informative than translating every gathering as a drinking party. They also leave room for nonparticipants rather than assuming that consuming alcohol defines belonging.

The [Tesgüino profile](../styles/tesguino.md) addresses maize preparation and the named beverage. [Mising starter knowledge](mising-starter-knowledge.md) offers a different example of exchange, this time involving fermentation material itself. Neither comparison establishes common ancestry or interchangeable social rules.

## Evidence limits

This is one situated narrative with translation and analysis, not a census of ceremony, a visitor etiquette guide or permission to reproduce restricted practices. Claims about sacred roles or detailed ritual sequences need additional community-grounded evidence and care.
