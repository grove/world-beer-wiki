# Boza vendors and the winter soundscape of Istanbul

The Istanbul artisans' association remembers *bozacılar*: boza producers and street sellers whose cries were associated with cooler autumn and winter weather. Its article names cinnamon and roasted chickpeas (*leblebi*) as common modern accompaniments. This is a rare reminder that the **way a drink reaches a customer**—a seasonal call, a familiar seller, a shared snack—is part of beverage culture alongside fermentation chemistry.[^v112-boza-artisans]

## An occupation is not a fermentation specification

The association also recounts stories of Ottoman *bozahane* shops and earlier sweet and sour varieties. It attributes old shop counts to Evliya Çelebi; we have not inspected that travelogue, so these figures are not presented as independently verified population data. The narrative is valuable as a craft association's public memory, with its own period and advocacy aims.[^v112-boza-artisans]

## When culture and laboratory evidence meet

A Turkish beverage review and a 1997 fermentation experiment document microbial processes, but cannot establish which vendors sold which strain or what every historical boza's alcohol content was. Historical questions require archival records; modern vending practices require dated observation. A street cry belongs to an identified tradition without becoming a permanent event schedule for visitors.[^v112-boza-review]
