# S’Tieng rượu cần: craft, livelihood and transmission

A 2022 Vietnam News Agency report does more than describe a rice beverage. It names S’Tieng craft practitioners in Binh Phuoc, recounts knowledge passed through families, and describes a local cooperative established by Dieu Thi Xia to preserve craft and create income opportunities for women. This is situated evidence of a **living craft economy**, not a claim that all people of an ethnic group brew in the same way.[^v19-stieng]

## What the report actually records

The article describes gathered leaves, bark and other plant materials in a prepared starter and long straws inserted into communal earthen jars. It reports national recognition of the S’Tieng *can*-wine craft as intangible cultural heritage. A maker’s practical knowledge of locally named ingredients is reported, but the source does not provide botanical voucher specimens or demonstrate safety for unfamiliar foraged materials.[^v19-stieng]

## A story within the story

The same report relates a legend about two sisters and the discovery of wine-making instruction from a forest deity. It is a **legend**, not a dated invention record. The report’s heritage and commercialization themes are more directly grounded by named contemporary individuals. Preservation has material stakes: transmission, women’s livelihoods, public presentation and decisions about who tells the story.[^v19-stieng]
