# Nahua maguey and first-pulque rituals in colonial records

Rodolfo Ramírez Rodríguez's INAH article studies the persistence of symbolic and religious aspects of maguey cultivation and the preparation of first pulque among some Nahua communities in colonial Mexico. The evidence he discusses comes through **Catholic clerical transcriptions** of what those authors described as incantations or survivals of an older religion. Those descriptions are historically situated, not neutral recordings of a living ceremony.[^v111-pulque-ritual]

## Interpret the archive, not just the drink

The report points to seventeenth-century material and to continuities discussed through the eighteenth century. But words such as 'superstition' in ecclesiastical discourse reflect the observer's theological and political position. A modern wiki should attribute that wording and not adopt it as a judgment of Nahua practice. Nor should it convert an archival phrase like 'first pulque' into an annually fixed event without date evidence.[^v111-pulque-ritual]

## What remains unverified

Only the article abstract was inspected for this entry. The colonial source texts, historical Nahuatl wording, and differing community memories need direct specialist and, where welcome, community review. Reconstructing restricted ritual instructions would be inappropriate. This research opens a way to ask how agriculture, religious power and fermented drinks intersect, while avoiding a claim that all present-day pulque makers share one rite.[^v111-pulque-ritual]
