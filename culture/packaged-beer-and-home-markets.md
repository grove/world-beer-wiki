# Packaged beer changed where people encountered brewing

## A market channel is not a flavor category

A brewery can serve beer to a customer at a pub, sell it in a bottle to take home, or move beer through an intermediary. The Smithsonian's handled Baltimore beer can records one material example of the relationship among a brewery, container maker and retail product in the American post-Prohibition market. A single museum object cannot tell the reader that all people preferred cans or that social drinking stopped at the pub.[^v118-smithsonian-can]

A 2022 life-cycle study of seven microbreweries, from a different place and period, shows that packaging and distribution routes still affect the environmental burdens of modern beer. Its modeled logistics results do not describe the social behavior of the Smithsonian can's original purchasers, but help explain why **where and how beer moves** is a separate question from what style it contains.[^v118-lca22]

## Social context needs local sources

A community pub, a retail chain and a household gathering may all involve similar beer names while organizing labor, hospitality, serving and payment differently. Packaging format can facilitate a channel but does not determine the cultural meaning of drinking or who participates. Marketing imagery should not be mistaken for actual consumer testimony.

For this wiki, preserve the named place, production date, container provenance and the source of any social claim. An archaeological bottle, museum can or LCA is not a first-person interview.
