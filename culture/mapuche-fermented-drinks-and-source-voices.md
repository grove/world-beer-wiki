# Mapuche fermented drinks and the voices in colonial sources

A 2024 review of fermented beverages in pre-Hispanic Chile discusses chicha in Andean and Mapuche settings, including descriptions of women's involvement in preparation and the role of shared drinks at gatherings. Many of the passages it examines are drawn from **colonial chroniclers or later interpretation**. They are clues to a documented historical conversation—not transparent records of what every Mapuche person did.[^v16-chile-review]

## The source problem is part of the story

The review contrasts centralized Andean political contexts with Mapuche leadership and social presentation. Historical colonial observers may have misunderstood or condemned drinking practices; their descriptions are neither neutral nor universally representative. Repeating one observer's judgment as contemporary community voice would reproduce that distortion.[^v16-chile-review]

## Many ingredients, many cultural identities

A 2026 review of Peruvian chicha de jora emphasizes variations in maize, adjuncts, processing and microbial systems. Similar descriptive words across Chile and Peru do not prove that present practices share one uninterrupted origin or belong to the same people's tradition.[^v16-chicha-2026]
