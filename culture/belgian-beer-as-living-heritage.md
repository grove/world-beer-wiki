# Beer culture in Belgium as living heritage

## Beyond style names

UNESCO inscribed *Beer culture in Belgium* on its Representative List in 2016. The inscription concerns the skills of making and appreciating beer, rather than certifying any particular brewery or recipe. It describes beer in everyday life, festivals, cooking, hospitality, and local beer communities. A living heritage inscription is a statement about social practices and their transmission, not a requirement that every Belgian drinker participate.[^v13-belgium-unesco]

## How knowledge travels

The UNESCO description places knowledge transfer in homes, social circles, brewery teaching, university courses, and public training. Such institutions play different roles: a family may transmit a serving custom, while professional training may formalize brewing science. Their coexistence challenges the idea that craft knowledge disappears whenever technology becomes sophisticated.[^v13-belgium-unesco]

## How to compare responsibly

Compare the inscription with [Trappist work and brewing](../culture/trappist-work-and-brewing.md), [Zoigl shared brewing](../culture/zoigl-communal-brewing.md), and the [Belgian and farmhouse family guide](../categories/belgian-and-farmhouse-ales.md). UNESCO recognition pertains to Belgian beer culture in a particular administrative heritage framework; it does not rank the traditions of countries without inscriptions.

## Evidence boundaries

The UNESCO inscription is institutional documentation of nominated cultural heritage, not an ethnographic census of all Belgian communities. All dated observations are tied to the source and location described above. This is a research draft.
