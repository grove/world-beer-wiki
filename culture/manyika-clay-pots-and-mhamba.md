# Manyika brewing vessels and mhamba

For some Manyika communities, a brewing vessel is a piece of everyday technology and a participant in ceremonies. A published ethnohistorical study names clay *hari dzedoro* and distinguishes fermented beverages rather than treating every pot as a generic beer jar.[^v14-manyika]

## Different drinks, different occasions

The study distinguishes *mhamba*, described there as an opaque alcoholic millet- or sorghum-based beer, from *magada*, a non-alcoholic cereal drink. It lists uses in work gatherings, rain petitions, harvest thanks and other occasions. Those associations belong to the documented region and account; they are not a national fixed timetable.[^v14-manyika]

## Objects hold information

Clay forms, use wear, contents and oral histories can be read together. An excavated vessel alone does not prove an alcoholic beverage was present or that one particular ritual was performed.
