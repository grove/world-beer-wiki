# Wari brewing, feasts and unequal access

Interpretations of Wari elite drinking and archaeological production evidence at Cerro Baúl and adjacent Moquegua sites.

## Hospitality as political infrastructure

At Cerro Baúl, large-scale chicha production occurred in a highly organized settlement. Excavators interpret the complex within a Wari political landscape, with ceremonial consumption, differentiated tableware and access varying by rank. Brewing can therefore be studied as work that supported hospitality and authority, not only as a way to produce alcohol.[^v15-wari-2005]

## What drinking vessels reveal

A separate study of *kero* cups treats serving vessels, sets and elite drinking arrangements as evidence of social relationships. This is not equivalent to recovering a Wari tasting note; it tells researchers about participants, display and occasions rather than precise aroma or alcohol strength.[^v15-wari-kero]

## Women’s labor and evidential caution

The original excavation notes metal shawl pins in the brewery and proposes a prominent role for elite women. Pins in archaeological context support an interpretation about participation; they cannot determine every worker’s gender, status, or whether production roles were identical elsewhere in the Wari sphere.[^v15-wari-2005]

## A useful contradiction

The grand production facility does not establish that every household brewed its own chicha. Research published in 2024 argues state-sponsored specialization was particularly important in the Wari province of Moquegua. Keep claims about household-scale work and elite facilities distinct.[^v15-wari-2024]
