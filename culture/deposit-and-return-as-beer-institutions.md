# Deposit and return systems are institutions, not just reusable objects

## The object needs a functioning chain of cooperation

A bottle that *could* be refilled and a bottle that actually completes multiple returns are not the same operational system. The 2025 LCA methodology review by Espinoza-Orias and Lundquist emphasizes modeling of reusable packaging networks and the possibility of error when product losses or hypothetical return conditions are omitted. The environmental question therefore involves people and logistics as well as glass or plastic.[^v118-lca-reuse25]

The EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation establishes a wider framework for reuse, collection and packaging design with staged requirements. Its rules should not be read as a statement that every retailer or producer currently operates the same deposit or wash-and-return scheme. Applicable responsibilities depend on product, business role and rules.[^v118-ppwr25]

## A reusable keg can have two legal roles

The Commission's June 2026 interpretive guidance distinguishes keg distribution to hospitality businesses from sales packaging offered to the final consumer when discussing certain reuse targets. That is an institutional distinction rather than a physics claim about whether the keg is sturdy enough to reuse.[^v118-ppwr-guidance26]

A cultural account must identify who owns containers, who pays deposits, who collects empty bottles and how returns work locally. A law, industry claim and published model do not automatically speak for participating workers or customers. Compare [actual reuse-cycle variables](../environment/returnable-bottles-and-system-losses.md), [the EU framework](../environment/eu-packaging-reuse-law-scope.md) and [hospitality cultures](cask-beer-pub-community.md). This is not legal or commercial compliance advice.
