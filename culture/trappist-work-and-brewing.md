# Trappist work, brewing and institutional identity

**Trappist is not a flavor.** The International Trappist Association describes its Authentic Trappist Product label through production relationships: work in the immediate surroundings of an abbey, supervision by monks or nuns, and proceeds supporting the community and solidarity or charitable purposes.[^atlas-trappist]

Those criteria concern an institution and its economic activity. They do not specify one color, alcohol strength, grain bill or yeast aroma. Nor does supervision mean that every production task must be performed personally by a monk. The association's label also extends beyond beer.[^atlas-trappist]

## Three questions that should not be merged

| Question | Useful evidence |
|---|---|
| What does the beer resemble? | A scoped style description and information about the actual beer. |
| How is production connected to an abbey? | The institution's documented arrangements. |
| Does this particular product currently carry a label? | Current product-specific information from the relevant institution. |

The distinction lets a reader compare [Belgian Single](../styles/belgian-single.md), [Dubbel](../styles/belgian-dubbel.md) and [Tripel](../styles/belgian-tripel.md) without converting those sensory categories into certifications. Conversely, an abbey-themed name or illustration is not a substitute for evidence of institutional production.

## A cultural question worth pursuing

What happens when the purpose of an enterprise is described through the needs of a religious community rather than only through sales growth? This article opens that question; it does not infer every abbey's finances, labor arrangements or historical motivations from the label criteria.

## Evidence note

This is the association's account of its own rules, consulted on the date below. It is not an independent audit of any producer, a guarantee of current licensing, or a claim that all monastic beers share one medieval recipe. Lists of active products and institutions require separate maintenance.
