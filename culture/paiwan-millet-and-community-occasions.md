# Paiwan millet, wine and community occasions

The Taiwan Council of Indigenous Peoples describes millet cultivation as more than a grain supply for Paiwan communities: planting, harvest, seed storage, ritual thanks and public gatherings each carry distinct meanings. Its English profile calls the harvest thanksgiving festival *Masalut* and describes grain storage and offerings within a **particular ceremonial sequence**. This is cultural documentation, not a recipe for millet alcohol.[^v112-paiwan-agency]

## Do not merge the ceremonies

The same agency describes a Five-Year Ritual among specified Vuculj Paiwan communities, with offerings that include millet wine. A harvest thanksgiving and a five-year ancestral observance are different kinds of occasions. They should not be flattened into an annual 'Taiwan millet-beer festival'; dates and degrees of public participation depend on communities and living practice.[^v112-paiwan-agency]

## Sources and consent

The agency publishes public-facing descriptions, but that does not authorize reproduction of restricted ritual details, instructions, performer identities, or a claim of community approval of this English synthesis. A separate 2013 microbial study of an Indigenous Taiwanese fermented millet beverage does not identify its participants in the indexed abstract and should not be assigned to Paiwan ceremonies. Local review and translation remain needed.[^v112-paiwan-agency]
