# The culture that contained more than thirty yeasts

## When one label hides a crowd

A 2026 study examined 1,760 yeast isolates from 44 household-style farmhouse cultures across Norway, Latvia, Lithuania and Russia. The authors found that some cultures were near-monocultures, while others contained more than 30 *S. cerevisiae* variants. Imagine handing down what everyone calls 'our yeast' and discovering the word refers to an evolving community.[^v13-farmhouse26]

## Heritage has biological dimensions

A separate 2026 genomic analysis of western Norwegian kveik investigated 64 isolates from 25 cultures. Together these studies illustrate that a name, a commercial strain, a family's actual culture, and an evolutionary lineage need not refer to the same biological object.[^v13-kveik26][^v13-farmhouse26]

## What the story cannot conclude

Genetics cannot establish exactly how a family celebrated brewing, what every historical beer tasted like, or a date of invention. Compare [Farmhouse genomics](../microbiology/farmhouse-yeast-diversity.md), [Kveik](../ingredients/kveik.md) and [Culture storage](../objects/yeast-storage-tools.md).

## Evidence boundaries

The striking number is a sample observation, not a property of every culture named kveik. All dated observations are tied to the source and location described above. This is a research draft.
