# The yeast that made sugar before another made alcohol

When people say “yeast makes beer,” one important step can disappear: a cereal’s starch is not automatically available as fermentable sugar. In a Philippine rice-fermentation study, the researchers identified a strong starch-breaking yeast, *Saccharomycopsis fibuligera*, alongside an alcoholic fermenter, *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*. The surprising part is that **a yeast can be crucial without being the organism making most of the alcohol**.[^v17-bubod-1990]

## The experiment, not a folktale

Kozaki and Uchimura isolated fungi and bacteria from studied bubod and tapuy samples in 1990. They examined physiological characteristics and inferred distinct functions. They did not prove that every starter contains a permanent two-organism partnership or that isolated microbes reproduce the entire social practice of preparing a starter.[^v17-bubod-1990]

## The next question

A 2025 study of tapuy lees again draws attention to variation among starter communities. A family of starters could share a name while differing in the organisms a laboratory cultivates. That is what makes research on starter-making compelling: culture, substrate, species and observed function are related but not identical.[^v17-bubod-2025]
