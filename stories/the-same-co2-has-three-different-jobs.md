# One molecule of CO₂ can be fermentation exhaust, cooling fluid or beer bubbles

## The same chemical formula is not the same evidence

A paper about fermentation CO₂ recovery and one about CO₂ heat pumps can sound as though the brewery has the same device performing two identical tasks. But Grand and colleagues' 2025 work focuses on gas emitted during alcoholic fermentation, its potential capture and how five French craft brewers viewed the economics of reuse. The gas may become an industrial resource if appropriately handled; the research does not declare it automatically beverage-grade.[^v119-craftco2]

Padullés and colleagues' 2026 thermal paper considers a **CO₂ heat pump** as part of a modeled brewing heat-recovery system. Here the formula names a refrigerant/working fluid circulating in refrigeration machinery, not CO₂ coming directly from wort or a fermenter to carbonate a glass of beer. The device's performance is an energy-model question, not a capture-efficiency study.[^v119-pinch26]

## The third role is in the drink

Dissolved CO₂ contributes to beer carbonation and service; its physical behavior depends on conditions distinct from the two industrial gas systems. Meanwhile the GHG Protocol separates direct, purchased-energy and value-chain emissions. Using a molecule twice does not mean it was permanently removed from the atmosphere, nor does a heat pump by itself erase upstream farming impacts.[^v119-ghgfaq]

For good documentation, ask whether a source studies gas capture, refrigerant energy transfer, beverage composition or greenhouse-gas accounting. The same chemical name does not merge those domains.
