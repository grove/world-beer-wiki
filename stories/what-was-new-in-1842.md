# What actually became new in Pilsen in 1842?

One of beer history's most memorable dates is **5 October 1842**. A neat version of the story says a brewer invented Pilsner that day and the world changed overnight. The evidence is more interesting: Pilsen had long had burghers with brewing rights; the town organized a new brewery; and Josef Groll's first batch took place inside that institutional transformation.[^v114-plzen]

A producer's chronology names the date and brewer, and traces the beer's later movement into new cities and markets. Historical scholarship also points to the brewery's own 1892 anniversary history. That source is close to the institutional memory, but a promotional or commemorative history deserves scrutiny. The full historian's paper was not accessible in this research pass; an indexed opening is not a license to fill in the missing pages.[^v114-plzen-history]

The boundary matters: **new brewery, named batch, influential later style** are not the same assertion as **first cold-fermented beer anywhere**. Nor can the exact 1842 flavor be recovered from a modern category description. Historians need dated records and material evidence to test stronger claims, not just a memorable anniversary.[^v114-plzen]
