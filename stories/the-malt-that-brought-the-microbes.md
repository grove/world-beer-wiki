# The malt that brought the microbes

Imagine reading a simple mahewu ingredient list: cooked maize, water and millet malt. Where did the microbes enter? One tempting answer is 'the air'. A 2019 study tested a more specific explanation, identifying selected lactic acid bacterial strains in **both millet malt and the finished mahewu**. The ingredient was not only grain; in this system it carried a plausible microbial inheritance.[^v112-mahewu-2019]

## The next experiment complicated the story

A 2026 study used defined mixtures of bacteria to ask how selected starter cultures performed at different temperatures. The laboratory community did not respond identically at 15°C and 25°C. That is the kind of science that answers one hypothesis while producing another: what happens in a home with different grain, water, storage, microflora and thermal conditions?[^v112-mahewu-2026]

## Why the human history still matters

Neither experiment tells us who taught a household to add malt, what a grandmother called her preferred grain, or how a beverage entered a community gathering. Those are cultural and historical questions for willing makers and local-language sources. This is an evidence-led detective story, not a claim that science has discovered the one true traditional starter or proved the drink's safety.[^v112-mahewu-2019]
