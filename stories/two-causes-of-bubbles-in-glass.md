# A stream of beer bubbles can have two different explanations

## What if the glass tells two stories?

A bartender sees bubbles sticking to one side of a poured beer. One possible explanation is a residue-related glass-cleanliness problem. The Colorado Brewers Guild's practical guidance treats certain wall-bubble and lacing observations as prompts to examine cleaning and service.[^v115-glass]

But the same visual phenomenon can arise for a different physical reason. An ACS Omega study explored CO2 nucleation at tiny gas pockets and surface irregularities. Some glassware is deliberately prepared to encourage a stream of bubbles, so visible bubbles by themselves cannot prove that a glass was poorly cleaned.[^v115-bubbles]

## Comparing observations with mechanisms

A cleaning guide gives a rapid service cue; a controlled physical study models when and where carbon dioxide can leave the liquid. Each has strengths and limits. The guide does not identify a particular microbe or chemical on the glass. The model does not declare that all bubbly glasses are clean or that a lager will always yield the same number of bubbles.

The better follow-up is to inspect the vessel's intended design, compare repeated pours under similar conditions and examine more than one cue. That is a research method, not a diagnosis from a photograph.
