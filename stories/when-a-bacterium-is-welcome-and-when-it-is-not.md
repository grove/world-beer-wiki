# A bacterium can be welcome in one beer and spoil another

Lactic acid bacteria can help create deliberate acidity, yet breweries also monitor related organisms as potential spoilage risks. The contradiction disappears when we ask **what process the brewer intended**, and what an organism actually did in that setting.[^v16-hop-review]

## The hop-defense puzzle

Hop iso-alpha acids inhibit many bacteria, but some beer-associated strains withstand them. An experimental *L. brevis* study found multiple adaptations in one resistant strain, including membrane and wall changes, without the single export mechanism that might have seemed an obvious explanation.[^v16-hop-primary]

## Name, observation, function

“Bacteria detected” does not mean “bacteria caused the sour taste.” “Sour beer” does not prove the wort was kettle-soured; some acid-producing yeasts exist too. Similarly, an unwanted organism in one packaged beer is not equivalent to its intentional management in another fermentation.
