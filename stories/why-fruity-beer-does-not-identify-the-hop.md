# A fruity beer does not identify its hop

Someone tastes tropical fruit in a beer and confidently names the hop. That can be a fun guess, but scientific measurements provide a cautionary plot twist. A 2024 study of 250 hop samples found that free thiols varied with more than the cultivar label. Growing area, crop year and harvest timing also mattered in the analyzed material.[^v18-hop-thiol-survey]

## The yeast changes the evidence

A different 2024 model-fermentation experiment added individual terpenoid standards and found multiple volatile products after fermentation with commercial US-05 yeast. A 2026 pilot-scale study showed thiol release changing across fermentation time and between selected ale and lager yeasts. Together these results explain why a final aroma is not a fingerprint of one raw material.[^v18-hop-terpenes][^v18-thiol-time]

## A sensory word is not an instrument trace

Chemical measurements identify and quantify molecules with a defined procedure. A tasting note records perception in a complex beverage. Either can be useful, but neither alone establishes a brewery's ingredient list. The additional uncertainties of packaging, oxidation, and serving are separate questions.

## Follow the clues
