# What ancient beer cannot tell us

A residue can be evidence of remarkable skill without being a recoverable menu. The [Shangshan](../styles/shangshan-beer.md) and [Mijiaya](../styles/mijiaya-beer.md) profiles describe scientific interpretations of archaeological material. Neither should be read as a surviving brewer's document specifying a modern ingredient bill and production schedule.[^atlas-shangshan-context][^atlas-mijiaya-context]

## The missing tasting note is not a failure

The Mijiaya entry explicitly separates inferred ingredients and processing from unrecovered properties such as exact aroma, color, carbonation and alcohol strength. Its modern site-based label is not a recovered historical style name.[^atlas-mijiaya-context]

That boundary can make the story more compelling: we can ask what kinds of traces survived, why researchers interpret them as they do, and what alternative explanations must be considered. Filling every blank with a vivid taste description would make the account less informative, not more complete.

## Two separate objects of knowledge

| Subject | What belongs to it |
|---|---|
| Archaeological material | The recovered traces, analytical methods and bounded interpretation. |
| Modern recreation | A newly brewed product, its documented choices and the experience of tasting it. |

A modern recreation may help explore a hypothesis. Its actual recipe and sensory results still belong to that recreation. They should not be copied backward into the ancient record without an argument and evidence.

## A better kind of historical curiosity

Follow [Historical and revival beers](../categories/historical-and-revival-beers.md) to compare archaeology with dated brewing records and living traditions. These sources answer different questions; their differences should remain visible.

The wiki also avoids presenting the age of one site as an evergreen world record. A claim to be “the oldest known” needs a precisely defined comparison and a fresh literature check, not repetition of an old headline.

## Evidence note

This is a reading route built from the existing archaeological profiles. It does not independently inspect artifacts, reproduce their laboratory analyses or establish continuity from an ancient preparation to any present-day community's beverage.
