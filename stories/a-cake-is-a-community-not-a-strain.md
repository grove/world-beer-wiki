# A starter cake is not a strain catalogue

Comparative narrative with bounded Himalayan, Korean and Vietnamese starter studies; no implied unbroken transmission.

## The small object with many jobs

Imagine cataloguing a dried starter cake by writing “yeast” on a museum label. That misses the cereal substrate, renewal practice, enzymatic starch breakdown, ethanol-producing organisms, bacteria and the households that transmit know-how. An Eastern Himalayan study documents several locally named cakes and multiple microbial groups; none is defined by the single most recognizable yeast name.[^v15-himalaya-2019]

## A laboratory opens the label

Korean nuruk genomics reveals that an amylolytic yeast such as *Saccharomycopsis fibuligera* can have a role other than being the main alcohol producer. Vietnamese starter studies reveal how fungal and bacterial communities differ between tested cakes. The findings add explanatory layers rather than superseding each maker’s terminology.[^v15-fibuligera][^v15-banhmen-2008]

## The twist

Microbiological resemblance is not proof of cultural borrowing. To investigate ancestry, researchers would need independently dated records, linguistic, material and community evidence. An isolate collection by itself cannot tell that story. This is precisely why a world beer wiki needs microbiology *and* anthropology on separate pages.[^v15-himalaya-2019]
