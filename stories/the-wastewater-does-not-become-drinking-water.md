# When brewery wastewater makes biogas, that does not mean it becomes potable water

## A treatment figure has a narrowly defined meaning

A brewery might report that a wastewater process removed a high percentage of COD. The 2026 brewery-waste review explains COD alongside biological oxygen demand, suspended solids, nutrient loads and very variable pH in liquid waste streams. A reduction in an oxygen-demand assay is useful, but it is not an assay proving that a water sample is fit to drink or use as a beer ingredient.[^v119-wastewater26]

Ahmadinejad and colleagues' 2026 bioelectrochemical digester experiment treated real brewery wastewater over a year and measured COD conversion alongside methane production. Its results belong to that reactor, load and sampling; it did not certify the outgoing water to every local standard or eliminate all pollutants.[^v119-bead26]

A 2025 renewable natural gas paper adds still another layer: it modeled multiple options for converting wastewater-derived biogas to fuel, with different hydrogen sources and costs. Those scenarios do not show that the liquid product became drinking water, that the gas was sold, or that any company had a negative lifecycle carbon footprint.[^v119-rng25]

The right questions are which pollutant was measured, where the sample was collected, what treatment followed, and what the relevant downstream regulatory use would be. See [COD versus footprint](../categories/wastewater-cod-is-not-water-footprint.md), [digestion research](../processes/brewery-wastewater-anaerobic-digestion.md) and [the fuel scenarios](../environment/brewery-wastewater-to-renewable-gas-scenarios.md).
