# One beer, two judging books

A short evidence-based story about why category labels diverge between two modern judging systems; no claim of shared lineage.

## An apparent contradiction

A reader searches for a named beer in a judging guide and cannot find it. Another official guide lists the name. Has a style appeared from nowhere? More likely the reader has compared two distinct institutions, editions and entry criteria. The BJCP’s 2021 introduction explicitly treats grouping as a judging convenience, not a historical family tree.[^v15-bjcp-2021]

## The calendar matters here too

The Brewers Association 2026 list and BJCP 2021 guide can both be current for their own organizations in October 2026. A date-stamped source identifier therefore matters more than treating the most recent number as a universal replacement. “Latest” must be evaluated *per publisher*, not across different publishers.[^v15-ba-2026][^v15-bjcp-2021]

## The practical resolution

A reference wiki can show both attributed classifications and let the beer’s documented process and ingredients have their own pages. It should not manufacture a BJCP entry to match an existing BA name or erase a living tradition because neither system includes it.[^v15-ba-2026]
