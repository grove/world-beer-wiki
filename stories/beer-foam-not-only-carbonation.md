# When beer foams unexpectedly, the bottle does not tell you why

## A dramatic opening invites an easy answer

An unopened beer is carried to a table, the cap comes off and foam overflows. One explanation could be excess dissolved gas. But scientists also study primary gushing, where fungal hydrophobins associated with grain history can contribute to vigorous foaming. A 2024 barley-and-Fusarium research paper describes that mechanism in the context of its own isolates and malt samples—not a universal cause established by watching a bottle.[^v117-fungal-strains]

A 2022 review of Fusarium-derived compounds in malt and beer is valuable for another reason. Chemical toxin presence, fungal growth and beer foam are different endpoints. The review cannot support the inference that any one overflowing package contains a specific toxin, or that the absence of excess foam proves safe grain handling.[^v117-fusarium-review]

## Which observation would answer the question?

The service worker sees an event. The packaging engineer may measure carbonation or package condition. A grain quality laboratory might investigate raw materials, and a food-safety assessment requires an entirely different evidence chain. Each account can contribute without eliminating the others.

The physical location matters too: bubbles on a serving glass are not the same observation as an eruption from a sealed package. Nor is a single visual event a reliable classification of the beer's quality or style.
