# The brewery they closed with fire

A sourced narrative of Cerro Baúl’s staged termination; no dialogue, motives or sensory details invented.

## A peculiar archaeological scene

An apparently productive monumental brewery is a striking thing to abandon. Excavations at Wari Cerro Baúl found destroyed ceramic vessels, hearths and structural burning within a wider sequence of closure and feasting. The team interprets this as deliberate ceremonial termination of the enclave—not a catastrophic brewery accident.[^v15-wari-2005]

## A story with a scientific twist

The rooms suggest milling, heating and fermentation, while drupes of molle occur in the deposits. But archaeologists cannot assert with certainty whether the last brews were solely maize, solely molle, or combined. Even a spectacular find has a blind spot where a tempting “last recipe” would be.[^v15-wari-2005]

## How later work changed the question

A 2024 ethnoarchaeological study compared archaeological dregs with informant-guided brewing experiments and argued for specialized provincial production rather than ubiquitous household brewing. This changes the narrative from a tale of exotic drink to one about work, power, preservation and archaeological inference.[^v15-wari-2024]
