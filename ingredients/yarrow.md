# Yarrow and the uncertainty of historical herb blends

## An herb on many lists

Yarrow (*Achillea millefolium*) appears in published reference descriptions of herbs historically associated with gruit. But a list of possible components is not a recipe, and it does not establish that every city, brewer or period used identical plants. The word 'gruit' also has administrative and economic histories beyond a botanical ingredient list.[^v13-gruit]

## The wrong kind of certainty

Modern beer inspired by herbal brewing sometimes foregrounds one named plant. That is a contemporary choice unless a particular archival or field source documents its use. A sensory association such as bitterness or herbal aroma cannot verify a plant species in an old beer merely from its historic name.[^v13-gruit]

## Follow the distinct layers

Compare [Gruit](../styles/gruit.md), [Bog myrtle](bog-myrtle.md), and [Juniper](juniper.md). These cases illustrate why herbal blends need exact species, materials, extraction context and geographically bounded history.

The cited dictionary is secondary synthesis, not a historical prescription, botanical safety study or proof of a standard medieval formula. All dated observations are tied to the source and location described above. This is a research draft.
