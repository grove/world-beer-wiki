# Spent grain and surplus yeast can be energy feedstocks, not interchangeable products

## Byproducts have different compositions and possible uses

Brewer's spent grain and surplus yeast are large solid byproduct streams of brewing, but they differ in fibre, protein, moisture and microbial composition. A 2026 systematic-style circularity review of spent grain and spent yeast surveys reuse in feed, food, biotechnology, materials and energy. It does not declare that all those routes yield equally low emissions or that an untested byproduct is approved for human consumption.[^v119-spent26]

A second 2026 bioenergy review examines conversion of brewery residues and waste streams into biogas or hydrogen-containing products through anaerobic digestion and related systems. It identifies substrate-specific microbial and process constraints. A theoretical methane potential for one sample is not automatically a confirmed fuel yield for any other brewery's spent grain or yeast.[^v119-bioenergy26]

A 2025 spent-grain valorization review surveys further possibilities including material uses and biosorbents. These are technology pathways rather than compulsory priorities for every local brewer, and they do not compare the full life-cycle value of a food use against an energy use without a defined functional unit.[^v119-wastewaterrngreview]

For an ingredient directory, retain the original grain or yeast source, whether the material was tested wet/dry, its current legal food/feed status, and the particular study's analytical boundaries. The existence of possible fuel use does not certify food safety or establish a climate benefit. Compare [spent grain](brewers-spent-grain.md), [surplus yeast](surplus-brewers-yeast-as-material.md) and [food-waste use versus energy digestion](../food/food-waste-brewing-is-not-wastewater-digestion.md).
