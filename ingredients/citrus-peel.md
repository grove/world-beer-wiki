# Citrus peel and citrus-like aroma

An actual citrus-peel ingredient and an aroma reminiscent of citrus are different observations. BJCP's Witbier account names dried Curaçao orange peel and allows sweet orange peel in its characteristic ingredient discussion. That is more specific evidence than the presence of “orange” in a tasting note.[^atlas-witbier]

## Keep form and purpose visible

Peel, juice, whole fruit, an extract and a flavor impression should not share one undifferentiated field. A useful ingredient entry preserves what was added, the source that identifies it, and its intended role. Neither the word “citrus” nor a style label answers those questions for every beer.

The guideline also describes the spice-and-citrus contribution as integrated with the underlying beer. It is a balance description rather than a requirement for maximum intensity or one universal dose.[^atlas-witbier]

## A counterexample from a different tradition

The [Vossaøl profile](../styles/vossaol.md) reports orange-peel-like and spicy impressions in an observed beer without orange peel in the documented ingredient list. The observer attributed those impressions to fermentation. This is an attributed field observation, not a laboratory identification of every aroma compound.[^atlas-voss-context]

Read [Coriander](coriander.md) and [Witbier](../styles/witbier.md) together, then compare the Voss account. The lesson is not that an aroma can never indicate an ingredient, but that it cannot establish the ingredient on its own.

## Evidence note

The page does not impose one botanical name or preparation on every commercial peel product. Exact material specifications require the relevant producer or research record. Nor does the comparison establish that all Voss cultures or all beers fermented with them have an identical citrus character.
