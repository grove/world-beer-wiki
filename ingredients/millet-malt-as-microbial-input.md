# Millet malt as starch resource and microbial carrier

Malted grain is often introduced in brewing as a **source of enzymes** that can make starch accessible to fermentation. Zimbabwean mahewu work demonstrates another possibility: malt supplied after heating can carry microorganisms into cooked cereal material. In a 2019 study, strain-specific tests supported millet malt as a major source of selected mahewu lactic acid bacteria.[^v112-mahewu-2019]

## The same material has several causal roles

Millet malt may contribute flavor, nutrients, potential enzymatic activity and a community of organisms; those roles should not be conflated. The 2026 study sequenced six finger-millet malt samples and showed substantial variation in the microbial profiles. It also found bacterial DNA that raised hygiene questions, and tested model starter cultures as one proposed control strategy. These studies are not a general endorsement of raw malt as a safe inoculum.[^v112-mahewu-2026]

## Neither universal ingredient nor recipe

Unheated millet malt is not an obligatory ingredient in all mahewu, nor is a sampled organism a compulsory strain for every regional drink. Regional grain availability matters, and no microbiological observation provides a home sanitation guarantee. Compare the maize/sorghum/millet ingredient guide and mahewu process page to keep substrate chemistry distinct from the microorganisms brought into a particular batch.[^v112-mahewu-2019]
