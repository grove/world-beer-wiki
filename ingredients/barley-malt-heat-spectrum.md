# Pale, kilned and roasted malt: the heat spectrum

## Heat changes both chemistry and capability

WSET’s brewing education distinguishes gently kilned pale malts from more intensively heated, toasted and roasted grains. Heat changes grain color and aroma while also affecting the enzymes available in subsequent mashing. Two malts that began as the same cereal can behave differently as ingredients.[^v13-malt-wset]

## Which browning reaction?

The educational account separates Maillard chemistry—involving amino compounds and sugars—from caramelization of sugars. Familiar descriptors such as toast, bread crust, nuts, caramel and roast are sensory consequences, not laboratory proof that one reaction occurred exclusively in a particular finished beer.[^v13-malt-wset]

## Why historical color is difficult

Compare [Barley and malt](barley-and-malt.md), [Smoked malt](smoked-malt.md) and [Malting and kilning](../processes/malting-and-kilning.md). The same beer-style name can survive technological changes in malt production, so historical color and intensity need dated evidence.

## Edition 1.16: newly sourced connections

The colour value given for a malt ingredient is not automatically the colour of the finished beer. BJCP's brewing-ingredient education describes colour as affected by malt selection as well as other processing conditions; translating one sack's colour specification into a specific beer's SRM requires further recipe and sampling evidence.[^v116-malt]

The BJCP's separate colour guide describes beer SRM as a 430-nanometre optical measurement. A visually hazy or deeply coloured pour can complicate a naked-eye estimate, while a controlled laboratory sample and a printed comparison aid serve different purposes.[^v116-color]

For historical or community ferment discussions, do not assign an exact malt-colour-derived beer number unless the recipe and method are actually documented. The [malt versus finished beer guide](malt-colour-versus-finished-beer-colour.md) and [beer colour laboratory method](../processes/beer-colour-measurement.md) keep physical ingredient identity and measured appearance distinct.
