# Aguamiel and maguey agave sap

Aguamiel is sweet sap collected from certain maguey agaves and fermented to make pulque. It is **not** mashed cereal wort, and pulque should not be conflated with distilled agave spirits. The botanical and handling conditions matter because sap already carries nutrients and may encounter microorganisms during collection before it joins a maintained ferment.[^v111-pulque-review]

## Sugar and microbial ecology

The pulque review explains how fermenting organisms use sap sugars and can produce ethanol, organic acid and extracellular polysaccharides. Sap's sucrose content and the conditions of collection influence which transformations occur. That does not make every plant or every species of agave equivalent, nor guarantee a particular alcohol or microbial outcome from sap alone.[^v111-pulque-review]

## Agricultural material and cultural meaning

INAH's colonial-ritual study describes historical symbolic practices around maguey cultivation and first pulque. Those cultural representations are not another chemical ingredient or automatically practiced by every present-day maker. Document the source voice separately, avoid collecting restricted rites, and prefer identified regional terminology in any future botanical species inventory. Plant taxonomy, collection technique and ritual evidence deserve their own citations.[^v111-pulque-ritual]
