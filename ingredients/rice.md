# Rice

Rice can supply starch in a barley-based mash or a rice ferment, and can carry a saccharifying culture. Record those roles separately: rice in a pale lager does not make it sake.[^ing-crisp-rice][^ing-jssa-process]

## Rice in a malt-based mash

Raw cereal preparations and pre-cooked brewing flakes have different processing needs. Crisp describes its torrefied rice as cooked so that the starch is gelatinised before flaking; its mashing guide distinguishes such preparations from rice requiring separate cooking. This makes “rice” alone an incomplete ingredient specification.[^ing-crisp-rice][^ing-crisp-mashing]

The supplier describes a relatively light contribution to wort colour and protein compared with an all-malt formulation. BJCP's [Pre-Prohibition Lager](../styles/pre-prohibition-lager.md) account also distinguishes relatively neutral rice-based versions from corn-associated character. Neither observation means every rice beer must be neutral: proportion, rice preparation and the rest of the fermentation matter.[^ing-crisp-rice][^bjcp-2021-27f]

## Rice with koji and other starters

In the sake process described by the Japan Sake and Shochu Makers Association, some steamed rice becomes koji through cultivation of *Aspergillus oryzae*. Koji enzymes break down starch while yeast ferments the released sugars in the mash. This is different from assuming that rice itself supplies the same enzyme system as barley malt.[^ing-jssa-process]

Gekkeikan's producer overview separates rice preparation, koji making, yeast-starter preparation and the main fermentation. These distinctions are more informative than the loose English phrase “rice wine”. Read [Sake](../styles/sake.md), [Makgeolli](../styles/makgeolli.md), [Sato](../styles/sato.md), [Haria](../styles/haria.md) and [Tuak](../styles/tuak-rice.md) through their own process accounts rather than treating them as aliases.[^ing-gekkeikan-process]

## Rice hulls are another material

Briess describes rice hulls as a filtering aid, especially useful in grists that are difficult to separate. Their presence is not evidence that rice starch is a major fermentable ingredient. An ingredient database should keep hulls and rice grain distinct.[^ing-briess-products]

## Evidence note

These sources illustrate different roles, not every rice variety or regional practice. A sake producer's process must not be generalized to all Asian cereal ferments, and an adjunct supplier's product description must not be treated as a definition of Asian brewing.

## Edition 1.9: newly sourced connections

The 2022 Huangjiu microbiome review describes multiple ways grain rice can become an alcoholic fermented drink: steaming the substrate, using wheat *qu* or related starters for enzymatic conversion, then managing a mixed fermentation. The grain itself does not explain the full resulting drink or its cultural name. Compare [Huangjiu](../styles/huangjiu-chinese-cereal-wine.md), [wheat qu](wheat-qu-and-huangjiu.md), [makgeolli](../styles/makgeolli.md) and [rượu cần](../styles/ruou-can-vietnamese-jar-ferment.md). Similar starch chemistry does not make these the same national tradition or a single standardized beer category.[^v19-huangjiu-review]
