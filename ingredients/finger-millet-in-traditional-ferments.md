# Finger millet in mbege and kodo ko jaanr

**Finger millet** (*Eleusine coracana*) appears in both an East African mixed-fruit-and-grain fermentation and a Himalayan grain ferment. The recurrence of the crop does not imply shared ancestry, equivalent starter organisms or the same way of using it. In northeastern Tanzanian mbege, germinated finger millet supplies the sweet **mso** portion that is later mixed with fermented banana **nyalu**.[^v18-mbege]

## A different system in Darjeeling and Sikkim

Thapa and Tamang's 2006 study examined *kodo ko jaanr*, a finger-millet beverage in the Darjeeling hills and Sikkim. Its observed fermentation involved changing populations of filamentous fungi, yeasts and lactic-acid bacteria. The authors linked starch breakdown to selected moulds and starch-active yeasts. This is not a field observation of Tanzanian mbege, nor does the study describe every Himalayan starter.[^v18-jaanr]

## Crop identity is only the beginning

“Malted millet” and “fermented millet” describe distinct process moments. Germination can generate a useful enzymatic substrate, while microorganisms in a starter can supply additional hydrolytic action. A grain name cannot by itself tell readers which starch-conversion route was used, which culture made the alcohol, or what the beverage tasted like.

## What the comparison can responsibly say

Both documented systems use finger millet and rely on transformations that make starch or sugars available to fermenters. The responsible next step is the [Mbege brewing account](../processes/mbege-nyalu-and-mso.md) beside [Kodo ko jaanr research](../microbiology/jaanr-microbial-succession.md), not a claim of historical diffusion. See the wider [millet reference](sorghum-and-millet.md) for distinguishing cereals.
