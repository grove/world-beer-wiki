# Barley proteins that help make beer foam

A head of foam is not generated by carbon dioxide alone. Foam films can contain surface-active molecules from grain and hops, and their behavior changes with brewing, fermentation and service. Barley contributes **lipid-transfer protein 1 (LTP1)** and a family of **Protein Z** proteins, both widely studied for foam-related behavior. A review describes the stability of these proteins and differences among barley forms.[^v18-foam-z]

## Why a protein name is not a foam guarantee

Two beers with comparable total protein may not have the same foam. Protein fraction, modification, interaction with hop-derived compounds, lipids and serving conditions all matter. Proteomics research followed different barley proteins through mashing and wort boiling, finding that relative abundances changed at distinct stages. A protein detected in beer has survived the process; its mere presence is not a complete sensory prediction.[^v18-foam-proteome]

## Protein Z versus LTP1

Protein Z belongs to a barley serpin family and has considerable brewing-process stability. LTP1 is a smaller lipid-transfer protein whose transformations affect surface properties. They are not a single combined ingredient, and they should not be treated as substitutes in a recipe. Their concentrations depend on cultivar and processing as well as later production steps.[^v18-foam-z]
