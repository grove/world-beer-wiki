# Maize

Maize, also called corn, can be a major fermentation substrate or part of a barley-based grist. The distinction is cultural as well as technical: a modern brewing adjunct and the maize foundation of a local tradition should not be described as if one were merely a substitute version of the other.[^ing-crisp-maize][^chicha-jora-study]

## Processing changes the brewing role

Commercial flaked maize can be pre-gelatinised. Crisp describes a heat treatment that makes its starch available without the separate pre-cooking needed for other maize preparations. The mashing guide distinguishes such flakes from grits requiring cooking. “Corn” therefore does not tell a brewer whether the preparation is ready for a malt mash.[^ing-crisp-maize][^ing-crisp-mashing]

The supplier also describes a lightening effect and a possible cornflake-like sensory contribution. These are capabilities of that product in context, not proof that every beer made with maize is sweet, light in alcohol or poor in flavour.[^ing-crisp-maize]

## Different modern reference styles

BJCP's [Cream Ale](../styles/cream-ale.md) includes maize among its characteristic ingredients, while the [Pre-Prohibition Lager](../styles/pre-prohibition-lager.md) description permits corn or rice and expects a substantially different overall balance. The same grain can therefore sit within different fermentation and hopping frameworks.[^bjcp-2021-1c][^bjcp-2021-27f]

## Tradition cannot be read from the grain alone

Bassi and colleagues examined chicha de jora from multiple Peruvian producers and found substantial microbial differences among samples. Their study is evidence for a living maize-based fermentation with local variation, not a single recipe applying to every beverage called chicha.[^chicha-jora-study]

Use [Chicha de jora](../styles/chicha-de-jora.md), [Tesgüino](../styles/tesguino.md), [Busaa](../styles/busaa.md) and [Umqombothi](../styles/umqombothi.md) as separate research routes. Their inclusion together here is an ingredient navigation choice. It does not erase differences in other grains, inoculation, saccharification, serving practice or community identity; consult the evidence on each profile for the particular formulation.

## Evidence note

The technical product accounts concern contemporary malt-based brewing. The chicha study concerns sampled Peruvian production. Neither supports ranking drinks by whether maize was used, nor turning all maize fermentations into a universally recognized style.

## Edition 1.6: sourced research connections

A 1997 experiment investigated a Zambian **cooked maize–Rhynchosia root** munkoyo system, illustrating that maize starch can be made available by a route other than conventional germinated-barley malt. The studied root extract supplied amylase activity under selected experimental conditions; not all maize beers or related ferments use this method.[^v16-munkoyo]

See [the ingredient](rhynchosia-root-for-munkoyo.md) and [process comparison](../processes/munkoyo-root-starch-conversion.md).
