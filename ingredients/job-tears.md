# Job’s tears in evidence for ancient Chinese fermented beverages

## The archaeological question

Job’s tears (*Coix lacryma-jobi*) is identified among the plant remains interpreted as ingredients in research on fermentation-related vessels at Mijiaya. The researchers combined microfossil, phytolith and chemical evidence, rather than inferring brewing solely from the presence of a grain. This is a documented possibility in an ancient mixed-plant system, not an asserted standard modern beer ingredient.[^v13-mijiaya]

## An older but distinct assemblage

At Shangshan, microfossil evidence also includes Job’s tears among several starch sources. The two sites differ in age, region, archaeological context and analytical claims. Shared plants do not establish that the makers shared a recipe or direct line of transmission.[^v13-shangshan]

## How to read the record

Compare [Mijiaya](../styles/mijiaya-beer.md), [Shangshan](../styles/shangshan-beer.md), and [Archaeological evidence](../history/how-archaeologists-identify-beer.md). Plant remains, fermentation-related wear and microorganism-like structures are separate parts of a multi-proxy argument.

## Evidence boundaries

No reconstructed flavor, required recipe proportions, or claim about an unbroken present-day tradition is supported. All dated observations are tied to the source and location described above. This is a research draft.
