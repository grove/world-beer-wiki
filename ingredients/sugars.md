# Sugars, syrups and lactose

Adding sugar does not necessarily leave a sweet beer. Fermentable sugars supply yeast with material that can become alcohol and carbon dioxide, while sweetness depends on what remains and how the finished beer is perceived. Lallemand's yeast-nutrition guide distinguishes simple sugars from carbohydrates whose use depends on strain and enzymes.[^ing-yeast-nutrition]

## Describe the sugar and its purpose

| Ingredient description | Question it raises |
| --- | --- |
| Glucose, fructose or sucrose | Was the addition fermented, and what did it contribute beyond fermentable extract? |
| Maltose-rich wort or syrup | Which yeast could use its carbohydrate mixture? |
| Dark or flavourful syrup, honey or other named sugar source | What additional colour or flavour was intended, and did it persist? |
| Lactose | Was it used to leave sweetness rather than simply increase fermentable extract? |

Yeast commonly consumes glucose, fructose and sucrose early in fermentation; maltotriose use varies, while dextrin use requires particular capabilities. An ingredient inventory that records only “sugar” loses this distinction.[^ing-yeast-nutrition]

## Lactose and the sweet-stout comparison

BJCP's modern [Sweet Stout](../styles/sweet-stout.md) account identifies lactose as a frequent addition for residual sweetness. It does not require every sweet stout, creamy beer or dessert-themed beer to contain lactose. A sensory impression of cream is not an ingredient declaration.[^bjcp-2021-16a]

[Oatmeal Stout](../styles/oatmeal-stout.md) offers a useful comparison because its name draws attention to [Oats](oats.md), a different type of input. [Pastry Stout](../styles/pastry-stout.md) is another reading route, where the actual additions should be documented instead of inferred from the label.

## Fermentable additions can also carry flavour

BJCP's specialty-fruit framework distinguishes extra fermentable sugars from sweeteners and notes that additions such as honey or molasses can contribute aroma and flavour. This separates two questions: whether an ingredient is fermented and whether it leaves recognizable character. They should not be collapsed into a single “sweetness addition” field.[^ing-bjcp-specialty-fruit]

For comparison, read [Belgian Tripel](../styles/belgian-tripel.md), [Belgian Golden Strong Ale](../styles/belgian-golden-strong-ale.md) and [Fruit Beer](../styles/fruit-beer.md) alongside their own cited ingredient descriptions. The page links do not imply one common syrup recipe.

## Evidence note

No generic syrup colour or trade name is treated as a complete chemical specification. Exact attenuation, alcohol and sweetness need the formulation and fermentation outcome. This reference does not make dietary or allergen claims about an individual finished beer.
