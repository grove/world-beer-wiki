# Brewing yeast

Yeast turns available sugars into alcohol and carbon dioxide while producing other compounds that contribute to beer character. It is also a living culture whose behaviour depends on its nutrients and environment. Recording a yeast name is useful, but it does not replace a description of the fermentation.[^ing-yeast-nutrition]

## Species, strain and culture

| Level | What it tells the reader |
| --- | --- |
| Species | A biological identity, such as *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*. |
| Strain | A particular lineage with its own practical characteristics. |
| Culture | What the brewer actually maintains or pitches; it may include more than one strain or organism. |
| Fermentation practice | How that culture is used with a particular wort, temperature and sequence. |

Gallone and colleagues' study of industrial *S. cerevisiae* found substantial genetic and functional differentiation associated with domestication. Sugar utilization, stress responses and flavour production were among the selected traits. “Ale yeast” should therefore not imply one uniform behaviour or flavour.[^ing-yeast-domestication]

## Ale and lager do not form a complete world taxonomy

Lager yeast, *S. pastorianus*, has ancestry from *S. cerevisiae* and the cold-tolerant species *S. eubayanus*. Libkind and colleagues' work identified that wild genetic contribution. It supports the hybrid ancestry, without establishing every detail of how or where historical lager brewing began.[^ing-lager-ancestry]

Other named fermentation systems need their own descriptions. Read [Kveik](kveik.md) for a domesticated farmhouse-yeast context, [Brett Beer](../styles/brett-beer.md) for a different yeast-associated category, and [Mould and mixed starters](mould-and-mixed-starters.md) for cultures where several biological roles may be combined.

## Yeast choice changes what remains in beer

Lallemand's technical overview distinguishes readily used simple sugars from carbohydrates whose utilization varies among strains. It also identifies nitrogen, vitamins and minerals as relevant to fermentation performance. These observations explain why an ingredient list does not independently predict attenuation, sweetness or alcohol.[^ing-yeast-nutrition]

Some yeasts can produce appreciable lactic acid as well as alcohol. Lallemand's comparison of souring cultures includes a *Lachancea* yeast and an engineered *Saccharomyces* product, alongside bacteria. Thus a sour taste alone does not identify a bacterial fermentation.[^ing-sour-solutions]

## Evidence note

Genomic ancestry, commercial strain descriptions and sensory categories answer different questions. This reference does not treat package names as complete regional traditions, warm fermentation as proof of an ale style, or a laboratory strain's limits as those of all brewing yeasts.

## Research extension 1.3: Dextrin-using brewing strains

A review of **diastatic *S. cerevisiae*** discusses the *STA1* glucoamylase gene and its relationship to continued fermentation of larger carbohydrates. A deliberately dry beer and unexpected package refermentation need different judgments. The [diastatic yeast page](../microbiology/diastatic-saccharomyces.md) distinguishes gene, measured behavior and process.[^v13-saison-sta1]
