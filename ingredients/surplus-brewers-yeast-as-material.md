# Brewer’s surplus yeast: culture versus by-product

Yeast is both an ingredient-like biological input and, after brewing, a potential residual material. That double role can be confusing. The yeast a brewer selects and manages for fermentation is not identical in composition, cell condition or intended use to the mixed solids removed from a completed process.[^v114-waste]

A 2025 chemical study examined selected hot-trub and residual-yeast samples from top- and bottom-fermented beer production. It measured elemental and organic composition and screened for a defined set of mycotoxins as a preliminary step toward agricultural reuse. The specific negative screening outcomes are **not** a certificate that all brewery residues are safe to spread on fields or use as food ingredients.[^v114-waste]

A 2026 review considers spent grain and surplus yeast across several possible valorization routes and their technology readiness. Such material can have value, but extraction, drying, collection, contamination, energy demand and economics determine whether a proposed reuse actually works. A review of technologies is not a result from a single working brewery.[^v114-circular]

The distinction keeps a living starter, a harvested fermentation residue and a processed commercial ingredient from being treated as interchangeable.[^v114-circular]
