# Coriander seed in spiced beer

BJCP's Witbier reference names **coriander seed** among characteristic ingredients and describes a lemony, perfumed contribution integrated with the beer rather than dominating it. “Coriander” in that context should not silently become an unspecified addition of fresh leaves.[^atlas-witbier]

## Identify the material before describing the flavor

A useful record names the plant part, preparation, stage of addition and source for the claim. “Spiced beer” is too broad to supply that information. The guideline's balance description is a sensory target, not a fixed spice weight or a complete recipe.[^atlas-witbier]

[Witbier](../styles/witbier.md) and [citrus peel](citrus-peel.md) provide the immediate context. Compare [Weissbier](../styles/weissbier.md), whose familiar clove-like impression is described as fermentation-derived rather than requiring actual cloves. An aroma word does not establish the presence of a similarly named spice.[^atlas-weissbier-context]

## Better questions for a tasting note

Was coriander actually declared by the brewer, or did the taster perceive a spice-like aroma? Was the sample assessed alone or with knowledge of the label? Which sensory description is supported, and which is merely an ingredient guess? These questions make the observation useful without pretending that tasting is an ingredient assay.

The [flavor-detective guide](../sensory/aroma-is-not-an-ingredient.md) develops this distinction across several beer families.

## Evidence note

This page does not identify a unique historical Witbier formulation or claim that every product in the style contains the same spice material. A cultivar, origin or chemical explanation would require additional direct evidence. No medicinal effect or safe dose is inferred from culinary use.
