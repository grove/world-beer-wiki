# Brewing bacteria

Bacteria can be intended participants in beer production, with roles that differ from those of alcoholic yeasts. A useful account identifies which organisms are present and what they do in the particular process. “Sour bacteria” is too broad to stand in for that evidence.[^ing-lambic-succession]

## Different groups, different contributions

In a lambic fermentation study, De Roos and colleagues linked *Pediococcus damnosus* to acidification and malolactic fermentation, and *Acetobacter pasteurianus* to acetic acid and acetoin production. The sequence changed as the wort and beer changed. This is evidence for a studied microbial succession, not a fixed recipe of organisms required in all sour beer.[^ing-lambic-succession]

Commercially selected cultures provide another approach. Lallemand identifies WildBrew Sour Pitch as *Lactiplantibacillus plantarum* and describes its use in souring, including kettle souring. Its published working range and hop tolerance belong to that selected product; they should not be generalized to every member of the species or to all lactic acid bacteria.[^ing-sour-pitch]

## Method is more informative than a sourness label

| Question | Distinction it preserves |
| --- | --- |
| When did acidification occur? | Before alcoholic fermentation, during it, or across extended maturation. |
| How were organisms introduced? | Deliberate culture addition, a maintained mixed culture, or environmental inoculation. |
| What else participated? | Alcoholic yeasts and other microbes can affect the outcome. |
| What was actually measured? | Organism detection, acid concentration and sensory sourness are different evidence. |

These questions help compare [Straight Sour Beer](../styles/straight-sour-beer.md), [Mixed-fermentation Sour Beer](../styles/mixed-fermentation-sour-beer.md), [Lambic](../styles/lambic.md), [Gose](../styles/gose.md) and [Berliner Weisse](../styles/berliner-weisse.md) without equating their processes.

## Acid does not prove bacteria were responsible

Lallemand's souring-culture comparison includes both bacteria and acid-producing yeasts. A *Lachancea* fermentation can produce lactic acid and alcohol, so acidity alone cannot establish bacterial souring or spontaneous fermentation. Conversely, identifying a bacterial species in a sample does not by itself quantify its sensory contribution.[^ing-sour-solutions]

## Evidence note

Traditional microbial communities may be described using older organism names in the underlying literature. Preserve the name and publication context instead of silently converting a laboratory detection into a mandatory ingredient. This page is a process reference, not instructions for isolating unknown microbes or evidence of probiotic benefit.
