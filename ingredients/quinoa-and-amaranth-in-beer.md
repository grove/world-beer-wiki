# Quinoa and amaranth in experimental beer

Quinoa and amaranth are often listed beside sorghum and millet as alternative starch sources for beer. One 2022 Food Chemistry study examined these materials and others as **40% unmalted substitutions** in a barley-malt brewing system. That experiment can test differences in extraction and process behavior, but a beer containing 60% barley malt is not thereby gluten-free.[^v110-gluten-study]

## The comparison measured process choices

The study compared gelatinized and ungelatinized forms and analyzed resulting beer characteristics. Overall extract and alcohol differed from the barley reference for most treatments, with a stated exception involving the tested quinoa beer. Those observations belong to the specific ingredient proportions, preparation methods and analytical setting rather than universal performance rankings of all quinoa or amaranth varieties.[^v110-gluten-study]

## Why the word alternative can mislead

A review of gluten-free brewing includes pseudocereals among many candidate materials and examines enzyme availability, flavor, body and filtration. Alternative raw material, experimental adjunct and validated gluten-free product are separate classifications. A reader should ask which grains entered the actual mash, which enzymes were needed and how finished-beer gluten status was verified, without assuming that this publication answers all three questions.[^v110-gluten-review]

## Evidence and limitations

This page describes tested material substitutions, not dietary safety, product eligibility or agronomic universality; the original experiment was accessible through a PubMed abstract. These limitations should be retained when quoting the article or extracting relationships.
