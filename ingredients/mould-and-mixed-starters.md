# Mould and mixed starters

Some brewing systems obtain starch-degrading enzymes from mould-grown material or mixed starters rather than relying entirely on malt. The ingredient record should distinguish the substrate carrying a culture, the organisms present and the enzymes or fermentation they supply. “Yeast cake” can obscure that complexity.[^ing-jssa-process][^ing-nuruk-communities]

## Koji and nuruk are distinct examples

For sake, the Japan Sake and Shochu Makers Association describes koji as steamed rice colonized by *Aspergillus oryzae*. The resulting enzymes participate in starch conversion while yeast produces alcohol in the mash. Koji, the mould and the alcoholic yeast are therefore connected but separate concepts.[^ing-jssa-process]

Nuruk is a Korean fermentation starter with a microbial community. A 2022 study followed changes in fungi, bacteria and enzyme activity as nuruk matured. A separate investigation of traditional and commercial wheat-based nuruk also found variation in bacterial composition. Neither supports assigning one fixed species list to all nuruk.[^ing-nuruk-communities][^ing-nuruk-bacteria]

## A starter comparison is not a cultural classification

An experimental rice-wine study compared selected *Monascus* koji and cultured rice-nuruk preparations. Fermentation yields, acids and volatile compounds differed under its conditions. The materials were deliberately prepared for that trial; its “nuruk” treatment cannot be assumed to represent every spontaneously developed traditional nuruk.[^ing-rice-starter-comparison]

This is why the same English word “starter” is not enough to equate products. Record at least the local name, grain or other carrier, whether organisms were selected or maintained as a community, and the process in which the starter is used.

## A useful comparison beyond Asia

Henkel's [Parakari](../styles/parakari.md) study documents a starch-degrading *Rhizopus* stage in a Guyanese cassava fermentation. It provides a cross-regional process comparison, without establishing that parakari derives from Asian starter traditions.[^parakari-study]

Explore [Sake](../styles/sake.md), [Makgeolli](../styles/makgeolli.md), [Haria](../styles/haria.md), [Kodo ko jaanr](../styles/kodo-ko-jaanr.md), [Bhaati jaanr](../styles/bhaati-jaanr.md), [Sato](../styles/sato.md) and [Tuak](../styles/tuak-rice.md) as separate named traditions. Their local starter vocabularies and practices should be retained where documented, not standardized into “koji” for convenience.

## Evidence note

The sources cover defined producers and studies. They do not justify assigning organisms by appearance, identifying every rice ferment as beer, or treating all mould-grown material as a suitable brewing ingredient. The comparison concerns biological roles, not interchangeability.

## Research extension 1.5: Measured microbial communities, not single starter names

A 2008 study detected fungi and bacteria in 52 Vietnamese bánh men starter samples; a 2019 paper documents bacterial diversity in Eastern Himalayan dry starters. Their sampling designs illuminate mixed functional roles but also expose substantial within-type variation, especially among bacterial detections.[^v15-banhmen-2008][^v15-himalaya-2019]

Compare the [Vietnamese starter study](../microbiology/vietnamese-banh-men-communities.md), [Himalayan samples](../microbiology/himalayan-dry-starter-diversity.md), and [tasks performed by starter cakes](../processes/starter-cakes-and-starch-conversion.md).


## Edition 1.7: newly sourced connections

Philippine **bubod** and Japanese cultivated **koji** offer a useful comparison of two ways to support sugar formation from rice starch. A 1990 isolate study of bubod describes several organism groups and distinct activities, while UNESCO’s 2024 Japanese sake-making inscription emphasizes trained craft teams managing cultivated koji. A shared *function* does not establish identical communities or starters.[^v17-bubod-1990][^v17-unesco-sake]

Read [bubod](bubod-starter.md), [sake heritage](../culture/japanese-koji-sake-heritage-and-apprenticeship.md) and [parallel conversion](../processes/parallel-starch-and-alcohol-fermentation.md). Isolate counts are not a recipe, and a heritage description is not a microbiome census.


## Edition 1.9: newly sourced connections

An Eastern Himalayan 2019 isolate study examined 35 samples representing several differently named dry starters, including Bhutanese *phab* and regional *marcha*, *paa*, *pee* and *phut*. Its 201 identified bacteria are a **subset of recoverable bacteria** from the studied materials, not a complete inventory of yeasts, molds or enzymatic functions. The reported ingredients and community names vary; there is no single interchangeable Himalayan starter. Compare the specific [phab case](../microbiology/phab-dry-starter-research.md) and [nuruk survey](../microbiology/nuruk-42-sample-survey.md) rather than assuming any one culture satisfies every grain-ferment method.[^v19-phab]

## Edition 1.10: newly sourced connections

Indonesian ragi tapai illustrates the distinction between a maintained starter and an individual cultured organism. A Java genetic survey describes fungal diversity in sampled ragi materials, while a much earlier 1972 experiment deliberately reconstituted a two-organism functional system from selected isolates.[^v110-ragi-genetics][^v110-tape-1972]

These studies should not be conflated: laboratory sufficiency does not imply historical completeness, and a detected taxon does not automatically explain all local variants. Follow the [ragi ingredient](ragi-tapai.md) and the [1972 experimental history](../history/tape-fermentation-laboratory-model-1972.md) for those distinct research questions.



## Edition 1.12: newly sourced connections

The Japanese review of [Amazake](../styles/amazake.md) explains a material boundary: **koji saccharification can sweeten starch without being the same operation as an alcoholic fermentation**. The sake-lees variant follows a different route and may retain alcohol.[^v112-amazake-2021] Compare [Koji amazake as a process](../processes/koji-amazake-saccharification.md). A mold starter's role cannot be inferred from the name of its final drink alone.

## Edition 1.6: sourced research connections

A 2024 southern Assam sampling study identifies distinct named **rice-beverage starters** among Hmar, Karbi and Tea tribes cohorts. This evidence reinforces that “starter” may be a culturally situated, multi-organism preparation, not a synonym for pure commercial yeast. Its bacterial amplicon data do not by themselves identify the enzymatic contributor in each finished beverage.[^v16-assam]
