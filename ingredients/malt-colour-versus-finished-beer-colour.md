# Malt colour does not transfer directly into finished beer colour

## An ingredient label is not a finished-beer measurement

Malt colour is a useful clue to processing and potential visual contribution, but a list of malts and their quoted colour ratings is not itself a measured finished beer. The BJCP's public malt guide describes kilning and malt selection as important influences alongside brewing operations. The proportions used, wort extraction and later handling are part of the path between grain and glass.[^v116-malt]

## The result depends on a method

The BJCP colour guide describes beer colour in SRM units based on absorbance at a particular wavelength and standardised light path. That is an analytical result for the beer sample; it cannot be read off a single sack of malt without a brewing model and observations.[^v116-color] A separate open laboratory guide illustrates the importance of a defined sample stage, dilution and preparation when measuring wort and beer colour.[^v116-lab]

A dark ingredient may contribute flavour even at a fraction too small to dominate colour, while a visually cloudy beer may not be properly represented by the same optical method as a clarified sample. Beer colour and roasted flavour should therefore be described as different dimensions. Neither a numeric malt specification nor the name of a grain is enough to reconstruct a culture's traditional appearance.

When comparing recipes or styles, retain the malt's supplier or processing description, the amount used, the beer's measured condition, and whether the colour is **predicted** or **actually assayed**.
