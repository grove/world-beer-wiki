# Pineapple rind and piloncillo in tepache

Studies of Mexican tepache describe pineapple peel or rind with sugar as a fermentation substrate. In a 2025 single-batch protocol, the researchers combined pineapple rind and core, piloncillo, water and spices. The measured microbiology belongs to that one preparation, not to an identity-defining national ingredient formula.[^v111-tepache-one]

## What the ingredients contribute

Pineapple residues supply fermentable material, acidity and compounds that influence taste; added unrefined cane sugar supplies more available carbohydrate. Microbes act on those materials to form additional acids and volatiles. A 2022 chemical and community study examined this ecosystem, showing why fruit, sugar, and environmental organisms should be considered together when interpreting the finished drink.[^v111-tepache-ecology]

## Ingredient is not safety or provenance

Pineapple's presence does not establish that a beverage is nonalcoholic, nutritionally beneficial or microbiologically safe. A spice in a study batch does not establish its use in every region. Compare aguamiel and cereal grists to see how different starting materials lead to overlapping biochemical questions without a shared historical recipe. Foraging and home safety instructions are outside this reference's scope.[^v111-tepache-one]
