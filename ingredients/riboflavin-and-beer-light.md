# Riboflavin and the photochemistry of beer

Riboflavin (vitamin B2) is not a typical recipe ingredient a brewer adds for aroma, but it matters to the behavior of beer exposed to light. A 2006 lager and model-beer experiment tracked loss of absorbance near 445 nm under illumination and related it to light-struck character. This is a measured chemical association, not evidence that riboflavin alone explains every beer defect.[^v110-photo-2006]

## Light, hop bitter compounds and a reaction network

The experiment tested how selected additives altered the measured process; catechins, tryptophol and ascorbic acid could reduce some observed changes without fully stopping them. In systems containing isohumulone, responses differed with its concentration. A separate 2001 paper studied radical chemistry during photodegradation of iso-alpha acids. These works investigate related steps with different methods.[^v110-photo-2006][^v110-photo-2001]

## From chemistry to packaging decisions

Two beers can have similar initial bitterness yet different light histories, dissolved components and packages. Mechanistic knowledge explains why protecting beer from relevant light is sensible, but it does not provide a universal exposure time, bottle-color score or guarantee for a specific commercial recipe. Packaging and flavor development require product-level evidence.[^v110-photo-2006]

## Evidence and limitations

Light-struck chemistry is not a health effect of riboflavin. The abstracts do not provide an all-products threshold or establish a complete causal pathway in every beer. These limitations should be retained when quoting the article or extracting relationships.
