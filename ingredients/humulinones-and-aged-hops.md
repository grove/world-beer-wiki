# Humulinones: aged-hop chemistry beyond bitterness

Hop chemistry does not stop at the fresh cone or at the familiar alpha-acid-to-iso-alpha-acid route. A Food Chemistry study examined **humulinone**, a compound associated with hop oxidation, in combination with the beer-foam protein known as protein Z. Its experimental question was not simply whether aged hops taste stale, but how one hop-derived molecule might alter a protein system relevant to foam.[^v114-humulinone]

The researchers used spectroscopic, structural and surface-tension methods to study the interactions. They reported improved foam behavior in the tested protein-Z system after adding humulinone. That finding is informative about a possible molecular interaction but does not prove that oxidizing hops deliberately will improve every finished beer or its flavor. Bitterness, astringency, aroma and head retention are separate endpoints.[^v114-humulinone]

The abstract also gives a binding characterization under laboratory conditions. Such numbers depend on concentrations, solution conditions and assumptions in the model; they should not become a universal prescription for a hopping schedule. Real beer contains competing proteins, lipids, ethanol, carbonation and many other substances.[^v114-humulinone]

A compound can be worth studying because it affects foam without making it a universal positive flavor ingredient.[^v114-humulinone]
