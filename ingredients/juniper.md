# Juniper branches, infusions and filtration

“Contains juniper” is only the beginning of a useful ingredient description. In the Holo field account, Per Dale used thin branches in an infusion and placed branches beneath the mash tun's false bottom as part of a filter. Some berries could accompany the material, but a measured berry addition was not the defining operation.[^atlas-holo]

## One plant name, several records

| Record | Question to preserve |
|---|---|
| Identity | Which plant did the source actually identify, and how? |
| Part | Branch tips, woody material, foliage or berries? |
| Preparation | Infusion, direct addition or filtration bed? |
| Function | Flavor, physical filtering, or several roles together? |
| Setting | Which maker, place and period? |

These fields turn a vague ingredient tag into something that can be compared. They also prevent the assumption that a branch filter is equivalent to adding berries to a kettle. The broader [herbs and spices reference](herbs-and-spices.md) uses this distinction between identity and use.[^atlas-juniper-context]

## The comparison should keep the differences

Read [Sahti](../styles/sahti.md) and [Gotlandsdricke](../styles/gotlandsdricke.md) alongside their own sources. The point of linking them is to compare documented uses, not to infer one shared juniper dose, botanical identification standard or flavor specification.

A useful future botanical extension would connect a securely identified species to the particular plant part and preparation. Until that evidence is available, this page does not silently convert the field account's common name into a universal foraging instruction.

## Evidence note

The Holo account concerns one brewer's method. It does not establish that all juniper species or plant parts are interchangeable, edible or appropriate for brewing. Familiarity from another beverage is not a substitute for identification and appropriate ingredient controls.
