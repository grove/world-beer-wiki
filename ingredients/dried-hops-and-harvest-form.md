# Dried hops: crop form, moisture and brewing identity

“Hops” is an ingredient name, but a brewer must still ask what *form* the crop is in. Historic England describes wet, freshly picked cones being transferred quickly to kilns, where drying made them suitable for storage and transport. It was a response to spoilage risk and the economics of getting a season's crop into a brewing supply chain.[^v114-oasts]

In the historical method described, green hops were spread on a kiln floor, cooled and pressed into large pockets. Those steps could change moisture and handling properties. The account is a study of English agricultural buildings and practices, not a controlled measurement of individual hop aroma compounds before and after drying.[^v114-oasts]

A modern tasting descriptor such as citrus or pine does not by itself tell us whether hops were used green, dried, stored cold, or processed into a modern concentrate. Cultivar, harvest year, storage, brewing technique and yeast interactions all matter. The building evidence provides one part of the ingredient's history, not a finished beer's complete sensory fingerprint.[^v114-oasts]
