# Kveik

Kveik refers here to traditional Norwegian farmhouse brewing yeasts and cultures, rather than to a single beer style. Preiss and colleagues investigated cultures from nine Norwegian sources and characterized a genetically distinctive group of domesticated *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*. The connection is to maintained brewing cultures, not to “wild yeast” as a general marketing expression.[^ing-kveik-genetics]

## Culture and commercial isolate are different records

The 2018 work examined isolates, genetics and fermentation performance. The traits often associated with kveik included efficient fermentation, flocculation and tolerance of warmer conditions. These findings concern the studied material; they do not turn every culture into the same organism with one predictable sensory profile.[^ing-kveik-genetics]

Lallemand describes its commercial Voss strain as obtained from Sigmund Gjernes in Voss, Norway. That is useful provenance for a particular product. A bag of a selected commercial strain should still be distinguished from a brewer's maintained culture and from the complete method of brewing that brewer's beer.[^ing-voss-product]

## Temperature tolerance varies

Foster and colleagues tested six kveik strains across a range of temperatures and found differences in fermentation performance and stress tolerance. Their work also examined trehalose accumulation as a possible contributor to tolerance. The practical lesson for a reference wiki is limited but important: “kveik” does not justify a universal fermentation temperature, time or guarantee of clean flavour.[^ing-kveik-temperature]

The contrast between shared ancestry and different performance also prevents a naming mistake. A high-temperature-tolerant yeast need not be kveik, while a beer made with a kveik isolate need not reproduce a Norwegian farmhouse tradition.

## Read the beer process as well as the yeast

[Vossaøl](../styles/vossaol.md), [Kornøl](../styles/kornol.md) and [Stjørdalsøl](../styles/stjordalsol.md) have separate profiles because regional brewing is more than a culture name. Consult those pages for documented malt, heating, smoke, juniper and fermentation practices. Do not use the links to infer that every maker in those regions uses the same yeast.

For comparison, [Brewing yeast](brewing-yeast.md) explains the broader species/strain distinction and [Herbs and spices](herbs-and-spices.md) separates juniper use from yeast identity.

## Evidence note

The studies give strong evidence about their tested cultures and strains, while the product account documents a named commercial isolate. None supplies a census of all surviving farmhouse cultures, establishes that every kveik is bacteria-free, or defines one mandatory “kveik beer” recipe.

## Research extension 1.3: New comparative farmhouse population research

A 2026 genomic study described **1,760 isolates from 44 traditional cultures** across Norway, Latvia, Lithuania and Russia. Its findings include a range from near-monocultures to multi-variant *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* populations. These samples should not be mistaken for all kveik cultures or used to equate commercial isolates with household lineages. See [farmhouse diversity](../microbiology/farmhouse-yeast-diversity.md) and [a sourced story](../stories/a-starter-is-more-than-a-strain.md).[^v13-farmhouse26]

## Edition 1.6: sourced research connections

Two studies published in 2026 illuminate different scales. One sequenced **64 isolates from 25 western Norwegian cultures** sampled with intergenerational heritage criteria; another examined **1,760 isolates from 44 farmhouse cultures** in Norway, Latvia, Lithuania and Russia. They report geographic genetic structure and different degrees of culture diversity. Neither sample inventory is a census of all Norwegian brewers or a timestamp for the invention of vossaøl.[^v16-kveik-2026][^v16-farmhouse-2026]

Follow [the population-history comparison](../microbiology/kveik-population-history-2026.md) rather than equating one genome with a family-maintained culture.
