# Wheat

Wheat can be a principal brewing grain or one component of a larger grist. Malted wheat supplies extract and can supply active enzymes; its role is not limited to making beer cloudy. Weyermann describes its pale wheat malt as enzymatically active and suitable for several beer families, illustrating why “contains wheat” is broader than any one style.[^ing-weyermann-wheat]

## Form matters

**Malted wheat** has undergone germination and drying. **Unmalted wheat** has not. A heat-processed flake is another distinct preparation, and should not silently be called malt. Record the form when the source supplies it: changing the processing changes the brewing question even when the botanical ingredient stays the same.[^ing-briess-malting]

Crisp's wheat-malt documentation identifies effects on foam and mouthfeel and warns that the grain is huskless. Thus a wheat-heavy mash has practical separation considerations; “more wheat” is not just a flavour adjustment. Supplier recommendations are tied to the malt and brewing system, not legal or universal style limits.[^ing-crisp-wheat]

## Wheat is not the source of every wheat-beer aroma

BJCP's Weissbier description separates bready or doughy wheat character from the banana-and-clove fermentation profile. Its ingredient account calls for malted wheat and a characteristic yeast. Inferring banana fruit or clove spice additions from those tasting words would misread the style.[^bjcp-2021-10a]

[Witbier](../styles/witbier.md) creates a different comparison: its modern guideline describes spice additions integrated with yeast character. That is a different evidential statement from the yeast-derived clove in Weissbier. [American Wheat Beer](../styles/american-wheat-beer.md) offers another useful comparison for a relatively clean fermentation expression.[^bjcp-2021-24a]

Wheat can also carry smoke character. The supplier account of oak-smoked wheat malt supports separating grain choice from smoking treatment; a pale wheat beer can be smoky without acquiring the same grain bill or identity as a dark smoked barley beer.[^ing-weyermann-smoked-wheat]

## Read across traditions

Compare [Weissbier](../styles/weissbier.md), [Witbier](../styles/witbier.md), [Grodziskie](../styles/grodziskie.md), [Lambic](../styles/lambic.md) and [Wheatwine](../styles/wheatwine.md). The common grain provides a useful retrieval link, while fermentation, strength, hopping and cultural history distinguish the entries.

## Evidence note

Commercial malt descriptions explain product capabilities. Guidelines describe contemporary reference beers. Neither source type establishes that all wheat beers are hazy, sweet, top-fermented or made from the same proportion and form of wheat.
