# DON-3-glucoside: a modified cereal mycotoxin, not a harmless synonym

## Why one parent-compound label can miss a transformation

Deoxynivalenol, or DON, is discussed in grain-contaminant research together with modified forms. One such form is DON-3-glucoside, often abbreviated DON-3G. A 2026 experimentally inoculated barley-to-beer study measured both forms and reported changes through malting, mashing, fermentation and maturation. Its result is a specific mass-balance and transformation investigation, not a claim that brewing removes every contaminant or that all beers have these concentrations.[^v117-fusarium-trial]

A systematic 2026 food-risk review examines why modified mycotoxins deserve distinct evidence handling. Their analytical detection, bioavailability and potential reconversion are different questions. The general review's methods and uncertainties cannot be converted directly into a product-specific safe level or nutritional risk score.[^v117-modified]

## What a detection establishes

A result for a parent compound is not necessarily a complete profile of the modified forms; analytical platforms and reference standards differ. Likewise, the biochemical change of one molecule does not prove that total hazard is gone. The original experimental paper used targeted assays under controlled conditions, and its reference samples differ fundamentally from consumer products.

Store the exact molecular form, sample matrix, concentration units, quantification limits, analytical technique and date. Avoid translating a high or low laboratory value into a health claim without a recognized, applicable exposure assessment.
