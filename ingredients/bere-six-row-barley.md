# Bere barley: island landrace, ingredient and changing use

## One grain name, several uses

Bere is an old six-row barley landrace that remains cultivated in some northern Scottish islands. Martin and colleagues' review explains how it persisted despite the arrival of higher-yielding modern barleys. Its uses include flour and animal feed as well as historical malting and distilling, which should not be conflated with one compulsory beer style or a uniform recipe.[^v117-bere-review]

A University of the Highlands and Islands research overview describes work on Bere traits, sustainable cultivation and local commercial supply chains. This is an institutional research perspective, not a substitute for present-day farmer or miller interviews. Its indexed material identifies nutrient-stressed soils and adaptation as research directions, while direct page access was unreliable when checked.[^v117-bere-institute]

## A heritage claim has limits

The fact that barley was cultivated in Neolithic Orkney does not establish that the precise present-day Bere landrace was already there. The reviewed paper explicitly treats its original introduction as uncertain. Nor should the term six-row be treated as a flavor recipe or a proof of identical plant genetics across all fields.

For an ingredient entry, record the regional scope, grain provenance, intended food or beverage use, observed trial conditions and actual source of any malting performance data. A conservation argument about crop diversity and a sensory claim about finished beer need separate evidence.
