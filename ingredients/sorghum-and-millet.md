# Sorghum and millet

Sorghum and millets are important brewing substrates that deserve examination on their own terms. “Millet” is not a precise specification of one grain variety, and a sorghum-based beer is not necessarily an attempt to imitate barley beer. For an ingredient record, preserve the grain identity, preparation and fermentation system supplied by the source.[^ing-sorghum-millet-malting][^ing-millet-trial]

## Malting and fermentability

Chiba and colleagues studied sorghum and millet under several malting temperatures. Both produced sugars and amino acids available for yeast after malting and mashing, with similar patterns under the tested conditions. This demonstrates brewing functionality without establishing that their malts behave identically to barley or that one mash schedule fits every grain.[^ing-sorghum-millet-malting]

A 2023 sorghum experiment compared malted and unmalted sorghum formulations with barley malt. Its sorghum treatments included commercial enzyme additions. That detail is essential when interpreting the result: performance of an enzyme-assisted laboratory mash cannot simply be attributed to the untreated grain, nor projected onto a traditional community process.[^ing-sorghum-trial]

## Flavour findings have boundaries

A 2026 millet experiment compared fermentation and volatile compounds under specified brewing conditions. The authors explicitly called for mash optimization and further work connecting measured compounds to perceived flavour. A list of detected volatiles is therefore not, by itself, a tasting description or a universal “millet flavour”.[^ing-millet-trial]

## Read by tradition as well as by grain

The wiki provides separate routes into [Dolo](../styles/dolo.md), [Pito](../styles/pito.md), [Tchapalo](../styles/tchapalo.md), [Tchoukoutou](../styles/tchoukoutou.md), [Ikigage](../styles/ikigage.md), [Mbege](../styles/mbege.md), [Oshikundu](../styles/oshikundu.md) and [Kodo ko jaanr](../styles/kodo-ko-jaanr.md). Consult each profile for which grain is documented, what other substrates are present and whose practice was studied. The links organize related evidence; they do not identify these names as synonyms.

When comparing an opaque regional beer with a filtered commercial beer, separate at least three questions: what supplied starch, how it was made available, and which organisms fermented it. A grain-only label cannot answer all three.

## Evidence note

The experiments cited here address brewing behaviour, with some work motivated by alternative-grain product development. They do not establish the dietary suitability of a particular finished beer, a universal alcohol range, or the history of every sorghum and millet tradition.

## Edition 1.8: newly sourced connections

Two studies identify **finger millet** (*Eleusine coracana*) in technologically distinct ferments. In a Tanzanian mbege field account, germinated grain formed the sweet mso preparation later mixed with fermented banana. In a Darjeeling/Sikkim kodo ko jaanr study, moulds, yeasts and lactic-acid bacteria changed over the observed finger-millet fermentation. Shared cereal identity does not establish matching starters or historical transmission.[^v18-mbege][^v18-jaanr]

Follow [finger millet as an ingredient](finger-millet-in-traditional-ferments.md), [Mbege](../styles/mbege.md), and [Jaanr research](../microbiology/jaanr-microbial-succession.md).

## Edition 1.11: newly sourced connections

Nigerian [kunun-zaki](../styles/kunun-zaki.md) supplies a useful counterexample to assuming that millet must become a fully alcoholic beer. A 2015 study describes wet milling, partial gelatinization, mild fermentation and a refreshment context; a 1995 study monitored changing bacteria and yeasts in its particular millet process. Neither should be merged into the defined identity of [Burukutu](../styles/burukutu.md).[^v111-kunun-study][^v111-kunun-cultures]

The new [process comparison](../processes/kunun-zaki-slurry-and-gelatinization.md) separates ingredients, preparation and observed microbial change. Grain names do not uniquely identify starch conversion, serving context, exact alcohol content or safety.
