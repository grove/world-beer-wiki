# Iso-alpha acids: bitterness and light reactions

Iso-alpha acids contribute much of the characteristic bitterness of hopped beer, yet their chemistry changes with conditions after brewing. The 2001 time-resolved electron-paramagnetic-resonance study examined how light exposure decomposes isohumulone-like compounds through radical pathways. The experiment used model solvents and a selected wavelength; its results help explain a mechanism, not the precise flavor of every beer sold in clear glass.[^v110-photo-2001]

## Two scales of observation

A mechanism study can identify photochemical intermediates and reaction pathways while a practical model-beer study observes changes in a beer-like matrix. In 2006 researchers connected light-exposed lager, riboflavin absorbance, isohumulone and light-struck character. Using these studies together is useful as long as the model reaction and the finished-beer sensory endpoint remain separate.[^v110-photo-2006]

## Hop aroma is a different question

The words 'hoppy' and 'skunky' sometimes appear together in casual descriptions, but light-struck off-aroma is not simply an unusually high dose of aromatic hop oils. The chemical route can depend on light exposure and bitter-compound chemistry. Compare the hop-alpha-acids overview with the dedicated sensory page before attributing an aroma to a hop cultivar or dry-hop practice.[^v110-photo-2001]

## Evidence and limitations

The articles give mechanistic or narrowly tested exposure results, not generalized recommendations about consumption or guaranteed protective coatings. These limitations should be retained when quoting the article or extracting relationships.
