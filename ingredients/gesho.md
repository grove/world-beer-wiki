# Gesho in tella

Gesho is identified in the existing [Tella profile](../styles/tella.md) as **Rhamnus prinoides**. It belongs to a particular Ethiopian brewing context and is not the hop plant, **Humulus lupulus**, described in the [Hops reference](hops.md). Similarity in a brewing role does not make the plants botanical synonyms.[^atlas-tella-context][^atlas-hops-context]

## Why “hop substitute” is an incomplete description

The tella research profile describes a staged process involving prepared grain, malt and gesho. Reducing that account to ordinary barley wort with a different bittering ingredient leaves out much of the process being documented.[^atlas-tella-context]

For an ingredient record, keep the plant identity, material used, preparation, stage of addition and stated purpose separate. The same fields also make it possible to identify where evidence is missing rather than fill gaps with assumptions borrowed from European hopping practice.

## An experiment is not a new definition of tradition

The registered 2021 study tested replacement of gesho with **Moringa stenopetala**. That experimental intervention must remain distinct from the baseline tella method. Studying a substitute does not establish that households traditionally used it, or that every maker follows the experimental formulation.[^atlas-tella-context]

The broader [herbs and spices reference](herbs-and-spices.md) provides a route into this distinction between an ingredient's identity and the role attributed to it. A future extension could add locally documented names, plant-part selection and maker testimony, with each claim tied to its actual setting.
