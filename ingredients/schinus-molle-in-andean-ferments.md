# Schinus molle: pepper-tree drupes in Andean ferment history

Archaeobotanical and experimental evidence for molle-associated Andean drinks, especially Cerro Baúl; not a plant-foraging or safety guide.

## Not black pepper

*Schinus molle*, commonly called the Peruvian pepper tree, bears drupes associated with Andean fermented beverages. The English common name does not make it the same species as black pepper (*Piper nigrum*). Distinguishing the botanical identity matters when comparing documented brewing materials.[^v15-wari-2005]

## What appears at Cerro Baúl

Excavators report abundant molle seeds in combustion-related deposits in a large Wari brewery. Their reconstruction describes using the fruit’s sugars for a beverage, but leaves unresolved whether a molle brew and malted-maize brew were separate or blended. Excavated botanical remains offer an ingredient clue rather than a sensory recipe.[^v15-wari-2005]

## Why the experiment matters

Nash and colleagues compared brewing with maize, molle and both in informant-guided experiments. Such experiments help interpret residues and dregs, but present-day controlled reconstruction is an analytical bridge—not automatic evidence that historic Wari production followed every modern step.[^v15-wari-2024]
