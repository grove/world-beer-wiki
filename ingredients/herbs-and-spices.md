# Herbs, spices and juniper

Botanical additions can provide flavour, affect a brewing process or hold a specific role in local practice. “Herbal beer” is therefore an ingredient description that needs further detail. Identify the plant, part used and purpose when the evidence provides them; do not assume that every botanical replaces hops in the same way.[^ing-juniper-fieldwork][^tella-research]

## Juniper: more than a berry flavour

Garshol's account of brewing at Holo Gardstun describes an infusion of slender juniper branches and the use of branches beneath the mash-tun false bottom. In that account, juniper had both liquid-preparation and filtration roles. It does not follow that a beer containing juniper berries recreates the same process, or that all Nordic farmhouse brewers use identical material.[^ing-juniper-fieldwork]

Explore [Vossaøl](../styles/vossaol.md), [Kornøl](../styles/kornol.md), [Sahti](../styles/sahti.md) and [Gotlandsdricke](../styles/gotlandsdricke.md) through their own evidence. The botanical connection is a navigation route rather than proof of one shared recipe.

## Gesho: preserve the local ingredient

An Ethiopian tella experiment prepared ingredients with experienced local women in Motta and used leaves and thin branches of gesho, *Rhamnus prinoides*. The study then tested substitutions and measured fermentation differences. That experimental design is useful evidence that changing a botanical can alter the process; it is not grounds for renaming gesho as a generic hop substitute or treating the modified formulation as the unchanged tradition.[^tella-research]

Read [Tella](../styles/tella.md) for its separate grain and fermentation context. Keep the local name and identified plant in the ingredient record.

## Added spice and spice-like flavour

The modern [Witbier](../styles/witbier.md) guideline describes coriander and citrus-related spicing alongside yeast expression. By contrast, the [Weissbier](../styles/weissbier.md) guideline identifies clove character with fermentation. A tasting word such as “spicy” does not establish an actual spice addition.[^bjcp-2021-24a][^bjcp-2021-10a]

For a further boundary, [Gruit](../styles/gruit.md) should be read as a historical and contemporary naming problem. Finding a herb in a beer does not automatically establish that the beer reproduces a documented medieval gruit mixture.

## Evidence note

The field account concerns one brewer, the tella paper concerns defined formulations, and the guidelines describe contemporary reference styles. None supports a universal botanical blend, a claim of medicinal benefit, or the safe interchangeability of similarly named plants.
