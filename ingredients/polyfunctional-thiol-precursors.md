# Bound thiol precursors in malt and hops

Some potent tropical- or blackcurrant-associated beer aromas begin as compounds that do not arrive in the fermenter as freely volatile odorants. A 2022 analytical study identified **disulfide-bound thiol** forms in malt and hops. Researchers used a controlled chemical reduction to reveal previously bound 3MH and related compounds and then examined changes during fermentation.[^v18-thiol-bound]

## A precursor is not the same as a finished-beer aroma

The source records measured chemical potential under experimental conditions. Whether a particular brewery creates an aroma-active finished concentration depends on yeast, available precursors, time, and losses after fermentation. It is unsound to declare that a named cultivar “contains” one finished beer flavor at a guaranteed strength.

## Why hop variety is only one variable

A 2024 hop survey measured free thiols in **250 samples representing 97 varieties** across multiple origins and crop years. It reported variation related not only to cultivar, but also to harvest and origin. The chemistry of a bag of hops is not a timeless species trait.[^v18-hop-thiol-survey]
