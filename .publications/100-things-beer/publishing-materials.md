# Publishing materials

## Title

100 Things Beer Teaches Us About History, Science & Culture

## Suggested subtitle

A world of discovery through fermentation

## Back-cover description

What can a beer glass tell us about civilization? Start looking, and an everyday drink opens doors to ancient poetry, women's working lives, invisible organisms, global trade, and the physics of a rising bubble.

In one hundred lively lessons, this book follows fermentation through history, science, and culture. Discover how archaeologists investigate a vanished drink, why a tasting experiment can answer the wrong question, how a brewery laboratory helped change chemistry, and why preserving a tradition involves much more than keeping a recipe.

Drawing on World Beer Wiki and its underlying research, these short journeys connect particular people, places, and experiments to larger discoveries about the world. They respect uncertainty, make room for diverse traditions, and invite you to follow the evidence further.

For beer enthusiasts, history lovers, science readers, and anyone who enjoys a good question, this is a book about human curiosity and ingenuity. No brewing knowledge required. Drinking is optional.

## Short promotional description

Ancient songs, invisible organisms, shared brewhouses, and the physics inside a glass: explore one hundred ways fermentation illuminates the world. This free e-book connects beer with history, science, and culture through lively, carefully sourced lessons. Written for curious readers, including those who never drink beer. No brewing knowledge is required.

## Publication positioning

- Audience: curious general readers, educators, travelers, history and science readers, and beer enthusiasts.
- Format: one canonical Markdown manuscript, reflowable EPUB, and a typeset PDF.
- Availability: a free reading edition, with no claim that a separate Creative Commons grant has already been authorized.
- Research positioning: an original, AI-assisted synthesis based on World Beer Wiki research edition 1.19, with additional source checks and documented access limits; not independently peer-reviewed.
- Cover: original conceptual artwork; no brewery logos, third-party photographs, or reproduced research illustrations.
