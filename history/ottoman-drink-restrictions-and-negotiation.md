# Alcohol prohibitions and negotiation in seventeenth-century Istanbul

Seventeenth-century Ottoman Istanbul did not have a single unchanging alcohol prohibition applied uniformly throughout its social life. Historian Sultan Toprak Oker argues that bans on alcohol and taverns were often issued for limited periods or neighborhoods and negotiated through courts and political claims. The published abstract challenges an easy story in which top-down decrees always produced universal, continuous enforcement.[^v112-ottoman-2024]

## What this source does not establish

The article concerns alcohol and taverns broadly; its accessible abstract does **not** establish whether every boza vendor or bozahane was regulated the same way. Istanbul's artisans' association separately recounts traditional boza shops and sweet versus sour variants, but its public narrative is not a direct transcription of a seventeenth-century legal record.[^v112-boza-artisans]

## Why careful comparison makes better history

The questions include what constituted an intoxicant, who operated premises, where a rule applied and what sources survive. A beverage name cannot tell us that a customer breached a law. The full historical article, court documents and contemporaneous Ottoman Turkish terminology remain research priorities. This page gives readers a path between a modern remembered craft and a historian's more nuanced account of civic authority.[^v112-ottoman-2024]
