# The limits of fermentation evidence in prehistoric Japan and Korea

Some past foods leave chemical residues or vessel traces; others leave almost nothing identifiable. Shinya Shoda’s review asks how fermentation in Japan and Korea can be investigated when its archaeological history remains sparse despite its importance today.[^v14-japan]

## Familiar modern foods are not dated proof

The review discusses the challenge of linking observed ingredients, preparation and broader ethnography to prehistoric fermentation. Modern sake, makgeolli and other foods cannot simply be projected backwards as direct survivals of an excavated vessel.[^v14-japan]
