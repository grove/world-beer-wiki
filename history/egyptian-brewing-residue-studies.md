# Ancient Egyptian beer: residues and experiments, not a modern lager

## Why microscope and chemistry both matter

Delwen Samuel's 1996 work examined Egyptian baking and brewing evidence using microscopy of ancient remains. Later open-access research applied infrared and mass-spectrometric methods to identify constituents in archaeological beer residues. Both represent attempts to study historical production from the materials that survived, not a surviving taste panel.[^v13-arch-egypt96][^v13-egypt]

## No single pharaonic recipe

Residues can indicate ingredients or transformations in a limited assemblage. They cannot establish every dynasty's preferred recipe, universal alcohol strength, public drinking rules or the ceremonial role of each vessel. Brewing practice may have changed with place, period and labor organization.[^v13-egypt]
