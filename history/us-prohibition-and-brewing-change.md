# U.S. Prohibition and the reshaping of beer production

## The law changed the industry

The Smithsonian National Museum of American History's oral-history collection overview describes the growth of immigrant-led lager brewing, the disruptions of national Prohibition beginning in 1920, and repeal in 1933. The historical change was institutional and commercial as well as culinary: many breweries closed and the surviving industry later consolidated.[^v13-america-smithsonian]

## What the archive is—and is not

The collection holds oral histories and contextual materials about U.S. beer, but its summary does not establish that every small brewery disappeared, all Americans stopped drinking, or one flavor preference was caused by a single law. It offers a route to the testimony of people who shaped the industry rather than a complete statistical time series.[^v13-america-smithsonian]
