# Raqefet Cave and the possibility of Natufian feast beer

## Stone mortars in a burial setting

Liu and colleagues (2018) analyzed three stone mortars in the Natufian burial context of Raqefet Cave. The team combined use-wear, residues and experiments to argue for grain preparation and brewing activities around 13,000 years ago. The paper characterizes likely cereal beer in connection with ritual feasting, but no participant narrative, surviving full recipe, or direct observation is possible.[^v13-raqefet]

## Why dates and terminology matter

The study cites a broader site date range of 13,700–11,700 calibrated years before present and draws a more approximate conclusion about brewing activity. It concerns forager groups before local cereal domestication, which challenges the popular one-way story that agriculture must have preceded fermented drinks. The stronger claim that beer **caused agriculture** remains disputed and is not proved by this site.[^v13-raqefet]

## What survives

## Evidence boundaries

The cited public abstract supports the authors’ interpretation, not an independently replicated reconstruction of all ancient procedures. All dated observations are tied to the source and location described above. This is a research draft.
