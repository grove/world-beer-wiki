# Shang and Zhou bronze vessels: different evidence from Jiahu

## Some archaeological materials survive differently

The 2004 study discussed liquid contents preserved in tightly lidded bronze vessels from late Shang and early Western Zhou contexts alongside the much earlier Jiahu pottery residues. The researchers treated these as different kinds of chemical evidence: one based on absorbed pottery residues, the other on surviving liquids and their accompanying elite mortuary contexts. Neither kind of survival automatically yields a modern beverage name or a recipe.[^v113-jiahu-2004]

## The objects were social technologies

Bronze drinking and presentation vessels belonged to a wider system of commemoration, status and ritual display. The research connects them with textual and funerary evidence but does not grant direct access to what individual drinkers thought or tasted. Vessel form can suggest a mode of service, yet chemical investigation and contextual interpretation must support any claim that a specific vessel once held a fermented drink.[^v113-jiahu-2004]

## A chronology is not an unbroken brewing lineage

Jiahu Neolithic pottery and Shang/Zhou bronze vessels are separated by many centuries and different social worlds. Placing them on the same historical timeline helps readers understand evidence development, but it cannot prove an uninterrupted route from one brewing technology to another. Modern replicas or ingredient descriptions remain educational interpretations, and the source’s archaeological context should travel with every cross-link.[^v113-jiahu-2004]
