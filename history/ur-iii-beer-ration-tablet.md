# An Ur III tablet records beer for messengers

A tablet described by the Cuneiform Digital Library Initiative records rations for **three named messengers** of the Ur III administration, dating to the twenty-first century BCE. The rations include two beer terms alongside foods and other items. This is direct evidence that administrators distinguished certain beer commodities in a specific context, but it is not evidence of how those beers tasted.[^v19-cdli-ration]

## The social world of the document

The catalog interpretation situates these disbursements in state messenger service and warns that beer compensation was not universally or regularly distributed to every worker. For this individual record, we can ask who received something, how a quantity was valued, and why the bureaucracy recorded it. We cannot reconstruct a drinking session or declare the listed quantities representative of every household.[^v19-cdli-ration]

## Why terms require caution

Damerow’s 2012 analysis shows that administrative documents usually capture transactions rather than complete brewing procedures. He also demonstrates difficulties translating *dida*, intermediate grain products and other ancient categories into modern production stages. Therefore, the two beer names on the catalog page should not be turned into, for instance, two standardized recipes or modern market grades.[^v19-damerow]
