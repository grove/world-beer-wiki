# Pilsen 1842: between company chronicle and historical source

Familiar Pilsner stories often compress several changes into one invention: a town's brewing-rights system, a new communal brewery, an identified brewer and a beer that later inspired many descendants. A producer timeline dates the new brewery's first batch to 5 October 1842 and names Josef Groll. This is solid evidence **for the company's historical claim**, though not by itself a critical edition of nineteenth-century records.[^v114-plzen]

Evan Rail's 2012 Brewery History article opens by using the brewery's own 1892 jubilee publication to examine the founding story. A commemorative work can preserve near-period recollections while also presenting its institution favorably. Here the indexed beginning was available but the complete article was blocked; it would be false to attribute every later interpretation or exact quotation to a full reading.[^v114-plzen-history]

The comparison warns against two tempting leaps. First, the specific first batch of a new Pilsen enterprise does not demonstrate the first bottom-fermented beer in Bohemia or the world. Second, a beer branded “Pilsner” in later commerce and a modern judging family are not mechanically interchangeable with the first brew. The exact sensory character of a nineteenth-century batch would require records and product evidence that these two pages alone do not establish.[^v114-plzen]
