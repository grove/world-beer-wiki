# Ice houses, ammonia machines and the unequal adoption of brewery cooling

## Cold beer was once dependent on seasonal ice

The University of Chicago Library's brewing-technology exhibition describes the reliance of earlier commercial brewers on harvested winter ice, ice houses and subsequently mechanical refrigeration. Its account highlights Carl von Linde's nineteenth-century ammonia-based vapor-compression systems, which expanded brewery capacity to manage temperature and ship beer. This is a technology-history synthesis rather than a dated account of the first refrigeration system used in every country.[^v119-chicagoice]

The Swiss National Museum's 2026 history explains how ice ponds, hillside cellars, wooden ice gallows, rail transport and traded blocks of ice supported brewing. It reports that Hürlimann adopted a Linde-type mechanical cooling system in **1879**, while other breweries changed at different times, sometimes after mild winters. A year cited for one Swiss brewery should not be turned into the worldwide date when beer ceased being seasonal.[^v119-swissice26]

## Surviving documents and equipment show only portions of the network

The Smithsonian catalogue of Fred W. Wolf Company's ammonia refrigeration fitting catalogues, including one dated 1899, documents components and sales materials in the United States. It does not prove that a particular machine in a catalogue was installed at a Swiss or Norwegian brewery.[^v119-smithamm]

A nuanced history distinguishes natural ice harvesting, cellars, exported ice, mechanical machines, electricity and the social geography of breweries and trade. Historic sources do not themselves calculate modern greenhouse-gas footprints or establish microbiological safety.
