# Chicherías in Arequipa’s records, 1780–1845

A 2026 paper by César Belan examines drinking and social interactions around Arequipa, Peru, between 1780 and 1845. Its primary base is historical criminal case material from the Regional Archive of Arequipa. These documents make visible moments of confrontation, policing, class tension and social interaction; they are not neutral surveys of every person who visited a chichería.[^v17-arequipa]

## Places of cohesion and conflict

The paper argues that drinking spaces participated in both community cohesion among subordinated social groups and conflicts involving violence, class and gender. This is a more complex picture than declaring chicherías either “dangerous taverns” or uniformly harmonious community centers. Authorities’ reasons for producing records shaped which moments were preserved.[^v17-arequipa]

## Chicheras as actors

Belan highlights women managing chicherías and interprets their roles as opportunities for autonomy and economic participation that crossed some patriarchal expectations. That finding describes the historical corpus and its period, not a general statement about all Andean women or an unchanged gender system from pre-Hispanic times onward.[^v17-arequipa]

## What cannot be read from a police file

Criminal records are excellent for reconstructing regulation and contested encounters but weaker at counting uneventful daily sales. This article intentionally keeps Belan’s interpretative conclusions attributed. For brewing technique, a modern ethnoarchaeological interview study is a different source, and archaeologists studying Chimu-era Manchan face another kind of evidence entirely.
