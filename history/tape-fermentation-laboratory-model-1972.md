# 1972 tapé experiment: what a two-microbe starter demonstrates

An early fermentation experiment offers a revealing boundary between local knowledge and laboratory models. Ko Swan Djien reported in 1972 that organisms isolated from Indonesian ragi could be selected to convert steamed glutinous rice into *tapé*. The authors found one two-organism combination with useful laboratory fermentation characteristics.[^v110-tape-1972]

## A reduced consortium

The experiment compared selected isolates and prepared cultures grown on rice. Its value lies in showing a reproducible functional system under controlled conditions. It does not show that all Indonesian ragi cakes historically contained exactly two organisms, or that a laboratory pair had the same cultural role as an inherited household starter.[^v110-tape-1972]

## The modern diversity question

A later Java study examined *Amylomyces rouxii* diversity in ragi tapai. Together these papers invite useful comparisons between starter composition, individual amylolytic organisms and chosen process conditions. They are separated by half a century and different experimental questions, so a shared taxon name does not validate an unchanged starter composition through time.[^v110-ragi-genetics]

## Evidence and limitations

The 1972 journal abstract was accessible, but full protocol and tables were not inspected. No food safety, strain identity or commercial production recommendation is inferred. These limitations should be retained when quoting the article or extracting relationships.
