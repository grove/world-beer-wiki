# A 1991 US marketing circular and the limits of regulatory continuity

## What a dated circular can establish

Industry Circular 91-10 has a day, an issuing US federal agency and a specified audience: the beer and beverage trade. It is unusually useful as a primary source because its words can be assigned to **20 August 1991**. Its discussion of non-alcoholic malt beverage marketing shows an administrative concern, but its existence alone cannot prove what manufacturers actually printed or what drinkers believed.[^v115-circular]

Present TTB guidance gives its own label requirements, including separate uses of “non-alcoholic,” “alcohol free” and “low alcohol.” Those requirements are current agency guidance in the cited US federal context, not an automatic quotation of the position in 1991. Comparison needs to preserve the two dates rather than silently backdating today's thresholds.[^v115-alcohol]

## Why a legal timeline needs more than two snapshots

A proper intervening history would inspect amendments, relevant rulings, rulemaking and enforcement records, trade archives, products and state-level variation. It would also distinguish warning law from class names: current TTB guidance traces federal alcohol-container warnings to the 1988 statute, but an obligation on qualifying containers cannot be read as the origin of the beverage category itself.[^v115-warning]

The [calendar event](../calendar/atf-nonalcoholic-marketing-circular-1991.md) records the archival day. This is deliberately a two-source-date comparison, not an exhaustive legal history or a direction for preparing regulated labeling. Consult current legal text and counsel for real market decisions.
