# Two federal US definitions: beer and malt beverage

## Two laws and different boundaries

The US Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau's public explanation distinguishes **beer** under the Internal Revenue Code from **malt beverage** under the Federal Alcohol Administration Act. Under the tax-oriented beer definition it describes a threshold of at least 0.5% ABV and specified fermentable materials. The separate FAA Act malt-beverage definition requires both malted barley and hops, and may encompass non-alcoholic or alcohol-free products.[^v115-defs]

That separation is counterintuitive only if “beer” is treated as one universal scientific species. The definitions serve different administrative tasks. A beverage can fall into one federal category without automatically meeting all elements of the other, and a product in another country may have yet a third legal classification.

## What the label says

TTB's class-and-type guidance adds a market-facing layer: names and descriptors depend on the covered product and its alcohol content. Judging-style language does not automatically authorize use of a regulated class designation on a US label.[^v115-clas] The federal health-warning guidance adds a separate container-label obligation for alcoholic beverages meeting its specified conditions, deriving from the 1988 Alcoholic Beverage Labeling Act; it is not a statement that every traditional cereal ferment everywhere shares that rule.[^v115-warning]
