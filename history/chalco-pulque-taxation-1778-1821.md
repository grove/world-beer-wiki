# Chalco pulque production and taxation, 1778–1821

Carlos Roberto Cruz Gómez studies pulque cultivation, production and fiscal control between 1778 and 1821 in the Chalco district southeast of Mexico City. His INAH abstract describes production across villages, ranches and estates and the officials collecting and administering pulque taxes. This evidence makes the beverage part of an agricultural and administrative history as well as a fermentation tradition.[^v111-pulque-tax]

## What changed and why

The author interprets high fiscal burdens, rural crises, epidemics and the Mexican War of Independence as pressures that constrained production and favored the gradual shift toward other drinks or crops in the studied area. Such an argument is a specific historian's reconstruction of one district. It should not be rewritten as 'tax killed pulque everywhere' or extrapolated mechanically to modern producer economies.[^v111-pulque-tax]

## Issue date versus upload date

The journal labels the article with issue 114 (2023), while its website posts it in 2025. Both dates are useful and record different things. The abstract, not original tax registers, was consulted for this article; exact tax rates and individual family circumstances require access to the full historical study and archives. Compare religious-ritual interpretations separately rather than collapsing colonial fiscal and clerical sources into a single neutral account.[^v111-pulque-tax]
