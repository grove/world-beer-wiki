# Richardson and the historical construction of brewing extract

## A number was made for a purpose

Modern brewing readers can take a gravity figure for granted. Historian James Sumner's 2001 abstract, however, examines how John Richardson promoted a commercial brewing quantity expressed in pounds per barrel and how the saccharometer helped establish its practical authority. The argument concerns an eighteenth-century effort to organize brewing economics and measurement rather than the discovery of a natural constant shared by every beverage culture.[^v116-sumner]

The Wellcome Collection catalogues Richardson's 1784 printed tables and directions for using the instrument. Its bibliographic record confirms the publication year; it does not mean the complete work, every table or every claim in its original edition was inspected for this article.[^v116-wellcome]

## A surviving later object

A Science Museum Group entry catalogues a Dring and Fage saccharometer around 1830. It provides material and manufacturer evidence from a later period, not a license to infer that Richardson used that particular example or that all breweries adopted the same scale.[^v116-museum]

## Reading historically

Commercial accounts, scientific instrumentation and local practice overlap, but they are not identical evidence classes. An instrument can help create a stable reference for comparisons without establishing that every brewer valued the same variables or that a measured extract reveals the final beer's cultural identity.
