# Cerro Baúl dregs and the question of domestic Wari brewing

The best archaeological question may be not 'Did the Wari drink chicha?' but 'Who could produce it, and where?' A 2024 study uses excavated Cerro Baúl evidence alongside experiments informed by contemporary makers, comparing maize, *Schinus molle* fruit and mixed ingredients to reason about production traces. The authors report that in the studied Wari province of Moquegua brewing was largely state-sponsored and specialized, rather than ubiquitous in households.[^v110-wari]

## Why dregs and vessels are not interchangeable

Fermentation vessels, residual deposits, architectural context and preparation evidence each support different inferences. According to the abstract, a fermentation vessel is a strong archaeological indicator, but multiple other lines may make a case where none survives. Experimental analogues help recognize possible traces without literally reproducing a known ancient recipe.[^v110-wari]

## Compare scale, not presumed identity

Hayashida's north-coast ethnoarchaeological research highlights labor and production scales as independent variables. The Wari argument concerns a particular period and provincial setting. It cannot prove that no household brewed anywhere in the Andes, or that later chicherías descended from a single Wari institution.[^v110-wari][^v110-northcoast]

## Evidence and limitations

Both studies were accessed through publisher abstracts, not complete analytical appendices. Claims about exact capacities, residue chemistry and the proportion of households remain outside the accessible evidence. These limitations should be retained when quoting the article or extracting relationships.
