# The 1892 crown-cork patent is not the invention of bottled beer

## First distinguish an archival invention from an industry origin

William Painter's US Patent 468,226 carries the date **2 February 1892** and describes a specific crimped, liner-equipped bottle sealing device. Carbonated drinks and bottled beverages existed before the patent. A precise patent date is evidence that a device was described and protected by that legal record; it is not the first date anyone put beer in glass or sold a packaged drink.[^v118-patent1892]

Crown Holdings' corporate history attributes the crown cork to Painter and describes later mechanization and international expansion. The company chronicle provides producer perspective on an invention's commercialization, not a complete independent census of adopters or proof that all breweries switched instantly.[^v118-crown-history]

## A machine changes the scale, not the beverage's origin

The ASME engineering landmark account puts a closure and filling equipment in a longer effort to seal carbonated drinks more reliably. The source describes industrial automation and a few surviving machines. These developments can be linked to distribution and preservation without inferring the original yeast, recipe, regional name or invention date of lager or ale.[^v118-asme-crown]

To write a defensible history, separate patent filing and grant, producer marketing claims, actual adoption by a named brewery, and surviving equipment evidence. Museum or corporate images also have separate rights. For the physical object, read [the crown and liner](../objects/crown-cork-and-sealing-liner.md); for the exact archived day, read [the 1892 calendar entry](../calendar/crown-cork-patent-1892.md). The calendar metadata describes a nonrecurring legal record, not a commercial holiday.
