# Cerro Baúl: an excavated Wari brewery

Wari-period Cerro Baúl excavation in the Moquegua region of Peru, around the close of the first millennium CE; site-specific reconstruction, not an Inca recipe.

## The production site

The excavators describe a purpose-built Wari brewery at Cerro Baúl, with distinct areas interpreted as milling, heating and fermentation. A series of ceramic-vat positions and hearths, together with botanical remains, allows a more specific account than the generic claim that the Wari “drank beer.” The published estimate of roughly 1,800 litres per batch is based on vessel capacity, not a modern production log.[^v15-wari-2005]

## Maize, molle and a contested recipe

The excavated assemblage includes evidence interpreted as sprouted maize preparation and *Schinus molle* drupes. Investigators explicitly leave open whether maize and molle were brewed separately or combined. This uncertainty is essential: archaeobotanical associations and experiments do not prove that one fixed mixture was the exclusive beer made at the complex.[^v15-wari-2005][^v15-wari-2024]

## Specialization and political work

A later study uses excavation and informant-guided experimental production to question a tempting assumption: that any society with a major brewery necessarily brewed equally in every household. For Wari-affiliated Moquegua it argues the evidence favors specialized, state-supported production. That is an inference about a defined province and period, not proof that no domestic Wari person ever fermented grain.[^v15-wari-2024]

## Ceremonial closure

The original excavation reports staged feasting and deliberate destruction associated with leaving the colony, including smashed serving vessels and the burning of structures. One can describe a site-level termination ritual; it does not establish a universal Andean annual festival or a living tradition unchanged since Wari times.[^v15-wari-2005]
