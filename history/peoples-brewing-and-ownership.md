# Peoples Brewing: Black brewery ownership in 1970

A specific Wisconsin brewery acquisition described by the Smithsonian, set against racial exclusion in US business history.

## A named change of ownership

The National Museum of African American History and Culture describes businessman Theodore “Ted” Mack and a group of Black business partners purchasing Peoples Brewing in Oshkosh, Wisconsin in 1970. The museum situates the acquisition within Black entrepreneurship and contemporaneous debates over economic opportunity.[^v15-smithsonian-peoples]

## Labor and ownership are different questions

The museum’s larger essay discusses enslaved labor chiefly in the context of distilleries and other alcohol production; those examples do not establish identical conditions at every historic brewery. The brewery acquisition in 1970 is a concrete beer-history episode with different surviving evidence from the colonial or antebellum period.[^v15-smithsonian-peoples]

## Why this belongs in a beer atlas

Corporate history is not only a sequence of styles and technical inventions. Who could own a brewery, obtain capital and reach a market shapes whose products and perspectives were visible. The Smithsonian oral-history collection provides another kind of evidence for entrepreneurs and their working lives, but does not itself retell Peoples Brewing’s full business history.[^v15-smithsonian-oral]
