# Mixed-fermentation sour beer

**Specialty Beer Category** · International · modern category

Mixed-fermentation sour beer combines the underlying beer with character from multiple kinds of yeast or bacteria. It is a wide modern category rather than a single regional tradition. The result should bring together acidity, fermentation aromas, and the character of the base beer.[^bjcp-2021-28b]

## Character

Examples range from gently tart and fruity to strongly sour and earthy. They often feel lighter and drier than the starting style suggests. Harsh vinegar-like acidity is not the defining objective; balance matters more than simply maximizing sourness.[^bjcp-2021-28b]

## Brewing and context

Different combinations of conventional brewing yeast, Brettanomyces, and acid-producing bacteria can be used. Wood aging may be involved, but is not required. The category describes the result and fermentation context rather than one mandatory production timetable.[^bjcp-2021-28b]

## History and interpretation

This BJCP category is useful when a beer does not fit a narrower established style. It does not make every mixed-fermented beer lambic, nor does it prove spontaneous fermentation. Named traditions should keep their own geographical and historical identities.[^bjcp-2021-28b]
