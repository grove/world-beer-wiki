# London Brown Ale

**Beer Style** · Europe · historical reference

London Brown Ale describes a notably sweet, low-strength brown-ale reference associated with bottled London examples. It is distinct from the broader, less uniformly sweet British Brown Ale category.[^bjcp-2021-27d]

## Character

Caramel, toffee, dark bread, and gentle dried-fruit impressions can appear. Bitterness is low, and roast should not dominate. The body can seem surprisingly rounded for the modest strength.[^bjcp-2021-27d]

## Brewing and context

Dark and caramel malt character combines with a sweet balance and restrained hopping. Historically documented commercial examples changed over time, so a modern low-strength reference should not be projected backward without checking the period.[^bjcp-2021-27d]

## History and interpretation

This entry follows a historical style description rather than making a claim about present-day market availability. The category is especially useful for understanding why “brown ale” does not identify one uniform British beer. Compare it with Dark Mild and British Brown Ale, but do not assume that low strength, dark color, or the use of a bottle makes those traditions identical.[^bjcp-2021-27d]
