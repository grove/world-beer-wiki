# Kvass — rye and bread tradition

**Related Fermented-Grain Tradition** · Europe / Eastern Europe / Central Europe · living tradition

Kvass is a lightly fermented cereal drink associated with Eastern and Central Europe. Rye bread, rye flour, or rye malt can provide its base. This entry concerns the grain-and-bread tradition, rather than assuming every beverage sold under the broader kvass name is the same product.[^kvass-study]

## Character

Its characteristic impression combines rye-bread aroma with a gentle sweet-and-sour balance. Alcohol is often low, but fermentation can continue; “kvass” alone does not guarantee an alcohol-free drink. Exact strength should come from a measured product or batch, not the name.[^kvass-study]

## Brewing and context

Both alcoholic and lactic-acid fermentation contribute. The cited experiment made kvass from rye-bread extract and compared plant additions, showing how ingredients and fermentation conditions can alter the resulting drink. Fruit or herb additions are variations, not requirements for all kvass.[^kvass-study]

## History and interpretation

The same study distinguishes bread-based production from commercial products made with malt concentrate and other additions. These can share a name without sharing every process detail. Its laboratory antioxidant results are not used here as evidence that drinking kvass provides a clinical health benefit.[^kvass-study]
