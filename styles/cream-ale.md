# Cream Ale

**Beer Style** · North America · living

Cream ale is a pale American beer that combines an easy-drinking lager-like character with ale brewing traditions. Despite its name, cream is not a defining ingredient, and the beer need not taste creamy or sweet.[^bjcp-2021-1c]

## Character

Expect mild grain flavors, restrained bitterness, a light body, and lively carbonation. Maize is a common ingredient alongside barley malt; a small amount of fruitiness can appear, but strong banana, clove, or heavy caramel would change the character.[^bjcp-2021-1c]

## Brewing and context

Brewers may use ale yeast, lager yeast, or a combination of approaches, followed by cold conditioning. This makes cream ale a useful reminder that familiar beer names do not always fit a simple ale-versus-lager division.[^bjcp-2021-1c]

## History and interpretation

Its American history includes competition between ale breweries and the expanding lager market. Modern versions are generally cleaner and lighter than some historical interpretations. The category is distinct from beers merely advertised as “cream” because they are served with nitrogen or contain milk-derived ingredients.[^bjcp-2021-1c]
