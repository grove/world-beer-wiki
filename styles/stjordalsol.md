# Stjørdalsøl

**Beer Tradition** · Northern Europe / Norway · living tradition

Stjørdalsøl is a farmhouse brewing tradition from the Stjørdal area of Norway. Its defining feature is locally made malt dried with wood smoke. The malt, and the people who make it, matter more than conformity to a single commercial recipe.[^garshol-stjordal]

## Character

A documented tasting found dark reddish-brown beers with little carbonation. Smoke was prominent, but sweetness, acidity, fruitiness, and roughness differed considerably between brewers. Think of a varied local tradition, not simply a Norwegian version of German smoked lager.[^garshol-stjordal]

## Brewing and context

Brewers often share a malt house through an association. Barley is germinated and dried on a perforated kiln floor above a fire. The field report identifies alder as the usual smoking wood. Some brewers mix their own malt with bought malt.[^garshol-stjordal]

## History and interpretation

Baker's yeast, commercial ale yeast, and lager yeast all appeared in the documented beers. Calling every Stjørdalsøl a kveik beer would therefore be misleading. The evidence comes from a particular tasting and fieldwork, not a census of every brewer or a fixed historical specification.[^garshol-stjordal]
