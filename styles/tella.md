# Tella

**Beer Tradition** · East Africa / Ethiopia · living tradition

Tella is an Ethiopian grain beer associated with gesho, the plant Rhamnus prinoides. Gesho may play a role comparable to a brewing herb, but it is not the hop plant used in most modern commercial beer.[^tella-research]

## Character

Grain preparation, herbs, and mixed fermentation shape its character. Tella can be cloudy, and both acidity and alcohol develop during fermentation. The cited study explicitly recognizes differences in ingredients and methods among Ethiopian communities.[^tella-research]

## Brewing and context

The documented preparations used barley, barley malt, and gesho. Some of the grain was roasted and prepared as flatbread or cooked cakes before joining staged fermentations. These preparations show why describing tella as ordinary barley wort with a substitute bittering ingredient is incomplete.[^tella-research]

## History and interpretation

The research also experimented with replacing gesho with moringa. That was an innovation being tested, not the definition of traditional tella. Names such as Farsoo and Siwa occur in related Ethiopian linguistic and regional contexts; this entry does not assume that every local expression is identical. No fixed nationwide recipe or health benefit is claimed.[^tella-research]

## Household knowledge and process variation

A Jimma University study combined interviews in five selected neighbourhoods with formulation and preservation experiments. All surveyed makers were women, and many had substantial selling experience. Its experimental results found that malt and adjunct combinations and fermentation time affected the measured beverage. This supports recording a maker's ingredients and process, instead of projecting one laboratory formulation across Ethiopia.[^trad-tella-jimma]

The preservation trials were interventions intended to change storage behaviour. Their results should be labelled as experimental adaptations; they are not evidence that household tella is normally pasteurized or stable for the same period.[^trad-tella-jimma]

## Fermentation organisms are sample-specific

A 2024 study selected seven lactic acid bacterial isolates from a larger collection and examined their properties in laboratory tests. Several genera were represented. Such isolate studies establish characteristics of the recovered strains; they do not establish one national tella microbiome or prove a health benefit from drinking the beverage.[^trad-tella-lab]

## Evidence note

Evidence now includes a full-text formulation experiment, a university thesis abstract, and a bacterial-isolate study abstract. The local scope and experimental treatments are retained. Names translated as “local hops” refer here to gesho, not botanical identity with Humulus lupulus; this is an ingredient comparison, not a claim of equivalence.

## Research extension 1.5: Botanical variation is not one prescribed recipe

A review of Ethiopian cereal-based fermented drinks describes use of gesho leaves and stems in tella, while emphasizing regional cereal and processing variation. Gesho is a local botanical with specific preparation forms, not merely an alternative name for hops.[^v15-ethiopia-review]

A 2021 experiment examined substitution with *Moringa stenopetala*. Its research question concerns measured fermentation outcomes, not proof that the substitute was part of all historic or contemporary household recipes.[^v15-ethiopia-moringa]

## Edition 1.7: newly sourced connections

A 2025 open review of tella preparation discusses the drink as hospitality, an accompaniment to social events and a household livelihood as well as a fermentation technology. This helps prevent an ingredient-only description that overlooks makers and guests, without implying every Ethiopian family brews or sells tella.[^v17-tella-2025]

The [cultural context article](../culture/tella-hospitality-and-household-economy.md) connects those claims with [gesho](../ingredients/gesho.md) and other cereal-brewing economies. The review's regional variation and source type remain explicit.

## Edition 1.8: newly sourced connections

A 2024 review compares several **named Ethiopian** cereal ferments: tella, borde and shamita differ in grain preparation, texture, context and fermentation, even when reviewers group them together as locally consumed alcoholic or low-alcohol beverages. It would be incorrect to reduce borde and shamita to recipe variants of tella or apply a universal style definition to all three.[^v18-ethiopian-review]

See [Borde](borde-ethiopian-fermented-cereal.md), [Shamita](shamita-ethiopian-fermented-barley.md), and [food-drink classification](../categories/fermented-cereal-beverages-as-food.md). The review synthesizes prior studies; it is not a new direct observation of every Ethiopian household.



## Edition 1.14: newly sourced connections

A 2024 synthesis describes Ethiopian tella as a cereal-based household beverage involving varied forms of grain and gesho. It also reports preparation of some fermentation vessels through washing and exposure to wood smoke. **Smoking a vessel is not the same step as smoking malt**, and a flavor impression alone cannot distinguish those possible pathways.[^v114-tella]

This is secondary review evidence about locally varying practices, not proof that all tella makers use an identical method or that smoke alone ensures safe sanitation. Explore [household hospitality and vessel work](../culture/tella-household-vessels-and-hospitality.md) and [the two smoke pathways](../processes/tella-vessel-fumigation-vs-smoked-malt.md).[^v114-tella]
