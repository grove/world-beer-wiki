# Indigenous Taiwanese millet and rice ferments: evidence scope

Millet and glutinous rice fermentation form part of several Taiwanese Indigenous culinary and ceremonial landscapes. A 2013 university-indexed study of a traditional fermented millet/alcoholic beverage used microbial cultivation and DNA methods to identify lactic acid bacteria during starter preparation and brewing. The study's abstract does **not name the sampled community**, so it must not be relabelled a 'Paiwan strain profile'.[^v112-taiwan-lab-2013]

## Different knowledge sources

The Council of Indigenous Peoples separately documents millet and millet wine in Paiwan rituals, including offerings in some specified ceremonies. That institutional cultural documentation does not tell us whether Chao and colleagues sampled Paiwan participants; combining the two would invent provenance.[^v112-paiwan-agency]

## Why the comparison is worth keeping

A chemical profile, a starter-culture assay and a public description of a ritual each answer a different question. A historical or present-day community term for a beverage matters more than an English 'millet wine' catch-all; future articles should be renamed with community guidance where appropriate. This page is deliberately a bounded, provisional route rather than a claim to one universal Taiwanese Indigenous recipe.[^v112-taiwan-lab-2013]
