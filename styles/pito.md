# Pito

**Beer Tradition** · West Africa / Ghana · living tradition

Pito is a West African sorghum beer, represented here by research on Ghanaian production. Sourness and alcoholic fermentation are both important to understanding it; it should not be described merely as lager made from a different grain.[^pito-research]

## Character

The beer's aroma depends on how it is fermented. A study comparing traditional spontaneous fermentation with selected bacterial and yeast cultures found broadly similar measures of acidity and alcohol but differences in aroma compounds, color, and cloudiness. Similar laboratory numbers therefore do not guarantee identical flavor.[^pito-research]

## Brewing and context

Sorghum malt is mashed and an acidification stage develops the sour wort. A separate study of dolo and pito production documented lactic acid bacteria throughout these stages, with substantial diversity among strains.[^dolo-pito-research]

## History and interpretation

The selected cultures used in the experimental beer were a research intervention, not proof that all traditional brewers use those exact organisms. The available evidence supports a fermentation tradition with variation, rather than a universal ingredient ratio or fixed alcohol range.[^pito-research]

## Evidence note

The description is limited to the sources below. A local observation or laboratory sample does not establish one recipe, strength, or flavor for every community or period.

## Edition 1.4: Local livelihoods and market conditions

Research in Wa municipality describes pita-making enterprises as a source of income for surveyed women; separate Northern Ghana fieldwork discusses water shortages and local market constraints. This is a *complement* to laboratory sampling, not evidence that every pito brewer has the same social role. See [the Wa case](../culture/pito-enterprises-in-wa.md).[^v14-wa]


## Edition 1.7: newly sourced connections

The word *pito* cannot describe one social setting or business structure. Lobnibe’s fieldwork follows sorghum beer into Brong Ahafo bars, where migration and changing identities are central to the question; Mumuni and colleagues instead surveyed 362 women brewers across purposively chosen Upper West communities about finance, packaging and trade constraints.[^v17-ghana-bars][^v17-ghana-enterprise]

These are **different study populations** and should not be averaged into one national account. Read the [bar-and-migration case](../culture/ghana-pito-bars-migration-and-identity.md) alongside the [women’s enterprise survey](../culture/ghana-pito-women-enterprise-survey.md). Both sources were accessed as abstracts; neither establishes one mandatory fermentation recipe.
