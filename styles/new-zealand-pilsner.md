# New Zealand Pilsner

New Zealand Pilsner is a pale, clean-fermented beer emphasizing New Zealand-type hop aroma over a supportive malt base. BJCP 2021 places it at X5 in its local-styles appendix rather than in the numbered German pilsner category.[^bjcp-2021-x5]

## Sensory and fermentation identity

The reference balances recognizable hop bitterness with a smooth, relatively soft palate. Fruit, citrus and grassy hop notes can coexist with bread or cracker-like malt. It permits lager yeast or a very neutral ale yeast, including cool ale fermentation. Thus the word “Pilsner” alone does not prove the yeast used in a particular example.[^bjcp-2021-x5]

## The Emerson's example

Emerson's calls its Pilsner a New Zealand Pilsner, lists it at 4.9% ABV and identifies Nelson-grown Riwaka hops. Its description combines citrus and passion-fruit associations with some initial malt sweetness and a long dry finish. These are the brewery's own product observations, not a tasting performed for this wiki.[^modern-emerson-pilsner]

The producer's Sauvignon Blanc comparison concerns aroma. It is not a declaration of added wine or grapes. Its page also uses the relative phrase “nearly 20 years ago” for the first brewing; because that statement lacks a clear publication date, this wiki does not subtract twenty from the present year to manufacture an origin date.[^modern-emerson-pilsner]

## Compare the whole balance

BJCP contrasts the style with a sharper, drier German Pils and with the stronger hop balance of IPA.[^bjcp-2021-x5] Compare [German Pils](german-pils.md), [New Zealand IPA](new-zealand-ipa.md), [Italian Pilsner](italian-pilsner.md) and [West Coast Pilsner](west-coast-pilsner.md) as neighboring descriptions rather than sorting solely by geography or hop variety.

For retrieval, keep “New Zealand” in at least two possible fields: the name of the style and the origin of a named hop. Neither automatically supplies the country where an individual beer was brewed.

## Evidence note

The guideline supports the category's intended balance; the producer supports one concrete formulation. Neither means all local examples use Riwaka, the same yeast, or the same alcohol content.
