# Oud Bruin

**Beer Style** · Europe · living

Oud Bruin, in the Flemish sour-brown-ale sense used here, combines brown-malt depth with gentle to pronounced acidity and fermentation-derived fruit. It is generally a softer, maltier comparison to Flanders Red Ale.[^bjcp-2021-23c]

## Character

Caramel, toffee, dark bread, raisins, and plum-like notes can meet a rounded sourness. Heavy roast is not central. The overall impression is complex brown beer rather than a dark version of a sharply acidic pale sour.[^bjcp-2021-23c]

## Brewing and context

Maturation and microbial fermentation shape the beer, and blending may be part of production. Wood flavor is not a universal requirement. Malt depth and acidity need to work together rather than cancel one another out.[^bjcp-2021-23c]

## History and interpretation

Names require care: this entry is not a description of every Dutch-language product called “old brown.” The modern competition category is a useful reference for the Flemish family, not proof that all historic brown ales used identical methods. Identify the producer, region, and period when comparing particular examples.[^bjcp-2021-23c]
