# Amazake: koji sweet rice drink and sake-lees variants

*Amazake* is a Japanese sweet drink whose name does not identify a single microbial or alcohol-production process. A 2021 review distinguishes **koji amazake**, prepared from rice koji and related material, from **sakekasu amazake**, made using sake lees. The authors discuss the forms together, but the input ingredients and potential residual alcohol are not interchangeable.[^v112-amazake-2021]

## Sweetness without a requirement for alcoholic fermentation

Koji mold enzymes can break rice starch into fermentable sugars, making a sweet liquid without requiring a subsequent substantial yeast fermentation. Sake lees, by contrast, carry a history of sake production and may contain alcohol. Therefore the familiar claim that all amazake is nonalcoholic needs a product-specific caveat.[^v112-amazake-2021]

## What this entry does not promise

The reviewed article discusses nutritional and functional hypotheses and is authored by an employee of a sake producer. We use it for **production-route distinctions**, not to recommend amazake as a treatment, certify a commercial label or convert it into a judging style. Compare sake, koji organisms and starch conversion to see why 'fermented rice' contains several technically and culturally distinct foods.[^v112-amazake-2021]
