# Specialty wood-aged beer

**Specialty Beer Category** · International · modern category

Specialty wood-aged beer combines a base beer with wood character and recognizable flavors from an alcoholic drink previously held in the wood. Bourbon-barrel beer is one example, but the category is broader than that single treatment.[^bjcp-2021-33b]

## Character

The beer may show vanilla, toast, caramel, wood, or notes associated with the previous spirit or wine. Those impressions should complement rather than bury the underlying beer. The result depends on both the base beer and the barrel's history.[^bjcp-2021-33b]

## Brewing and context

A dark strong beer is a familiar starting point, but the category is not defined by stout alone. Color and flavor can also be affected by the former contents of a barrel. A meaningful description names both the base style and the prior contents when known.[^bjcp-2021-33b]

## History and interpretation

BJCP separates this category from ordinary Wood-Aged Beer because the previous alcoholic product makes a noticeable contribution. This is a classification distinction, not a separate ancient lineage. “Barrel-aged” by itself does not identify the original beer or prove how it was fermented.[^bjcp-2021-33b]
