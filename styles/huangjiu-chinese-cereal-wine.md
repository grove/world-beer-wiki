# Huangjiu: Chinese fermented cereal wine

*Huangjiu* is commonly rendered “Chinese rice wine,” but the reviewed literature includes rice, glutinous rice and sometimes millet, combined with starters such as wheat *qu*. This is a **fermented cereal-wine family**, not the wine of grapes and not automatically a BJCP beer category. Its regional varieties and commercial processes differ; a single factory’s sampling plan cannot define their ancestry or taste.[^v19-huangjiu-review]

## Grain, starter and changing mash

A 2022 microbiome review describes grain preparation, starch conversion, fermentation and later clarification or aging as distinct stages. It also documents variation among starter and microbial systems. The central technological point is familiar across starch fermentation: enzymes must render starch fermentable before and during alcohol production. The identity and timing of the enzyme source matter more than a simple “rice beer” synonym.[^v19-huangjiu-review]

## How newer studies complicate the picture

A 2026 comparative paper contrasted mechanized and traditionally managed Huangjiu fermentations and reported differing microbial and metabolic profiles in its study. That does **not** establish that every manual batch is more flavorful, nor that the two techniques represent fixed centuries-old categories. Fermentation management, grain choice and sampled environments all require their own evidence.[^v19-huangjiu-2026]
