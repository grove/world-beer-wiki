# Mijiaya fermented-grain beverage

**Archaeological Brewing Evidence** · East Asia / China / Shaanxi · archaeological

This entry describes archaeological evidence for a fermented-grain beverage at Mijiaya in China about 5,000 years ago. It is not the recovered name of an ancient style. “Mijiaya beer” is a convenient modern label for the beverage inferred from material remains.[^mijiaya-study]

## Character

The study identified evidence consistent with broomcorn millet, barley, Job's tears, and tubers. It does not recover a dependable taste, color, carbonation level, or alcohol strength. Any detailed tasting description of a modern recreation belongs to that recreation, not directly to the ancient evidence.[^mijiaya-study]

## Brewing and context

The researchers combined starch, plant-silica, and chemical-residue analyses of pottery. Their interpretation connects the ingredients and vessels with brewing and fermentation. These are scientific inferences from residues, rather than a written recipe specifying exact quantities and temperatures.[^mijiaya-study]

## History and interpretation

The discovery shows why ancient brewing cannot be reduced to today's barley-and-hops model. However, the study's statements about the earliest evidence reflect its 2016 publication context. This wiki does not convert them into a claim that Mijiaya remains the oldest known beer in China.[^mijiaya-study]

## Evidence note

Residues support an archaeological interpretation, not an exact written recipe. Flavor, original name, precise strength, and continuity to a living style remain unestablished here.
