# Kornøl

**Beer Tradition** · Northern Europe / Norway · living tradition

Kornøl is a Norwegian farmhouse tradition represented here by brewing documented in the Hornindal area. In that observed method, the collected wort is not boiled. “Raw ale” describes that missing wort-boiling step, not a complete absence of heating.[^garshol-kornol]

## Character

The fieldwork describes unusually complex fermentation character, with both yeast and bacteria contributing in the investigated culture. That finding should not become a rule that every Kornøl contains the same organisms or tastes identically.[^garshol-kornol]

## Brewing and context

In the observed method, hot juniper infusion heated the mash. Juniper branches helped filter the wort, and the hot liquid passed through a bag of hops on its way to collection. The wort was then cooled for fermentation rather than boiled in a kettle.[^garshol-kornol]

## History and interpretation

The brewer learned from a relative, making this evidence of transmitted local practice rather than a newly invented competition recipe. A modern “Hornindal kveik” product is not, by itself, a definition of Kornøl. Recipe, culture, heating, and local knowledge belong together in the description.[^garshol-kornol]

## Regional naming and variation

Garshol's broader comparison places kornøl in northwestern Norway, especially Nordfjord and Sunnmøre, with some production in Sunnfjord. Hornindal is an important documented example, not the whole geographical meaning of the name. He describes predominantly pale, lightly carbonated beers with grain, juniper, and varying fermentation aromas.[^trad-norwegian-regions]

The same account notes that some brewers have started boiling the wort. A process change does not automatically erase a regional name. For retrieval, distinguish **kornøl as a local brewing tradition** from **raw ale as a process description**, and preserve the maker and observation date when available.[^trad-norwegian-regions]

## Compare the right features

With [Vossaøl](vossaol.md), compare wort heating and fermentation practice; with [Stjørdalsøl](stjordalsol.md), compare local malting and smoke. A shared Norwegian origin, juniper, or a yeast product labelled “kveik” does not settle all three comparisons.[^trad-norwegian-regions]

## Evidence note

The detailed process account follows one Hornindal-area brewer; the 2017 regional synthesis broadens the context. Both are firsthand research by the same author, not independent surveys. The observed culture’s bacteria and yeast should not be generalized to every commercial Hornindal isolate or every household beer.
