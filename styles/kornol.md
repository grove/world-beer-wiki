# Kornøl

**Beer Tradition** · Northern Europe / Norway · living tradition

Kornøl is a Norwegian farmhouse tradition represented here by brewing documented in the Hornindal area. Its important distinction is that the collected wort is not boiled. “Raw ale” describes that missing wort-boiling step, not a complete absence of heating.[^garshol-kornol]

## Character

The fieldwork describes unusually complex fermentation character, with both yeast and bacteria contributing in the investigated culture. That finding should not become a rule that every Kornøl contains the same organisms or tastes identically.[^garshol-kornol]

## Brewing and context

In the observed method, hot juniper infusion heated the mash. Juniper branches helped filter the wort, and the hot liquid passed through a bag of hops on its way to collection. The wort was then cooled for fermentation rather than boiled in a kettle.[^garshol-kornol]

## History and interpretation

The brewer learned from a relative, making this evidence of transmitted local practice rather than a newly invented competition recipe. A modern “Hornindal kveik” product is not, by itself, a definition of Kornøl. Recipe, culture, heating, and local knowledge belong together in the description.[^garshol-kornol]
