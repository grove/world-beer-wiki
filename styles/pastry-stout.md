# Pastry stout

Pastry stout is a descriptive family name for stouts deliberately built around dessert-like flavors and richness. Actual products show why the label should be read alongside the ingredient list: Lervig calls Sweet Tooth an imperial pastry stout with coconut and vanilla, while its Dulce De Leche is a bourbon-barrel-aged imperial stout with vanilla and caramel.[^modern-lervig-sweettooth][^modern-lervig-dulce]

## Concrete examples, different ingredients

| Producer example | Declared approach | What it demonstrates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lervig Sweet Tooth | 11% ABV; coconut flavoring and vanilla extract; tagged vegan by the producer | Pastry terminology does not require lactose. |
| Lervig Dulce De Leche | 15.1% ABV; lactose, vanilla and caramel flavor; 13 months in bourbon barrels | A dessert profile can combine added ingredients and barrel maturation. |

These are dated producer declarations, not universal strength ranges or independent tasting results.[^modern-lervig-sweettooth][^modern-lervig-dulce] They also show why a flavor word should not be promoted into a recipe claim: “coconut” might refer to a listed flavoring, while other products could use a different ingredient form.

## Category and recipe are not interchangeable

BJCP suggests 31B, 30D or 29C for dessert/pastry stouts according to lactose, spice and fruit combinations.[^modern-bjcp-entry-suggestions]

These are example placements for particular formulations, not proof that every product called pastry stout contains dairy sugar or has one mandatory category.

For a useful profile, record the base style, each declared addition, whether it is a whole ingredient or flavoring, any barrel treatment, and the actual ABV. Keep those fields independently searchable. A barrel-aged dessert stout can be retrieved by both treatment and sensory family without inventing a separate origin story for every flavor combination.
