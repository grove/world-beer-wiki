# West Coast Pilsner

West Coast Pilsner joins a pilsner identity to the hop expression associated with contemporary IPAs. In the Brewers Association's 2026 guideline, assertive modern hops distinguish it from noble-type dry-hopped Italian Pilsener.[^modern-ba-2026]

## A producer example

Highland Park Brewery describes Timbo Pils as a 5.8% ABV West Coast pilsner using Mosaic and Citra. Its account emphasizes a clear appearance, aromas reminiscent of grapes, passion fruit and citrus, and a soft bitterness over a floral, earthy hop base. This illustrates the brewery's sensory target; fruit descriptors in that account should not be converted into a claim that those fruits were added.[^modern-hpb-pilsners]

Timbo is a case study, not proof of a sole inventor or a recipe every West Coast pilsner follows. A well-documented individual example is more useful than assigning a speculative universal origin date to a developing label.

## Judging systems differ

BJCP suggests entering West Coast Pilsner in 34B Mixed-Style Beer.[^modern-bjcp-entry-suggestions]

The Brewers Association has a named category. Preserve the system when importing either classification into a database.

## Compare by dimension

This wiki recommends recording the beer's stated base, fermentation information, hop varieties and sensory balance separately. In particular, a hop-forward lager is not automatically [Cold IPA](cold-ipa.md), and the shared words “West Coast” do not make it [West Coast IPA](west-coast-ipa.md). Use the individual source accounts before equating producer labels.

[Italian Pilsner](italian-pilsner.md) and [New Zealand Pilsner](new-zealand-pilsner.md) provide other ways brewers and judging guides connect pilsner to hop aroma. These links are comparison routes, not a proposed evolutionary tree.
