# Shamita: roasted-barley fermented food-drink

Shamita is an Ethiopian fermented cereal drink commonly associated in the literature with roasted barley flour and short fermentation. It can function as a substantial food-drink as well as a beverage; a review describes it in a different social and material context from tella. This matters because a barley ingredient does not make shamita a lager, or even a direct equivalent of a conventional barley ale.[^v18-shamita][^v18-ethiopian-review]

## What a fermentation study tells us

A study published in a 1999 issue of *SINET* investigated shamita by culture-based microbiology. Its journal page was posted online much later. The authors reported lactic-acid bacteria and yeasts increasing through their studied preparation; the pH fell during the roughly one-day observation. Their results belong to studied conditions and sampling methods, not a microbial recipe shared by all households.[^v18-shamita]

## Grain treatment, texture and use

Roasting or other treatment of the barley changes the substrate before fermentation. A thick fermented preparation can be consumed as food or refreshment, which suggests comparison with [Borde](borde-ethiopian-fermented-cereal.md), not only conventional [wheat beer](../categories/wheat-and-rye-beers.md). The yeast, acid-producing organisms, use of retained material and handling of equipment need producer-level description.

## Evidence and safety boundary

The source does not warrant a claimed probiotic benefit, a standard alcohol level, or a one-day guarantee of safety. A reported fall in pH cannot establish absence of every possible pathogen. The broader [fermented-food context](../categories/fermented-cereal-beverages-as-food.md) keeps questions of nourishment and classification distinct.
