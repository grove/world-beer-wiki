# Tapai ketan: fermented glutinous rice as food and drink

*Tapai ketan*, a fermented glutinous-rice food associated with Indonesian ragi starter systems, shows why a world beer encyclopedia needs room for neighboring ferments without renaming them. Depending on stage and local use, a cooked-rice fermentation can be soft, sweet, aromatic or liquid-rich; calling all such products 'rice beer' obscures the food's identity. A Java-based study describes regional tapai forms and ragi's dual saccharifying and fermenting activities.[^v110-ragi-genetics]

## Starch must first become sugar

Cooked glutinous rice is rich in starch rather than the simple sugars most alcoholic yeasts directly ferment. Starter-associated amylolytic organisms release sugars; other organisms can then use them in fermentation. Laboratory work in 1972 demonstrated that a selected pair of isolates could perform a simplified conversion, which is evidence of capability, not a mandatory traditional formulation.[^v110-tape-1972]

## Regional names are not interchangeable

The Java paper distinguishes peyeum, tapai ketan and tapai manis in different contexts. An atlas reference should preserve those source-reported names and not treat a laboratory isolate label as a recipe or a national definition. The Philippine rice ferment tapuy and Indonesian tapai both involve starch conversion, but common chemistry alone says nothing about ancestry or equivalence.[^v110-ragi-genetics]
