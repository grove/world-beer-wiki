# Pre-Prohibition Lager

**Beer Style** · North America · historical reconstruction

Pre-Prohibition Lager is a modern historical reference for fuller, more assertively hopped American lagers associated with brewing before national Prohibition. It is not a description of every American beer made before 1920.[^bjcp-2021-27f]

## Character

The beer combines pale grain or lightly sweet malt with firm bitterness and recognizable hop flavor. Corn-containing and rice-containing interpretations can differ. The overall impression is more substantial than a very light contemporary mass-market lager.[^bjcp-2021-27f]

## Brewing and context

American barley malt, adjunct grains, and lager fermentation reflect the adaptation of European brewing to available ingredients and local conditions. Adjunct use does not by itself mean the beer was flavorless or carelessly made.[^bjcp-2021-27f]

## History and interpretation

Historical breweries varied in recipe, equipment, and market. A modern reconstruction should therefore name its source rather than claim one universal national formula. This category is useful for comparison with American Lager and German Pils, but its historical label is a research frame, not proof that all earlier examples shared identical measurements or taste.[^bjcp-2021-27f]

## Evidence note

This profile paraphrases a modern judging reference. Its flavor description is better supported than any claim of a single origin or an unchanged historical recipe. It is not a complete brewing recipe.
