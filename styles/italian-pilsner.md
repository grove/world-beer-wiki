# Italian Pilsner

Italian Pilsner is a modern pilsner interpretation associated with dry hopping and a fragrant hop profile. Birrificio Italiano identifies its Tipopils as a foundational example, dating that beer to 1996. The brewery's claim to the first dry-hopped pilsner is an attributed origin claim, not an independently established global first.[^modern-tipopils]

## Character and brewing context

Tipopils is described by its producer as a bottom-fermented, 5.2% ABV beer with herbal and cereal character. The account connects a familiar pilsner base with aromatic hopping. It documents one beer; the name “Italian Pilsner” should not be reduced to the exact strength or recipe of Tipopils.[^modern-tipopils]

The Brewers Association's 2026 description emphasizes late and dry hopping with noble-type hops. Its separate West Coast Pilsener description instead emphasizes modern IPA-like hop expression.[^modern-ba-2026]

Highland Park Brewery provides a useful contemporary comparison: its Pleasant Pils is explicitly called an Italian-style pilsner, using Michigan-grown Zuper Saazer and German-grown Hallertau Saphir. This is direct evidence of the name being used outside Italy, rather than a geographical guarantee about where a beer was brewed.[^modern-hpb-pilsners]

## Classification and adjacent names

BJCP suggests 34B Mixed-Style Beer for Italian/Hoppy Pilsner: German Pils plus dry hopping.[^modern-bjcp-entry-suggestions]

That entry mechanism should remain distinct from the Brewers Association's named category.

For a catalogue, record the chosen guide and edition; for a tasting comparison, record hop character, bitterness and finish. Neither “Italian” nor “dry hopped” alone identifies a complete recipe. Follow [German Pils](german-pils.md), [West Coast Pilsner](west-coast-pilsner.md) and [New Zealand Pilsner](new-zealand-pilsner.md) to compare the neighboring reference points without treating them as interchangeable.
