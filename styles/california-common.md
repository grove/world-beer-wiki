# California Common

**Beer Style** · North America · living

California Common is an amber, firmly bitter beer associated with lager yeast fermented warmer than in conventional cold-lager production. Toasty malt and distinctive herbal or woody hops give it its familiar modern profile.[^bjcp-2021-19b]

## Character

The beer can taste of toasted bread, light caramel, and rustic herbal hops. A little fruitiness is possible, but the finish should be reasonably dry and crisp rather than heavy or sweet.[^bjcp-2021-19b]

## Brewing and context

The defining idea is not simply “any warm-fermented lager.” Yeast selection, fermentation conditions, malt balance, and hop character all contribute. A neutral lager brewed warm with an entirely different recipe may taste unlike this reference style.[^bjcp-2021-19b]

## History and interpretation

The modern guideline is strongly shaped by the twentieth-century Anchor interpretation of steam beer. It should not be projected unchanged onto every nineteenth-century California brewery. “Steam beer” also has a complicated commercial naming history. This article describes the modern sensory reference, not a complete reconstruction of all Gold Rush brewing practices.[^bjcp-2021-19b]
