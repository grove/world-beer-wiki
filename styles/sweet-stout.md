# Sweet Stout

**Beer Style** · Europe · contemporary

Sweet stout is a dark beer in which sweetness softens and balances roasted-malt flavors. The familiar milk-stout interpretation often uses lactose, a milk-derived sugar that ordinary brewing yeast does not readily ferment.[^bjcp-2021-16a]

## Character

The flavor can suggest sweetened coffee or chocolate, with a fuller texture and lower apparent bitterness than dry stout. It should still taste like beer rather than simply like sugar syrup.[^bjcp-2021-16a]

## Brewing and context

Roasted grains provide the dark character. Lactose is common but not a requirement for every beer in the broader sweet-stout family; recipe and fermentation choices can also affect residual sweetness.[^bjcp-2021-16a]

## History and interpretation

The style belongs to the modern history of sweetened stout and related marketing traditions. Historical promotional claims about nourishment or restorative effects are not evidence of health benefits. “Milk stout” does not mean that ordinary liquid milk is necessarily added, but ingredient information remains important. Do not infer the complete recipe from a flavor description or from the words “cream” and “sweet.”[^bjcp-2021-16a]
