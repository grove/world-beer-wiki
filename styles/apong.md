# Apong — Mising rice-beer traditions

**Beer Tradition** · Assam, India · living tradition

Apong names rice-based drinks associated here with Mising communities of Assam. The cited fieldwork distinguishes **Poro Apong**, which incorporates ash from rice bran, from **Nogin Apong**, which does not use that addition. These are local process distinctions within a living tradition, rather than internationally fixed beer styles.[^trad-apong-epop]

## Ingredients and the starter

The starter, **ÉPOP**, is a preparation of rice and plant material maintained through inherited practice. Mili and Sundriyal documented 31 plant species across their survey, with experienced women contributing knowledge of collection and preparation. Older starter is used to seed newly formed cakes. A list recorded across households is not a requirement to put every listed plant into one cake.[^trad-apong-epop]

## Character and variation

The sources support differentiation by ingredients and processing more firmly than a universal tasting profile. A 2019 study examined Apong alongside Ahom Xaaj and Bodo Joubishi; those other names are **not aliases for Apong**. Its 27 beverage samples were pooled into nine location-based composites. Reported alcohol values therefore describe particular sampled preparations, not a specification for what every household must serve.[^trad-assam-microbiota]

## Culture and interpretation

The Sivasagar field account describes sharing starter among families and offering the drink at social and ritual occasions. Preserving the tradition involves the makers' knowledge and access to plants as well as the liquid itself.[^trad-apong-epop]

The laboratory paper used bacterial sequencing and chemical analyses. Neither predicted microbial functions nor laboratory antioxidant measurements demonstrate a clinical benefit from drinking apong. This wiki includes them as evidence about a fermentation system, without turning the paper's proposed health applications into established facts.[^trad-assam-microbiota]

## Evidence note

This profile is restricted to the named communities and research locations. It does not claim a single first-brewing date, a mandatory strength, or a standardized herbal recipe. The field study primarily documents starter knowledge; the laboratory study asks different questions about selected beverage samples.

## Research extension 1.3: Calendar and named Mising spring festival

The Government of India describes **Ali-Aye-Ligang** as a Mising agricultural festival beginning with the spring sowing cycle. This locates Apong in one public cultural setting, but does not mean every producer uses a festival-only recipe or that every participant consumes it. See [the festival calendar](../calendar/ali-aye-ligang.md) and [Mising starter knowledge](../culture/mising-starter-knowledge.md).[^v13-assam-festival]
