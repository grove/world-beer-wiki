# Tapuy: a Philippine rice-fermented beverage

*Tapuy* is a Philippine rice-fermented drink commonly called rice wine in English-language research. It belongs in this atlas as a **related cereal ferment**: rice starch is made available to alcoholic yeasts through a mixed starter traditionally called *bubod*. This is a distinct local beverage, not a generic category for every Southeast Asian rice beer.[^v17-bubod-1990]

## A starter can do more than one job

In a 1990 study of sampled bubod and tapuy material, Kozaki and Uchimura isolated moulds, acid-producing bacteria and yeasts. Their tests attributed especially strong starch-breaking activity to *Saccharomycopsis fibuligera* isolates, and an alcohol-producing role to *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*. These functions can overlap temporally rather than following a completely separate industrial malting step.[^v17-bubod-1990]

## Variability is part of the research question

A 2025 paper studying tapuy lees notes that traditional bubod can contain variable microorganisms that affect the fermented product and residue. This makes starter provenance and processing conditions important, while warning against one fixed fermentation organism list or sensory specification for all tapuy. One study of residue composition also cannot justify a clinical anti-aging claim from a title.[^v17-bubod-2025]

## Classification and community scope

This page neither relabels tapuy as lager nor assumes that the Philippine term has a single meaning across every language and producer. Living practice, microbiology and published laboratory isolates answer different questions. Further community-authored Filipino sources would improve discussion of celebrations, ownership of knowledge and local serving etiquette.
