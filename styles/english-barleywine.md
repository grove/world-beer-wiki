# English Barleywine

**Beer Style** · Europe · living

English barleywine is a very strong ale with concentrated malt flavor and a substantial body. The word “wine” refers to its strength and sipping character, not to grapes being a required ingredient.[^bjcp-2021-17d]

## Character

Paler examples may suggest bread, biscuit, and toffee; darker ones can add caramel and dried-fruit notes. Hops range from supportive to quite assertive, and their effect often softens with age.[^bjcp-2021-17d]

## Brewing and context

A strong malt-based wort is fermented with ale yeast. The result may be rounded and sweet-seeming, but it should not taste unfinished or syrupy. Maturation can bring wine-like or nutty complexity, though not every bottle benefits from indefinite storage.[^bjcp-2021-17d]

## History and interpretation

The family has varied in color and balance over time. Modern English barleywine should be distinguished from more hop-forward American interpretations and from Old Ale, where the impression of aging is more central to the definition. A label alone does not establish how long a beer was matured or how an earlier beer with the same name tasted.[^bjcp-2021-17d]
