# Caxiri

**Related Starch-Fermentation Tradition** · South America / Brazil / Amazonia · living tradition

Caxiri is an Indigenous Brazilian fermented-beverage tradition. The preparation examined in the cited study combined cassava, maize, and sweet potato. This makes it a useful example of brewing with several starch sources, rather than a variation of European barley beer.[^caxiri-study]

## Character

The studied drink contained both ethanol and substantial lactic acid. Those measurements support describing that preparation as alcoholic and acid-producing, but they do not establish a universal strength or a complete flavor profile. Its aroma was chemically complex; exact tasting notes need direct sensory evidence.[^caxiri-study]

## Brewing and context

The researchers detected brewing yeast alongside lactic-acid bacteria and other microorganisms. Fermentation therefore involved a community of organisms, not simply one laboratory yeast strain. The available abstract does not provide enough detail for a dependable production recipe.[^caxiri-study]

## History and interpretation

This entry preserves caxiri as its own named tradition. The ingredient combination and laboratory results belong to the studied preparation, not automatically to every Indigenous community or drink with a similar name. Further community-specific research would be needed to describe its full regional range.[^caxiri-study]

## Evidence note

The source abstract was consulted, not the full methods. Ingredient and microbiological statements are limited to the studied preparation; the entry does not invent a tasting profile or production recipe.
