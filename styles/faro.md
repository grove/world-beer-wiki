# Faro

Faro is a sweetened expression of the lambic family associated with Brussels. A current producer example is Lindemans Faro-Lambic, described as young lambic combined with brown candied sugar. That provides direct evidence for one commercial interpretation, not a timeless specification for every Faro.[^wiki-lindemans-faro]

## Character and ingredients

In that example, sweetness and an acidic base coexist, with amber-brown color and sugar-associated aromas. The reported product strength belongs to that product, so this article does not turn it into a universal style range. Sugar here has a different stated purpose from assuming every added sugar will ferment completely dry.[^wiki-lindemans-faro]

Read [sugars](../ingredients/sugars.md) alongside [Lambic](lambic.md): the ingredient and the base tradition answer separate questions. A sweet beer need not be Faro, and a lambic-family beer need not be dry in every commercial interpretation.

## Place in the lambic family

HORAL treats Faro among the named lambic-related products in its account of the tradition. Keep it separate from [Gueuze](gueuze.md), whose defining comparison is blending and bottle conditioning, and [Fruit Lambic](fruit-lambic.md), where the fruit is central to the profile.[^wiki-horal-tradition]

## History and evidence limits

Lindemans dates its own Faro offering to 1978. That is a producer's product history, not the invention date of Faro. Its promotional historical narrative is not used here to establish an origin in a particular century.[^wiki-lindemans-faro]

This entry is intentionally narrower than a full historical monograph. Earlier blending, sweetening and serving practices require dated records before they can be made universal. Neither the producer source nor a category name establishes that every current example is prepared, stabilized or packaged in the same way.

For comparative questions, ask which producer and period are meant, then examine the base beer, sweetening and service separately.
