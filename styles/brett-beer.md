# Brett beer

**Specialty Beer Category** · International · modern category

Brett beer is a broad category defined by fermentation with Brettanomyces yeast, either alone or alongside conventional brewing yeast. It is not one fixed color or strength. The original beer and the particular fermentation together shape the result.[^bjcp-2021-28a]

## Character

It may be fruity, earthy, lightly smoky, or reminiscent of hay. Younger examples can emphasize fruit, while more aged examples may develop stronger earthy notes. The beer often finishes drier than its starting style would suggest; excessive unpleasant aromas are not the goal.[^bjcp-2021-28a]

## Brewing and context

The yeast can alter the beer during fermentation and maturation. Wood aging is possible but not essential. Importantly, using Brettanomyces does not automatically make a beer sharply sour or prove that it fermented spontaneously.[^bjcp-2021-28a]

## History and interpretation

BJCP treats this as a fermentation-defined specialty category. That makes it useful for comparing experiments, but it should not replace the identities of named traditions that also involve complex fermentation. The base beer and the brewer's intent remain important context.[^bjcp-2021-28a]
