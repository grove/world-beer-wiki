# Baltic Porter

**Beer Style** · Europe · living

Baltic porter is a strong, dark beer family associated with countries around the Baltic Sea and neighboring regions. It combines a smooth fermentation character with rich malt, dried-fruit notes, and restrained roast.[^bjcp-2021-9c]

## Character

The flavor can suggest chocolate, toast, caramel, raisins, or plums. It is usually less sharply burnt than an aggressively roasted imperial stout. Strength and fullness are important, but the finish should not be sticky or harsh.[^bjcp-2021-9c]

## Brewing and context

Many modern examples use lager fermentation, although the wider historical family includes other practices. Dark malts contribute color and flavor without necessarily dominating the whole beer with roast.[^bjcp-2021-9c]

## History and interpretation

The tradition is connected to the international movement and local adaptation of porter. It is not one identical recipe shared by every Baltic country. Nor should “porter” be taken to mean that every example uses ale yeast. When comparing historical and modern beers, identify the country, brewery, period, and fermentation method rather than relying on the family name alone.[^bjcp-2021-9c]
