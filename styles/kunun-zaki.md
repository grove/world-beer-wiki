# Kunun-zaki

*Kunun-zaki* is a Nigerian cereal drink commonly associated with millet, though research also describes sorghum and maize versions. It is generally presented as a **non-alcoholic or low-alcohol food-drink**, not a standardized beer style. A 2015 fortification study describes millet processing, spices and a short fermentation; its experimental recipe is not a universal Nigerian household formula.[^v111-kunun-study]

## Its process differs from malt beer

The studied method included steeping, wet milling, partial gelatinization, blending of portions, fermentation, sieving and sweetening. A much earlier microbiological paper describes changes during millet steeping and fermentation in its own sampled system, with lactic acid bacteria becoming prominent and yeast also noted. Study-specific microbiology does not certify safe consumption or establish a minimum ethanol level.[^v111-kunun-study][^v111-kunun-cultures]

## Why it belongs in this atlas

A drink can be culturally important and microbiologically complex without fitting a beer judge's categories. Kunun-zaki helps readers distinguish fermented grain food, lightly fermented beverage and alcoholic beer without creating a hierarchy. The 2015 paper also mentions dry-season use in background discussion; that is a reported seasonal association, not an instruction to put a recurring festival date on a calendar.[^v111-kunun-study]
