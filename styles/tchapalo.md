# Tchapalo

**Beer Tradition** · West Africa / Côte d’Ivoire · living tradition

Tchapalo is a traditional sorghum beer of Côte d’Ivoire. Its identity belongs to a regional brewing practice, not to every commercial beer that happens to include sorghum.[^tchapalo-research]

## Character

The cited research places tchapalo among opaque sorghum beers. It investigates how different fermentation cultures alter the resulting drink. That evidence is stronger for the role of fermentation than for any claim that all tchapalo has one exact aroma or alcohol strength.[^tchapalo-research]

## Brewing and context

The study compared beer fermented with a selected Saccharomyces cerevisiae culture against beer made with a mixed, traditionally obtained yeast culture. The resulting differences show that choosing and maintaining fermentation cultures can affect the beer, even when its grain base stays similar.[^tchapalo-research]

## History and interpretation

The paper concentrates on chemical compounds, not a complete regional history or a consumer tasting standard. It should not be used to promise medical benefits from drinking tchapalo. This wiki retains the local name and separates it from neighboring dolo and pito instead of treating them as interchangeable labels.[^tchapalo-research]

## Research extension 1.5: Selected microbes in an Abidjan study

Researchers isolated non-*Saccharomyces* yeasts from traditional sorghum beer in the Abidjan district of Côte d’Ivoire and tested fermentation-related traits. The paper reports identified isolates, not a definitive starter for every producer or a claim of historical invention by a microbial species.[^v15-cotedivoire-2022]
