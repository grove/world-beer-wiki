# American Barleywine

**Beer Style** · North America · contemporary

American Barleywine is a very strong, malt-rich ale with an especially assertive hop contribution. It shares barleywine's concentrated depth while often finishing more bitter than the English reference.[^bjcp-2021-22c]

## Character

Bread, caramel, toffee, and sometimes dried fruit meet citrus, pine, or resinous hops. A young example can seem particularly hop-forward; maturation may soften that edge and change the balance. The body remains substantial.[^bjcp-2021-22c]

## Brewing and context

A concentrated malt-based wort supplies both strength and flavor. Fermentation must handle that richness without leaving an unfinished, syrupy impression. Wood or spirits-barrel aging is possible but not required.[^bjcp-2021-22c]

## History and interpretation

The difference from Double IPA is not simply a higher alcohol number. Barleywine normally retains more malt depth and body, while Double IPA aims for a leaner hop-led finish. Aging potential also varies by beer and storage conditions; a strong label is not a promise of indefinite improvement. This entry describes the modern American interpretation, not all historical barley wines.[^bjcp-2021-22c]
