# Sake

**Related Fermented-Grain Tradition** · East Asia / Japan · living tradition

Sake is a Japanese rice-brewing tradition. It belongs in this wiki as a neighboring grain-fermented beverage, not because calling it “rice wine” or “rice beer” captures its full identity. This introductory entry focuses on the starch-conversion method described by a working brewery.[^sake-producer]

## Character

The cited producer account is about making koji, not a tasting survey. It is therefore insufficient to assign all sake a single sweetness, aroma, or texture. This file deliberately avoids treating one brewery's production explanation as a definition of every sake category.[^sake-producer]

## Brewing and context

Rice is steamed and some is inoculated with koji mold under controlled conditions. The mold supplies the means to turn rice starch into sugars that yeast can ferment. That is different from relying on enzymes developed by germinating barley for conventional malt beer.[^sake-producer]

## History and interpretation

The brewery describes careful cooling, temperature monitoring, and handling of the rice. These are observations from contemporary production, not evidence for an unchanged ancient recipe. A fuller taxonomy of sake would need separate research into production categories and terminology; it is not hidden inside this beer-style overview.[^sake-producer]
