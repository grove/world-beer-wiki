# Pulque

Pulque is produced from **aguamiel**, the fresh sap collected from certain maguey agaves, rather than from mashed malt. The documented process commonly adds fresh sap to an existing ferment, creating a mixed ecological system rather than one purified yeast culture. For that reason pulque belongs here as a related ferment, **not** as a BJCP-style beer or a generic category for every agave beverage.[^v111-pulque-review]

## What fermentation changes

A review describes several contributors: yeasts and *Zymomonas* can participate in alcoholic fermentation, lactic acid bacteria contribute acidity, and some bacteria synthesize extracellular polymers associated with pulque's characteristic viscosity. Which microbes predominate depends on the sampled fermentation and the stages involved; the role of a specific species should not be assumed from a beverage name.[^v111-pulque-review]

In a five-stage shotgun-sequencing study, reported relative abundances shifted as sucrose fell and ethanol and lactic acid rose. Metagenomic co-occurrence describes association, not proof that every detected organism caused a measured chemical change. The five stages are not five compulsory steps for every household or maker.[^v111-pulque-stages]

## Why it deserves a cultural entry

Historical descriptions of agave use and contemporary sale describe a social beverage as well as an ecosystem. Commercialization, farming and local terminology vary. Compare the separately documented histories of ritual and taxation before treating the word *pulque* as an unchanging precolonial recipe. Neither the review's suggested nutritional benefits nor a detected bacterium constitutes medical advice or a safety certification.[^v111-pulque-review]
