# Merissa — Sudanese sorghum brewing

**Beer Tradition** · Sudan · documented in twentieth-century research

Merissa is a Sudanese sorghum-fermentation tradition documented by Hamid A. Dirar. His studies are valuable dated accounts of brewing practice, not evidence that every present-day maker follows a national standard.[^trad-merissa-process][^trad-merissa-context]

## Three linked fermentations

Dirar's 1978 account separates **ajeen**, a lactic souring stage, **deboba**, an active starter-building stage, and the final merissa fermentation. Malted and unmalted sorghum have different roles. Cooked preparations are combined with malt and previously fermented material before the finished drink is strained. The final two phases are alcoholic fermentations; this is more informative than describing the drink merely as “spontaneous beer.”[^trad-merissa-process]

## Character and service

The account describes a sour, caramel-like cooked intermediate and continuing fermentation after straining. Those observations should not be converted into a detailed tasting specification for the final drink. Production stage and time since preparation affect what is being measured or tasted.[^trad-merissa-process]

## Historical and cultural context

Dirar's earlier 1976 paper treats merissa as a food-beverage embedded in Sudanese social life and discusses brewing, social questions, and scientific analysis together. Only that paper's bibliographic abstract was available here. It supports a broader cultural framing, but not reproduction of its detailed arguments or uninspected legal history.[^trad-merissa-context]
