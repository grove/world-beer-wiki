# Black IPA

**Beer Style** · North America · contemporary

Black IPA brings a dry, hop-led IPA balance into a dark-colored beer. The dark malt should add a restrained supporting character rather than turn the beer into a strongly roasted stout.[^bjcp-2021-21b-black]

## Character

Citrus, pine, resin, or fruit-like hops lead, with gentle cocoa, coffee, or toast behind them. Bitterness is firm, but ash, burnt harshness, and a thick sweet body are not central to the intended impression.[^bjcp-2021-21b-black]

## Brewing and context

Brewers often use darker malts selected to provide color without overwhelming roast. A relatively clean fermentation and sufficiently dry finish keep the beer recognizably IPA-like. Dark appearance alone does not establish the style.[^bjcp-2021-21b-black]

## History and interpretation

This is a modern craft-beer variant with several regional naming traditions. “Cascadian dark ale” overlaps with it but can carry its own local identity. The useful comparison with American Porter or Stout concerns balance: Black IPA gives hops and dryness the leading role, whereas the darker malt is deliberately kept more restrained.[^bjcp-2021-21b-black]
