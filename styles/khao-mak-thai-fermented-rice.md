# Khao mak: Thai sweet fermented rice

*Khao mak* appears in an indexed overview of Southeast Asian fermented foods as a sweet, lightly alcoholic rice preparation associated with *lookpang* starters. The source distinguishes it from drinks reported under other Thai names and from rice distillates. In the atlas it belongs among related rice ferments, not automatically in the same class as malt beer or a single standardized Thai style.[^v110-asia-review]

## Starter is a category, not one strain

Traditional Asian dry starters can contain multiple microbial functions: enzymes that release sugars from starch, yeasts that ferment those sugars, and additional organisms affected by preparation. A separate laboratory study purchased loog-pang in Nan province and used it to investigate fermentation of rice-bran material. Its measured mixed culture describes that purchased research sample, not every lookpang used in Thai households.[^v110-loogpang]

## Why boundaries help readers

A method that saccharifies rice, a sweeter food-like result, and a product distilled after fermentation answer distinct questions. The names khao mak, sato and other rice products should be treated according to the exact source and process, not arranged as stages of an invented universal recipe. Compare Indonesian tapai and Philippine tapuy as neighbors, not synonyms.[^v110-asia-review]

## Evidence and limitations

Khao mak coverage currently relies on a secondary review table and a separate loog-pang laboratory study. Local-language practitioner sources are a research priority; no assertions about safe homemade starter selection are made. These limitations should be retained when quoting the article or extracting relationships.
