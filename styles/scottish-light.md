# Scottish Light

**Beer Style** · Europe · living

Scottish Light is the lowest-strength member of the modern Scottish ale reference family. It is malt-led, softly textured, and restrained in hops, with enough flavor to avoid seeming diluted.[^bjcp-2021-14a]

## Character

The malt can suggest bread, light caramel, and a small roasted-grain edge. The finish may be gently sweet or relatively dry. Strong smoke and peat are not defining traditional features.[^bjcp-2021-14a]

## Brewing and context

Pale malt, small quantities of darker grains, and sometimes brewing sugars can create the profile. The category is not built around whisky ingredients or a requirement for peat-smoked malt.[^bjcp-2021-14a]

## History and interpretation

Shilling names are useful historical labels, but their relationship to strength changed over time. The modern light-heavy-export ladder should not be treated as a fixed system extending unchanged into the distant past. “Light” here refers to the place in that family, not necessarily a pale color, a dietary claim, or an alcohol-free product.[^bjcp-2021-14a]

## Evidence note

This profile paraphrases a modern judging reference. Its flavor description is better supported than any claim of a single origin or an unchanged historical recipe. It is not a complete brewing recipe.

## Edition 1.17: newly sourced connections

A Scottish beer-style name does not specify which barley landrace a producer used. Martin and colleagues' Bere review describes historical and current uses of an island-associated grain, including malting, food, feed and distilling. This is agricultural and historical source evidence, not a claim that every Scottish Light recipe uses Bere or that a current judging guideline preserves one old malt bill.[^v117-bere-review]

A 2025 hot-steep malt sensory paper tested selected heritage and modern spring barley malts under a small-scale design. The named varieties in that abstract are not Bere, and malt-extract sensory comparisons cannot establish the flavor of a finished Scottish Light beer without a separately tested brew.[^v117-hot-steep]

Distinguish the modern [style profile](scottish-light.md) from [Bere as a crop](../ingredients/bere-six-row-barley.md), [malt sensory evidence](../sensory/malt-hot-steep-experiments.md) and actual producer-specific recipes. Neither an ingredient nor a regional label provides a universal historical lineage.
