# Boza — Turkish cereal-fermentation tradition

**Related Fermented-Grain Tradition** · West Asia / Türkiye · living tradition

Boza is a fermented cereal beverage. This entry focuses on the Turkish preparation examined in the cited research, not every regional drink bearing the name. It is included as a neighboring grain-fermentation tradition rather than forced into a modern beer category.[^boza-study]

## Character

The study records rising acidity and a small amount of alcohol during fermentation. The important contrast is with a strongly alcoholic, hop-led beer: here the cooked cereal mixture and acid-producing fermentation are central. The measured alcohol content belongs to that experiment, not a universal boza specification.[^boza-study]

## Brewing and context

The researchers used cooked maize, wheat, and rice flours, then followed fermentation involving yeasts and lactic-acid bacteria. A previous boza preparation supplied the starter. The grain combination in this experiment should not be treated as the only authentic recipe.[^boza-study]

## History and interpretation

This is useful evidence for how one Turkish boza process works, but it does not settle the beverage's complete history or regional taxonomy. A fuller Balkan and West Asian comparison would require additional local sources rather than extending this study beyond its scope.[^boza-study]

## Evidence note

The description is limited to the sources below. A local observation or laboratory sample does not establish one recipe, strength, or flavor for every community or period.

## Edition 1.12: newly sourced connections

Turkish research on a **particular 24-hour experimental boza** reported an ethanol measurement of 0.79%, but this measured batch does not establish a universal limit for all Turkish or Balkan boza.[^v112-boza-1997] A broader review records different cereal substrates and processes; one drink name cannot certify its alcohol level, microflora, or food-safety properties.[^v112-boza-review] A Bulgarian genomic study isolated selected bacteriocinogenic lactic-acid bacteria from **one boza sample**. Antimicrobial assays performed on isolates are not evidence of medicinal benefit from drinking all boza.[^v112-boza-2022]
