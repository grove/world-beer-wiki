# Kodo ko jaanr

**Beer Tradition** · South Asia / India / Nepal / Bhutan · living tradition

Kodo ko jaanr is a fermented finger-millet preparation of the eastern Himalayas. It is closely associated with the tongba way of drinking: fermented grain is placed in a vessel, water is added, and the liquid is sipped through a narrow straw.[^kodo-research]

## Character

The cited study describes good examples as sweet with a mild alcoholic flavor. Water draws a cloudy liquid from the fermented grain. The vessel can be topped up, so the strength of the drink cannot be read directly from a laboratory measurement of the undiluted grain preparation.[^kodo-research]

## Brewing and context

Cooked finger millet is cooled and mixed with marcha, a traditional fermentation starter. Starch conversion and fermentation develop before the grain is served. The grain is Eleusine coracana; the word kodo here must not be used to substitute a different millet species without checking local usage.[^kodo-research]

## History and interpretation

Researchers examined samples from Darjeeling and Sikkim and described a wider Himalayan context. Tongba names the vessel and associated serving practice, not a separate universal recipe. This wiki therefore keeps them together rather than manufacturing two independent beer styles from the same evidence.[^kodo-research]
