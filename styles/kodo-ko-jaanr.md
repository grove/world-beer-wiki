# Kodo ko jaanr

**Beer Tradition** · South Asia / India / Nepal / Bhutan · living tradition

Kodo ko jaanr is a fermented finger-millet preparation of the eastern Himalayas. It is closely associated with the tongba way of drinking: fermented grain is placed in a vessel, water is added, and the liquid is sipped through a narrow straw.[^kodo-research]

## Character

The cited study describes good examples as sweet with a mild alcoholic flavor. Water draws a cloudy liquid from the fermented grain. The vessel can be topped up, so the strength of the drink cannot be read directly from a laboratory measurement of the undiluted grain preparation.[^kodo-research]

## Brewing and context

Cooked finger millet is cooled and mixed with marcha, a traditional fermentation starter. Starch conversion and fermentation develop before the grain is served. The grain is Eleusine coracana; the word kodo here must not be used to substitute a different millet species without checking local usage.[^kodo-research]

## History and interpretation

Researchers examined samples from Darjeeling and Sikkim and described a wider Himalayan context. Tongba names the vessel and associated serving practice, not a separate universal recipe. This wiki therefore keeps them together rather than manufacturing two independent beer styles from the same evidence.[^kodo-research]

## Changing organisms, changing product

A later study followed fermentation over time. Mould populations declined while yeast and lactic acid bacterial populations changed at different rates; acidity and alcohol increased. The authors associated starch conversion with *Saccharomycopsis fibuligera* and *Rhizopus* species. The useful lesson is microbial succession, not a fixed list of organisms supposedly present at identical levels in every serving.[^trad-kodo-dynamics]

The study also tested selected organism combinations under laboratory conditions. A successful experimental culture demonstrates a possible production method; it does not prove that household marcha everywhere has that same composition.[^trad-kodo-dynamics]

## Record grain, ferment, and serving separately

For a clear catalogue entry, retain three layers: the finger-millet ingredient, the fermented preparation, and the tongba/toongbaa serving vessel or practice. Keep the sampling stage attached to a laboratory result. A strength measured before serving is not automatically the strength of the first or later water extraction. See [Chhang](chhang.md) for a related comparison, without treating the names as interchangeable.

## Evidence note

The 2004 product study supports the grain and serving description. The added 2006 study was available only as a publisher abstract and is used for its reported fermentation changes, not for an uninspected detailed protocol. Neither source defines all Himalayan millet beverages.

## Research extension 1.5: A fermentation ecosystem rather than a single organism

Eastern Himalayan starter research identifies marcha as a mixed, dried cereal-flour culture associated with finger-millet beverages, including kodo ko jaanr. Saccharification and alcoholic fermentation can involve different microbes; tests of one starter do not define every makers' method.[^v15-himalaya-2019]

The [starter-cake process](../processes/starter-cakes-and-starch-conversion.md) and [local knowledge guide](../culture/marcha-and-himalayan-starter-knowledge.md) help keep preparation, microbiology and cultural practice distinct.

## Edition 1.8: newly sourced connections

A 2006 study by Thapa and Tamang measured changing microbial populations and chemistry during **finger-millet** fermentation in the Darjeeling hills and Sikkim. Their abstract reports changing yeasts, lactic-acid bacteria and mould counts, and links saccharification to starch-active organisms such as *Saccharomycopsis fibuligera* and *Rhizopus*. A sampled trajectory is not a universal culture composition.[^v18-jaanr]

The paper also describes laboratory reconstruction with selected strains. Its acceptability result cannot certify that one isolate mixture is the true traditional starter for every maker. The full paper was not independently reviewed for this edition, so its indexed abstract remains the claim limit.[^v18-jaanr]
