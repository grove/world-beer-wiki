# Session IPA

Session IPA combines an IPA identity with a lower-strength designation. BJCP 2021 treats it through 21B Specialty IPA, specifies an American IPA base for the named example, and defines its session-strength band as 3.0–5.0% ABV. That is a competition definition, not a universal definition of the word “session.”[^modern-bjcp-specialty-ipa]

## Character and a producer example

Founders' All Day IPA is listed at 4.7% ABV and 42 IBU on the brewery's page consulted for this edition. The producer describes a citrus-led profile with floral and tropical notes, moderate bitterness and a clean finish, and lists several hop varieties alongside wheat malt, flaked maize and oats. These details illustrate one design rather than an ingredient rule for the family.[^modern-founders-allday]

The useful comparison is balance at the stated strength. Merely lowering a beer's alcohol does not explain its aroma, sweetness, body or bitterness. When comparing two session IPAs, keep those observations separate from the number printed on the label.

## Category boundaries

BJCP permits strength-qualified versions of otherwise defined IPAs through the specialty mechanism, while its Double IPA already has a dedicated category. The system therefore mixes style identity and entry administration; a reader should retain both the base style and strength modifier.[^modern-bjcp-specialty-ipa]

For this wiki, “Session IPA” is a useful searchable profile, not a new claim that all lower-strength hoppy beers share one origin. Read [American Pale Ale](american-pale-ale.md) and [British Golden Ale](british-golden-ale.md) alongside it when comparing neighboring beer descriptions. Do not replace a producer's named style solely because alcohol ranges overlap.

## Evidence note

A “session” label should not be substituted for [low-alcohol or non-alcoholic](../categories/low-and-no-alcohol-beers.md) labeling information. Preserve the actual ABV and the classification system. The Founders figures are a dated product example and may change with formulation or market.

## Edition 1.15: newly sourced connections

Session IPA describes a drinking-strength and sensory intention within a modern style vocabulary; it is not the same as a regulatory claim of “low alcohol” or “non-alcoholic.” In US TTB guidance for federally regulated malt beverages, “low alcohol” has a specific below-2.5% ABV labeling threshold, while “non-alcoholic” needs an adjacent less-than-0.5% disclosure. Neither designation follows from the word *session* on a judging profile.[^v115-alcohol]

The BJCP introduction explains that style categories organize comparisons for judging, not label legality or evidence of historical descent. A product calling itself Session IPA therefore needs an independently observed or declared ABV and the appropriate market-specific label before a catalog can say what its legal alcohol status is.[^v115-bjcp]
