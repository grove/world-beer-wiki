# Pre-Prohibition Porter

**Beer Style** · North America · historical reconstruction

Pre-Prohibition Porter describes a historical American porter interpretation associated especially with the eastern United States. It reflects local adaptation of dark-beer traditions rather than an unchanged English recipe.[^bjcp-2021-27g]

## Character

The modern reference combines dark malt, restrained roast, and a relatively approachable body. Caramel, chocolate, or grain character may appear. It should not automatically be expected to resemble a very strong modern American Porter.[^bjcp-2021-27g]

## Brewing and context

Historical versions could use adjuncts and different fermentation practices, including lager fermentation. Ingredients used to supply color and flavor varied among breweries. Those differences are part of the subject rather than deviations from one pure recipe.[^bjcp-2021-27g]

## History and interpretation

The name does not mean “all colonial American porter.” The guideline focuses on a later historical family, including adaptations within nineteenth-century American brewing. Treat any revival as an interpretation unless it is tied to a specific surviving record. A beer's name and dark color alone cannot establish its yeast, ingredients, strength, or ancestry.[^bjcp-2021-27g]

## Evidence note

This profile paraphrases a modern judging reference. Its flavor description is better supported than any claim of a single origin or an unchanged historical recipe. It is not a complete brewing recipe.
