# West Coast IPA

West Coast IPA names a dry, bitter American IPA interpretation. BJCP's entry guidance places it within 21A American IPA.[^modern-bjcp-entry-suggestions]

## A documented example

Stone describes its flagship IPA as a West Coast interpretation, dates that beer to its first-anniversary brewing in 1997, and identifies citrus, tropical-fruit and pine aromas over restrained malt. Its current product page also says that the brewery has increased dry hopping over time. These statements document one producer's beer and its evolution; they do not establish who invented West Coast IPA.[^modern-stone-ipa]

That example is useful because it makes a rigid “old pine versus new fruit” division unhelpful. A named beer can retain its West Coast identity while its hopping changes. Record the brewery, product version and date when using a commercial example to characterize a period.

## How to compare it

Use [American IPA](american-ipa.md) for the broader reference and [Hazy IPA](hazy-ipa.md) for a different emphasis on body and perceived bitterness. For an individual beer, ask three separate questions: how dry is the finish, how prominent is the bitterness, and which hop aromas are evident? This is the wiki's comparison method, not a claim that every producer uses identical boundaries.

“West Coast” also appears on [pilsners](west-coast-pilsner.md), so retain the complete style name in a catalogue. A shared modifier is insufficient to merge two records. Likewise, [Cold IPA](cold-ipa.md) has a producer-defined process identity that should be recorded separately from resemblance in the glass.

## Evidence note

The guideline mapping and Stone's product account answer different questions: where a judge might enter a beer, and how a brewery presents its own beer. Neither source is a census of West Coast brewing. No independent first-invention claim or universal alcohol limit is asserted here.
