# Burukutu

**Beer Tradition** · West Africa / Ghana · living tradition

Burukutu is a traditional sorghum beer documented in Ghana. The cited field study distinguishes it from pito, which was more commonly encountered in that setting. Their similar grain base is not a reason to erase the separate names.[^burukutu-research]

## Character

Souring is an intentional part of production. Grain character, acidity, and alcoholic fermentation should therefore be understood together. The study does not provide enough broad sensory evidence to assign every burukutu a precise aroma, color, or strength.[^burukutu-research]

## Brewing and context

The documented sequence includes malting, mashing, souring, and alcoholic fermentation. Researchers sampled actual production sites and identified several types of lactic acid bacteria during the souring stage. The beer is not adequately explained as a single commercial yeast fermenting unsoured wort.[^burukutu-research]

## History and interpretation

This profile is deliberately tied to the Ghanaian evidence. It does not claim that the same method applies to every community using the name elsewhere. Laboratory microbial counts and measurements from sampled batches are observations, not mandatory style specifications or guarantees of safety.[^burukutu-research]

## Evidence note

The description is limited to the sources below. A local observation or laboratory sample does not establish one recipe, strength, or flavor for every community or period.

## Edition 1.11: newly sourced connections

The new [kunun-zaki profile](kunun-zaki.md) offers a useful but **non-identical** Nigerian cereal comparison. A 2015 experiment describes kunun-zaki as a mildly fermented, commonly nonalcoholic millet refreshment, whereas the cited Ghanaian burukutu field study here concerns an alcoholic sour sorghum beer. Similar grains do not authorize renaming the drinks or merging their fermentation timelines.[^v111-kunun-study]
