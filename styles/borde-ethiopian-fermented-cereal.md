# Borde: an Ethiopian cereal-based ferment

Borde is an Ethiopian fermented cereal beverage whose classification is better explained by ingredients, fermentation and serving context than by a conventional barley-beer judging category. A 2024 study examined locally produced samples from three Ethiopian locations and described an acidic, actively fermenting cereal matrix. The broader review places *borde* alongside, but not inside, the same formulation as tella or shamita.[^v18-borde][^v18-ethiopian-review]

## What was actually measured

The field-linked laboratory study measured microbial counts, physicochemical properties and rheology. Early stages included several bacterial groups; later samples were dominated by lactic acid bacteria and yeasts in the measured preparations. The material was shear-thinning rather than a clear, filtered beverage. These observations do not establish a universal microbial starter or alcohol target.[^v18-borde]

## What counts as a useful description?

A useful local description asks which cereals and malted materials were used, how porridge or gruel was prepared, how long fermentation proceeded, and whether the sampled drink was still actively fermenting. Laboratory values from one sampling scheme cannot be converted into a commercial style specification. Compare the [Shamita account](shamita-ethiopian-fermented-barley.md) for a different grain preparation and the [Tella profile](tella.md) for a named Ethiopian beer with its own process.

## Limits of the health narrative

In-vitro antioxidant and enzyme-inhibition results are laboratory measures—not evidence that drinking borde treats diabetes or is microbiologically safe. The research measured organisms at specific times, not pathogen-free assurance for every producer. Read the [fermentation survey](../microbiology/borde-microbial-succession.md) before interpreting organism lists as nutritional guarantees.
