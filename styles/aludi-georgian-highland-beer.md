# Aludi: Georgian highland beer

*Aludi* is a name for traditional beer in parts of the Georgian highlands. Georgia's official tourism account describes barley malt, hops and communal production, and associates named preparations with mountain gatherings. It identifies regions such as Pshavi, Khevsureti and Tusheti, whose practices should not be flattened into one identical recipe or entire nation's beer preference.[^v112-aludi-georgia]

## Brewing embedded in place and time

The official description notes preparation before certain celebrations and beer presented during summer gatherings, including ceremonial blessings. A regional ritual is a social relationship, not a flavor specification: this evidence cannot tell us exactly what each village's beer tasted like, how much alcohol it contained or whether every participant drank it.[^v112-aludi-georgia]

## Heritage is not a genome

The national source describes aludi brewing as Georgian intangible cultural heritage, **not a UNESCO inscription**. The description is an institutional cultural account rather than independent testimony by named makers; we preserve the distinction. Compare Georgian highland beer to Norwegian juleøl or Finnish sahti for brewing's social role, not to assert a common ancient ancestor or yeast lineage.[^v112-aludi-georgia]
