# Muratina — a Kenyan fibre-carried fermented drink

## Why the name belongs beside beer, not underneath a universal beer style

A University of Nairobi thesis investigated Muratina production in Kenyan traditional breweries and experimental batches. In the cases it describes, sweetened sugarcane juice fermented in contact with fibrous material from the sausage tree, *Kigelia*. This is a sugar-based fermentation, not necessarily malted-grain brewing; describing it as a modern barley beer would mislead readers about both substrate and process.[^v113-muratina-2003]

## An ingredient can also carry a living culture

The thesis describes porous fruit material acting as a carrier for yeast as it passes between brewing cycles. That is different from treating a fruit as simply an aromatic flavor addition. The particular organism counts, fermentation duration, acidity and alcohol concentration reported in the thesis belong to its surveyed preparations and experiments, not a certification of a consistent Kenyan style.[^v113-muratina-2003]

## Social practice needs a place and a source

A 2026 Pwani University study of Agikuyu Muslims in Sagana records Muratina as one subject of negotiation where local customary marriage practices and Islamic teaching intersect. It does not show that every Agikuyu family uses the drink or that all marriage ceremonies include alcohol. The paper's abstract is an account of those interviews, not a new community rule or an instruction for a ceremony.[^v113-muratina-2026]
