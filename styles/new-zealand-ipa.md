# New Zealand IPA

New Zealand IPA is an IPA label associated with New Zealand hop expression. It appears as class 2A, New Zealand-Style India Pale Ale, in the Brewers Guild of New Zealand's corrected 2026 awards guide. That local competition uses the BA description for the entry.[^modern-nzba-guide]

## Character and ingredients

The BA 2026 description pairs dominant hops with a dry finish and supportive malt, allowing fruity fermentation character rather than prescribing absolute neutrality.[^modern-ba-2026]

“New Zealand hops” is not one flavor. NZ Hops' own cultivar descriptions distinguish Nelson Sauvin's white-wine association from Nectaron's pineapple, passion-fruit and stone-fruit associations. These are supplier descriptions of ingredient potential, not guaranteed sensory results in every finished IPA.[^modern-nz-hops]

That distinction is useful for both readers and retrieval systems: preserve named cultivars when a recipe or producer supplies them, and do not invent them from a style name. The cooperative also documents exports, so a New Zealand hop identity does not require a beer to be brewed in New Zealand.[^modern-nz-hops]

## A producer example and neighboring styles

Sawmill identifies its 5.8% ABV India Pale Ale as a New Zealand-style IPA. The product page provides a concrete example of commercial use; it is too brief to support a complete recipe or a claim about the whole country's IPA production.[^modern-sawmill-ipa]

Read [New Zealand Pilsner](new-zealand-pilsner.md) for another named hop-focused tradition, then compare [American IPA](american-ipa.md) and [Hazy IPA](hazy-ipa.md). In this wiki's organization, hop geography, fermentation, strength and haze remain separate descriptors. Do not automatically call every New Zealand-made hazy beer a New Zealand IPA in the same judging sense.

## Evidence note

The corrected 2026 New Zealand competition guide matters: its publisher notes that an earlier version contained errors in other categories. This entry uses the corrected guide and does not infer that the category's first appearance marks the invention of the beer.[^modern-nzba-2026]
