# American IPA

American IPA is a hop-led ale whose malt and fermentation character support an assertively bitter, relatively dry balance. BJCP 2021 defines it as 21A, allowing a broad hop-aroma range and some light fruitiness rather than prescribing one cultivar or an exclusively pine-and-citrus profile.[^bjcp-2021-21a]

## Character and brewing

In that reference, pale malt is the main base, crystal malt is restrained, and neutral or lightly fruity ale fermentation is appropriate. Hops can suggest flowers, resin, citrus or other fruit. Light haze is permissible; extreme brilliance is not the sole criterion. The reference is stronger and more hop-emphasized than American Pale Ale, but below Double IPA in strength.[^bjcp-2021-21a]

## An evolving producer example

Stone identifies its flagship as a West Coast-style IPA, dates that beer to 1997, and says its dry hopping has increased over time. The brewery now describes both pine and tropical/citrus character.[^modern-stone-ipa] This is a useful warning against treating a style label as a frozen recipe: a producer can retain a name while changing its execution.

## Classify the whole beer

BJCP places dry, sharply bitter West Coast examples within American IPA. It also allows an IPL with appropriate sensory character to enter 21A, while directing other mixed examples elsewhere.[^bjcp-2021-21a] A competition placement therefore does not prove the species of yeast used in a particular beer.

## Evidence note

The guideline establishes a modern target, while Stone's account documents its own beer. Neither independently settles the entire historical transition from [English IPA](english-ipa.md) to modern American usage. Ingredient geography also does not determine where a beer was brewed.
