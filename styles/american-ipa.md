# American IPA

American IPA is a hop-led ale whose malt and fermentation character support an assertively bitter, relatively dry balance. BJCP 2021 defines it as 21A, allowing a broad hop-aroma range and some light fruitiness rather than prescribing one cultivar or an exclusively pine-and-citrus profile.[^bjcp-2021-21a]

## Character and brewing

In that reference, pale malt is the main base, crystal malt is restrained, and neutral or lightly fruity ale fermentation is appropriate. Hops can suggest flowers, resin, citrus or other fruit. Light haze is permissible; extreme brilliance is not the sole criterion. The reference is stronger and more hop-emphasized than American Pale Ale, but below Double IPA in strength.[^bjcp-2021-21a]

## An evolving producer example

Stone identifies its flagship as a West Coast-style IPA, dates that beer to 1997, and says its dry hopping has increased over time. The brewery now describes both pine and tropical/citrus character.[^modern-stone-ipa] This is a useful warning against treating a style label as a frozen recipe: a producer can retain a name while changing its execution.

## Classify the whole beer

BJCP places dry, sharply bitter West Coast examples within American IPA. It also allows an IPL with appropriate sensory character to enter 21A, while directing other mixed examples elsewhere.[^bjcp-2021-21a] A competition placement therefore does not prove the species of yeast used in a particular beer.

This wiki recommends keeping four fields: the producer's label, the reference category, the stated ABV, and the observed sensory balance. Follow [West Coast IPA](west-coast-ipa.md) for the regional term, [Session IPA](session-ipa.md) for the strength modifier and [Hazy IPA](hazy-ipa.md) for a separate reference emphasis. These pages should remain connected without collapsing their names into exact synonyms.

## Evidence note

The guideline establishes a modern target, while Stone's account documents its own beer. Neither independently settles the entire historical transition from [English IPA](english-ipa.md) to modern American usage. Ingredient geography also does not determine where a beer was brewed.

## Edition 1.4: Storage changes more than bitterness numbers

A brewing stability review distinguishes oxidation involving metals and oxygen from non-oxidative aging. Stored hop-forward beer may change through several routes, so packaged flavor cannot be predicted by a single hop measurement. Explore [oxidative aging](../sensory/oxidative-aging.md).[^v14-aging]

## Research extension 1.5: The limits of a single bitterness number

Controlled sensory research tested whether consumers distinguished bitter stimuli even when their intensities were matched in a nonalcoholic beer model. A dry-hopping review also notes that analytical bitterness measures may diverge from perception when compounds and context vary. Thus a numeric IBU alone does not describe an IPA’s flavor.[^v15-bitter-lab][^v15-dryhop-review]

See [different kinds of bitterness](../sensory/bitterness-is-not-one-number.md) and [hop-derived astringency](../sensory/hop-polyphenols-and-astringency.md).

## Edition 1.16: newly sourced connections

An American IPA can have a style-guideline bitterness range and still create different sensory impressions in two drinkers. The ASBC's public Beer 23 summary describes bitterness units as an analytical extraction/absorbance result that can help breweries compare production runs; it is not a direct tasting-panel score.[^v116-bitterMethod]

A chemistry review explains why humulinones, oxidized hop components and polyphenols complicate a simple equation between measured IBU and experienced bitterness, especially with dry hopping.[^v116-bitterReview] One abstracted experimental comparison even reports beers with similar IBU results but unlike iso-alpha acid concentrations. That is a bounded study, not a recalibration of the entire IPA family.[^v116-ibuStudy]

Record whether an IPA's bitterness number is predicted or measured, and retain the source and date of any judging range. The [IBU method guide](../processes/bitterness-units-and-hop-chemistry.md) and [sensory comparison](../sensory/bitterness-is-not-one-number.md) explain why sensory identity cannot be compressed to a hop mass or single reading.
