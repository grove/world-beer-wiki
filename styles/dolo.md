# Dolo

**Beer Tradition** · West Africa / Burkina Faso / Mali · living tradition

Dolo is a West African sorghum beer especially associated with Burkina Faso and Mali. It belongs to a living brewing economy and social tradition, not simply to a generic category of “African beer.” Local brewing knowledge and small-scale production are central to its identity.[^dolo-review]

## Character

Its character combines grain with fermentation acidity. Sorghum brewing involves both acid-producing bacteria and alcohol-producing yeasts. The balance can change with the producer and the progress of fermentation, so the name alone does not promise a uniform sweetness or strength.[^dolo-review]

## Brewing and context

Research at production sites in Burkina Faso and Ghana followed sorghum malt through mashing and acidification. It documented a substantial increase in lactic acid bacteria during souring and considerable variation between microbial strains. That explains why the acidification stage deserves attention alongside alcoholic fermentation.[^dolo-pito-research]

## History and interpretation

Dolo and pito have important technical similarities, but this wiki keeps their regional identities separate. Evidence about one production site does not establish a recipe for all West Africa. Neither a modern pale sorghum lager nor any beer containing sorghum is automatically dolo.[^dolo-review]
