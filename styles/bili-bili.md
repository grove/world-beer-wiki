# Bili bili — Chadian sorghum beer

**Beer Tradition** · Chad · living tradition

Bili bili is a Chadian sorghum-beer tradition. Its fermentation cannot be adequately described by attaching the modern label “ale” to sorghum: the documented production sequence combines malting, separation and heating of mash fractions, acidification, and alcoholic fermentation.[^trad-bili-process]

## Brewing and character

A preliminary survey of 71 women brewers informed Maoura and colleagues' process study. The researchers then followed four productions by one maker. They recorded two distinct acidification stages, as well as changes during wort heating and final fermentation. Acidity is therefore part of the studied method, not automatically evidence that the beer has failed.[^trad-bili-process]

The detailed chemical analysis used clarified liquid fractions because suspended material made representative sampling difficult. That limitation matters: a measurement of the separated liquid cannot simply describe the composition or texture of the whole opaque drink.[^trad-bili-process]

## Fermentation evidence

A separate study identified and typed 76 yeast isolates from bili bili and other samples. It reported diverse *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* strains as prominent in alcoholic fermentation and *Kluyveromyces marxianus* in earlier processing stages. Trial fermentations investigated potential starter strains. These findings identify organisms recovered in a particular study; they are not a complete census of every bili bili fermentation.[^trad-bili-yeasts]

## Naming and regional context

This page uses the authors' Chadian scope. Other Central and West African beers can share grains and operations without being culturally interchangeable. [Dolo](dolo.md), [pito](pito.md), and [tchoukoutou](tchoukoutou.md) remain separate entries. Comparison should identify what is shared—substrate, acidification, or starter practice—rather than assuming that different local names are aliases.

## Evidence note

The process evidence comes from indexed passages of the author-shared article; the yeast paper was available as a publisher abstract. Its analytical focus does not justify an invented aroma profile or mandatory alcohol range. The process paper's issue is dated 2006, although the AJOL website's deposit citation displays 2010; this profile uses the date printed on the article.
