# Shangshan fermented-grain beverage

This page describes a 2024 study of early Shangshan pottery in China's Lower Yangzi region, dated to approximately 10,000–9,000 calibrated years before present. The researchers interpret starch, phytolith and fungal remains as evidence of rice-based fermentation using a mould-and-yeast starter.[^wiki-shangshan-study]

## What the evidence supports

The reported remains include rice and other starchy plants. Fungal morphology is interpreted as consistent with Monascus and yeast. These are archaeological identifications and process inferences; the study did not recover a living ancient starter or a written brewing recipe.[^wiki-shangshan-study]

## What remains unknown

This wiki assigns no ancient flavor, alcohol percentage, color, commercial-style identity or original beverage name. “Shangshan beer” is a modern reference label. Proposed ritual significance and links to rice cultivation should remain interpretations, not a claim that one beverage caused domestication.[^wiki-shangshan-study]

## Research extension 1.3: Fungal morphology is not a recovered living starter

At **Shangshan**, researchers interpreted fungal-like microfossils together with rice and other starch evidence as signs compatible with early fermented cereal beverage production. The material cannot genetically identify an ancient living strain or prove continuity with current starter makers. Compare [Monascus-like evidence](../microbiology/monascus-in-archaeology.md) and [archaeological methods](../history/how-archaeologists-identify-beer.md).[^v13-shangshan]
