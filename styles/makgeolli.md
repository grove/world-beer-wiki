# Makgeolli

**Related Fermented-Grain Tradition** · East Asia / South Korea · living tradition

Makgeolli is a Korean fermented rice drink often described in English as rice wine. It is included here as a related grain-fermentation tradition, not relabeled as a modern barley-beer style.[^makgeolli-research]

## Character

Cloudiness and a substantial mouthfeel are important reference points. A study of twelve commercial examples found meaningful differences in sweetness, sourness, cereal character, fruit aroma, and bubbles. There is no single balance that describes every bottle.[^makgeolli-research]

## Brewing and context

Traditional production uses cooked rice and nuruk, a grain-based starter carrying a community of microorganisms. The starter helps break rice starch into sugars, while fermentation converts sugars into alcohol and other flavor compounds. Water and yeast can also be added during production.[^makgeolli-research]

## History and interpretation

The study demonstrates variation among actual products, not just theoretical recipes. Some examples emphasized sweet-sour cereal character; others were more associated with yeast, fruit, body, or alcohol. Its sample set is not a survey of all Korean brewing history, and the presence of living microorganisms should not be taken as a proven health benefit.[^makgeolli-research]

The description is limited to the sources below. A local observation or laboratory sample does not establish one recipe, strength, or flavor for every community or period.

## Research extension 1.3: Heritage designation is about making and sharing

The Republic of Korea's Cultural Heritage Administration identified **makgeolli making and sharing** as a national intangible-cultural-heritage practice in 2021. This recognizes skills and social transmission, not just a grain-ferment recipe or an international UNESCO listing. See [the cultural account](../culture/makgeolli-making-and-sharing.md) and [nuruk yeast ecology](../microbiology/nuruk-mixed-yeasts.md).[^v13-korea-heritage]
