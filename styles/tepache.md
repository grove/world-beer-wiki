# Tepache

Tepache is commonly described as a Mexican beverage fermented from pineapple peel or rind with sugar. Unlike a malted-grain beer, its main substrates are fruit-associated materials and added sweeteners. A 2022 study characterized its microbiological community and chemical properties, reporting a mixture of acid-producing organisms and yeasts in sampled beverage material.[^v111-tepache-ecology]

## One batch cannot stand for a tradition

A 2025 study examined **one** home-prepared batch that included pineapple, piloncillo and spices. Its microbial results and antibiotic-resistance observations are important questions for research, not population estimates of tepache safety or health effects. Simulated gastric-fluid passage and resistance assays do not show that consuming a specific homemade beverage is beneficial or safe; different batches and handling practices can differ sharply.[^v111-tepache-one]

## Compare carefully

Tepache can be read alongside pulque and cereal-based drinks because all depend on microbes transforming available substrates, but pineapple rind is not aguamiel and neither is sorghum malt. A shared word such as 'traditional ferment' need not imply direct origin, common ritual use, or particular alcohol percentage. Local accounts, seasonal availability, and actual source provenance should lead future expansion rather than a laboratory taxon list.[^v111-tepache-ecology]
