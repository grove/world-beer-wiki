# Lichtenhainer

**Beer Style** · Europe · revived

Lichtenhainer is a historical Thuringian beer tradition that combines smoke and sourness in a relatively light wheat-containing beer. That combination distinguishes it from both ordinary smoked lager and unsmoked pale sour beer.[^bjcp-2021-27c]

## Character

The flavor can mix pale grain, tart citrus-like acidity, and a clear but balanced smoke note. The body is light and the finish dry. Harsh soot, heavy sweetness, or overwhelming acidity would distort the intended balance.[^bjcp-2021-27c]

## Brewing and context

Smoked malt provides smoke, while lactic fermentation provides sourness. Modern reconstructions vary in their exact methods and grain bills, so a contemporary tasting is not automatically a complete reconstruction of a nineteenth-century batch.[^bjcp-2021-27c]

## History and interpretation

The style belongs to a regional German historical context and has returned through specialist brewing interest. It is not simply “smoked Berliner Weisse,” although that comparison helps explain the broad flavor combination. Grodziskie is another light smoked beer, but its traditional reference emphasizes oak-smoked wheat and bitterness rather than intentional sourness.[^bjcp-2021-27c]
