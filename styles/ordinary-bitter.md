# Ordinary Bitter

**Beer Style** · Europe · living

Ordinary bitter is the lower-strength end of the English bitter family. It is intended to offer a complete balance of malt, hops, and gentle fruitiness without the weight of a stronger beer.[^bjcp-2021-11a]

## Character

Expect bread or biscuit-like malt, noticeable bitterness, and a dry or lightly rounded finish. The color can vary from gold to copper. Cask-served examples generally have a softer carbonation than heavily carbonated bottled beer.[^bjcp-2021-11a]

## Brewing and context

Pale malt forms the base, sometimes with colored malt or brewing sugars. English-type yeast may contribute modest fruitiness, while hops provide both bitterness and a restrained earthy, floral, or herbal character.[^bjcp-2021-11a]

## History and interpretation

“Ordinary” identifies a place within a strength range, not poor quality. “Bitter” is also a historical beer name rather than a promise that it will be more bitter than modern IPA. Serving method affects the experience considerably, so a cold, highly carbonated package and a carefully conditioned cask may present the same family differently.[^bjcp-2021-11a]
