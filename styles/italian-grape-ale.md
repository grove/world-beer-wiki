# Italian Grape Ale

Italian Grape Ale connects beer brewing with grapes or grape must and Italian local ingredients. BJCP 2021 keeps an Italian-focused X3 profile and points uses outside Italy to 29D Grape Ale. These are related reference descriptions, not interchangeable proof of how every producer names a beer.[^bjcp-2021-x3][^modern-bjcp-grape]

## Ingredients and character

In X3, recognizable grape character should coexist with beer character, with hops generally restrained and strong roast discouraged. Grapes or must can be introduced at different stages, and neutral, fruity or mixed yeast choices may occur. Mild wild or acidic notes can fit, while explicitly wild or sour interpretations require their own context.[^bjcp-2021-x3]

The international 29D description specifically discusses grape must fermented with beer, rather than a simple mixture of finished wine and beer.[^modern-bjcp-grape] A database should therefore preserve whether a source reports grapes, fresh must, cooked must or finished wine, and at what stage it was used.

## A revealing producer comparison

Birrificio Barley describes BB10 as an imperial stout made with cooked Cannonau grape must, or sapa. The brewery's account emphasizes its Sardinian ingredient and gives dark, rich flavor associations including cacao and caramel.[^modern-barley-bb10]

There is a real difference in emphasis here: the guideline discourages a stout-like roast profile, while the producer calls this grape beer an imperial stout. That does not make the source unusable. It shows that a judging target and a brewery's own product vocabulary answer different questions. This wiki preserves both rather than removing the inconvenient example or turning every dark grape beer into the guideline's norm.

## Names and interpretation

Record the grape variety, ingredient form, producer, location and fermentation account where available. “Wine-like” can be a sensory description; it cannot replace ingredient evidence. Similarly, a [Brut IPA](brut-ipa.md) can invoke sparkling-wine imagery without becoming an Italian Grape Ale.

Read [Fruit Beer](fruit-beer.md) and [Fruit Lambic](fruit-lambic.md) for other contexts in which fruit and fermentation meet. These are comparison links, not claims of shared ancestry or identical microbiology.

## Evidence note

The guideline's historical account is not independently re-proven here. The producer documents its own ingredient and naming choices. No single grape proportion, color, yeast or legal category is asserted for the entire Italian tradition.
