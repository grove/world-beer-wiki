# Kentucky Common

**Beer Style** · North America · revived

Kentucky Common is a historical Louisville-area everyday beer, now reconstructed and revived. The modern reference is a quick-turnaround, corn-containing, darkish ale with a light, dry drinking character.[^bjcp-2021-27b]

## Character

Grain, gentle caramel, and a small darker-malt edge can appear, with restrained hops and lively carbonation. The color should not suggest that it must taste like stout or be especially strong.[^bjcp-2021-27b]

## Brewing and context

Barley malt and corn form much of the base, with small darker-grain additions influencing color and balance. The beer was intended for relatively prompt consumption rather than prolonged maturation.[^bjcp-2021-27b]

## History and interpretation

A persistent modern story describes Kentucky Common as a sour-mash beer by analogy with whiskey. The BJCP historical account explicitly rejects treating sourness as a defining feature without documentary support. Historical sour mash, accidental souring, and a deliberately sour finished beer are different claims. Modern revival recipes should identify their evidence rather than simply repeating the whiskey connection.[^bjcp-2021-27b]
