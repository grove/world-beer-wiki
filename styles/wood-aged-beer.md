# Wood-aged beer

**Specialty Beer Category** · International · modern category

Wood-aged beer is a process-defined category in which contact with wood adds an identifiable contribution to a base beer. It is not one color or strength, and this reference distinguishes wood character from flavors left by spirits or wine previously held in a barrel.[^bjcp-2021-33a]

## Character

Depending on the wood and its treatment, flavors may suggest fresh wood, toast, vanilla, caramel, nuts, or cocoa. These are possibilities rather than a checklist. A successful example still lets the underlying beer remain recognizable.[^bjcp-2021-33a]

## Brewing and context

Wood variety and the degree of toasting or charring affect the result. The BJCP category focuses on beers without a clear contribution from a barrel's previous alcoholic contents; those belong in its Specialty Wood-Aged Beer category.[^bjcp-2021-33a]

## History and interpretation

This is a modern classification of a process, not a claim that every historical beer stored in wood tasted strongly woody. For comparison, record the base style and the actual treatment rather than treating “aged” as a complete description.[^bjcp-2021-33a]

## Evidence note

This profile paraphrases a modern judging reference. Its flavor description is better supported than any claim of a single origin or an unchanged historical recipe. It is not a complete brewing recipe.
