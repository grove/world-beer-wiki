# Parakari

**Related Starch-Fermentation Tradition** · South America / Guyana / Rupununi · living tradition

Parakari is a cassava beverage documented in Indigenous communities of Guyana. A community account from Parikwarinawa in the Rupununi presents it as part of living agricultural and food knowledge, rather than a disconnected historical curiosity.[^parakari-community]

## Character

The community source emphasizes preparation and cultural significance, not standardized tasting measurements. It is insufficient to assign every parakari one sweetness, color, or alcohol strength. Those details should be recorded for particular makers and occasions.[^parakari-community]

## Brewing and context

A microbiological study identifies starch-breaking Rhizopus mold in parakari fermentation. That makes mold-assisted starch conversion an important point of comparison with some Asian ferments, without implying that the traditions share an origin.[^parakari-study]

## History and interpretation

The community account describes knowledge passed between generations through practical participation. It also stresses that bitter cassava needs proper processing before consumption. This overview is not a preparation guide: the expertise involved must not be reduced to “leave cassava to ferment.”[^parakari-community]

## Evidence note

The description is limited to the sources below. A local observation or laboratory sample does not establish one recipe, strength, or flavor for every community or period.
