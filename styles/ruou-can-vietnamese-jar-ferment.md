# Rượu cần: Vietnamese jar-fermented drink

The Vietnamese expression *rượu cần* refers to an alcoholic drink served from a jar using long straws. In Binh Phuoc, the Vietnam News Agency documented the S’Tieng *d’rap s’lung* / *ro nom d’rap*, where a grain-and-leaf-starter preparation sits in a jar. Vietnam’s tourism authority separately described a Ma community practice in Lam Dong. Their common serving object does **not** make every named local recipe, festival or lineage the same.[^v19-stieng][^v19-ma-tourism]

## What makes a jar-ferment distinctive?

In the S’Tieng account, glutinous rice and other local plant materials are combined with a prepared starter and fermented in covered terracotta jars. The national news report describes named craftswomen, intergenerational skill and a Binh Phuoc cooperative. The Ma account describes differently prepared earthen vessels, local botanical knowledge and sharing through bamboo straws. Specific plant choices and the duration of fermentation belong to the individual account, not to a universal specification.[^v19-stieng][^v19-ma-tourism]

## Classification and service

*Rượu* is often translated into English as “wine” or “liquor”; that translation should not decide whether a drink fits a modern beer judging family. A review of drinking cultures in Vietnam separates household-fermented rice beverages, distilled rice alcohol and commercial products, and warns against a single account of national drinking customs. The wiki includes jar ferments for **comparison**, not to rename the community’s beverage “beer.”[^v19-vietnam-review]
