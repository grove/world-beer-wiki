# Tchoukoutou

**Beer Tradition** · West Africa / Benin / Togo · living tradition

Tchoukoutou names local grain beers documented in Benin and Togo. Research in Togo describes beers made with sorghum or millet, while the Benin yeast study examined a sorghum-based product.[^tchoukoutou-togo][^tchoukoutou-yeasts]

## Character

The name covers variation rather than one industrial formula. Regional sampling and different methods of measuring alcohol are important when comparing published results. A number measured in one study should not automatically become a limit defining the tradition.[^tchoukoutou-togo]

## Brewing and context

Research on market samples in Benin found a diverse yeast population, including Saccharomyces cerevisiae and other yeasts. Different analytical methods did not reveal exactly the same organisms. This supports describing a complex fermentation rather than attributing every batch to one standard strain.[^tchoukoutou-yeasts]

## History and interpretation

The evidence does not justify merging all sorghum beers under this name. Dolo, pito, and tchapalo remain useful separate entries because geography, local terminology, and brewing knowledge matter alongside ingredients. This profile is a cautious regional summary, not an exhaustive account of every community's version.[^tchoukoutou-togo][^tchoukoutou-yeasts]

## Evidence note

The description is limited to the sources below. A local observation or laboratory sample does not establish one recipe, strength, or flavor for every community or period.
