# Tesgüino — Rarámuri tradition

**Beer Tradition** · North America / Mexico / Chihuahua · living tradition

Tesgüino is a maize-based fermented drink with a central place in Rarámuri communal life. This profile follows a documented account from Norogachi, including Alberta Espino Romero's own description and Regina Espino Moreno's translation. Batáli is the local name used in that account.[^tesguino-community]

## Character

The narrator explicitly recognizes that batches do not always come out equally strong or equally good. That is a valuable warning against imposing a narrow industrial-style flavor or alcohol specification. The source does not provide a sensory panel or a fixed strength range.[^tesguino-community]

## Brewing and context

The described preparation includes soaking and germinating maize, spreading and grinding it, cooking, cooling, and fermentation in pots. The account also names an added ingredient, triguillo. Its local terminology should be retained rather than silently translated into a universal brewing formula.[^tesguino-community]

## History and interpretation

The article links the drink with reciprocity and collective work. Those social relationships are part of the tradition, not optional decoration around a recipe. This is a community-specific Rarámuri account, not a claim that every Mexican beverage called tesgüino is made identically.[^tesguino-community]

## Evidence note

The description is limited to the sources below. A local observation or laboratory sample does not establish one recipe, strength, or flavor for every community or period.
