# Cauim

*Cauim* appears in work comparing accounts of Guarani and Tupinambá preparations and in microbiological studies of Tapirapé food-drinks. Those works describe different people, periods, materials and research purposes. **One name does not mean one batch process or one organism list.** A late-twentieth-century archaeological-ethnohistorical paper analyses early European descriptions alongside vessel evidence, not an exact contemporary recipe.[^v111-cauim-history]

## More than cassava

The 2007 Tapirapé study investigated a cassava-based preparation and recorded progressive acidification along with lactic acid, ethanol and acetic acid. A 2010 study instead followed peanut and rice materials using both culture-dependent and molecular approaches. Both demonstrate how substrate and sampling design matter: the word cauim alone cannot tell a reader whether the material is cassava, rice, maize or a mixture.[^v111-cauim-bacteria][^v111-cauim-dynamics]

## Food, drink and historical voice

Some sources treat cauim as food as well as drink. English 'beer' may illuminate the grain- or starch-fermentation comparison but should not overwrite community categories. Historical chronicles were written by outsiders, and an archaeologist's reconstruction is an interpretation. Keep the Guarani, Tupinambá and Tapirapé cases separate; neither the overlapping term nor the presence of lactic acid proves a shared lineage of unmodified practice.[^v111-cauim-history]
