# Judima

**Beer Tradition** · South Asia / India / Assam · living tradition

Judima is a fermented rice drink associated with Dimasa communities in northeastern India. The cited researchers observed its preparation in the North Cachhar Hills area of Assam and documented both brewing practice and the drink's composition.[^judima-research]

## Character

Their description includes a creamy-white liquid with a characteristic fermentation aroma. That supports a picture of a rice-based drink with visible substance, but does not support one precise sweetness, alcohol level, or tasting vocabulary for every maker.[^judima-research]

## Brewing and context

The process begins with starter cakes called umhu or humao. Rice and plant materials are involved in preparing the cakes, and material from an older starter helps establish the next one. Cooked rice is then fermented, with the liquid separated for drinking.[^judima-research]

## History and interpretation

Starter-making is part of the cultural and practical knowledge, not merely a substitute for buying brewer's yeast. The paper's laboratory tests of plant extracts and the beverage must not be turned into medical claims. Its field observations also do not establish a single unchanging recipe for all Dimasa households.[^judima-research]

## Evidence note

The description is limited to the sources below. A local observation or laboratory sample does not establish one recipe, strength, or flavor for every community or period.
