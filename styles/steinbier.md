# Steinbier and stone-heated beer

**Brewing Process Tradition** · Europe / Northern Europe / Central Europe · historical and revival

Stone-heated beer is defined by a heating method: stones heated in a fire transfer heat into the brewing material. Steinbier is therefore better treated as a process tradition than as one fixed color, strength, or fermentation style.[^garshol-stone]

## Character

It is tempting to give all stone beer a single smoky or caramelized flavor. The historical evidence does not justify that shortcut. The stage of brewing that receives the stones, the ingredients, and the rest of the process all need to be identified.[^garshol-stone]

## Brewing and context

Garshol compared recorded methods from several European regions. In the examples where the operation was clear, the stones heated the mash; most did not include a subsequent wort boil. That differs from the familiar modern story in which stones are mainly dropped into finished wort.[^garshol-stone]

## History and interpretation

The comparison draws on particular ethnographic records, not proof that every prehistoric heated stone was used for brewing. Modern stone-brewed beers may adapt the technique in different ways. Identify the documented method before claiming that a revival reproduces a historical beer.[^garshol-stone]
