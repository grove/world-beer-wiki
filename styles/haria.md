# Haria / Handia

**Beer Tradition** · South Asia / India · living tradition

Haria is a rice-fermentation tradition found in eastern and central India. The related name handia is documented in Odisha. This entry brings the spellings together for discovery without claiming that every community makes the same drink.[^haria-research][^handia-research]

## Character

It is a mildly alcoholic, acidifying rice preparation rather than a clear, hop-bitter lager. Its consistency and the way it is served matter as much as its alcohol content. The cited research investigates fermentation and composition, not a universal tasting standard.[^haria-research]

## Brewing and context

In the Odisha study, dehusked rice was cooked and fermented using starter tablets known as ranu or bakhar. Plants were included in preparation of the starter, and the researchers found both yeasts and lactic acid bacteria. A separate haria study followed changes in the bacterial community during fermentation.[^handia-research][^haria-research]

## History and interpretation

The local starter and the knowledge needed to maintain it are part of the tradition. A list of plants recorded in one survey is not a compulsory recipe for all handia. Laboratory investigation of nutrients or microbial activity also does not demonstrate that drinking the beverage prevents or treats illness.[^handia-research][^haria-research]
