# Chhang — Himalayan grain-fermentation traditions

**Beer Tradition** · Indian Himalayas · living traditions

Chhang is a name used for Himalayan grain ferments. This entry compares two documented settings: barley chhang in Ladakh and Bhotiya chhang in Uttarakhand. Their shared name does not establish one recipe or one starter culture across the Himalayas.[^trad-chhang-ladakh][^trad-chhang-balma]

## Ladakhi barley chhang

Targais and colleagues observed production in Leh and Khaltsi. Cooked barley receives a tablet starter called **phabs**; fermentation is followed by water extraction and straining. Successive extracts differ in strength and can be blended. This makes the serving process part of the beverage's identity, rather than a minor packaging detail. The paper also records chhang in hospitality and marriage ceremonies.[^trad-chhang-ladakh]

## Bhotiya starters and fermentation

Research on **Balma**, the Bhotiya starter, identified yeasts, lactic acid bacteria, and starch-degrading bacteria. It supports a system in which starch conversion and lactic and alcoholic fermentation overlap. The paper distinguishes Bhotiya Balma from Jaunsari Keem and describes the reuse of older Balma in the communities studied. A mixed starter is more than an interchangeable packet of brewing yeast.[^trad-chhang-balma]

## Character and comparison

The Ladakh study's measurements apply to its finished blends. They should not become universal alcohol or acidity limits for every drink called chhang. The Bhotiya paper is principally microbiological; it does not supply a sensory map of the whole Himalayan region.[^trad-chhang-ladakh][^trad-chhang-balma]

Read [kodo ko jaanr](kodo-ko-jaanr.md) alongside this article to compare grain fermentation with water-extraction service. Similar service does not make the names synonyms. The regional label, grain, starter, and extraction method are all useful retrieval fields.

## Evidence note

The Ladakhi full text was inspected; access to the Bhotiya paper was limited to its abstract and indexed full-text passages. A wider account would require additional local-language and community evidence from Nepal, Bhutan, Tibet, and other Himalayan regions. This page does not substitute those missing accounts with assumptions.

## Research extension 1.3: A situated ethnographic account

A 2024 ethnographic paper studies the position of **chang** in the Nubri region of Nepal, including social relations and drinking contexts. Its locally situated account must not be projected onto all Tibetan-speaking communities or every beverage also spelled chhang. The [Nubri study guide](../culture/nubri-chang-and-social-life.md) explains the access limits; [tongba service](../objects/tongba-service.md) is a distinct comparative practice.[^v13-tibet-nubri]
