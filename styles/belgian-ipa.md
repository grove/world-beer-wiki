# Belgian IPA

**Beer Style** · Europe / North America · contemporary

Belgian IPA combines an IPA's hop intensity with the fruit and spice of Belgian-type fermentation. The result should be dry and lively rather than simply a sweet Belgian ale with extra bitterness.[^bjcp-2021-21b-belgian]

## Character

Citrus, tropical fruit, or herbal hops can meet pear, apple, pepper, or clove from yeast. Hop and fermentation aromas need to complement one another. A harsh clash is not a required feature of the hybrid.[^bjcp-2021-21b-belgian]

## Brewing and context

Pale malt, sometimes sugar, and an expressive yeast provide the base. Hop choices vary between European and newer international varieties. Thorough fermentation helps maintain a relatively lean finish despite the beer's strength.[^bjcp-2021-21b-belgian]

## History and interpretation

The modern style emerged through related but different experiments: adding Belgian yeast character to IPA and increasing the hopping of stronger Belgian pale ales. It is therefore a contemporary cross-style family rather than a centuries-old uniform national recipe. Compare it with Tripel and American IPA to understand the balance it is trying to combine.[^bjcp-2021-21b-belgian]
