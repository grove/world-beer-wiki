# Chicha de jora

**Beer Tradition** · South America / Peru / Andes · living tradition

Chicha de jora is a Peruvian maize-beer tradition, not a single standardized recipe. Its defining starting material is sprouted maize, called jora. The cited study documents contemporary artisanal production and places it within a much older Andean brewing tradition.[^chicha-jora-study]

## Character

The source describes a yellowish, lightly alcoholic, effervescent drink. Individual producers may add other grains, sugar, herbs, spices, or fruit. This diversity makes a single sweetness or flavor description less useful than identifying the producer and ingredients.[^chicha-jora-study]

## Brewing and context

Maize is germinated, dried, ground, and cooked with water before fermentation. The study describes fermentation vessels and the reuse of material from earlier batches. Its microbiological survey found substantial differences between producers, including communities of lactic-acid and acetic-acid bacteria.[^chicha-jora-study]

## History and interpretation

The researchers examined 27 samples from 14 producers in seven Peruvian provinces. That is evidence of real variation, not permission to describe every Andean drink called chicha as identical. This entry concerns maize-based chicha de jora specifically; it does not absorb cassava-based traditions under the same name.[^chicha-jora-study]
