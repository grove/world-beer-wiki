# Research edition 1.15 — terminology, serving physics and water boundaries

**19 new source-scoped subject articles, 13 substantive existing-article revisions, seven cross-disciplinary reading trails, and 662 total subjects across thirteen reference sections.** No established canonical profile URL is replaced. These are bounded subjects, not 19 newly standardized styles or a measure of worldwide completeness.

## Why these questions matter

### Words and regulation

Official US TTB definitions differentiate Internal Revenue Code **beer** and Federal Alcohol Administration Act **malt beverage**; class/type designations and below-0.5% descriptors are jurisdictionally scoped. The historical **20 August 1991 ATF circular** is an administrative marketing source, not a universal invention date or a recurring festival. The 2023 Department of Health and Social Care consultation applies to **England**; consultation proposals are not assumed to have become law. These cases integrate naming, style, alcohol disclosure, historical records and food-service descriptions without collapsing them into a global ABV chart.

### From dissolved CO2 to the glass

The open 2021 ACS Omega lager study measures and models bubble nucleation under a defined system. Practical Colorado Brewers Guild glass-cleaning guidance provides different observational evidence. A bubbling glass can have multiple explanations; a model's estimate is not an appearance-quality ranking, microbiological certificate, or an all-beer carbonation standard. New ingredient, process, object, sensory and narrative pages distinguish those layers.

### Water accounting and microbial scope

The Water Footprint Network's public method separates direct brewery water use from indirect supply-chain and water-footprint components. The Brewers Association's water manual has a public overview but member-only detail: no numeric operating benchmark is invented. A published pathogen challenge study of low-/no-alcohol beer remains bounded to its modeled matrices and conditions; an ABV descriptor is never a safety certificate.
