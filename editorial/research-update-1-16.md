# Research edition 1.16 — a number is a source, not the whole beer

**23 new source-scoped subject articles, 16 source-cited revisions to canonical pages, 19 external source works, 94 article-to-source uses and eight cross-section reading trails.** The atlas now comprises **685 subject articles in thirteen sections**. No original profile is removed, renamed or elevated to an official style; a researched subject is not necessarily a beer category.

## What the new work establishes and what it does not

### Modern lab methods and chemistry

The open 2019 yeast-and-brewing laboratory guide, the BJCP's public colour references, and ASBC's Beer 23 method description distinguish **measured beer colour**, **analytical bitterness**, **gravity/extract** and **estimated alcohol**. Measured IBU is not itself a bitterness preference score; a study abstract compares chemically unlike beers with similar bitterness-unit results. The open chemistry review explains hop alpha-acid transformations, oxidation products and other bitter fractions. Clarified optical colour at 430 nm and an amber-looking pint in a glass are distinct observations.

ASBC public pH and titratable-acidity method summaries distinguish hydrogen-ion activity from base consumption to a defined endpoint. Neither a low pH nor a specific fermentation duration guarantees microbiological safety. Apparent attenuation likewise cannot reveal yeast identity, real residual carbohydrate or product compliance without separate evidence.

### Difference, preference and presentation

**ISO 4120:2021** was confirmed current in 2026, but its public abstract, not the full paywalled standard, was inspected. A triangle test addresses detectability under specified conditions; it does not report which product is better liked or what attribute changed. The public **ISO 8589** scope describes controlled test-room design. BJCP judging guidance and a consumer expectation-study abstract are different evidence classes. A pairing test, consumer preference and one taster's impression should not become interchangeable claims.

### Measurement has a history and a culture

A 2001 scholarly abstract by James Sumner, Wellcome's 1784 Richardson catalogue record, and a Science Museum saccharometer dated **circa 1825–1835** explain why an extract quantity has a documented commercial and material history. No exact 1784 day is invented, and the museum instrument is not assigned to Richardson personally. Commercial authority, historical practice, laboratory proof and a locally named brewing tradition must remain separated. The new [culture guide](../culture/brewing-numbers-and-institutional-authority.md) discusses these voices without pretending to be a community interview.

### Environment and source accuracy

ASBC's laboratory proficiency-method list concerns comparability of **beer-sample assays**, not environmental life-cycle inventories or product footprint certificates. The [boundary guide](../environment/analytical-consistency-is-not-a-life-cycle-footprint.md) separates those dimensions. Historic editions remain dated rather than rewritten to make their old counts look current.
