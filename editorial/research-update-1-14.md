# World Beer Atlas research edition 1.14

## Summary and reasons for the choice of research

**29 newly researched subjects, 18 source-cited existing article revisions, eight reading trails, and 643 total atlas subjects across thirteen sections.** No pre-existing canonical beer profile is renamed or removed. This edition deliberately follows fewer well-defined source clusters across fields, buildings, institutions, experiments, service systems and brewery waste, instead of inventing dozens of loosely supported beverage names.

### Historical and living-cultural evidence

- **Pilsen 1842:** Plzeňský Prazdroj's company chronology gives an exact batch date and named brewer; a historical scholar's indexed opening points to the 1892 jubilee text. Corporate commemoration, first commercial batch and worldwide fermentation invention are distinct claims. An exact historical edition is stored as a date, not a recurring festival.
- **English hops:** Historic England's 2024 work ties harvesting to drying-floor buildings, cooling and packing. Named oast forms describe the English landscape, not universal modern production equipment or a fixed international hop holiday.
- **Belgian beer culture, American history and Ethiopian tella:** UNESCO documents knowledge transmission; the Smithsonian finding aid frames labor and immigration but not specific uninspected interviews; an Ethiopian literature review distinguishes reported vessel-smoke treatment from smoked malt. No institutional claim becomes community approval or a safety protocol.

### Analytical and industrial evidence

- **Industrial lager yeast** is studied through a specific 2025 high-gravity brewery experiment involving pitching, aeration, filling duration and cell endpoints. Its factor ranges are not universal operating setpoints.
- **Draught service** uses Brewers Association guidance to distinguish intentional production microbiology from equipment deposits and maintenance. Glass, gas, keg, line and faucet are different objects and transformations.
- **Foam chemistry** compares humulinone/protein-Z experiments with heat-modified LTP1 chemistry without asserting that aged hops always improve beer, or that model solution results determine consumer preferences.
- **Circular material flows** compare 2025 hot-trub/yeast chemical sampling, a 2026 literature review and a specific Chinese closed-loop brewing case. Waste screening is not a universal fertilizer or food safety certification, and modeled system improvements are not transferable carbon rankings.
