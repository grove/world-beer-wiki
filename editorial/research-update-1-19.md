# Research edition 1.19 — three resource systems, distinct questions

## Major findings

### Heat-pump potential is not a metered brewery outcome

A **2026 open Danish brewery pinch-analysis study** models several designs involving high-temperature heat pumps, CO₂ refrigeration loops and vapor recompression. It gives a reference brewery and evaluates energy use and investment tradeoffs. A **2025 global model** estimates a wider technical potential under assumed boiler and electricity conditions, while a **2025 field study** measures or evaluates thermal processes at one Chinese brewery. Those are three different forms of evidence: generalized process design, modeled regional/global potential, and one-site case. The [comparison](../environment/china-brewhouse-field-test-vs-global-model.md) and [heat recovery guide](../processes/wort-cooling-and-heat-recovery.md) do not convert them into a single universal savings percentage.

### Fermentation CO₂ reuse is not an automatic carbon-removal credit

A **2025 study by Grand, Jenkins, Maskell and Zhuang** evaluates capture ideas and interviews **five French craft breweries**. A regional three-level CO₂ sharing approach is proposed, not demonstrated to be operating. The open **Tsingtao** industrial case reports source-specific CO₂ capture, water and packaging measures, but its Scope 1/2 intensity changes are not independent full-life-cycle proof for all brewers. The GHG Protocol distinguishes direct, purchased energy and value-chain emissions. Reusing CO₂ for carbonation, circulating CO₂ as a heat-pump refrigerant, and storing carbon permanently are different claims. [One molecule, three jobs](../stories/the-same-co2-has-three-different-jobs.md) makes the distinction clear without providing industrial gas or food-contact instructions.

### Wastewater COD removal, biomethane yield and clean water are not synonyms

A 2026 *Journal of Visualized Experiments* report follows a **365-day** bioelectrochemically enhanced reactor using real brewery wastewater. A separate 2026 microbrewery waste-stream experiment studies substrate composition and digestion in a different system. A **2025 techno-economic LCA** models four renewable-gas production scenarios from wastewater with differing hydrogen sources, and 2026 review literature surveys anaerobic consortia and byproduct use. These are not identical plants, verified renewable-gas sales, final effluent discharge approvals or potable-water tests. The [COD and water-footprint guide](../categories/wastewater-cod-is-not-water-footprint.md) and [wastewater story](../stories/the-wastewater-does-not-become-drinking-water.md) keep their evidence endpoints separate.

### The industrial past has ice, labor, machinery and uneven adoption

Two museum histories document how natural ice, cellars, winter harvesting, shipping and mechanical refrigeration changed brewing and the transport of beer. The **Norwegian Museum of Science and Technology** uses archive records from breweries such as Ringnes, Frydenlund and Schous for beer exports around **1860–1900**; the **Swiss National Museum** describes ice ponds and early mechanical cooling, including Hürlimann's adoption in **1879**. A **Smithsonian catalogue containing an 1899 Wolf ammonia fittings list** adds material evidence. The new [historical study](../history/ice-houses-to-mechanical-brewery-cooling.md) preserves approximate dates and does not claim to date the first ice cellar in the world, equate modern GHG values with nineteenth-century ice trade, or speak for all brewing laborers. A [seasonal ice-harvest entry](../calendar/historic-natural-ice-harvest-season.md) deliberately invents no fixed annual festival.

### Circular food use is not a wastewater digestion protocol

An open 2025 qualitative study involves seven European craft practitioners discussing food-waste ingredients, while 2026 literature reviews survey spent-grain and yeast routes to food, feed, materials and bioenergy. Those sources cover different products, markets and microbial processes. The [food versus digester guide](../food/food-waste-brewing-is-not-wastewater-digestion.md) explicitly avoids contaminant-handling or consumer food-safety instructions.
