# Research edition 1.17 — from barley field to the glass

**28 newly researched source-scoped subjects, 18 substantial source-backed canonical revisions, 19 research works, 95 article-source placements, and nine evidence-led reading trails.** The wiki now covers **713 subject articles across 13 reference sections**. A subject is not necessarily a newly recognized beer style, and all original canonical beer-profile filenames are preserved.

## Why these topics were selected

### Barley is a crop before it is a brewing ingredient

A 2026 review of pre-harvest sprouting separates wet-weather premature germination from intentional malting. A 2026 three-cultivar drought-proteomics abstract records different grain-filling stresses; those mechanisms should not be combined into a generic claim that one weather event changes every future beer. A 2025 enzyme review explains why malt quality is multidimensional. New ingredient, process, calendar and environment articles trace sample stage, season, cultivar and method without inventing a worldwide harvest holiday.

### Fungus, chemical contaminant, foam and fermentation are unlike claims

The 2024 Manitoba field study tested **two barley cultivars and seven Fusarium graminearum isolates** across field years; this is crop-pathogen evidence, not an account of a living beer starter. A 2026 deliberately inoculated malt-to-beer experiment tracked fungal DNA, DON, DON-3G and other chemical endpoints. It is not proof that fermentation neutralizes a commercial grain lot, or that a particular beer is safe. The 2022 review and 2026 food-risk literature clarify measurement and transformation. Primary gushing associated with fungal hydrophobins is a possible foam mechanism, not a way to diagnose toxins, carbonation or glass cleanliness from a photograph.

### Farm, livelihood and historic landscape need their own voices

Open agricultural-economics studies from **Arsi, Central and Southern Oromia, and Estie** describe different cooperative and contract arrangements in Ethiopia and household outcome estimates. These studies are distinct populations and methods, not one national farmer opinion, a universal contract recommendation or a description of every Ethiopian household beer. Scottish **Bere** is a historic landrace with contemporary local food, animal-feed, malting and distilling uses. The open review treats its original introduction date as uncertain; ancient barley on Orkney is not proof of the exact present-day landrace or a prehistoric Bere beer recipe.

### Tests, law and resource reuse have separate boundaries

A 2025 hot-steep malt experiment with selected heritage cultivars is a malt-sample sensory study, not a final-beer preference ranking. EU Regulation 2023/915, as consolidated in August 2026, and its 2024/1022 DON amendment show that contaminant limits depend on **product category, amendment and applicability**, not one universal beer threshold. Spent-grain circularity, food/feed eligibility, chemical assays and environmental footprints each require their own evidence. This release provides **no contaminant-removal, unsafe fermentation, toxin testing or animal feed instructions**.
