# Research edition 1.18 — from package to consumer, without losing evidence boundaries

**27 new source-scoped subjects, 15 cited canonical article revisions, 22 external research works and 101 article-source uses.** The corpus now has **740 canonical subject articles across thirteen reference sections**, without renaming or replacing established beer and tradition profiles. An article in an environmental, legal, historical or technical directory is not a newly recognized beer style.

## Research findings and scope

### Two beer LCAs can prefer different delivery formats

A **2022 seven-microbrewery LCA** by Morgan, Styles and Lane found that PET-keg substitution was not automatically a lower-impact alternative to reusable steel for short delivery routes, and that delivery coordination could change results. A **2024 beer LCA** by Marrucci, Daddi and Iraldo found a favorable PET-keg draught case under a different comparison against glass and aluminium. These findings are not contradictory measurements on identical systems. A **2025 methodological review** highlights modeled reuse counts, washing, transport, loss and food waste, which must be explicit before anyone declares a universal low-carbon package. The [comparison](../categories/why-packaging-lca-rankings-disagree.md) and [two-case account](../environment/seven-breweries-versus-pet-keg-case.md) retain these boundaries.

### Oxygen, light, microbes and aging are different observations

The indexed EBC method 11.5.1 distinguishes dissolved and headspace oxygen in packaged beer. A 2025 open study followed **one unpasteurized top-fermented beer**, finding storage-, filtration- and yeast-dependent outcomes rather than a universal shelf-life rule. Separate open reviews describe aldehyde formation before and after filling, along with changing esters, terpenes and hop compounds. A 2005 mechanistic study of excited riboflavin and hop-derived bittering molecules and a 2023 **pulsed-light** experiment support a particular lightstruck pathway, which is not the same as oxidative aldehyde aging. The latter experiment's microbial reductions did not automatically imply an industrial food-safety process. A February 2026 Brewers Association warning identifies package over-pressurization from **unintended** refermentation as a producer responsibility, not a consumer troubleshooting procedure.

### Legal material rules are not sensory or product-compliance tests

EU **Regulation 2025/40** began applying on **12 August 2026**. Its reuse, collection and recyclability provisions have different scopes, exemptions and future milestones. A June 2026 Commission interpretation distinguishes large kegs supplied business-to-business to hospitality operators from consumer sales packaging for some reuse targets. An entirely different food-contact measure, **Regulation 2024/3190** and its February 2026 correction, addresses BPA and related compounds in applicable materials and coatings. It cannot reveal the liner or safety status of a particular beer can without product-specific evidence. Those exact administrative dates are not annual beer holidays.

### An archival closure and a museum can are not beer origin stories

William Painter's US **Patent 468,226** is dated **2 February 1892**, describing an engineered crown closure and liner. That exact patent date is neither the invention of bottled beer nor an annual public celebration. The Smithsonian's handled Baltimore *American Beer* can is a specific museum object with manufacturer and collection provenance; its image rights and historical context are not a consumer preference poll or evidence of every can's liner chemistry. Two new historical calendar entries preserve the nonrecurring nature of the dated sources.
