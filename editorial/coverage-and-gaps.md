# Coverage and known gaps

## What the count means

This edition contains **153 canonical profiles**, citing **161 distinct source records**. Most source records are individual official style pages, not separate books or independent studies. A title variant or local spelling is an alias, not an additional profile.

The categories are not equally narrow or equally well documented. “Beer Style” is an editorial file type, not a declaration that all classification systems recognize exactly the same boundary.

## What is strongest

The most systematic coverage is of conventional styles in BJCP's 2021 reference: pale and dark lagers, British and Irish ales, Belgian styles, modern American styles, wheat beers, and selected historical and local categories. Additional research expands the corpus into Nordic and Baltic farmhouse practice, African grain beers, South and East Asian fermentation, and Indigenous American maize and cassava traditions.

The [region index](../navigation/by-region.md) gives the actual membership of each geographical route. Inclusion of a region does not imply complete national coverage. For example, the China entry is archaeological evidence, not a survey of contemporary Chinese brewing.

## What is not complete

This is **not an exhaustive world census**. Coverage remains uneven across languages and communities. Missing areas include many local names and variants in Central and East Africa, South and Southeast Asia, Latin America, the Middle East, and the Pacific. Europe also has numerous historical regional beers not yet researched here.

Historical depth varies. Only selected profiles use original archival analysis or archaeology; a guideline's short historical background does not make every conventional-style entry a full historical monograph. Sake and other related ferments are introductory comparisons, not complete taxonomies of their own beverage cultures.

The corpus does not include a systematic brewery or product catalogue, exact recipes, food-pairing recommendations, legal designations, nutrition advice, or a numerical range for every style. It also avoids creating extra pages for every marketing modifier merely to inflate the count.

## Priorities for a future edition

The most valuable expansion would be deeper, locally grounded coverage rather than more unverified names: community-reviewed terminology, additional historical records, fuller sensory documentation for underdescribed traditions, and independent review of the source-backed claims already present.
