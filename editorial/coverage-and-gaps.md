# Coverage and known gaps

## Where coverage is strongest

Conventional styles are anchored chiefly in BJCP 2021. Selected newer categories have explicit BA 2026 or other documented judging context; producer definitions and entry-placement suggestions remain distinct from standalone guideline entries. Additional research covers Nordic and Baltic farmhouse brewing, African grain traditions, South and East Asian fermentation and Indigenous American maize and cassava practices.

## Gaps that remain

This is not an exhaustive world census. Numerous regional names and practices in Africa, South and Southeast Asia, Latin America, the Middle East and the Pacific still need local-language and community-grounded research. European historical regional brewing also remains incomplete. The two Chinese archaeological profiles do not constitute a survey of contemporary Chinese beer.

The ingredient directory is a selected foundation, not a cultivar catalogue or an article for every botanical, malt product, hop variety, yeast strain or root. Further pages should be added when evidence and a meaningful conceptual distinction justify them. Ingredient comparisons cannot replace community accounts of a beverage's identity.

Historical depth varies. A judging guideline's background paragraph is not an archival monograph. Related ferments such as sake and makgeolli remain introductory comparisons, not full taxonomies of their own cultures. Quantitative recipe parameters and legal labels are not standardized across the corpus.

## Access limitations are part of the evidence

**39 subject articles cite at least one source whose access metadata mentions abstract, indexed, excerpted or partial material.** This is a conservative text-based inventory, not a quality score: an article can combine limited access to one paper with full access to another source. See each page's source notes and the [source register](../references/source-register.md) for the actual restriction.

## Next research priorities

Seek community review and local-language sources for terminology; original records for historical claims; independent sensory and process observations where only producer descriptions exist; and broader regional sampling before generalizing a laboratory study. Preserve the distinction between observed practice, an experimental adaptation and a contemporary judging category.

The corpus has no systematic brewery catalogue, current product-availability service, recipe database, dietary assessment or legal classification service. Its [validation](validation-report.md) checks structure and provenance consistency, not the truth of every interpretation.

## Scope after edition 1.6

The corpus now has **402** mixed subject articles. Four new drink profiles are **local or related-ferment references**, not four newly recognized universal judging styles. Several cultural case studies derive from just one primary sampling project or from secondary/producer sources and need independent community-language checking. Laboratory detection does not prove cultural practice, metabolic causality or food safety. Two environmental case studies have incompatible life-cycle boundaries. ## Edition 1.12 reading guardrails

Still needed: local-language and community review, inaccessible full methods, taxonomy and starter-source verification. The [source queue](source-review-queue-1-12.md) records tasks; do not infer public rituals or safety claims from indexed laboratory abstracts.

## Current coverage: research edition 1.15

The wiki now has **662 subject articles**, including **191 profiles, 65 ingredient references, 42 comparative categories, 65 cultural cases, 25 calendar entries, 60 microbiology articles, 52 process references, 21 serving-object guides, 33 sensory articles, 35 stories, 41 historical cases, 20 environmental articles and 12 food articles**. These represent unlike conceptual units and not 662 officially recognized styles. The [complete generated index](../navigation/all-atlas-subjects.md) is authoritative for present counts; earlier edition figures in this page remain archival snapshots.

[Edition 1.15](research-update-1-15.md) deepens three bounded questions: US federal tax/label classes and the dated 1991 ATF circular do not settle international or community vocabulary; a 2023 UK government consultation **applying to England** is not a legal outcome; and a 2021 one-lager bubble experiment and service glassware guide cannot certify each beer or bar. Direct brewery water ratios and supply-chain water footprint categories have different system boundaries, while laboratory pathogen challenges do not establish retail product safety. Access-limited BA water manuals and one-dish pairing studies need independent follow-up. The [1.15 research queue](source-review-queue-1-15.md) keeps specific work open, and **ATLAS-012 external review remains planned**.
