# World Beer Atlas research edition 1.13

## What was delivered

Edition 1.13 continues from the merged 1.12 Git tree with **35 new article files and 19 substantive source-cited revisions**, for **614 subject articles across thirteen sections**. All 186 previous named beer/tradition profile URLs are preserved. Some new entries are related food-drinks or archaeological evidence—not standardized beer styles. The work is indexed and linked from every subject directory and from [nine new evidence-led trails](../navigation/edition-1-13-reading-trails.md).

### Major research findings and comparisons

- **Jiahu, revisited:** A 2004 chemical-residue model and a 2026 starch/fungal microfossil reassessment answer different questions about Neolithic fermentation. Readers can compare the separate evidence types, vessels, possible substrates and process inferences, without inventing a modern recipe or lineage.
- **Sorghum-beer livelihoods and microbiology:** A 2007 Beninese household-enterprise paper is paired with 2011, 2013 and 2025 yeast studies whose sampling plans and identification methods differ. Tchapalo research separates fermentation yeasts, LAB inhibition assays and selected storage-spoilage challenges.
- **Kenyan Muratina:** University thesis evidence describes sugarcane-based fermentations involving porous *Kigelia* fruit fibres as yeast carriers. A different 2026 local ethnography mentions the beverage in Agikuyu Muslim marriage-practice discussions; neither source gives a general recipe, compulsory ceremony or safety guarantee.
- **Ugandan cereal drinks and commercial opaque beer:** Household bushera, a cautiously included Malwa name and commercial Zimbabwean Chibuku demonstrate why grain type, alcohol content, industrial specification and local food identity cannot be collapsed into one style.
- **Flavor and environment:** Three-concentrated-lager aldehyde-binding experiments, antioxidant assay results, water-reuse LCA and pilot engineering, and Czech packaging comparisons each retain their analytical endpoints and system boundaries.

### Technical and editorial improvements

The new subject files include **18 source-qualified factual relations across 16 articles** with local source IDs and written limitation qualifiers, in addition to existing editorial comparison links. The scoped name-and-alias index now covers **all 13 atlas subject families**, instead of the original three. The specification adds **seven stable IDs (ATLAS-087–093)**; the progress tracker, all directories, current inventory figures, and annotated source register are derived or updated alongside the research.

The [1.13 source register](../references/atlas-sources-1-13.md) maps **21 distinct works to 93 source uses** in new and revised pages, preserving actual full-text/abstract and indexed-excerpt limitations. The [independent-review queue](source-review-queue-1-13.md) records outstanding community, archaeological, brewing-science and local-language checks. Older edition source records and release reports remain untouched as historical evidence.

### Research limitations

Original source access varied. Some publisher pages returned only indexed abstracts or selective excerpts; references identify those limitations. Laboratory isolate counts, ancient residue proxies, observed household reports, industrial quality tests and environmental models must not be promoted into universal statements. No independent community or specialist approval occurred, and no public-health, food-safety or clinical recommendation is inferred.
