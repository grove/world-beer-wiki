# Research edition 1.9 — grain, law, ritual and microbial evidence

**Edition 1.9 · 28 October 2026 · 31 new subject articles, 18 existing-subject research extensions.** This edition begins from merged PR #9 (1.8). Prior reports are retained as historical evidence of what was checked at the time.

## What research adds

- **Vietnam:** rượu cần as a named fermented jar drink, distinct Ma and S’Tieng community reporting, nationally recognized craft, household economies, bamboo-straw service and occasions without fabricated dates.
- **Bhutan and the Eastern Himalayas:** ara's social role (abstract-limited) compared with the phab/neighboring starter bacterial isolation study, without equating a distilled drink with beer.
- **Ancient Mesopotamia:** CDLI catalog documentation of specific Ur III messenger rations, a full specialist analysis of brewing terms, material evidence and the limits of reconstructing a recipe.
- **England and Munich:** the 1830 parliamentary licensing dispute, the official law listing, Munich’s attributed 1812 beer-garden narrative, season and food-service connections.
- **Korea and China:** 42 nuruk samples, a separate makgeolli fermentation time course, Huangjiu process, its wheat qu, and contrasted manual/mechanized study designs.
- **Nubia:** Qasr Ibrim barley row-type ancient DNA, recorded as agricultural evidence and expressly not proof of beer brewing.

## Editorial distinctions

A heritage report is neither independent community endorsement nor microbiological safety evidence. A literature review is not a second independent experiment. A source accessible only by abstract is never described as fully read. No source is used to promise medical benefits, universal flavor, future calendar dates or a direct ancient-to-modern lineage. Individual titles and original-language terms are maintained with reported provenance.
