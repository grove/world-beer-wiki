# Coolship — a vessel within a fermentation system

In the traditional lambic process described by Spitaels and colleagues, hot wort cools overnight in a shallow open vessel before transfer for fermentation and maturation. The coolship is therefore a particular piece of equipment in a sequence, not a synonym for the complete beer.[^atlas-lambic-2014]

## What an equipment record should say

Describe the vessel's function, the material entering it, how it fits the process, and the source that documents its use. Dimensions, construction material, location and exposure conditions should be added only when the particular account provides them.

[The lambic brewing-season entry](../calendar/lambic-brewing-season.md) explains why local night conditions matter to the described cooling practice. [Spontaneous fermentation](../processes/spontaneous-fermentation.md) separates the inoculation question from the later behavior of the culture.

## A vessel is not the whole microbial environment

The existing [Lambic profile](../styles/lambic.md) also discusses maturation vessels and their microbial habitats. An account that attributes everything only to the coolship or only to outdoor air misses other documented parts of the production environment.[^atlas-lambic-context]

This provides a useful reading test: can a claim about a beer be supported by the equipment record alone, or does it require observations of the whole process? A picture of a shallow vessel cannot determine the final acidity, organisms, regional identity or sensory quality.

## Evidence note

This is not a construction plan, a guide to capturing unknown microorganisms or a claim that all beers using similar equipment are lambic. Historical uses of open cooling beyond the cited context remain a separate research topic.
