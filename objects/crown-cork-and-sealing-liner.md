# The crown cork is a crimped closure plus a sealing liner

## Look at the closure, not only the metal

William Painter's US Patent 468,226, granted on 2 February 1892, describes a bottle-sealing device combining a metal cap with a corrugated flange and compressible material against a specially formed bottle mouth. The original patent is evidence for an engineered seal and a dated intellectual-property record, not a general statement about the taste or microbiological quality of every beer bottle.[^v118-patent1892]

The American Society of Mechanical Engineers' landmark account places this invention in a broader evolution of filling and capping machinery. The original objective included retaining carbonated liquid and separating metal from the drink. A preserved or described machine shows a historic approach to packaging, not the precise equipment used by every brewery then or now.[^v118-asme-crown]

## An object belongs to a system

A cap needs a compatible glass finish, an appropriate liner, industrial equipment and a filled product. Its design may affect closure integrity and gas retention without determining whether the contents contain hop compounds, are shielded from light, or have been stored correctly. A cap is also not itself a returnable container or a sustainability life-cycle result.

For historical work distinguish a patent drawing, a product supplied commercially, a brewery's actual order records and a later museum description. The exact patent date is entered separately as a historical calendar event rather than misrepresented as a recurring beer festival.
