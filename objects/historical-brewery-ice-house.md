# An ice house is part of a cold-supply network, not a modern chiller

## Ice storage is a physical and seasonal technology

The Swiss National Museum's account of nineteenth-century brewing describes hillside cellars, ice ponds, ice gallows and block-ice transport. The ability to keep a cellar cool depended not only on the existence of a building but on winter weather, labor, insulation, transport and the amount of ice that survived until later use.[^v119-swissice26]

The University of Chicago Library's brewing exhibition similarly explains how ice houses preceded and overlapped mechanical cooling. It places natural ice within a changing network of lager production and distribution rather than presenting a single fixed architectural design that all breweries adopted.[^v119-chicagoice]

## The object cannot speak for all its workers

A preserved cellar photograph or diagram may show geometry and construction. It cannot by itself establish the source of ice, a particular worker's pay, the volume of beer sold, hygienic condition of every product or the greenhouse emissions of its supply chain. Technical heritage, local labor and later machine adoption require different records.

For an atlas object entry, store region, approximate period, documented ice source, building or apparatus, named brewery and the actual source of evidence. Do not offer guidance on ice harvesting, ammonia or entering historic machinery. The museum photographs are not reproduced without rights review.
