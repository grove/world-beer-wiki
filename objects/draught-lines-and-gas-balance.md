# Draught lines, taps and dispensing gas

A keg is not simply a small tank at the end of the brewery. Between keg and customer, beer may pass through a coupler, hose, trunk line, cooler and faucet, depending on the installation. Brewers Association guidance covers these components together with gas balance and maintenance because they function as a single service system.[^v114-draught]

Dispensing gas has more than one role: it helps move beer and can affect carbonation conditions. A nitrogenated product, a conventionally carbonated lager and a cask-conditioned bitter cannot be assumed to require identical equipment behavior. The public guidance describes why system balance matters but is not a substitute for specifications from an installation engineer or beer supplier.[^v114-draught]

Organic deposits and biofilms can accumulate in lines, as discussed in the retailer manual excerpt. That creates a maintenance concern even if the brewery delivered beer in good condition. Conversely, a healthy-looking foam head is not a microbial safety test for the line or glass. Equipment state and intentional serving style are separate questions.[^v114-draught-retail]
