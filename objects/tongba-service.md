# Tongba — vessel, extraction and serving practice

In the [Kodo ko jaanr profile](../styles/kodo-ko-jaanr.md), tongba connects a drinking vessel with a way of serving fermented finger millet: water draws liquid from the grain, and the drink is taken through a narrow straw. Refilling is part of the described practice.[^atlas-kodo-context]

## What exactly was measured?

The distinction between **fermented grain**, **added water** and **served liquid** matters. A laboratory alcohol value for one material cannot simply be assigned to every subsequent extraction or refill. The existing research profile explicitly keeps the serving context alongside the measured preparation.[^atlas-kodo-context]

| Record | Why retain it? |
|---|---|
| Fermented material | Identifies what entered the vessel. |
| Added water and serving stage | Identifies the drink actually being discussed. |
| Vessel and drinking tool | Describes service without inventing a separate recipe. |
| Local name and source | Avoids assuming all spellings or communities mean precisely the same thing. |

## A useful comparison without duplicate styles

The wiki keeps the vessel-associated term connected to the [named finger-millet preparation](../styles/kodo-ko-jaanr.md) instead of counting it as an automatically separate beer style. [Bhaati jaanr](../styles/bhaati-jaanr.md) and [sorghum and millet](../ingredients/sorghum-and-millet.md) offer adjacent grain and fermentation comparisons, but they do not establish identical serving practices.

This is an example of why an encyclopedia needs object and process pages as well as beverage pages: the object changes what a reader needs to know without necessarily creating a new beverage identity.

## Evidence note

This entry uses the existing corpus's source-backed serving account. It does not assign one compulsory vessel material, volume, alcohol strength or drinking etiquette to the entire Himalayan region. A fuller account should add locally reviewed terminology and directly documented objects.
