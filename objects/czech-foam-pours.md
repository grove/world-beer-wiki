# Czech foam pours in Pilsner Urquell's presentation

Pilsner Urquell presents **šnyt**, **hladinka** and **mlíko** as three named ways of pouring its beer, distinguished by their amount of foam. The producer describes different drinking impressions from those presentations. This is a serving distinction, not a claim that the three glasses contain separately brewed styles.[^atlas-pilsner-urquell]

## Production does not end the story

A tasting record that identifies only the beer and recipe can miss the presentation the drinker actually encountered. The producer's emphasis on trained tapsters makes service part of its account of quality, while remaining a producer's claim rather than an independently controlled sensory finding.[^atlas-pilsner-urquell]

For comparison, record the pour name, serving conditions and what was perceived. Do not infer exact liquid-to-foam fractions from an abbreviated description, or assume that the same name is used identically by every venue.

## Keep three levels separate

The [Czech Premium Pale Lager profile](../styles/czech-premium-pale-lager.md) describes a beer reference. [Decoction](../processes/decoction.md) explains a production operation. This page describes how one producer presents the finished beer. A reader can connect those levels without confusing them.

The same reasoning underlies the [flavor-detective guide](../sensory/aroma-is-not-an-ingredient.md): what is observed in the glass is valuable evidence, but it does not automatically identify every ingredient or process that preceded it.

## Evidence note

This introductory source does not supply a complete pouring protocol, a universal Czech etiquette rule or an objective ranking of the pours. It also does not demonstrate that a present serving practice has been unchanged since the brewery's foundation. Dated service records and independent sensory work would support a deeper article.



## Edition 1.14: newly sourced connections

Intentional Czech foam-pour choices belong within the broader service system. Brewers Association documentation explains that delivered beer depends on gas balance, line condition, cleaning, faucets and pouring; these are operational variables distinct from the cultural meaning of a named pour.[^v114-draught]

The manual does not define or rank all Czech traditions and cannot determine a drinker's preferred foam proportion. Compare [draught hardware](draught-lines-and-gas-balance.md) and [service-dependent sensory experience](../sensory/draught-service-vs-brewery-flavor.md). Technical quality and traditional serving form answer related but different questions.[^v114-draught]
