# Hydrometers, sample temperature and readable gravity

## A floating scale has a context

A brewing hydrometer is a physical instrument for comparing a liquid's density with a reference condition. Its position changes with the liquid's density, so a number on the stem needs a recorded scale and sample temperature. The published laboratory guide by Thesseling and colleagues distinguishes wort gravity from beer gravity and notes that carbon dioxide bubbles, sample condition and how the meniscus is read can affect a result.[^v116-lab]

## One object does not measure everything

An original-gravity measurement describes pre-fermentation wort. A final-gravity measurement describes an alcohol-containing sample; the two readings can be used for an approximate strength calculation, but that equation is not a direct measured ABV or a species-identification test. The ASBC/BA public method series lists specific-gravity determination and apparent-extract methods separately, underscoring how instruments and reporting terms differ.[^v116-methodVideos]

A temperature at which a hydrometer scale was calibrated is not automatically the sample's actual measurement temperature. Similarly, a handheld instrument's decimal display does not prove that it was correctly calibrated or that a foamy, stratified or poorly sampled vessel was representative.

## Interpret rather than prescribe

For documentation, record the instrument scale, calibration reference, sample temperature, degassing or mixing status, reading convention, and whether the result was observed or derived. This page is not a hardware-use procedure or an endorsement of uncalibrated home measurements.
