# Wari brewing vats and kero drinking cups

Archaeological interpretation of large brewing equipment and elite serving sets at Cerro Baúl; vessels evidence social practice but not taste.

## Production objects

The Cerro Baúl brewery contains positions for large ceramic fermentation vats and heating installations, allowing archaeologists to propose a sequence of milling, cooking and fermentation. These vessels are evidence for organized work and substantial capacity; they are not samples of an intact modern production system.[^v15-wari-2005]

## Serving objects

A separate study uses Wari *kero* cups and ceramic sets to examine how people interacted at elite feasts. A kero can communicate status and host-guest relationships. That is a different evidential role from a ceramic fermentation vat, even if both were associated with chicha.[^v15-wari-kero]

## Why distinction matters

Readers should not assume that a serving cup was a fermentation vessel, or that an archaeological cup can reveal serving temperature, foam, or the complete botanical recipe. Material evidence becomes more persuasive when matched with architectural position and other residues.[^v15-wari-2005]
