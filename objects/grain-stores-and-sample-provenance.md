# Grain stores, samples and moisture history in malting evidence

## A store is part of the material chain

Grain can remain between harvest and malting long enough for storage conditions to affect its properties. A 2024 scoping review of chemical pollutants in brewing discusses grain and malt storage and how elevated water activity may permit mould growth. These are literature-derived risk observations, not safe storage durations or recommended grain-moisture settings for a particular facility.[^v117-pollutants]

A 2026 review of the barley–malt–beer chain describes raw-material control, process stages and potential contaminant redistribution. It supports keeping storage and acceptance of the crop separate from later process chemistry; it does not convert a warehouse label into a verified laboratory result.[^v117-chain]

## Why the physical object matters

A sample taken from the top of a container, from one bag or from a prepared composite can tell different stories about the entire lot. The container's material, handling history, moisture exposure and time in storage may matter to an interpretation, even when the final research paper reports only a chemical concentration. A historical malt store cannot be retroactively judged by a modern test without an actual preserved sample.

The documentation record should include the type of store and its location if known, crop lot, dates, material condition, sampling method and source of any assay. It should not prescribe handling of suspected contaminated cereal or imply that a visually clean bin certifies grain safety.
