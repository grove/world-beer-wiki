# A metal beer can is not just bare aluminium or steel

## The container has more than one material layer

A beverage can's external shell may be described as steel or aluminium, but that is not enough to identify the full set of materials touching the beer. The EU's 2024 Regulation 2024/3190 addresses use of BPA and certain other bisphenols in food-contact materials, including some coatings. Its requirements have conditional scope and transitional rules; the act is not an analytical test of one named can or an automatic claim that all old or new liners use the same substances.[^v118-bpa-law]

The European Commission's legislative index notes the original regulation and a correction issued in 2026. This is a reason to verify the version, material category and date rather than copying a simple global statement like “all beer cans have BPA” or “a BPA alternative is necessarily safe.”[^v118-bpa-explainer]

## A historical steel can cannot reveal a modern liner

The Smithsonian preserves a Baltimore American Beer can and provides a physical description and business context. The catalogue does not identify the exact coating chemistry of every later can or certify its present food-contact compliance.[^v118-smithsonian-can]

A useful entry records the body material, documented inner surface or liner, product use, market, date and verified tests separately. Visual appearance and a tinny taste descriptor cannot substitute for chromatography or a regulatory compliance declaration.
