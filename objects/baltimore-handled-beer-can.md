# The Smithsonian's handled Baltimore beer can is an industrial object

## A single surviving object carries several histories

The Smithsonian National Museum of American History describes a metal can that held twelve fluid ounces of American Beer made by American Brewery, Inc. in Baltimore. Its catalogue identifies Continental Can Company, a hand-attached metal handle, a federal-tax inscription and the Walter Voigt brewing-history collection. The museum reports donor notes connecting the practice of attaching handles with a specific early-to-mid-twentieth-century manufacturing period. Those details describe one documented object and its provenance, not every American can or the exact day cans first went on sale.[^v118-smithsonian-can]

Crown Holdings' own corporate history records separate developments in metal closures, canmaking and industry expansion. This is producer-authored chronology, and it cannot be substituted for independent records of the museum object's ownership or use by consumers.[^v118-crown-history]

## Object catalogues are not sales totals

A museum can demonstrate what was manufactured and preserved. It does not by itself reveal how many people preferred canned beer, the flavor of the beer when fresh, the packaging liner formulation, or whether the exact format offered an environmental improvement. Rights notices apply to museum photographs; this wiki links to the collection record without copying media.
