# A dated 1899 ammonia-fitting catalogue is industrial material evidence

## What the museum object actually records

The Smithsonian National Museum of American History catalogues two printed sets of ammonia fittings and refrigerating-machine equipment from the Fred W. Wolf Company. One smaller catalogue is dated **1899**; the larger is described as the thirteenth edition without the same explicit date on the object page. The object documents the market and equipment vocabulary of the period, not the installation date of every American brewery refrigeration plant.[^v119-smithamm]

A University of Chicago Library historical exhibition describes Carl von Linde's early 1870s ammonia-based refrigeration machinery and how industrial cooling widened the opportunity to store and ship beer. That institutional overview is a secondary synthesis, not proof that a particular Wolf catalogue was used by Linde or on a named brewery floor.[^v119-chicagoice]

## Why it belongs in a beer atlas

Brewing has always depended on managing heat and fermentation conditions, but modern electrical cooling equipment should not be projected unchanged into nineteenth-century archives. Natural ice houses, vapor-compression systems and current industrial heat pumps are different physical objects and economic systems.

A museum photograph may have rights constraints even where catalogue metadata is public. This page links to the catalogue without reproducing images or offering ammonia equipment instructions.
