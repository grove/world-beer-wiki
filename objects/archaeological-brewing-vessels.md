# Ancient brewing vessels: what residues can and cannot show

## A vessel is a context, not a recipe

At Mijiaya, researchers combined ceramic form, use context, starch grains, phytoliths and chemical signals when proposing brewing activity. At Raqefet Cave, the study's tools included stone mortars and microscopic wear/residue evidence. Either case shows why context matters: a pot of grain traces is not automatically a beer mug.[^v13-mijiaya][^v13-raqefet]

## Chemical evidence is another kind of clue

Ancient Egyptian vessel analyses examined residue chemistry using infrared and mass spectrometry. Such instruments can help identify classes of materials after centuries, but do not reproduce the precise sensory experience of a finished drink, a complete recipe or who participated in its service.[^v13-egypt]
