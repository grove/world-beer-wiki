# English hop oasts: stowage, kiln and cowl

A picturesque round tower in Kent is also a piece of industrial equipment. Historic England describes the linked parts of a hop-drying complex: an adjoining stowage building for handling and cooling, a heated drying floor, furnace arrangements and roof ventilation. The familiar cowl is only one design detail in a broader family of regional building forms.[^v114-oasts]

Site placement was functional. Hop cones arrived wet and perishable from nearby fields; a drying system had to absorb the flow of harvested crop. Once dried, cones cooled before being pressed into pockets. The same structure therefore supported material movement and storage as well as heat exchange.[^v114-oasts]

The source notes architectural variation between southern and western English hop regions, including square and round kilns. A surviving structure may have been rebuilt, enlarged or adapted after hop production declined. It must not be assigned a date of construction or a former throughput from appearance alone.[^v114-oasts]
