# A brewery water-use ratio is not a product water footprint

## Two numbers with different boundaries

A brewery can record how much water enters its site and compare it with beer volume. A product water footprint asks a broader question that may include indirect supplies, crop cultivation and the type and location of water use. The Water Footprint Network's small-business calculator explicitly separates direct and indirect components and green, blue and grey categories. A site ratio is therefore not interchangeable with a supply-chain footprint. The calculator was designed for a European context in 2015, not as a single global water-scarcity benchmark.[^v115-wfn]

The Brewers Association's public sustainability directory treats energy, water, wastewater and waste reduction as connected but distinct operational topics. These are useful categories for asking what a brewery actually measures rather than assuming one efficient process proves an environmental ranking.[^v115-basust]

## Beware inaccessible benchmarks

The association's 2026 Water & Wastewater manual landing page describes conservation and wastewater-management aims, but its full technical manual requires member access. It cannot be cited here for exact numerical targets or industry-wide percent improvements.[^v115-baWater]

A defensible comparison reports the **numerator, denominator, geography, reporting period and system boundary**. It asks whether withdrawals, consumptive use, discharge volumes, pollution or upstream agriculture are included. A high agricultural water footprint and a low onsite water ratio can both be true without contradiction.

Read [direct versus indirect brewery water](direct-and-indirect-water-in-brewing.md), [wastewater](brewery-water-and-wastewater.md) and [LCA boundaries](how-to-compare-brewing-footprints.md). Neither a database interface nor a manual title supplies a verified global average.
