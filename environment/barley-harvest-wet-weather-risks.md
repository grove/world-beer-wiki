# Rain near barley harvest changes risk, but not every field equally

## More than a change in annual rainfall

A 2026 barley pre-harvest sprouting review links rain, humidity, temperature, seed dormancy and plant traits to the risk of untimely germination. A weather event near maturity can affect crop value for malting, but the review does not assign one global threshold that tells when every farm's crop will sprout. Cultivars and exposure histories modify susceptibility.[^v117-sprouting-review]

A separate 2026 proteomics abstract reports a drought-gradient trial involving three barley cultivars and altered grain proteins, kernel metrics and malt quality. Water limitation during grain filling is not the same event as wet pre-harvest conditions: the timing and mechanisms differ. Combining the two into an undifferentiated “climate harms beer” statistic would erase their research designs.[^v117-drought-study]

## Why projections need stated boundaries

A barley supply argument might concern potential crop yield, kernel quality, crop disease, post-harvest storage or the downstream energy burden of malting. These are different endpoints. A regional growing season can also vary from year to year and need not coincide with a fixed beer festival.

Record the region, time of the reported event, cultivar, crop stage and measured endpoint. Estimates about future climate or brewery costs require additional modelling and economic data beyond the two studies.
