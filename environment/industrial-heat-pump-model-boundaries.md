# A 2026 brewery heat-pump model is not an electricity bill for every brewhouse

## One generalized brewery as an analytical reference

Padullés and colleagues' open 2026 paper proposes three ways to electrify brewing heat through pinch analysis: a centralized high-temperature heat pump, a two-pump approach and a more integrated system that includes vapor recompression. Their generalized brewery has a reference thermal demand drawn from prior studies; the reported electricity scenarios compare design concepts rather than recorded bills at each brewery worldwide.[^v119-pinch26]

Liu and colleagues' 2025 global model makes an even broader projection of heat-pump decarbonization under assumed boiler fuels and regional electricity conditions. The resulting technical potential must not be treated as current verified reductions. It also covers Chinese baijiu separately; the scale of spirits processing cannot silently become a beer-specific plant result.[^v119-heatglobal25]

## Why a heat pump is not just a replacement fuel

An industrial heat pump transfers heat from a cooler stream to a warmer one using external energy. Its potential depends on temperature lift, when cooling and heating demands coincide, electricity carbon intensity, equipment and control, and how much usable heat is otherwise rejected. The Brewers Association's public Energy Sustainability directory identifies this as a management domain, but the full manual's detailed benchmarking has not been independently inspected for this edition.[^v119-baenergy26]

For technical interpretation, report model assumptions, function unit, plant scale, delivered thermal energy, electricity demand, investment, and any measured baseline. Do not read a payback-period scenario as a proposal suitable for every small brewery. See [existing heat and refrigeration](brewery-heat-and-refrigeration.md), [wort cooling heat recovery](../processes/wort-cooling-and-heat-recovery.md), and [the contrasting field study](china-brewhouse-field-test-vs-global-model.md).
