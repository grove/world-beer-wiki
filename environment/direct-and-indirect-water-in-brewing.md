# Direct and indirect water in brewing supply chains

## Where is the water being counted?

Water can enter a brewery as an ingredient, cleaning supply or utility flow; a separate accounting exercise can trace water associated with barley and other upstream supplies. Water Footprint Network's public small-business tool distinguishes **direct** from **indirect** water footprints. This 2015 European-context interface treats water supply, discharge and pollutant information separately, instead of equating all water drawn with permanently consumed water.[^v115-wfn]

The network's background material points to the 2011 Water Footprint Assessment Manual as a methodological reference. That establishes provenance for the framework, not a published numeric footprint for this specific wiki or any of its example beers.[^v115-wfnref]

## The place of practical guidance

The Brewers Association describes water and wastewater as important management issues for breweries. Its public 2026 manual description does not disclose the member-only technical chapters and cannot resolve whether any proposed intervention meets local environmental discharge requirements.[^v115-baWater]

To use a claim responsibly, ask whose water is counted, whether supply-chain crops are included, whether a volume represents withdrawal or consumption, and where any pollution impact falls. Water scarcity is geographically situated: two identical liters of consumption are not automatically equivalent in different basins and seasons. Product footprints need functional-unit and methodological context.

The source tools here are explanations, not results of a brewery audit. Follow [on-site ratios and product footprints](brewery-water-ratio-versus-water-footprint.md) and [the brewing water ingredient](../ingredients/water.md).
