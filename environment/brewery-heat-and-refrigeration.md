# Thermal energy and refrigeration in brewing

Brewhouse heat, wort cooling and refrigeration have real energy costs. A 2026 systematic review identifies water heating, mashing, boiling, cooling, fermentation and cold storage as important stages, with brewery scale and equipment changing the balance.[^v14-energy]

## One stage can solve several problems

Boiling can contribute to bittering reactions and volatile removal, while also requiring thermal energy. A pilot study of [dimethyl sulfide](../processes/wort-boiling-and-dms.md) illustrates why chemical and energy goals are considered together, not as unrelated concerns.[^v14-dms]

## Avoid magic numbers

A single brewery energy intensity hides differences in heat recovery, brewery size, packaging and climate. The review reports illustrative values, but this article does not rank beer traditions by emissions or imply that a process used by a community is intrinsically wasteful.[^v14-energy]

## Edition 1.19: newly sourced connections

Heat recovery is not just a matter of adding a heat pump to a brewery diagram. Padullés and colleagues' 2026 open model analyzes a generalized brewery's heating and cooling streams with pinch analysis and compares different industrial heat-pump integrations. Its assumed demand and derived electricity cases are design comparisons rather than measurements across all operating breweries.[^v119-pinch26]

Huang and colleagues' 2025 field study evaluates process heat and wort cooling at a large Chinese industrial brewery. Its reported losses and improvements concern the particular site, baseline and modeled alternatives; they should not be quoted as global craft-brewery efficiency standards.[^v119-fieldheat25]

To compare energy claims, report the hot and cold streams, seasonal or daily scheduling, purchased fuel and electricity and study boundary.
