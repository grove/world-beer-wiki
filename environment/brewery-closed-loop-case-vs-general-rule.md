# A closed-loop brewery claim is not a universal LCA result

A 2025 open-access paper examines closed-loop supply chains and circular interventions in an industrial Chinese brewery context, reporting changes in operational greenhouse-gas intensity, water use, carbon capture and packaging recovery. The combination is useful because it treats brewery operations, incoming resources and distribution systems together rather than treating a single recycling measure as the whole footprint.[^v114-closedloop]

The results require reading the study's boundaries. Operational Scope 1–2 intensity, a modeled product life cycle, and a returnable-packaging scenario use different denominators. A large brewery's reported improvements do not automatically apply to a small brewery, a different electricity grid or a distribution network with long transport distances. The paper's scenario assumptions are part of the finding.[^v114-closedloop]

A separate 2026 review treats spent grain and yeast reuse as possible circular pathways but stresses technical and economic readiness. That literature context is a counterweight to thinking that every claimed valorization route is already deployed at scale. A complete comparison would need functional units, transport routes, process energy, material quality, downstream substitution and independent measurement.[^v114-circular]

Compare [environmental footprint methods](how-to-compare-brewing-footprints.md), [water and wastewater](brewery-water-and-wastewater.md), and [residue studies](hot-trub-and-spent-yeast-valorization.md). None of these records justifies giving every brewery a numeric carbon grade or ranking traditional and industrial practices by assumed virtue.[^v114-closedloop]

## Edition 1.19: newly sourced connections

Wu and colleagues' 2025 open Tsingtao case reports a combination of fermentation CO₂ capture, packaging reuse and wastewater or energy-related measures. Its operational greenhouse-gas intensity figures and claimed improvements reflect one study boundary and should be retained as an attributed case rather than a universal climate benefit for every brewery.[^v119-closed25]

The GHG Protocol's categories distinguish direct emissions, purchased electricity and other indirect emissions from raw materials and logistics. A circular measure can affect more than one part of the value chain, but total reductions still require a consistent organizational boundary, double-counting checks and independently checked activity data.[^v119-ghgfaq]

This source-grounded comparison adds [scope 1/2/3 accounting](scope-1-2-3-brewing-emissions.md) and [fermentation CO₂ is not automatic net zero](../categories/fermentation-co2-recovery-is-not-net-zero.md). Single-site numbers should not become promises about other beer styles, geographic regions or consumers.
