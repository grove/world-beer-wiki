# Hot trub and spent yeast: what reuse studies actually establish

Environmental discussions sometimes label every residue a “resource” before examining its composition and treatment requirements. Brewing produces distinct streams, including spent grain, hot trub and surplus yeast. A 2025 study analyzed selected hot-trub and residual-yeast samples from top- and bottom-fermented beer production as **candidate materials** for agricultural applications.[^v114-waste]

The analytical work included elemental composition, phenolics and screening for a specified panel of 57 mycotoxins. Its findings inform whether particular sampled streams deserve further testing. They do not constitute permission to apply any brewery sludge to soil or a guarantee that all brewery by-products have identical contaminants, concentrations or legal status.[^v114-waste]

A 2026 literature review widens the lens to spent grain and yeast valorization pathways, considering technology maturity, economics, feed, food, biotechnology and energy uses. A potentially useful material does not automatically produce an environmental benefit: collection, drying, processing and downstream substitution all need a defined functional unit and local scenario.[^v114-circular]
