# Brewery water reuse: contrasting Italian LCA and Portuguese pilot evidence

## Water savings do not settle every environmental endpoint

A 2026 life-cycle assessment of brewery water reclamation in southern Italy compared a reuse scenario with conventional water supply. The indexed study highlights lower fresh water demand alongside additional burdens in some impact categories, with ultrafiltration identified as an important modeled hotspot. The result depends on the facility’s assumptions and system boundaries; it cannot rank all breweries or water technologies by a single environmental number.[^v113-italy-water-2026]

## A pilot asks a different question

A 2025 Portuguese B-WaterSmart pilot examined several treatment technologies, including ultrafiltration, ozone, biologically active carbon and reverse osmosis, then reported a demonstration brewing run using reclaimed water. Its monitored results describe those treatment trains and test conditions. Showing that selected schemes met specified laboratory criteria is not an unconditional declaration about all wastewater, all operators or public-health safety.[^v113-portugal-water-2025]

## Compare the boundaries before drawing a lesson

The Italian paper asks about potential life-cycle tradeoffs, while the Portuguese pilot investigates performance and acceptance at a particular treatment plant. Combined, they show why water footprint, energy use, treatment residuals and regulatory oversight belong in separate rows of a useful environmental comparison. A technology can reduce one burden while increasing another; independent full methods review remains necessary before transferring a result to another site.[^v113-italy-water-2026][^v113-portugal-water-2025]
