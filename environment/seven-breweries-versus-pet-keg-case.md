# Seven microbreweries and a PET-keg LCA: different transport assumptions

## What the first comparison actually covered

The 2022 work of Morgan and colleagues analyzed seven small brewery value chains, with scenarios replacing single-use glass bottles and evaluating PET versus reusable steel kegs. In their modeled cases, steel-to-PET substitution could increase impacts on shorter delivery routes, while savings were possible under some longer-distance van deliveries. Their abstract also attributes importance to transport coordination. These findings are conditional on modeled deliveries and local supply-chain characteristics.[^v118-lca22]

## Why a different study can prefer PET

The 2024 paper by Marrucci and colleagues compared PET-keg draught beer against glass bottles and aluminium cans in a different product environmental footprint system. Its abstract reported a particularly favorable PET-keg option. It does not follow that a reusable steel keg delivered in the first paper's logistics system was tested against that option on identical assumptions.[^v118-lca24]

A third, 2025 Czech brewery-scale study introduces still another context: production scale, packaging, exporting and transport are part of the result. Its open abstract highlights the burdens of nonreturnable bottles in export situations. It is not independent proof that every can or all returnable bottles have the same impact in every region.[^v118-lca-czech25]

## Compare systems before drawing conclusions

A fair table would record beer volume at consumption, brewery size, transport mode and distance, reusable-container round trips, washing energy and water, packaging losses, recycled content and end-of-life choices. This page is a comparison of published methods and reported conclusions, not an updated footprint calculation or certification.
