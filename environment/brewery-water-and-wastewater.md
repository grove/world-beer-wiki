# Brewery water use, cleaning and wastewater

Water enters the brewery in the beer itself and also in cleaning, cooling and other processes. A 2025 Greek case study measured water use and explored wastewater reuse options rather than merely invoking a generic “liters per liter” slogan.[^v14-water]

## What one site found

The study describes an industrial brewery with total water consumption of 5.6 hectoliters per hectoliter of beer and a treatment plant with greater energy use than anticipated. Those are observations for its named case, not targets applicable to farmhouse batches, small breweries and every factory worldwide.[^v14-water]

## Reuse requires boundaries

The researchers distinguish proposed reuse pathways, including restricted irrigation, from utility water needs. An attractive numerical reduction in freshwater demand does not by itself demonstrate that wastewater is fit for direct ingredient use. Link this article with [brewing water](../ingredients/water.md) and [energy-intensive brewing](brewery-heat-and-refrigeration.md).

## Edition 1.13: newly sourced connections

A 2026 Italian life-cycle study of brewery water reclamation reports water-saving potential alongside new burdens attributable to treatment. A 2025 Portuguese pilot separately investigates treatment performance and a limited brewing demonstration. An LCA result and a monitored treatment pilot answer different questions; neither grants a general regulatory or safety exemption for water reused in another brewery.[^v113-italy-water-2026][^v113-portugal-water-2025]
