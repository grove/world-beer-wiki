# A Chinese brewhouse field test and a global heat-pump scenario answer different questions

## A plant measurement and a theoretical potential are not interchangeable

A 2025 field study by Huang and colleagues examined brewhouse thermal-energy flows at a large brewery in China, identifying losses in steam-supplied processes and considering improvements to wort cooling and waste-heat recovery. The public publisher abstract reports process-specific savings and estimated paybacks. These results refer to that plant's steam distribution and equipment, not all plants in China or any traditional brewery.[^v119-fieldheat25]

Liu and colleagues' 2025 global model, by contrast, estimates how industrial heat pumps *could* reduce emissions under different boiler and electricity scenarios. Its avoided-carbon figures are technical potential, not an independently metered change at the field-test brewery.[^v119-heatglobal25]

Padullés and colleagues' 2026 generalized brewery model goes further into temperature matching, pinch analysis and different electrification designs. That work is a useful *third* comparison, but it does not reproduce Huang's measured baselines or establish that the same retrofit is financially optimal in every region.[^v119-pinch26]

## A better way to compare the papers

Keep the study object (observed site, global model, representative process), input fuels, sampling time, thermal/electric units, electricity grid and payback assumptions distinct. A % reduction at one site is not the same denominator as modeled global tonnes CO₂.
