# Mahewu grain choices and the limits of climate-smart claims

Food-science researchers interested in Southern African cereal drinks have investigated replacing part of the maize input with sorghum or millets, crops often discussed as potentially climate-resilient. A Zimbabwean mahewu survey found ingredient availability was an important reason for grain choice. This is a legitimate *food-system* question, but the survey does not measure global warming potential or water use for a standardized litre of drink.[^v112-mahewu-2024]

## Processing matters as much as the crop name

Cooking a porridge uses heat; cereal production requires land and water; storage and packaging create further impacts. A comparison would have to account for yield, transport, fuel, waste and the actual functional unit. The 2026 starter-culture paper focuses on microbial performance and storage at different temperatures, not on a complete life-cycle assessment.[^v112-mahewu-2026]

## What could be researched next

Direct measurements of cultivation, household fuel, batch losses and competing uses for millet or sorghum would be more valuable than an unsupported blanket claim that a single 'ancient grain' always reduces environmental impact. This page connects household supply and fermentation ecology while refusing to assign a numeric carbon footprint that neither study measured.[^v112-mahewu-2024]
