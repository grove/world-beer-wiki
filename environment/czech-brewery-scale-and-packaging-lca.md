# Czech brewery scale, packaging and export: an LCA case

## The measured system is larger than the brewhouse

A 2025 life-cycle assessment compared Czech brewing operations using production data and modeling of malt, packaging and distribution. Its abstract reports different impacts across studied brewery scales, with energy and wastewater contributing to differences. The useful lesson is to declare the functional unit and facility boundaries, not to publish “small breweries are always worse” as an ecological law.[^v113-czech-lca-2025]

## Returnable glass and international shipping change the answer

The study examines packaging and transport routes, highlighting burdens associated with nonreturnable glass in international trade and the potential advantages of other systems under the conditions modeled. A can, refillable bottle and single-use bottle should not be compared without recycling, weight, journey and return-cycle assumptions. Brewery location and the consumer distribution system are part of the calculation.[^v113-czech-lca-2025]

## Avoid turning case studies into universal league tables

The Italian water-reclamation LCA illustrates the same underlying problem: environmental improvements in one category can create tradeoffs in others. Studies using different electricity grids, facilities, volumes and impact methods should not have their headline emissions figures casually ranked. This page explains what a rigorous comparison would need, while leaving a full independent appraisal of the 2025 paper’s model for later review.[^v113-czech-lca-2025][^v113-italy-water-2026]

## Edition 1.18: newly sourced connections

The 2025 Czech brewery-scale LCA considers malt production, plant scale, packaging and transport in its particular study setting, and its public abstract highlights nonreturnable export bottles. The result cannot be treated as the footprint of every Czech, European or imported beer.[^v118-lca-czech25] The 2022 seven-microbrewery LCA in another setting reports potential gains from coordinated distribution and reusable glass, while some PET-keg substitutions remain context-dependent.[^v118-lca22]

Different datasets, electricity systems, delivery distances, return rates and functional units must be retained when comparing the two. Follow [the two-case interpretation](seven-breweries-versus-pet-keg-case.md) and [returnable cycle assumptions](returnable-bottles-and-system-losses.md). No universal glass-or-can environmental ranking is asserted.
