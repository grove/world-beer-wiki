# Beer, agriculture and environment

The chain from growing crops to serving a beer has resource and climate consequences. These are evidence-based comparisons—not a ranking of cultures by environmental virtue.

## New in research edition 1.4

- [Extreme weather and the barley-to-beer supply chain](climate-and-barley-supply.md) — 2018 modelled drought and heat scenarios and their potential impacts on barley and beer trade.
- [Climate change, hop harvests and alpha-acid content](climate-and-hop-crops.md) — European regional observations and 2050 projections, not worldwide variety-specific predictions.
- [Brewers’ spent grain and circular resource use](spent-grain-and-circular-use.md) — Brewery spent grain after mashing and the limits of general life-cycle claims.
- [Brewery water use, cleaning and wastewater](brewery-water-and-wastewater.md) — Industrial Greek brewery case study on water use, treatment and reuse, not a universal benchmark.
- [Thermal energy and refrigeration in brewing](brewery-heat-and-refrigeration.md) — Systematic review of energy-intensive brewery operations, particularly scale differences.
- [How to compare brewing environmental footprints](how-to-compare-brewing-footprints.md) — Method-focused comparison of competing resources and limits of brewery LCA studies.
