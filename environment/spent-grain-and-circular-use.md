# Brewers’ spent grain and circular resource use

The solid grain residue after wort separation is a by-product, but often a potential resource. A 2024 critical review describes uses ranging from feed and composting to food ingredients, energy recovery and other valorisation pathways.[^v14-circular]

## Why the “best” use is contextual

The review notes that breweries differ in local animal-feed demand, energy supply, transport and access to processing infrastructure. A route attractive for one site may create more energy or material demand elsewhere. The authors highlight a shortage of comparable lifecycle and cost assessments for some alternatives.[^v14-circular]

## What remains in the material

Studies of barley malt and its by-products describe fiber- and protein-rich residual material, but its exact composition depends on grain and extraction. See [spent grain as an ingredient](../ingredients/brewers-spent-grain.md) and [malt quality](../ingredients/barley-beta-glucans.md) before assuming every lot is suitable for a particular food use.[^v14-malt]

## Edition 1.7: newly sourced connections

A 2026 open review broadens the possibilities for spent grain utilization while emphasizing its high moisture content, spoilage risk, food safety, cost and technology readiness. A theoretical reuse pathway is not automatically a viable commercial product or a net environmental improvement.[^v17-spent-2026]

The companion [handling and circularity article](spent-grain-food-safety-and-circularity.md) separates feed, food, materials and biorefinery options. Any comparative carbon claim needs a stated system boundary, distribution assumptions and credible substituted products.



## Edition 1.14: newly sourced connections

A 2026 literature review groups spent grain and surplus yeast as potential circular-bioeconomy materials, but distinguishes technologies by readiness and economics. Agricultural application, extraction for food, energy recovery and disposal each require separate material-quality and functional-unit considerations.[^v114-circular]

A 2025 study's chemical screening of selected hot trub and residual yeast streams is an early laboratory step, **not** proof that all residues are suitable fertilizers or safe ingredients. Compare [hot trub and residual yeast evidence](hot-trub-and-spent-yeast-valorization.md) and [surplus yeast as material](../ingredients/surplus-brewers-yeast-as-material.md).[^v114-waste]
