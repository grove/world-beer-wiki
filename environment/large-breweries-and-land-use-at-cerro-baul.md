# Brewing scale, irrigation and land use at Cerro Baúl

Historical resource-landscape connections at one Wari site, not a numerical sustainability or carbon-footprint estimate.

## Why a brewery needs a landscape

The Cerro Baúl study places Wari colonization and brewing within irrigated agricultural development of the Moquegua region. Brewing capacity, crop production, fuel, vessels and organized labor connected the large facility to its settlement environment. Such context adds depth to ingredient histories but is not a quantified ecological life-cycle assessment.[^v15-wari-2005]

## Specialization changes the question

A subsequent study argues against assuming ubiquitous household brewing in the sampled Wari province. If activity was concentrated in state-linked production, archaeological signatures and material distribution will differ from a hypothetical network of domestic producers. This inference concerns the excavation study area, not an empire-wide economic census.[^v15-wari-2024]

## What cannot be calculated

The excavation does not report the modern-style mass balance of heating fuel, water withdrawal, waste treatment and packaging required to estimate liters-per-unit environmental impact. It would be misleading to rank Wari chicha against industrial beer on carbon or water intensity based solely on ancient vessel capacity.[^v15-wari-2005]
