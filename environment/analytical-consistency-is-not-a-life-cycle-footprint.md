# A consistent beer test is not a comparable environmental footprint

## Two very different uses of the word comparable

A brewery may collect sample results for colour, bitterness, gravity or pH at different stages of production. The ASBC Laboratory Proficiency Program lists distinct recommended methods by analyte, including colour, bitterness and alcohol. Consistency of analytical methods helps interpret a *beer-quality variable*; it does not supply the energy, land, water or packaging inventory needed for a life-cycle comparison.[^v116-proficiency]

An open laboratory brewing paper similarly separates endpoints such as colour, extract and estimated strength. Each requires sample and instrument context. None can be converted into a climate impact per hectolitre without additional environmental data and a separately defined functional unit.[^v116-lab]

## A brewery result is not a supply-chain result

A lab may report a repeatable SRM measurement on a filtered sample and still know nothing from that assay about barley cultivation, electricity, wastewater treatment or package return rates. Conversely, a life-cycle study's modelled greenhouse-gas intensity need not explain why a particular glass looks hazy.

The distinction matters for comparing small and large breweries. A repeatable analytical protocol, a sample count, a factory process ratio and a cradle-to-grave environmental result are evidence on different boundaries. Treating one as the other can create unjustified sustainability rankings.

A defensible environmental comparison records site, date, functional unit, included stages, measured versus modelled flows, uncertainty and data source, rather than adding beer-quality metrics into an unsupported score.
