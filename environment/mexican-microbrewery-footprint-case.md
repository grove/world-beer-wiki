# Footprint estimates from surveyed Mexican microbreweries

Perea-Eguia, Wakida and Hernandez published a 2024 study using questionnaires and external emission factors to estimate carbon and water footprints for surveyed **Mexican microbreweries**. The abstract identifies electricity as a major direct carbon contributor and raw-material transportation as an indirect contributor under its calculation framework.[^v16-mexico-lca]

## Read the water number correctly

The paper reports a large fraction of its calculated water footprint associated with **raw-material production**. That is a different concept from the number of litres a brewery draws and cleans per litre of beer. Comparing those figures without matching system boundaries can produce enormous but spurious differences.[^v16-mexico-lca]

## Compare a Japanese study carefully

A separate 2025 Japanese small-brewery assessment modeled a litre of bottled beer with a specified cradle-to-gate boundary and **excluded crop cultivation**, while including packaging and processing. Its greenhouse-gas result is not a substitute for the Mexican paper's measurement approach. Differences in scope, geography, packaging and data must be stated before interpreting rankings.[^v16-japan-lca]

## Evidence boundary

The Mexican estimates are outputs of a reported model and survey, not measurements for every beer or every Mexican brewery. No consumer-facing carbon ranking is justified without compatible methods.
