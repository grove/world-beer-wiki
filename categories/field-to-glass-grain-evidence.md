# From barley field to beer glass: which evidence answers which question?

## A connected chain does not mean a single kind of proof

Barley may be described in an agricultural contract, tested for fungal infection, assayed for metabolites, processed into malt, and finally consumed as beer. Those are different objects, institutions and sample populations. A 2026 controlled study tracks particular Fusarium-associated compounds through malt and beer using an inoculated batch. It provides analytical process evidence, not a market census or the safety status of other products.[^v117-fusarium-trial]

A contract-farming study in Ethiopia, by contrast, uses economic survey and statistical techniques to ask how market arrangements affected sampled malt-barley producers. It does not measure toxin transfer in any particular beer or establish that a private buyer's quality category is a cultural tradition.[^v117-contracts-2021]

The European Union's food-contaminant regulation organizes legal maxima by **specified products and food matrices**, with amendments and dates. A grain threshold is not automatically a finished-beer figure. Nor does an existing regulation mean a particular commercial batch was inspected or approved. Questions of applicability need the current consolidated text and appropriate specialists.[^v117-eu-contaminants]

## A practical research record

Keep the evidence layers separate: field and harvest conditions; named farm contract and household survey; grain and malt analytical sample; processing experiment; finished beverage label; and any regulator or community testimony. Use typed comparisons only when the source and uncertainty justify them.

This directory is an educational map, not an inspection, legal opinion, ingredient procurement plan or claim about a living tradition.
