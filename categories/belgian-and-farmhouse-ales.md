# Belgian families and farmhouse comparisons

Belgian-associated beer names describe several kinds of things: sensory families, local brewing traditions, and sometimes institutional provenance. “Farmhouse” introduces another level of context. This guide keeps those meanings visible rather than grouping them under one supposedly universal Belgian yeast character.

## Trappist is a provenance question

The International Trappist Association describes Trappist identity in terms of origin, with separate criteria for its Authentic Trappist Product label. Those criteria concern production near the abbey, community supervision and the destination of profits. They do not specify one color, strength or fermentation flavor. The association itself rejects treating Trappist as a single brewing style.[^wiki-trappist-faq]

For this wiki, record a style name and a documented institutional identity separately. Do not infer the latter from words such as *dubbel*, *tripel* or *abbey*, or from a beer's packaging. This guide does not maintain a current list of certified products.

## Saison and Bière de Garde need different descriptions

The modern Saison reference emphasizes dryness, expressive fruity-spicy fermentation and a range of colors and strengths. Spices and sourness are optional. A peppery impression is not proof that pepper was added, and Brettanomyces is not required.[^bjcp-2021-25b]

[Bière de Garde](../styles/biere-de-garde.md), from northern France, is a separate malt-led comparison: the cited reference permits blonde, amber and brown forms and either ale or lager yeast followed by cold conditioning. A farmhouse association does not make it a less bitter Saison. The guideline also warns that mustiness from poorly handled imports should not be mistaken for authentic character.[^bjcp-2021-24c]

## Farmhouse is broader than a modern style label

Read [Sahti](../styles/sahti.md), [Kornøl](../styles/kornol.md), [Stjørdalsøl](../styles/stjordalsol.md), [Vossaøl](../styles/vossaol.md), [Keptinis](../styles/keptinis.md) and [Gotlandsdricke](../styles/gotlandsdricke.md) as individually documented traditions. Their shared placement in a farmhouse comparison is not a claim of common recipes, organisms or sensory targets. The [traditional and Indigenous brewing guide](traditional-and-indigenous-beers.md) provides the cultural scope.

Likewise, [kveik](../ingredients/kveik.md) is a culture reference, not a replacement name for every Nordic farmhouse beer. Keeping ingredient, local practice and market style separate lets the wiki describe modern adaptations without erasing the traditions that inspired them.
