# Gluten-free beer: ingredients, process and evidence

'Gluten-free beer' can refer to a finished product property, but research on alternative starch sources often asks a different question: whether an ingredient can be brewed at all. The review literature covers sorghum, millet, rice, maize and pseudocereals as substitutions for some or all conventional malt inputs, noting extract, flavor, nitrogen and foam complications. A list of ingredients cannot certify an individual product's gluten status.[^v110-gluten-review]

## Beware the 40-percent substitution trap

A 2022 trial used a 40-percent share of alternative grains alongside barley malt, with gelatinized and ungelatinized comparisons. Its measured process results may inform wort design, but they must not be relabeled 'gluten-free beer produced experimentally' without qualification. This common category error illustrates the difference between using a gluten-free *grain* and producing a tested gluten-free *beer*.[^v110-gluten-study]

## Useful questions for reading research

Check the grain mixture, whether malting or enzymatic conversion was used, how fermentable sugars and soluble protein were measured, and whether finished-product gluten validation was actually performed. Even if a trial achieves desirable sensory performance, that alone says nothing about cross-contact or legal labeling requirements in another market. The atlas provides chemical comparisons, not medical or regulatory advice.[^v110-gluten-review]

## Evidence and limitations

No product-level coeliac safety judgment or single regulatory threshold is inferred. Technical descriptions belong to the actual study and jurisdiction. These limitations should be retained when quoting the article or extracting relationships.
