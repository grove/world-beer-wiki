# Fermentation acidity is not a safety certificate

Several fermentation studies report a fall in pH and increased prevalence of lactic acid bacteria. This is evidence of chemical and ecological change in the studied samples, **not a certificate that a drink is safe to consume**. Different hazards, handling conditions and microbial methods require separate questions. The Tapirapé cauim study reports progressive acidification but was not a clinical safety assessment.[^v111-cauim-bacteria]

## What a detected organism does not establish

The 1995 kunun-zaki study identified organisms under specific culture conditions; such methods do not prove the absence of other microbes. A 2025 analysis of one tepache batch found antibiotic-resistant microorganisms, but that one batch also cannot establish the risk prevalence of an entire beverage tradition. Opposite overstatements—'always safe' and 'always unsafe'—both exceed the data.[^v111-kunun-cultures][^v111-tepache-one]

## A better evidence checklist

Ask about sampling design, handling, culture methods, chemistry, measured hazards and the precise claim being made. Label laboratory results and participant accounts separately. Do not infer medical benefit from projected microbial functions, or recommend uncontrolled home fermentation as a replacement for drinking-water treatment. This is a literacy guide, not a practical food-safety protocol.[^v111-tepache-one]
