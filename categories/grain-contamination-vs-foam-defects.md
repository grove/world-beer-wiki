# An overfoaming beer can involve grain history, not only carbonation

## The visible event does not identify the cause

A bottle opening with unexpected, extensive foaming is often called gushing. It would be tempting to explain every example as overcarbonation, a dirty glass or an unusually foam-positive recipe. A 2024 barley–Fusarium study discusses fungal hydrophobins as one mechanism associated with primary gushing. The authors compare particular fungal isolates, barley cultivars and years; they do not diagnose every foaming event in a retailer's stock.[^v117-fungal-strains]

The 2026 controlled brewing study by Biehl and colleagues asks yet another question: what happened to fungal metabolites and beer chemistry when specifically inoculated barley was malted and brewed. It documents chemical and colour effects from infected grain in its experimental sequence. That chemical study is not evidence that all anomalous foam events are caused by the same toxins or that a gushing observation measures deoxynivalenol.[^v117-fusarium-trial]

## Three forms of evidence

One observes a package opening. Another measures dissolved gas or service conditions. A third tests grain or beer for fungal components or compounds by an assay. These are related but not equivalent. A visual incident cannot determine a microbial species, toxin concentration or consumer risk on its own. Conversely, an experimental association cannot replace tracing the actual commercial lot.
