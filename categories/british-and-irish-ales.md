# British and Irish ales, cask and dispense

British and Irish beer labels are easier to compare when recipe family, strength and serving method remain separate. This guide connects the existing profiles and adds the conditioning vocabulary needed to read them. It is not a claim that one serving format defines all beer from these regions.

## Cask is a conditioning and dispense description

CAMRA describes cask-conditioned beer as beer that develops further in its cask through live yeast and is served without injected carbon dioxide. Its account connects the final conditioning, cellar handling and serving method. That does not give cask beer one fixed color or ingredient list.[^wiki-camra-cask]

CAMRA's wider term *live beer* includes continuing conditioning in other final containers, including bottles, cans, cellar tanks and membrane kegs. Consequently *live*, *cask* and *bottle-conditioned* are related descriptions with different scopes. Their definitions here are attributed to CAMRA, whose campaigning vocabulary should not be converted into an objective ranking of all other beer.[^wiki-camra-live]

## Serving changes the comparison

Irish Stout provides a useful second example: BJCP explicitly separates the creamy impression of nitrogen dispense from what should be expected of a conventionally bottled example. The recipe family and the way the beer reaches the glass describe different aspects of the experience.[^bjcp-2021-15b]

When comparing two beers, record container and dispense where known. A description based on draught beer should not be applied without qualification to every packaged version carrying the same name. “Creamy” also does not establish that a beer contains lactose; consult [sugars](../ingredients/sugars.md) and the actual producer's ingredient information.

## Historical labels need dates

Use [historical London porter](../styles/historical-london-porter.md) and the [history guide](historical-and-revival-beers.md) before making claims about old recipes. A current guideline range answers a present-day judging question. It does not establish what every brewery meant by a label across wars, changing ingredients and different local markets.

For retrieval, keep the exact named style, the time period and the serving method in the query. “Compare an ordinary bitter served on cask with a bottled English barleywine” is a much better-defined request than “describe British ale.”
