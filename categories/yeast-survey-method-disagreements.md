# When fermentation surveys disagree about the yeast

## The denominator is the first question

A 2011 tchoukoutou study reports yeasts identified from selected beers, a 2013 study follows time courses in two productions, and a 2025 study sequences identified yeasts from beverages collected in several municipalities. Their apparent contradictions need to be evaluated using sampling frame, batch stage and denominator. A percentage of recovered isolates is not the proportion of all microbial cells in every market or household beer.[^v113-tchou-2011][^v113-tchou-2025]

## Detection and function require different experiments

Culturing can miss organisms that do not grow under selected conditions; DNA-based methods may detect organisms without proving they are metabolically active. The 2013 study explicitly identified species by direct molecular analysis that its culture approach missed. Moreover, identifying yeast does not measure how much alcohol, acid or aroma it produced in a real ferment.[^v113-tchou-2013]

## Storage introduces another question entirely

A 2022 Ivorian tchapalo study tested selected organisms in a sterile-filtered product for three days and judged sensory changes. That answers a different question from the organisms present during spontaneous fermentation. Good citation practice records medium, inoculum, analytical method, stage, elapsed time and endpoint before converting results into an atlas relationship. None of these surveys alone is a universal cultural recipe or consumer-safety certification.[^v113-tchapalo-2022]
