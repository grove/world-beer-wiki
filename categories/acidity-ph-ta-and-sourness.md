# pH, titratable acidity and perceived sourness are different observations

## Why two acidic beers can report different numbers

A pH measurement reports the hydrogen-ion activity of the sampled liquid through a calibrated meter. The ASBC's public Beer 9 explanation emphasises the electrode, appropriate calibration and the fact that the reported value is tied to the actual sample. It does not quantify the complete concentration or composition of the organic acids in the beer.[^v116-ph]

Titratable acidity answers a different question: how much standard base is consumed before a defined endpoint. The ASBC Beer 8 explanation describes this as a laboratory titration with a specified endpoint, and notes that expressing the result as an equivalent mass of one acid does **not** mean that every acid present is that molecule.[^v116-ta]

## Neither one is the whole sensory profile

A reader may experience sourness differently depending on sweetness, other taste and aroma compounds, beer temperature and context. pH, titratable acidity, acid-species analysis and a sensory description therefore belong in different fields. Two samples with similar pH may have different buffering and acid composition; a lower pH should not be translated mechanically into a guaranteed higher sourness score.

An acidity result is also not a food-safety certificate. Monitoring acidification is one of several process observations, not proof that an untested beverage is free of harmful organisms. This guide intentionally provides conceptual comparisons rather than laboratory titration instructions or treatment limits.

For reporting, record sample stage, date, preparation, method or instrument and units. Follow [how the two assays differ](../processes/ph-versus-titratable-acidity.md) and [why acidification is not a safety certification](acidification-is-not-safety-certification.md).
