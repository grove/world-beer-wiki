# Wastewater COD, water withdrawal and product water footprint are unlike metrics

## Concentration is not a volume

Brewery wastewater can contain sugars, yeast, cleaning residues and other materials that contribute to a chemical oxygen demand (COD) result. A 2026 review reports broad wastewater COD, BOD, nutrient and pH ranges drawn from different brewery settings. COD describes the oxidizable content of a tested liquid sample, not directly how many liters a brewery withdrew or what a crop's basin-dependent water footprint was.[^v119-wastewater26]

An experimental 2026 bioelectrochemical digestion paper followed **real brewery wastewater** in one continuous reactor for 365 days, evaluating organic-matter removal and methane production under studied operating conditions. High removal of COD in that reactor does not prove the treated effluent meets every discharge standard or that the site has reduced agricultural water consumption.[^v119-bead26]

## The scopes of the observations

The Brewers Association sustainability-benchmark resource distinguishes water use, wastewater, energy, solid waste and greenhouse-gas reporting as related but separate management indicators. Its public page identifies what is tracked; it does not provide a verified baseline for an individual brewery.[^v119-babench26]

A useful comparison stores sampled wastewater concentration and flow, treatment stage, location and date, then separately records water withdrawal or consumptive use and supply-chain accounting. A high percent COD removal and a low onsite water-to-beer ratio can both be true without describing product water scarcity.
