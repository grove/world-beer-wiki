# Lagers and cold-conditioned beers

Lager is a broad route through this wiki, encompassing beers with very different color, strength and balance. Cold conditioning is a process that also occurs in some top-fermented beers. Keeping those meanings separate makes the unusual cases easier to understand.[^wiki-bjcp-basic-categorization]

## A useful comparison map

| Reading route | Profiles | Main comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Pale and malt-led | [Munich Helles](../styles/munich-helles.md), [German Helles Exportbier](../styles/german-helles-exportbier.md) | Delicate pale malt balance versus a somewhat fuller export reference. [^bjcp-2021-4a][^bjcp-2021-5c] |
| Pale and hop-led | [German Pils](../styles/german-pils.md), [Czech Premium Pale Lager](../styles/czech-premium-pale-lager.md) | Dry, firm bitterness versus a richer Czech balance; neither label is a universal synonym for pale lager. [^bjcp-2021-5d][^bjcp-2021-3b] |
| Amber and toasted | [Vienna Lager](../styles/vienna-lager.md), [Märzen](../styles/marzen.md) | Toasty malt expressions with different weight and balance. [^bjcp-2021-7a][^bjcp-2021-6a] |
| Dark | [Munich Dunkel](../styles/munich-dunkel.md), [Schwarzbier](../styles/schwarzbier.md) | Bread-crust richness versus a leaner dark lager with restrained roast. [^bjcp-2021-8a][^bjcp-2021-8b] |
| Strong | [Doppelbock](../styles/doppelbock.md), [Baltic Porter](../styles/baltic-porter.md) | Rich malt in pale or dark bock expressions; darker, regionally varied porter expressions. [^bjcp-2021-9a][^bjcp-2021-9c] |

These are reading routes, not a single progression from light to strong. A darker color does not place a beer higher on an alcohol scale: the separate references above describe both pale strong beers and moderate-strength dark beers.

## Cases that defeat a simple yeast-temperature rule

[Kölsch](../styles/kolsch.md) uses top fermentation followed by cold conditioning. [California Common](../styles/california-common.md) is described around lager yeast fermented relatively warm. These examples show why yeast identity, fermentation temperature and maturation need separate fields.[^bjcp-2021-5b][^bjcp-2021-19b]

[Baltic Porter](../styles/baltic-porter.md) spans regional practices; the modern BJCP reference allows lager yeast or cool fermentation with ale yeast. Its name alone cannot determine an individual brewery's culture. [Bière de Garde](../styles/biere-de-garde.md) likewise allows either yeast group in the cited reference and is cold conditioned.[^bjcp-2021-9c][^bjcp-2021-24c]

## Contemporary branches

Use [Italian-style Pilsner](../styles/italian-pilsner.md), [West Coast Pilsner](../styles/west-coast-pilsner.md), [Rice Lager](../styles/rice-lager.md), and [New Zealand Pilsner](../styles/new-zealand-pilsner.md) to examine newer category boundaries. Their individual pages identify the relevant guideline or producer evidence. They should not all be merged under an undefined label such as “craft pilsner.”

Read [rice](../ingredients/rice.md) for the difference between a grain adjunct and a beverage identity, [hops](../ingredients/hops.md) for aromatic treatments, and [brewing yeast](../ingredients/brewing-yeast.md) for culture terminology. A grain name or dry-hop treatment adds information to a beer description; it does not replace the fermentation and sensory evidence.
