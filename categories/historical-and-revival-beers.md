# Historical evidence, living traditions and revivals

A beer can be traditional and currently brewed, historically named but substantially changed, or reconstructed from incomplete evidence. The age of an ingredient does not resolve those differences. This guide sorts the **kind of evidence**, so a plausible story does not become an unsupported lineage.

BJCP's Historical Beer category is a competition accommodation for several kinds of less common or recreated beer. Its introduction explicitly says inclusion does not mean production has ceased; traditional and Indigenous beers can also be entered there. It should not be read as a list of extinct beverages.[^wiki-bjcp-historical-category]

## Four evidence routes

| Evidence | What a profile can describe | What needs separate support |
|---|---|---|
| Archaeological residues and vessels | Materials and processing inferred from the analyzed remains | Original name, flavor, exact strength or a complete recipe |
| Dated brewing records | A particular brewer's recorded ingredients and practices | Every brewery in the region or the same name in another century |
| Fieldwork and community accounts | The observed practice and its locally understood context | An unchanged ancient origin or a census of all makers |
| Modern guidelines and recreations | A current interpretation or judging target | Exact reproduction of a historical drink |

The table states this wiki's evidence policy. It does not assign one type of source greater cultural value than another; each answers a different question.

## Documentary example: porter

Martyn Cornell examines the familiar Harwood/three-threads origin narrative against earlier writing and finds no contemporary support for that popular invention story. Ron Pattinson's transcription of Whitbread grists instead makes change visible within one brewery over time. Together they offer reasons to ask about dates and records before repeating a single origin myth.[^porter-cornell][^porter-whitbread]

The [historical London porter](../styles/historical-london-porter.md) profile uses that work. [English Porter](../styles/english-porter.md), [American Porter](../styles/american-porter.md) and [Baltic Porter](../styles/baltic-porter.md) remain separate contemporary comparisons. Their relationship is a research question, not a license to copy a modern recipe into the past.

## Archaeological example: Chinese grain fermentation

The Mijiaya study identifies evidence from starch, phytoliths and chemical residues and interprets a mixture of grains and other starches in brewing. Its evocative word “recipe” does not mean a written recipe or preserved drink was recovered. The [Mijiaya profile](../styles/mijiaya-beer.md) keeps those limits explicit.[^mijiaya-study]

Compare the earlier material discussed in [Shangshan fermented-grain beverage](../styles/shangshan-beer.md). These are distinct sites and research contexts; geographical inclusion under China does not establish an uninterrupted style lineage.

## Living practice and contemporary reconstruction

[Sahti](../styles/sahti.md), [Stjørdalsøl](../styles/stjordalsol.md), [Kornøl](../styles/kornol.md), and [Keptinis](../styles/keptinis.md) should be read through their named makers, communities and documented methods. A modern commercial adaptation may reproduce some ingredients while changing production scale or technique. State the connection actually supported by the source.

For modern reference interpretations, compare [Pre-Prohibition Lager](../styles/pre-prohibition-lager.md), [Pre-Prohibition Porter](../styles/pre-prohibition-porter.md), [Kentucky Common](../styles/kentucky-common.md), and [Roggenbier](../styles/roggenbier.md). Each page identifies its scope. Do not assume all entries have equivalent archival depth.

## Useful questions for the next research pass

Ask which primary record supports an origin claim, whose terminology is being translated, whether a date concerns a first surviving reference or an invention, and which substitutions a recreation makes. If those answers are missing, preserve the uncertainty in the profile rather than filling it with a precise-looking date.
