# Herbal beers, gruit and contemporary botanical revival

## A category with more than one meaning

Gruit can refer to herbal mixtures used in some European brewing contexts, but research and historical dictionaries also discuss the administrative and trade systems governing such materials. A brewing label derived from one period does not guarantee use of one shared botanical recipe. Hopped beer and herbal flavoring have not been mutually exclusive everywhere.[^v13-gruit]

## Plant-based cases should be precise

Bog myrtle is one recorded plant; yarrow, heather and other botanicals also appear in accounts. Modern heather ale producer Williams Brothers documents its own Fraoch revival, while its deep-antiquity claims remain a producer narrative. Botanical *identity* and a beer's *historical descent* need separate evidence.[^v13-bog][^v13-heather]
