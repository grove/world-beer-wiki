# Food-contact compliance is not a beer flavor description

## Material regulation and sensory quality answer different questions

A metal beverage can can include coatings or contact layers that have to be considered separately from the metal shell. EU Regulation 2024/3190 addresses BPA and certain other bisphenols in food-contact materials. The official legal text includes conditions, derogations and transitions. Reading it does not reveal the composition of a particular manufacturer's liner, nor does a substance's regulation demonstrate that a sampled beer is chemically or microbiologically safe.[^v118-bpa-law]

The European Commission's food-contact legislation overview identifies the underlying act and a 2026 correction. For legal interpretation, source date, article scope, effective provisions, applicable category and actual material construction matter. No universal claim about cans outside the EU follows from that page.[^v118-bpa-explainer]

## A taste change is not a migration assay

In a 2025 experiment on unpasteurized top-fermented beer, changing filtration, yeast and storage conditions affected particular sensory and chemical endpoints. The study did not test migration of BPA or the compliance of metal-can coatings. A stale aroma in a product therefore cannot be assigned to a regulated packaging substance based only on tasting.[^v118-storage25]

A wiki should store **food-contact law**, **documented material composition**, **migration or compliance tests** and **consumer sensory observations** as distinct evidence types. Technical terms on packaging may describe performance, not a competition style or a food-safety certificate. ## Evidence boundary

The published source is neither proof of universal causality nor approval of a specific product or operator. Studies, institutional guides and laws have distinct authority and do not license untested safety or compliance claims.
