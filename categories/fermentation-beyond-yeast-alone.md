# Beyond ale versus lager: fermentation ecosystems

## One axis cannot describe everything

Ales and lagers are useful families in many modern beer contexts, but microbial systems also include mixed household cultures, mold-assisted starch conversion, deliberately acidic fermentations and spontaneous environmental contributions. A 2026 analysis found substantial within-culture diversity among farmhouse yeasts; Korean starter studies found multiple yeast lineages with different laboratory phenotypes.[^v13-farmhouse26][^v13-korea-yeast26]

## A grain beverage needs access to sugars

Shangshan researchers interpreted fungal and starch microfossils as consistent with a mold-assisted grain fermentation. A modern mixed starter and an archaeological claim are not identical evidence: one can be experimentally sampled as living cells, while the other is reconstructed from preserved traces.[^v13-shangshan]

## Research extension 1.5: Dry-starter ecosystems across Asia

Eastern Himalayan starter-cake research and Vietnamese bánh men studies both describe microbial ecosystems where starch hydrolysis, ethanol production and microbial acidification can involve different organisms. This is a useful functional comparison, but naming all such drinks “beer” would override locally documented beverage categories.[^v15-himalaya-2019][^v15-banhmen-2008]

For a more precise comparison see [starter technologies and names](beer-starters-and-beverage-names.md) and [laboratory isolation versus community maintenance](../processes/isolates-and-living-starter-communities.md).
