# Beer as intangible cultural heritage, not just a style

## What receives recognition

UNESCO's 2016 Belgian inscription addresses the making, sharing, appreciating, teaching and social uses of beer. The Republic of Korea's 2021 national designation addresses makgeolli making and sharing. These are different institutions and processes; the latter is not a UNESCO inscription. Neither designation invents the tradition or establishes one authentic recipe.[^v13-belgium-unesco][^v13-korea-heritage]

## What a list cannot capture

Heritage lives in households, agricultural practice, workplace training, public celebrations and contested identities. A list of recognized practices reflects selection and documentation; unlisted practices may remain equally meaningful to their practitioners. A wiki should record recognition as a dated institutional act and the community practice as an ongoing subject.[^v13-belgium-unesco][^v13-korea-heritage]

## Edition 1.9: newly sourced connections

A Vietnam News Agency article reports that S’Tieng *rượu cần* craft was recognized under **Vietnam’s national intangible-heritage framework**, including transmission of preparation knowledge and cooperative activity. National recognition is not the same legal or institutional thing as inscription on UNESCO’s representative list. A careful entry records the listing authority, community, practice and date, rather than treating “heritage” as a universal quality seal. Read [the S’Tieng craft account](../culture/stieng-ruou-can-and-transmission.md) and the [beverage profile](../styles/ruou-can-vietnamese-jar-ferment.md) for the limits of the public report.[^v19-stieng]
