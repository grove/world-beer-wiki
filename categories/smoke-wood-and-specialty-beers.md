# Smoke, wood, fruit and specialty categories

Some beer categories describe a base identity; others describe something done to that beer. Smoke, fruit and wood can be central to a named tradition or added to another recognizable style. Describe both the base and the treatment when the evidence permits.

## Smoke can enter at the malt stage

Schlenkerla explains that beechwood smoke passes through its malt during kilning. Its Oak Smoke beer instead uses malt dried with oak smoke. These are producer-specific examples of smoke supplied before fermentation; “oak-smoked” does not mean the finished beer was aged in an oak barrel.[^wiki-schlenkerla-process][^wiki-schlenkerla-oak]

In BJCP 2021, [Rauchbier](../styles/rauchbier.md) is a narrower Märzen-like smoked lager reference. Other smoked base styles are handled separately. This is why the literal meaning of the German name and a particular judging definition should not be treated as identical scopes.[^bjcp-2021-6b]

Read [Grodziskie](../styles/grodziskie.md), [Lichtenhainer](../styles/lichtenhainer.md), [Stjørdalsøl](../styles/stjordalsol.md), and [Gotlandsdricke](../styles/gotlandsdricke.md) for different traditional contexts. Their inclusion here is comparative; it does not make them variants of Bamberg Rauchbier.

## Wood flavor and wood vessels are different questions

The [Wood-aged beer](../styles/wood-aged-beer.md) reference concerns a perceptible contribution from wood. BJCP permits wood alternatives such as staves or cubes as well as barrels. It distinguishes that category from [Specialty wood-aged beer](../styles/specialty-wood-aged-beer.md), where previous barrel contents add recognizable character.[^bjcp-2021-33a][^bjcp-2021-33b]

Lambic presents another use of wood: research documents barrel surfaces as a possible source of fermentation organisms. A statement that beer matured in wood is therefore not enough to assign it to a wood-flavor category or to infer bourbon-like notes.[^wiki-lambic-barrels]

## Evidence boundary

Do not infer meat from a savory smoke descriptor, lactose from a creamy texture, or spontaneous fermentation from a barrel photograph. Follow the profile's ingredient and process evidence. Likewise, a modern herb beer can be described on its own terms without claiming to reproduce the historical ingredient and taxation system discussed in [Gruit](../styles/gruit.md).
