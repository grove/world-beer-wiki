# Capturing fermentation CO₂ does not automatically make beer net zero

## A gas molecule can be recovered without erasing a whole supply chain

Alcoholic fermentation releases carbon dioxide as sugar is converted to ethanol and other products. Grand, Jenkins, Maskell and Zhuang reviewed craft-brewing CO₂ recovery options and interviewed **five breweries in France**. They describe possible uses of the gas and barriers to adopting capture systems, not proof that those five breweries implemented a zero-emission network.[^v119-craftco2]

The GHG Protocol distinguishes direct emissions at a site, purchased-energy emissions, and other indirect emissions such as raw materials and transport. It also treats certain biogenic CO₂ flows separately in corporate accounting. Capturing a fermenter stream, even when valuable, cannot alone cancel emissions from heating, refrigerants, farming, packaging or delivery.[^v119-ghgfaq]

## Site changes and organizational footprints differ

A 2025 open paper on Tsingtao describes reported operational changes involving fermentation CO₂, packaging return loops and water/energy management, including a Scope 1/2 intensity reduction within its study. This is a particular industrial case, with assumptions and data sources; it is not proof that identical savings would occur in a small or traditional brewery.[^v119-closed25]

For a carbon claim, identify whether CO₂ was captured, purified, sold, re-used, permanently stored or later re-released; also specify energy input, material origin, displaced purchase, report year and system boundary. Recirculation may have resource value without being permanent removal. Compare [fermenter capture](../processes/fermentation-co2-capture-vs-carbonation.md), [the craft interviews](../culture/five-french-breweries-and-co2-reuse.md), and [GHG scopes](../environment/scope-1-2-3-brewing-emissions.md).
