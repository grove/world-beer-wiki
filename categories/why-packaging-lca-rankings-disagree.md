# Why two beer packaging life-cycle studies rank keg options differently

## A result is conditional on the delivery system

Morgan, Styles and Lane's 2022 LCA considered seven microbreweries and compared packaging and distribution changes. Their abstract reports that switching from reusable steel kegs to PET kegs is not necessarily environmentally preferable for short journeys, although particular long-distance van-delivery scenarios could show savings. The authors also found that delivery coordination and reusable glass could affect results.[^v118-lca22]

Marrucci, Daddi and Iraldo's 2024 LCA used a different packaging and consumption model comparing PET kegs, glass bottles and aluminium cans. Its abstract selected PET-keg draught service as the best case under that assessment and tested factors including transport loading and recycled content. The two publications do not establish that one of the teams measured every condition in the other's model.[^v118-lca24]

## What to investigate before adopting a ranking

A 2025 methodological review of reusable packaging LCAs warns that omitting product losses or relying on hypothetical return systems without checking actual operation can distort comparison. Container manufacturing, transport distance, refill counts, empty returns, cleaning, disposal, energy source, beer loss and functional unit all have potential relevance.[^v118-lca-reuse25]

No single bare label—steel, PET, glass, aluminium, reusable or recyclable—supplies those missing inputs. Keep every study's geography, distribution mode, reference flow and assumptions attached to its result. This page compares published analyses; it does not assert a new carbon footprint for a particular brewery.
