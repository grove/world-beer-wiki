# IPA and hop-forward beers

IPA is useful as a family name, but modern IPA labels encode several different things. Some describe an established sensory reference; others add a strength modifier, a grain, a fermentation choice or an ingredient. This guide keeps those dimensions visible so that related beers can be compared without making every compound label a separate historical lineage.

## A named beer is not the same as an entry category

BJCP describes 21B Specialty IPA as an entry framework, not one distinct style. It accommodates defined variants and strength-qualified interpretations, while requiring information about what the entrant intends.[^modern-bjcp-specialty-ipa] This explains why a real commercial label may be useful enough for its own wiki page without receiving its own numbered slot in that guide.

The distinctions matter in an LLM answer. “There is a page about this term,” “a brewery uses this term” and “a judging organization defines this term” are three different statements. The presence of one does not establish the other two. Cite the relevant evidence rather than treating all named entries as equally standardized.

## A practical comparison record

For each beer or style description, record the following independently:

1. **Name and source:** the exact label, brewery or guide, edition and observation date.
2. **Strength:** stated ABV, separating a product's measurement from a guideline's illustrative band.
3. **Hop expression:** aroma and flavor observations; keep identified hop varieties distinct from inferred sensory associations.
4. **Balance:** perceived bitterness, finish and body. Do not substitute an aroma adjective for a bitterness measurement.
5. **Process evidence:** yeast, fermentation conditions, dry hopping or enzyme use only when the source supplies them.
6. **Added ingredients:** distinguish a fruit-like description from a declaration that fruit was used.

This is a research template proposed by the wiki. It is deliberately reusable across neighboring families and does not impose a recipe. If a product page omits fermentation details, leave that field unknown rather than deriving it from a geographical name.
