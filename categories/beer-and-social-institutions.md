# Beer, work, markets, monasteries and the pub

## A beverage can organize work

Studies of West African sorghum beer describe women brewers and markets, grain supply, malting and everyday livelihoods. UNESCO's Belgian dossier describes beer education and social transmission, while CAMRA's pub history documents how cellar technology changes the work and architecture of service. The cases cannot be ranked on a universal scale of modernity.[^v13-dolo-review][^v13-belgium-unesco][^v13-camra-dispense]

## Different institutions, different powers

A community brewhouse, family household, rural market stall, monastic institution, national heritage agency and commercial pub may all interact with beer, but with different rules of access, labor and meaning. One name rarely captures every social relationship. Pay attention to who controls ingredients, who earns income, and who may tell the history.[^v13-dolo-review]
