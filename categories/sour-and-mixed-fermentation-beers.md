# Sour, mixed and spontaneous fermentation

**Sourness, mixed fermentation and spontaneous inoculation answer different questions.** Sourness describes perceived acidity; mixed fermentation describes a combination of organisms; spontaneous inoculation describes how fermentation begins. Modern “wild” beer can also be deliberately inoculated. A reliable description states what is actually known instead of using these words interchangeably.[^wiki-bjcp-basic-categorization]

## Compare the terms and their limits

| Term or profile | What it communicates | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| [Brett beer](../styles/brett-beer.md) | Brettanomyces contributes to fermentation or maturation. | Strong acidity, spontaneous inoculation or barrel aging. [^bjcp-2021-28a] |
| [Mixed-fermentation sour beer](../styles/mixed-fermentation-sour-beer.md) | A modern broad category combining acidity and mixed-culture character. | A particular local tradition, vessel or fixed timetable. [^bjcp-2021-28b] |
| [Straight sour beer](../styles/straight-sour-beer.md) | The cited category emphasizes clean acidity without the characteristic funk of Brett or complex mixed fermentation. | One mandatory kettle-souring process. [^bjcp-2021-28d] |
| [Lambic](../styles/lambic.md) | A named Belgian tradition with its own ingredients, environment and maturation practices. | That every spontaneously fermented sour beer is lambic. [^bjcp-2021-23d] |
| [Gueuze](../styles/gueuze.md) | A lambic blending and bottle-conditioning tradition. | A synonym for Gose or all sparkling sour beer. [^bjcp-2021-23e] |

The distinction between a cultured process and a sensory result matters. Brettanomyces is not a universal souring shortcut; the BJCP Brett reference treats fruit and fermentation character separately from lactic acidity.[^bjcp-2021-28a]

## Compare named traditions before inventing a universal sour style

[Berliner Weisse](../styles/berliner-weisse.md), [Gose](../styles/gose.md), [Flanders Red Ale](../styles/flanders-red-ale.md), [Oud Bruin](../styles/oud-bruin.md), and [Catharina Sour](../styles/catharina-sour.md) have their own reference profiles. Use their pages to compare grain, strength, fruit and regional context. Shared acidity is a reason to compare them, not evidence of identical ancestry.

[Fruit Lambic](../styles/fruit-lambic.md) supplies another useful distinction: fruit can participate in a tradition with a specific base beer. A red color, fruit aroma or cherry name alone cannot identify that base. Compare it with the wider [fruit beer](../styles/fruit-beer.md) category and the [fruit ingredient reference](../ingredients/fruit.md).

## Spontaneous does not mean microbes come only from outdoor air

Research on six wooden barrels in a traditional lambic brewery detected fermentation-related organisms by sequencing after cleaning, with few colonies still culturable. The authors identify those surfaces as a possible additional source of inoculation, alongside air and equipment. This supports treating the brewery environment as part of the process; it does not prove that any exposed wort will become lambic.[^wiki-lambic-barrels]

Wood can therefore be relevant as a vessel and microbial habitat without a strongly oaky taste being the objective. The [wood and specialty guide](smoke-wood-and-specialty-beers.md) separates that question from a wood-flavor judging category.

## Read microbial evidence at the right scale

The [brewing bacteria](../ingredients/brewing-bacteria.md), [brewing yeast](../ingredients/brewing-yeast.md), and [mixed starter](../ingredients/mould-and-mixed-starters.md) pages explain different microbial roles. A species found in sampled batches does not establish every maker's culture. Likewise, identifying a sour beer's organisms is different from specifying a safe production process or complete recipe.

Keep individual traditions visible when a regional fermentation also involves acids, mixed cultures or grain starters. The European and American judging vocabulary used in this guide is not a replacement for local beverage names.
