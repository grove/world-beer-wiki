# An intangible-heritage inscription is not a beer style

UNESCO's 2016 inscription for beer culture in Belgium recognizes the social transmission of knowledge and beer's position in daily, festive, educational and culinary life. It does not establish that every Belgian beer belongs to a single flavor category or that the inscription licenses a brewery to claim ownership of a centuries-old practice.[^v114-unesco]

The need for distinction is easy to see in Pilsen. A brewing company can document an institutional beginning and a named historical batch, while a contemporary judging guide defines how a type of beer should be evaluated today. Neither document alone proves all claims about community ownership, unchanged recipe or direct ancestry. “Recognized heritage,” “brand origin,” “historical method” and “style” answer different questions.[^v114-plzen]

A careful wiki uses separate article kinds and source labels. Heritage records establish documented practice and safeguarding claims; producer histories establish what a producer reports; laboratory papers establish sampled mechanisms; judging guidelines define their edition's rules. For a living tradition, community voices matter independently of institutional recognition.[^v114-unesco]
