# Detecting a difference is not rating beer quality or liking

## Three questions that are often confused

A triangle test is designed to ask whether participants can distinguish two product samples under a specified design. ISO 4120:2021 describes a forced-choice method in which a sample can be perceptibly different without the test revealing which sensory attribute changed or which product is preferred. Its public abstract also cautions about homogeneous products and strong lingering flavours.[^v116-triangle]

A competition scoresheet instead evaluates how a sampled beer fits a style-oriented judging framework. The BJCP's educational material discusses aroma, appearance, flavour, mouthfeel and overall impression; it does not transform a statistically detected difference into a higher quality score.[^v116-bjcpEval]

A consumer liking study is yet another question. The published abstract of Blackmore and colleagues' beer expectation experiment illustrates that labels and colour can influence expectations and reported perception in the tested setting. That is a study-specific finding, not proof that every person is led by the same cue.[^v116-expect]

## How to choose the evidence

If a brewer changes a hopping method, a well-designed blinded discrimination test might address detectability. A trained descriptive panel could characterize the direction and intensity of a change. A consumer study might address preference in the sampled population. None of these can stand in for the others.

The data model should preserve question, participant eligibility, blinding, sample randomization, control conditions, total responses and uncertainty. See [triangle testing](../sensory/triangle-tests-for-beer.md) and [pairing research limits](../food/difference-tests-do-not-rank-pairings.md). The full paid ISO standard and full consumer paper were not reviewed here.
