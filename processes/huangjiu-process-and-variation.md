# Huangjiu: preparing grain, converting starch and fermenting

Huangjiu processes provide a useful counter-example to the assumption that every grain alcoholic drink is mashed and pitched like a standard European barley beer. The reviewed literature distinguishes grain washing or soaking, steaming, starter addition, starch conversion, fermentation, pressing and possible clarification or aging. There is no single stage duration or temperature that can safely be transferred from a named Shaoxing production method to all Chinese regions.[^v19-huangjiu-review]

## The central biochemical challenge

Rice, glutinous rice and other starch sources must be rendered accessible to enzymes; microorganisms then transform sugars and other substrates. The starter can contribute both hydrolytic activity and organisms involved in later fermentations. Describing the steps separately makes it possible to compare *qu* with nuruk, koji and other culture systems without claiming they are the same biological mixture.[^v19-huangjiu-review]

## Traditional and mechanized approaches

A 2026 study compared selected traditionally managed and mechanically managed fermentations, identifying microbiological and chemical differences. The article’s laboratory findings are about sampled conditions, not proof that every hand-operated practice is older, purer or tastier. The term “mechanized” must be tied to actual operations being compared.[^v19-huangjiu-2026]
