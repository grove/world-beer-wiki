# Decoction and the distinction between mash and wort

Decoction is a mashing technique involving additional heating of mash portions. Pilsner Urquell describes its triple-decoction process as heating a portion of the mash three times; Schlenkerla's account explicitly describes boiling within its decoction method. Both locate the operation before wort separation.[^atlas-pilsner-urquell][^atlas-schlenkerla-process]

## Why the material name matters

The mash still contains grain material. The wort is the sugar-containing liquid separated from it. Schlenkerla's process description places lautering between mashing and the subsequent hopped wort boil, making these separate heating operations rather than two names for one event.[^atlas-schlenkerla-process]

A description saying only “the beer was boiled three times” loses that distinction. A more useful account identifies the material, how a portion was handled, and where the operation fits in the whole production sequence.

## Compare outcomes carefully

The producers associate their methods with particular flavor and color results. Those are accounts of their own practice, not controlled evidence that every drinker will prefer a decocted beer or that no other technique can produce a related impression.[^atlas-pilsner-urquell][^atlas-schlenkerla-process]
