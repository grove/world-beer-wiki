# Gravity dispense and the changing architecture of pubs

## Before a beer engine

CAMRA's historical account describes direct gravity dispense from a cask as an earlier technique. A full cask could be near the bar or in storage, and staff drew beer by opening its tap. The arrangement affected staffing and the physical organization of the establishment as much as it affected serving temperature.[^v13-camra-dispense]

## The pump changes location

A beer engine draws liquid from a cask below the bar. The account associates Joseph Bramah with a 1797 patent, carefully separating the patent from any definitive claim of first invention. The pump made cellar placement practical and changed the bodily work of serving a pint.[^v13-camra-dispense]

## Not a recipe

Compare [Beer engine](../objects/beer-engine.md), [Cask conditioning](cask-conditioning.md), and [Pub community](../culture/cask-beer-pub-community.md). These accounts explain how beer is delivered, not what malts, microorganisms, or styles the drinker must receive.

## Evidence boundaries

A modern history of dispense cannot establish every establishment or equipment innovation in Britain and other countries. All dated observations are tied to the source and location described above. This is a research draft.
