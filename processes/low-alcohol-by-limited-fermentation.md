# Limiting alcohol through yeast sugar utilization

Low- and no-alcohol beers can be made by different routes. One approach changes **how much fermentable sugar a selected yeast can consume**; another physically removes alcohol after a more conventional fermentation. A 2026 review analyzes maltose-negative strains and their effects on residual carbohydrates, aroma and process robustness.[^v16-nolo-review]

## What changes chemically

A maltose-negative strain may ferment small sugars while leaving much of the wort's maltose. Less ethanol does not automatically mean the beer tastes like a full-strength counterpart: sweetness, grainy/wort-like notes, acidity, esters and stability may differ. Process variables such as wort strength and aeration can materially alter the outcome.[^v16-nolo-review]

## Controlled evidence instead of a magic strain

A separate 2026 primary study compared selected yeasts and process modifications, including glucose oxidase treatments, and evaluated resulting aroma and alcohol. It establishes a **tested experimental possibility**, not an assurance that choosing a species name produces a legally compliant beer everywhere.[^v16-nolo-primary]

## Reading caution

Residual sugar, microbial stability and final alcohol measurement matter. This reference is a process comparison and does not replace commercial quality assurance, food-safety controls or local labeling rules.
