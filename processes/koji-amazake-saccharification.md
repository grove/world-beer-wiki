# Koji amazake: starch sweetening without inevitable ethanol

Amazake is a useful counterexample to the assumption that fermentation always means substantial ethanol production. In *koji amazake*, enzymes originating from rice-associated koji molds break down starch into simpler sugars, creating sweetness. The technical review distinguishes this form from *sakekasu amazake*, in which sake lees supply material already associated with alcoholic sake brewing.[^v112-amazake-2021]

## Conversion and alcohol fermentation are separate operations

A food scientist may call enzymatic saccharification part of a fermentation-based food technology, while a drinker may assume the word 'fermented' predicts alcohol. That is not reliable: rice starch conversion by fungal enzymes and subsequent sugar metabolism by yeast are **different biochemical steps**. In sake production they may overlap; in some amazake routes no substantial yeast alcohol stage is required.[^v112-amazake-2021]

## Food and label boundaries

Amazake is not automatically sake, beer, alcohol free or medically beneficial. The source is a review written by a sake producer's employee and includes wider nutritional hypotheses we have not independently tested. The wiki records the input and biochemical contrast without promoting health claims or recommending consumption to sensitive groups. Pair this with the general parallel-starch-fermentation guide to see why different rice beverages should retain their own names.[^v112-amazake-2021]
