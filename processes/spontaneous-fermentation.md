# Spontaneous inoculation and managed fermentation

Spontaneous fermentation describes how a fermentation is established through environmental microorganisms rather than simply by pitching one deliberately selected brewing culture. It does **not** mean that the brewer makes no decisions. The existing [Lambic profile](../styles/lambic.md) describes deliberate choices around wort, cooling, vessels and maturation within a spontaneously inoculated tradition.[^atlas-lambic-context]

## Separate three questions

| Question | Possible evidence |
|---|---|
| How was fermentation established? | A producer's observed inoculation and cooling practice. |
| Which organisms participated? | Microbial investigation at specified stages. |
| What did the beer taste like? | Sensory observations of the resulting beer. |

The answers are related, but none is an automatic substitute for the others. A deliberately pitched mixed culture is not thereby spontaneous. Conversely, a sensory label such as “funky” is not proof that the culture arrived through open-air cooling.[^atlas-bacteria-overview]

## The brewery is part of the environment

The existing lambic article includes research on maturation vessels as microbial habitats. That evidence complicates a story in which organisms arrive only from distant outdoor air: equipment and production space also deserve attention in a specific brewery account.[^atlas-lambic-context]

Read [Coolship](../objects/coolship.md) for an object used in the described cooling operation, and [Microbial succession](../microbiology/microbial-succession.md) for what can happen after inoculation. Neither an object photograph nor an endpoint species list reconstructs the complete process.

## Evidence note

This article reorganizes existing evidence into a clearer process model; it is not a newly conducted microbial survey. It does not offer instructions for capturing unknown cultures or claim that environmental fermentation guarantees a safe, desirable or locally unique outcome. Named traditions should retain their identities rather than being absorbed into one international “wild beer” category.
