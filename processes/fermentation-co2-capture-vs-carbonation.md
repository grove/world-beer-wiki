# Fermentation CO₂ recovery and carbonation are connected, not identical operations

## A fermenter gas stream is not necessarily beverage-ready gas

CO₂ is created during alcoholic fermentation. Grand and colleagues' 2025 craft-brewing research compares commercial recovery with emerging utilization concepts and interviews five French brewery operators. Their proposed three-level shared arrangement is a research framework and a set of interview findings, not evidence that every participant captured and reused its fermentation gas.[^v119-craftco2]

A 2025 Tsingtao industrial case describes CO₂ capture and reuse alongside other closed-loop measures. The paper reports a particular industrial operation; its capture efficiency and claimed savings should not be transferred directly to artisanal breweries without comparable source, energy and process evidence.[^v119-closed25]

## Reuse, carbon removal and drink quality answer separate questions

A gas stream from fermentation requires appropriate assessment and treatment before any proposed food or beverage use. Capture is the interception of an emitted stream; purification is a quality task; carbonation or dispense is a later application. Re-release of recycled CO₂ is different from permanent geological carbon storage. The GHG Protocol's accounting framework separates direct, purchased-energy and value-chain emissions, so recovery itself does not prove net-zero beer.[^v119-ghgfaq]

Record where gas originated, what happened to it, where any energy inputs are counted, whether product-grade specifications were independently met, and whether the result represents actual reuse or a modeled scenario. This is not a gas purification or beverage handling procedure.
