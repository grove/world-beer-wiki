# When archaeological brewing experiments help—and when they do not

Method guide grounded in Cerro Baúl excavation and informant-guided experimental replication; not a historical reconstruction recipe.

## Why reproduce an old process?

Experimental brewing with maize, molle or both can show what kinds of sediments and residues different materials leave. Researchers then compare those observations with excavated dregs and vessels. It is a way to test a material inference, not a time machine that produces the exact flavor once served at a Wari court.[^v15-wari-2024]

## Corroboration is layered

At Cerro Baúl, room layout, hearths, grinding stones, botanical traces and fermentation vessels each add information. An isolated seed or a distinctive pot is rarely enough on its own. Archaeological interpretation is strongest when multiple independent traces support the same functional explanation.[^v15-wari-2005]

## Knowledge and uncertainty

Informant-guided reconstructions respectfully draw on contemporary makers’ practical understanding. Their methods should be attributed as contemporary evidence, not assigned automatically to ancient brewers or framed as proof of direct descent. A modern brewer can reproduce a chemical transformation without reproducing the surrounding political ritual.[^v15-wari-2024]
