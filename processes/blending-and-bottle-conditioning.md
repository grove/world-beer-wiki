# Blending and bottle conditioning are different operations

A blender chooses components; a further fermentation changes a mixture after it has been assembled. In the [Gueuze profile](../styles/gueuze.md), younger and older lambics contribute different qualities, while fermentable material supports bottle refermentation and carbonation. The blend and the subsequent biological process are connected but distinct.[^atlas-gueuze-context]

## Ask what the mixture is intended to achieve

For an actual blend, useful records include component identity, age, sensory assessment and purpose. “Old plus young” is not a complete formula: two beers of the same age need not have the same character, and an age description alone does not identify the final balance.

The existing gueuze account deliberately preserves producer-specific choices instead of asserting one mandatory age combination for every blend.[^atlas-gueuze-context] Likewise, the collaborative [Toer de Geuze](../calendar/toer-de-geuze.md) example should not be treated as the recipe for every participating producer's ordinary beer.

## Bottle conditioning is not a lambic-exclusive technique

[Australian Sparkling Ale](../styles/australian-sparkling-ale.md) supplies a different context in which bottle conditioning and the handling of sediment affect the served beer. A similar packaging operation does not make the beverages the same tradition.[^atlas-sparkling-context]

Keep **where carbonation develops**, **what fermentation culture participates**, and **how the drink is poured** as separate observations. A bottle, a haze or lively bubbles alone cannot answer all three.

## Evidence note

This reference supplies no sugar dose, conditioning duration or pressure limit. Further fermentation in a sealed package is an operational matter requiring a validated process, not something that can be inferred safely from a style name. The page synthesizes the existing corpus rather than claiming a new experiment or independent examination of each producer.

## Edition 1.4: What the package can still change

A technical review of bottle refermentation describes how viable yeast and fermentable substrate may affect carbonation, oxygen and sensory compounds after filling. The discussion is not a priming or packaging-pressure recommendation. See [package yeast](../microbiology/refermentation-and-package-yeast.md).[^v14-bottle]
