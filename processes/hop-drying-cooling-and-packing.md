# Hop drying: a three-stage historical supply process

Hop production does not end with picking. Historic England describes the need to move moisture-rich green hops rapidly from field to kiln, spread them on a drying floor, cool them in an adjacent stowage space, and press them into large pockets for sale or shipment. Each stage addresses a different handling problem.[^v114-oasts]

The historical account gives an example of hot-air drying over a furnace and explains why closeness to the field mattered. Such figures belong to the buildings and period under discussion; they should **not** be copied as current equipment recommendations. A modern controlled kiln, field-dried hops and a laboratory dryer may all have different air flow, humidity and compound-retention outcomes.[^v114-oasts]

The process joins agriculture, building design and brewing chemistry. Fresh cones are a perishable crop, dried cones a storable ingredient, and the finished beer an outcome affected by far more than drying alone. Heating and oxygen exposure can alter plant compounds, but this architectural history does not measure those molecules or prove a sensory result in a particular beer.[^v114-oasts]
