# Kodo ko jaanr: starch conversion and fermentation as a sequence

*Kodo ko jaanr* is a fermented finger-millet drink documented in the Darjeeling hills and Sikkim. A 2006 investigation of its process tracked microorganisms, sugars, pH and alcohol over time. The report described early starch-active organisms and changing lactic-acid bacterial and yeast populations. This is a studied succession, not a list of organisms every household must deliberately pitch.[^v18-jaanr]

## Sugar production and alcohol production are related but distinct

In the study, saccharification and liquefaction activity peaked early, while reducing sugars and alcoholic strength changed across later measurements. The authors identified roles for *Saccharomycopsis fibuligera* and *Rhizopus* in starch conversion. Yeasts and other community members had different trajectories during fermentation. A single species name does not explain the whole starter ecology.[^v18-jaanr]

## Distinguish assay from practice

The researchers also compared selected isolated strains in a laboratory attempt to make a similar product. An isolate blend scoring highly in one acceptability assessment does not establish the cultural identity of all jaanr starters. Living, mixed starters and selected industrial cultures are different levels of description.
