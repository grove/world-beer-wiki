# Total package oxygen includes both beer and headspace

## A dissolved reading is only one part of the package

Beer transferred into a sealed container contains a liquid phase and, in many formats, a gas headspace. The publicly indexed description of Analytica EBC method 11.5.1 explicitly concerns **total oxygen in packaged beer**, involving both dissolved oxygen and oxygen in the gas phase. It lists bottles, cans, kegs and casks as relevant packages. A tank's dissolved-oxygen result is therefore not identical to the package's combined oxygen inventory after filling.[^v118-ebc-tpo]

The Brewers Association freshness series publicly identifies monitoring methods and sensors during brewing, cellaring and packaging, but its full articles are members-only. The accessible overview does not provide a validated universal threshold for every beer or a calculation that can be performed without the method and actual sample conditions.[^v118-ba-fresh24]

## Oxygen and flavor change are not one measurement

Ditrych and colleagues' 2025 review explains that staling aldehydes can arise through multiple pathways and stages involving heat, oxygen, yeast, metals and earlier processing. A low package oxygen number does not guarantee that a beer lacks aldehyde precursors or has a fixed consumer shelf life.[^v118-aldehyde25]

A defensible record keeps sample point, package format, headspace volume, gas composition, temperature, instrument, timing and reporting units. This is analytical literacy, not instructions for manipulating pressurized packages or setting safety limits. See [package oxygen pathways](beer-package-oxygen-pathways.md), [sealing objects](../objects/crown-cork-and-sealing-liner.md) and [aldehyde origins](../sensory/aldehyde-aging-begins-before-the-package.md).
