# From aguamiel to a maintained pulque ferment

Pulque fermentation begins with **aguamiel**, the sweet sap collected from maguey. In the documented process, producers add fresh sap to mature ferment, a form of culture carryover that contrasts with pitching a purified brewing yeast into boiled malt wort. The sap also encounters organisms during harvest and handling. The result is a changing mixed ecology, not a fixed industrial starter composition.[^v111-pulque-review]

## Three biochemical strands

A review describes alcoholic fermentation by yeast and other organisms, acid production by lactic acid bacteria, and synthesis of extracellular carbohydrates that contribute to viscosity. Those products can appear together, so alcohol content alone is not a complete description of this beverage. A separate sequencing study records successive changes across five sampled stages as sugar decreases and ethanol and acid increase.[^v111-pulque-review][^v111-pulque-stages]

## Boundary and research questions

This is **process interpretation**, not a step-by-step guide for culturing uncharacterized microbes or handling collected sap. The review discusses general practice; the time-course paper measures particular stages. Neither can verify the safety of an arbitrary household preparation, or establish that every community starts with one recipe. The history of colonial ritual and tax systems belongs beside—not inside—a purportedly universal fermentation protocol.[^v111-pulque-stages]
