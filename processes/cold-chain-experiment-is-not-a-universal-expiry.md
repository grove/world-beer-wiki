# A cold-stored unpasteurized ale experiment cannot define every beer's expiry

## The experimental comparison

De Francesco, Sileoni and Marconi's 2025 open study compared filtration, storage temperatures and adding fresh yeast for refermentation in a **particular unpasteurized top-fermented beer**. The paper reported that after six months, cold-stored samples preserved more of some hop- and fruit-associated sensory qualities than room-stored samples. It also reported process-dependent effects of microfiltration and yeast. These results cannot be transferred without qualification to every traditional farmhouse beer, pasteurized lager or no-alcohol beverage.[^v118-storage25]

A 2024 storage review describes why beers may age differently depending on composition, oxidation, temperature and time. Aldehydes, terpenes, esters, iso-alpha acids and haze systems can shift in different directions. A fixed time in a single experiment is a timepoint, not a worldwide expiry date or safety determination.[^v118-aging24]

## A more useful comparison framework

Store the base beer, biological state, filtration conditions, package, temperature treatment, storage duration and instrumental versus sensory endpoints. A study might detect changes in aroma intensity without establishing any pathogen challenge, market acceptability or legal shelf-life labeling criterion. Temperature also interacts with packaging and distribution, so an outcome from one controlled lab does not demonstrate what happened during actual retail transport.
