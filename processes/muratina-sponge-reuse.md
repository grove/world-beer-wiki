# Reusing a fibrous culture carrier in Muratina fermentation

## The material changes function through use

Wahome’s 2003 thesis describes fibrous fruit sponges immersed in sweetened sugarcane juice, where yeasts colonize the structure during fermentation. When used again, the material can carry microbial biomass into a subsequent batch. The relevant transformation is the maintenance and transfer of a living community on a solid carrier, not starch breakdown by malted grain or flavor extraction from fruit flesh.[^v113-muratina-2003]

## Attached cells and suspended cells are different observations

The thesis measured populations associated with the sponge and the fermenting liquid. These observations give a mechanistic explanation for why a nonliquid starter can contribute to fermentation, but a measured yeast count is not an identification of a single ancient lineage. Repeated use, cleaning, handling and the condition of the substrate could change which organisms persist and what byproducts are formed.[^v113-muratina-2003]

## An explanatory process is not an operating protocol

The published abstract reports variation across traditional preparations and experimental trials; it does not certify that uncharacterized *Kigelia* fibre, cane juice or ambient cultures will yield a safe product in an unrelated setting. This entry does not give batch times, incubation procedures or collection instructions. Readers should use it to understand culture transfer and compare it with other maintained-starter systems while preserving the distinct material and social contexts.[^v113-muratina-2003]
