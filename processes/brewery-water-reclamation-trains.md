# Water reclamation trains for brewing: evidence and boundaries

## Treatment is not a single machine

A Portuguese pilot described treatment combinations involving ultrafiltration, ozonation, biologically active granular carbon filtration and reverse osmosis. These operations address different contaminants and water-quality objectives, and a pilot compared them under monitored conditions before a beer demonstration. This is not an endorsement to use untreated wastewater, a home reuse method or evidence that every treatment train meets every jurisdiction’s drinking-water requirements.[^v113-portugal-water-2025]

## Water-quality compliance and life-cycle burden are distinct tests

The pilot investigated treatment performance, while an Italian case study used life-cycle assessment to explore reclamation against another supply scenario. The Italian researchers identified ultrafiltration as a modeled environmental hotspot even as the reuse route reduced freshwater demand. Technical performance, energy burden and compliance criteria therefore need separate evidence instead of being merged into a single yes-or-no claim.[^v113-portugal-water-2025][^v113-italy-water-2026]

## Scope before a claim of transferability

Real brewing water decisions require site-specific contaminant characterization, regulations, traceability and independently verified system performance. The reported pilot beer volume is an engineering demonstration, not proof that the process can replace every water source or guarantee consumer safety without controls. This wiki records transformations and tradeoffs at a conceptual level, leaving design or operation to qualified water-treatment professionals.[^v113-portugal-water-2025]

## Edition 1.19: newly sourced connections

A wastewater-treatment train can remove or transform a targeted organic load without proving that its output is suitable for every form of industrial reuse. A 2026 review describes wide wastewater COD, BOD, nutrient and pH variation among brewery streams, making both sampling and the intended discharge or reuse category central to interpretation.[^v119-wastewater26]

The 2026 continuous bioelectrochemical digestion experiment recorded methane and COD changes at a particular experimental reactor. Those treatment results cannot substitute for the monitoring and authorization required to discharge or reuse water in a named locale.[^v119-bead26]

Separate collection, analysis, anaerobic conversion, any later treatment and verified reuse from one another. Compare [COD versus water footprint](../categories/wastewater-cod-is-not-water-footprint.md) and [microbial digestion](brewery-wastewater-anaerobic-digestion.md); no operating recipe or discharge guarantee is provided.
