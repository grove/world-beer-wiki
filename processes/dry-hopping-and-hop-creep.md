# Dry hopping and hop creep

Dry hopping adds hop material later in production to emphasize aroma rather than relying only on kettle additions. The existing [Hops reference](../ingredients/hops.md) also describes **hop creep**: hop-associated enzymatic activity can release fermentable material, allowing additional fermentation when viable yeast is present.[^atlas-hops-context]

## An ingredient can have more than one job

The registered 2018 Cascade-hop experiment is important because it investigated a transformation, not merely a tasting description. It supports the possibility of changes in fermentability, while its measured magnitude belongs to the tested conditions. It does not justify adding a fixed alcohol increase to every dry-hopped beer.[^atlas-hops-context]

A useful record separates the intended aroma addition from any observed fermentation change. Relevant questions include which material was used, when it entered, whether yeast remained active, and what was actually measured before and afterward. “Dry-hopped” alone leaves those questions open.

## Compare at the right level

[American IPA](../styles/american-ipa.md) gives a style context, while [starch conversion](starch-conversion.md) provides a broader route into enzyme activity. Their connection is explanatory: a shared enzyme-related question is not a claim that dry hopping, malting and mold-assisted starters are interchangeable operations.

The practical lesson for a knowledge base is to permit more than one role for an ingredient. A hop entry may concern aroma, bitterness, material form or an observed effect on fermentation. Flattening all of those into “flavoring” loses useful information.
