# Malting, kilning and roasting as distinct operations

## What malting changes

Malting begins with controlled grain hydration and germination. The process develops or activates enzymes involved in breaking down grain reserves; drying arrests most growth and creates a storable ingredient. In some West African grain-beer systems malted sorghum is produced by specialized suppliers, so this technical step is also a form of work and commerce.[^v13-malt-wset][^v13-dolo-review]

## Kilning is not all roasting

Relatively gentle kilning preserves more enzymatic capability than intensive roasting. The heat history also contributes to color and aroma. WSET's guide distinguishes Maillard browning from caramelization; neither should be treated as a universal explanation of every toast or nut note perceived in a beer.[^v13-malt-wset]

## Where systems differ

Compare [Barley malt heat](../ingredients/barley-malt-heat-spectrum.md), [Sorghum malt](../ingredients/sorghum-malt.md), and [Starch conversion](starch-conversion.md). Malting is one path toward fermentable sugars; mold-assisted starters and other processes use different materials and ecological systems.

## Evidence boundaries

This is an explanatory model, not a validated germination schedule or food-safety protocol. Grain, moisture and kiln construction matter. All dated observations are tied to the source and location described above. This is a research draft.

## Edition 1.17: newly sourced connections

Germination in a malt house is designed to develop properties useful for beer production, but that same process stage must be distinguished from raw-material safety. A 2022 review of Fusarium mycotoxins describes circumstances in which grain-origin fungi or chemical metabolites can persist or change during malting. It is a literature synthesis, not an instruction for making affected grain acceptable.[^v117-fusarium-review]

Biehl and colleagues' 2026 deliberately inoculated experiment tracked named toxins and modified forms into later brewing stages. Its controlled mass balances show why different chemical forms and sample matrices must be followed separately; they do not prove a particular kiln setting will reliably remove every contaminant.[^v117-fusarium-trial]

An account of malting must therefore separate grain viability, intentional enzyme modification, field disease, microbial growth and chemical analysis. See [germination and unwanted fungi](malting-germination-and-grain-fungi.md) and [the specific experimental transfer](fusarium-malt-to-beer-transformation.md). No decontamination parameters or food-safety guarantees are provided.
