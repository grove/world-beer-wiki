# Hop alpha-acid isomerization during wort heating

Heating wort containing hops converts some alpha acids into iso-alpha acids, which are much more important for finished-beer bitterness. This is a chemical change, not simply the dissolving of powdered hops into water.[^v14-hop]

## Transformation and loss

The hop chemistry review explains that only a fraction of raw bittering potential ultimately reaches packaged beer; temperature, time, solubility, adsorption and storage can matter. Dry-hop aroma pathways therefore should not be treated as identical to kettle bittering.[^v14-hop]

## Light matters later

The resulting compounds can be light-sensitive, connecting the kettle to [lightstruck beer](../sensory/lightstruck-chemistry.md). See [hop alpha acids](../ingredients/hop-alpha-acids.md) and [Hops](../ingredients/hops.md); this page offers mechanism, not a universal IBU calculation.
