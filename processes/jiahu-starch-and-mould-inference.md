# Jiahu: inferring mold and malt starch conversion

## Chemical residues asked what materials were present

The 2004 Jiahu paper interpreted pottery chemistry and contextual plant evidence as a rice, honey and fruit fermented beverage. It raised questions about how carbohydrate materials became fermentable, but surviving sherds cannot show a living fermentation in real time. Authors inferred a beverage from their chemical signals and archaeological context rather than measuring present-day enzymatic performance in an original pot.[^v113-jiahu-2004]

## Microfossils provide a different line of evidence

A 2026 team reexamined Jiahu residues and reported starch damage, phytoliths and fungal remains including features interpreted as *Monascus*. Their interpretation supports a central role for mold-assisted, qu-like saccharification and indicates some signatures compatible with malt-based processing. Identifying microfossils is not a direct measure of enzyme activity thousands of years ago, and the actual composition could vary among vessels.[^v113-jiahu-2026]

## An inference is not an archaeological recipe

Modern mold starter and germinated-grain techniques are useful analogies for how starch could become sugar, but they do not establish that Jiahu people used later named recipes, strains or equipment. This article records evidence types and inferential steps rather than recommending a recreation. It also avoids presenting the newer paper as wholly disproving the older one: the studies investigate partially different residue signals.[^v113-jiahu-2026][^v113-jiahu-2004]
