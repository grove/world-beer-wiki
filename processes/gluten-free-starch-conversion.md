# Starch conversion when brewing without barley malt

Barley malt provides starch and enzymes that help convert that starch into yeast-fermentable material. Replacing it with rice, sorghum, millet or a pseudocereal changes both parts of the equation. A review of gluten-free brewing alternatives describes challenges in extract yield, soluble nitrogen, flavor, foam and filtration, with processing options ranging from modified mashing to selected external enzymes.[^v110-gluten-review]

## Preparation cannot be inferred from the grain name

Buckwheat brewing literature reports limited endogenous amylolytic activity and potentially viscous wort. A study of unmalted alternative ingredients compared gelatinized and ungelatinized forms in a partial barley-malt substitution system. Such experiments clarify process choices without establishing that every starch source requires the same cooking step or produces the same results.[^v110-adjunct-review][^v110-gluten-study]

## The product claim is separate

A process that contains a gluten-free grain is not necessarily a gluten-free process or finished beer. In particular, the cited 2022 study used 40% alternative grains with barley malt, so its samples do not support a gluten-free product claim. Source contamination, other ingredients and actual product testing remain distinct questions outside this biochemical explanation.[^v110-gluten-study]

## Evidence and limitations

No dietary advice, coeliac safety threshold or operational enzyme dosing is provided. The references describe comparative brewing experiments and reviews. These limitations should be retained when quoting the article or extracting relationships.
