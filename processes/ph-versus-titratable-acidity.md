# How pH and titratable acidity answer different questions

## Two instruments, two endpoints

The ASBC's public Beer 9 method guide explains pH measurement using a calibrated electrochemical probe. A resulting pH is meaningful only in the context of the sampled liquid and instrument condition; pH during mashing, fermenting and serving need not be one constant number. An instrument's precision also does not make a sample representative of an entire batch.[^v116-ph]

Beer 8 instead determines a titratable acidity endpoint by observing the amount of a standard base consumed. Its public method description specifies an endpoint around pH 8.2 and warns that reporting the result in terms of one chosen acid is an **equivalent** expression, not proof that this is the only organic acid present.[^v116-ta]

## Why time and matrix matter

Fermentation can change the mix of acids and other constituents. A specific reading made before fermentation is not the finished drink's pH, and a beverage with similar pH to another may have different total titratable capacity. None of these results by itself proves that an untested product is wholesome or microbiologically safe.

For a defensible scientific report, state the sample stage, sampling time, method or instrument, calibration status, units, and the distinction between a direct reading and a derived measure. Comparing pH values without these fields can turn a technique into unsupported cross-community claims.
