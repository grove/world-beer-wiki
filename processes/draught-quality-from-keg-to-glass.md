# From keg to glass: draught-system quality

Brewing does not stop mattering when a keg leaves the cellar. Brewers Association draught guidance treats dispensing as a system: beer lines, fittings, gas, temperature and glassware interact before the guest receives the beer. A correctly described style cannot guarantee the quality of a poorly maintained service route.[^v114-draught]

The association publishes its manual for installers, distributors, brewers and retailers. The public overview identifies line cleaning, system design, pressure balance and pouring as key topics. A retailer excerpt discusses microbial buildup and mineral or organic deposits. We use those statements as **technical guidance**, not as sampled evidence that any particular bar is dirty or that a beer is harmful.[^v114-draught-retail]

This distinction also protects cultural accounts. A Czech foam-pour tradition explains intentional service choices, while a fault in a draft installation is an equipment issue. Carbonation, foaming and perceived mouthfeel depend on conditions between keg and glass, but a brief manual page cannot establish each consumer's preferred serving temperature or universal pressure settings.[^v114-draught]

Follow [the gas-and-line apparatus](../objects/draught-lines-and-gas-balance.md), [the microbial habitat](../microbiology/draught-lines-as-a-different-microbial-habitat.md) and [sensory consequences](../sensory/draught-service-vs-brewery-flavor.md). Always consult the latest professional manual and applicable local regulations for an actual installation.[^v114-draught]
