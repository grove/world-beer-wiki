# Raw ale and unboiled wort

**Raw ale means an omitted wort boil, not an unheated brew.** In Garshol's Hornindal account, hot juniper infusion heats the mash, branches form part of the filter, and hot runnings pass through hops. The collected wort is cooled for fermentation instead of receiving a kettle boil.[^atlas-hornindal]

## Locate the missing step

A conventional shorthand—grain, mash, wort, fermentation—contains several different materials. Heat can be applied to brewing water, the grain-and-water mash, a separate hop preparation or the collected wort. Saying only “not boiled” without naming the material creates avoidable ambiguity.

For comparison, the [Vossaøl profile](../styles/vossaol.md) describes a lengthy wort boil in a practice that also uses juniper and kveik. The presence of those ingredients does not establish that a beer is raw ale.[^atlas-voss-context]

| Observation | What it does not settle |
|---|---|
| The mash was hot | Whether the collected wort was later boiled. |
| Hops were used | Whether they were boiled in the whole wort. |
| Kveik was used | Which heating sequence the brewer followed. |
| The beer was made for Christmas | Whether it followed a single national holiday recipe. |

## Evidence note

Unboiled wort is not a microbiological safety certificate, and neither the word “raw” nor a traditional pedigree establishes the composition of a particular batch. This article explains the sequence without supplying an operating schedule, storage guarantee or universal sensory specification.
