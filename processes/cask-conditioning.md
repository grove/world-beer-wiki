# Cask conditioning, cellar care and serving

## Conditioning after filling

In CAMRA's account, cask-conditioned beer continues to mature in its dispensing vessel, typically with active yeast and careful cellar management. It need not be uniformly filtered, carbonated, or served at one temperature. This is a particular service and maturation tradition, not a universal synonym for beer from a tap.[^v13-camra-cask]

## Dispense is a separate decision

Gravity service draws beer directly from a cask tap; a beer engine moves it through a hand pump from a cellar. CAMRA traces both arrangements in British pub history. These methods shape handling, foam, and social space, but the mere presence of a handpull does not prove the beer has undergone conditioning in that same vessel.[^v13-camra-dispense]

## Beer keeps changing

Yeast, oxygen exposure, temperature, condition and handling affect the served beer. Compare [Beer engine](../objects/beer-engine.md), [Cask as object](../objects/cask-and-cellar.md), and [Pub community](../culture/cask-beer-pub-community.md). Technology, maintenance and hospitality meet at the bar.

## Evidence boundaries

CAMRA publishes an advocacy definition. The page is not cellar-training certification or a claim that every cask example conforms. All dated observations are tied to the source and location described above. This is a research draft.
