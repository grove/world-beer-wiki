# Germination can change malt enzymes and fungal risk at the same time

## Two simultaneous biological stories

Controlled malting encourages a grain embryo to restart growth so that starch- and protein-modifying systems become active. Those changes make barley useful for making fermentable wort. A 2022 review of Fusarium mycotoxins also explains that moist germinating grain can provide conditions in which some unwanted field-origin fungi persist or produce metabolites. Malting therefore has both a desired enzyme story and a separate raw-material quality and safety story.[^v117-fusarium-review]

A 2026 review of the barley–malt–beer chain considers how grain history and processing influence what enters the finished beverage. It distinguishes contaminant presence before malting, changes during germination and passage through the mash. It does not establish that the process invariably fixes a diseased crop, nor that every malt produced by small-scale brewers has a particular fungal profile.[^v117-chain]

## Why product stage matters

Barley before steeping, green malt during germination, finished kiln-dried malt and brewed wort are not interchangeable samples. If a study measures a fungal metabolite at two stages, it cannot automatically say when it was produced without controlled timepoints and mass balances. The organism and the toxin also require distinct assays.

Enzymatic malt quality, agronomic yield, visible mold, polymer chemistry and analytical toxin measurement are different axes. A commercial laboratory must apply appropriate sampling, safety and legal requirements; readers should not infer safe lot acceptance from a general wiki explanation.
