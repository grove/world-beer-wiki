# Barley sprouted in a field is not the same as deliberately malted barley

## Timing gives the same germination chemistry different meaning

Barley can begin to germinate while still standing in the field after weather-related dormancy changes. Hull and colleagues' 2026 review calls this pre-harvest sprouting and discusses how dormancy genetics, spikes, humidity and rainfall interact. It is undesirable for consistent malting quality and storage, even though the later malt house intentionally triggers germination.[^v117-sprouting-review]

A separate review of malt enzymes describes why enzymes that act on grain reserves become useful as malting proceeds. But a useful biochemical activity in a managed process cannot by itself indicate whether grain that sprouted prematurely in the field is viable, clean, uniform or suitable for a given production purpose.[^v117-enzymes]

## What one laboratory figure omits

A high or low enzyme reading might refer to harvest grain, green malt or finished malt, with different expected ranges and uses. Cultivar, seed dormancy, weather history and sampling can all affect the comparison. Pre-harvest sprouting can be patchy within a field, so a crop-wide inference requires a defensible sampling plan.

This distinction also matters for historical accounts: a village or community's use of germinated grain cannot be defined by one modern standardized barley-malting protocol without direct ethnographic or archival evidence. Technical comparison does not create an invented recipe.
