# Kettle souring and other routes to clean acidity

Kettle souring places an acidification stage on the wort side of production, before the subsequent alcoholic fermentation. The existing sour-beer guide distinguishes this approach from keeping a mixed microbial community active through extended fermentation and maturation.[^atlas-sour-category-context]

## Follow stages rather than the adjective “sour”

For a meaningful process description, record when the acidifying culture entered, whether a subsequent heat treatment ended that stage, which organisms performed alcoholic fermentation, and whether further fermentable material was added later. These questions expose differences that a finished beer's sour taste cannot resolve.

Lallemand's description of its selected *Lactiplantibacillus plantarum* preparation identifies kettle souring as one intended application. This establishes a documented culture-and-process pairing without defining the whole species by that one commercial product.[^atlas-sour-pitch]

## The neighboring methods matter

[Clean sour-beer categories](../styles/straight-sour-beer.md) do not require one mandatory souring method. The existing reference permits different ways of achieving the intended result.[^atlas-sour-context] Compare [Lactiplantibacillus plantarum](../microbiology/lactiplantibacillus-plantarum.md) with [Lachancea](../microbiology/lachancea.md) to keep bacterial souring distinct from an acid-producing yeast fermentation.

A retrieval system should therefore store **category**, **organism** and **sequence** separately. “Straight sour” is not a reliable synonym for “kettle sour,” and “sour” does not prove spontaneous inoculation.

## Evidence note

This is an explanatory page, not a procedure for validating food safety. It supplies no universal pH threshold, treatment schedule or storage guarantee. Those require an actual process specification and appropriate expertise. A manufacturer's suggested applications also do not make a quick-soured beer historically equivalent to every tradition named in its marketing.
