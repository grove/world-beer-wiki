# Process measurement in industrial opaque sorghum beer

## Many changing variables, not one recipe number

An industrial engineering study used multivariate statistical process monitoring in opaque beer production, examining why measured properties could vary across a manufacturing sequence. Unlike a static style guide, a control chart is a way to notice combinations of variables that depart from an established production baseline. It does not prove that one pH, viscosity or alcohol value is desirable for every sorghum beverage.[^v113-chibuku-2018]

## An active product can continue changing

The authors describe a commercial sorghum and maize beer in which both lactic and alcoholic fermentations contribute to character and the liquid can remain microbiologically active. That creates practical concerns about shelf life and consistency. These concerns are product-specific engineering questions; the work does not test every household ferment nor authorize consumers to infer safety from the mere presence of lactic acid bacteria.[^v113-chibuku-2018]

## Process evidence and cultural classification are different

A factory monitoring protocol can improve repeatability within a defined industrial system, while local brewing practices may intentionally differ in grain selection, vessel, serving window or degree of fermentation. Keeping those distinctions visible prevents Chibuku’s monitored specification from silently becoming a “traditional African beer” standard. The relevant comparisons are methodological: what was measured, when, and for which product.[^v113-chibuku-2018]
