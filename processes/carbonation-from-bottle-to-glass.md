# From packaged CO2 to bubbles in a glass

## The pour changes the physical system

Carbonated beer in a sealed package and beer in an open glass are under different gas-pressure conditions. Liger-Belair and Cilindre measured CO2 in the bottle and after pouring a particular commercial lager, then used a model to investigate bubble nucleation during natural degassing. Opening a package is therefore more than moving a liquid: it changes the gas equilibrium.[^v115-bubbles]

The study shows why tiny gas pockets in crevices and particles can provide nucleation sites. A model can estimate the **order of magnitude** of bubbles under specified assumptions, but a real glass has a distribution of surface shapes and sites. It is incorrect to assign its model output to all glasses or to treat an attractive cascade as proof of product composition.

## Separate brewery, dispense and service

The Brewers Association identifies draught gas balance, pouring, equipment and sanitation as different operating topics. A chemical explanation of bubble release does not by itself show that a line is maintained, a beverage is safe, or every serving will have the same sensory impact.[^v115-ba]

For a wiki, record **package state**, **dispense method**, **glass and temperature conditions**, and **measurement or observation type** separately. Carbonation may affect mouthfeel and aroma release, but it does not define a beer's legal class, heritage identity or judging style.

See [dissolved CO2](../ingredients/dissolved-carbon-dioxide-in-beer.md), [glass nucleation sites](../objects/glass-surface-nucleation-sites.md) and [draught quality](draught-quality-from-keg-to-glass.md). These are scientific distinctions, not instructions for handling pressurized containers.

## Edition 1.19: newly sourced connections

The physical release of CO₂ bubbles from beer in a glass is distinct from the industrial origin of the carbonation gas. Grand and colleagues' 2025 craft-brewing study examines capture of gas emitted during fermentation and five French brewer interviews. The paper does not test the bubble behavior of every beer or certify a recovered stream as ready for beverage use.[^v119-craftco2]

A 2025 industrial winery analysis studies the feasibility of capturing biogenic fermentation gas at winery scale. It is useful as another alcoholic-fermentation research context, but cannot establish beer-specific purification, carbonate content or dispensing quality in a particular brewery.[^v119-wineryco2]

Preserve origin, actual gas quality evidence, package/serve conditions and physical nucleation measurements independently. Read [the recovery process](fermentation-co2-capture-vs-carbonation.md) and [the ingredient distinction](../ingredients/fermentation-co2-as-a-recovered-material.md). A chemical formula does not define carbonation method or climate performance.
