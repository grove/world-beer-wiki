# From infected grain to beer: metabolite transfer is not detoxification

## Following compounds across a process

A contaminant found in grain need not remain at the same concentration, chemical form or location as material moves through malting, mashing, wort production and fermentation. Biehl and colleagues' 2026 paper followed particular Fusarium-related trichothecenes in a *deliberately inoculated* barley-to-beer experiment. They reported release during wort production, conversion of DON conjugates during fermentation and residues in the final experimental beer. This is not evidence that normal fermentations reliably eliminate the toxins.[^v117-fusarium-trial]

A 2022 malt-and-beer literature review describes why hydrophilic compounds can move from cereal solids into liquid wort, while processes can redistribute material between fractions. Its synthesis should not be read as a tested treatment that can make any suspect grain acceptable.[^v117-fusarium-review]

## Transformations do not resolve exposure questions

The 2026 barley–malt–beer review surveys food-safety evidence along the process chain. It emphasizes the relationships among raw-material selection, measurements, mass balance and processing stage. It does not supply a single endpoint that makes all contaminant species absent or proves a commercially packaged beer complies with a specific legal limit.[^v117-chain]

A percentage of an analyte transferred in one experiment is conditional on starting matrix, extraction, quantification range and process conditions. Reductions in one phase may reflect physical redistribution, adsorption or chemical conversion; those are not necessarily reductions in total hazard.

For responsible description, state the tested species, exact compounds, source of contamination, method and sample stages. Consult [modified DON-3G](../ingredients/don-glucoside-as-modified-mycotoxin.md), [plant conjugation and sampling](../microbiology/plant-conjugated-don.md) and [normal malting concepts](malting-and-kilning.md). This page is a conceptual reference, not a contaminant-processing or food-safety protocol.
