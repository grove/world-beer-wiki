# Brewery wastewater digestion converts organics but does not certify discharge water

## Organic waste can support a different microbial process

A brewery produces liquid waste streams that can include wort and beer losses, yeast, cleaning materials and organic matter. A 2026 review documents their diverse chemical oxygen demand and other parameters across facilities. These streams are not all equally suitable feedstocks and should not be presented as a single constant matrix.[^v119-wastewater26]

Ahmadinejad and colleagues' 2026 experimental reactor operated for 365 days on real brewery wastewater and compared bioelectrochemically assisted anaerobic digestion with conventional digestion under study conditions. It measured COD removal, methane production and volatile fatty acids. Successful performance in the tested reactor does not establish safe effluent discharge, a brewery's economic viability or the absence of all pollutants.[^v119-bead26]

A different 2026 microbrewery waste-stream study describes separating acidic organic streams and alkaline cleaning wastewater and assessing integrated methane potential. Its public abstract is an experimental comparison, not a broadly approved instruction to mix industrial cleaning chemicals or use the byproduct for food.[^v119-microwaste26]

## Keep treatment, fuel and water status apart

Anaerobic microbes transform organic substrates and produce a gas mixture. Measuring methane volume is not identical to measuring usable renewable natural gas, which may need further separation and assessment. COD reduction reports a particular treatment endpoint; irrigation, release or brewing-water use need their own applicable standards and verification.

For documentary comparisons store stream origin, sample, time, reactor type, organism evidence and measured gas, liquid and solids separately.
