# Hop polyphenols, lingering bitterness and dryness

Findings synthesized in a 2023 dry-hopping review; experimental contributions are not identical across every beer.

## More than volatile aroma

Dry hopping is associated with extracting aromatic compounds, but also introduces non-volatile material such as polyphenols. A 2023 review discusses studies in which hop polyphenol fractions influenced perceived bitterness, lingering character and astringency. Effects depend on extraction, other beer components and the tasting task.[^v15-dryhop-review]

## Bitterness versus astringency

Bitterness is a taste response; astringency is often described as drying or tightening mouthfeel. Drinkers can use similar everyday words for both. The model-beer experiment with matched bitter stimuli helps explain why a single “bitterness” scale misses aspects of experience.[^v15-bitter-lab]

## Reading a hazy IPA responsibly

Visible haze cannot by itself identify a hop cultivar, a sensory defect or one chemical compound. When describing a heavily hopped beer, separate volatile aroma, hop-derived bitterness and tactile astringency; brewing water, grain composition and conditioning may affect the full impression.[^v15-dryhop-review]
