# Protein Z and beer foam: more than one barley molecule

**Protein Z** refers to barley serpin proteins that can survive brewing and are associated with foam stability. A 2021 review summarizes evidence for variants including Protein Z4 and interactions among proteins, hop bitterness materials, and process conditions. This is not equivalent to saying that beer foam can be predicted by measuring the concentration of one protein.[^v18-foam-z]

## Where a protein can disappear—or persist

A 2019 proteomics study sampled the proteins present across mashing and wort heating. Several abundant proteins changed markedly over time, while selected lipid-transfer proteins and serpin-family proteins remained detectable late in brewing. Detection shows persistence under studied methods; it cannot by itself identify a precise sensory outcome for all beers.[^v18-foam-proteome]

## Foam science has multiple levels

The barley cultivar, malting steps, heating, breakdown by enzymes, hop addition and serving can all influence the finished matrix. Experiments that isolate Protein Z ask a different question from trials of a full brewery's foam retention. A style description that mentions a rich head is a sensory observation, not a molecular ingredient declaration.
