# Heating can alter LTP1 and foam behavior

Foam is sometimes described as if one intact barley protein simply travels unchanged from grain to glass. A 2025 experiment shows a more complicated pathway. Researchers examined heat-induced chemical and physical changes in **lipid-transfer protein 1 (LTP1)** and its lipid-bound form, **LTP1b**, using separation and mass-spectrometry techniques, together with foam measurements.[^v18-foam-ltp]

## The protein's history matters

The team reported changes including dissociation of lipid adducts and the abundance of chemically modified hydrophobic protein forms after heating. Certain changes correlated with reduced foam quality in their experiment. This is evidence for studied fractions under controlled conditions—not proof that all heating harms foam or that shortening wort boil always improves a commercial product.[^v18-foam-ltp]

## What other measurements would be needed?

A finished beer contains many proteins and hop-derived materials, along with gas, lipids and other compounds. Comparative fermentation, packaging and dispense trials would be needed to attribute a particular foam defect to one molecule. The studied process stage and exact molecular form should accompany a claim.
