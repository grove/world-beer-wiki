# DMS and cooked-cereal aroma: precursor, process and perception

## Where it comes from

The Oxford Companion to Beer identifies dimethyl sulfide (DMS) as an aroma-active volatile associated with the heat-related breakdown of precursors such as S-methylmethionine in malt. Boiling and ventilation can influence how much remains. A perceived sweet-corn or cooked-vegetable note does not establish that maize was used.[^v13-dms]

## Defect in one setting, characteristic in another?

BJCP study materials emphasize that sensory faults are contextual: the intensity and appropriateness of a characteristic depend on the intended beer. A sensory judge should distinguish a compound association from a specific processing diagnosis and refer to the relevant guideline edition.[^v13-bjcp-flavor]
