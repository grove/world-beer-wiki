# Why beer flavor changes during storage

Cardboard-like, muted or changed flavors in old beer often prompt the word “oxidation.” A 2022 review explains why oxygen and transition metals matter while also warning that beer can age through chemistry that does not require additional oxygen.[^v14-aging]

## The beer is a reactive system

Metal ion state, pH, temperature, malt-derived compounds, hop chemistry and oxygen all affect pathways. Some aroma-active aldehydes can emerge or be released during storage even when packaging was tightly controlled.[^v14-aging]

## No universal expiry flavor

The review cannot predict one beer’s exact shelf life without product data. Compare [lightstruck](lightstruck-chemistry.md), [package refermentation](../microbiology/refermentation-and-package-yeast.md), and [brewing packaging](../processes/packaging-and-aging.md).

## Edition 1.11: newly sourced connections

A 2022 open review makes oxygen, iron and copper part of a **reaction system**; some flavor changes can occur without free oxygen. A storage experiment with eleven hoppy ales and one lager found that several aldehydes rose during aging while esters and terpenoids showed different stabilities. No one 'oxidized' note, hop cultivar or oxygen reading predicts a complete sensory outcome.[^v111-metals][^v111-hoppy-storage]
