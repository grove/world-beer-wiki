# Bitterness: a measured stimulus is not a universal taste

An experimental beer-model system and open chemistry review; sensory observations are sample- and task-specific.

## Same reported intensity, different compounds

A 2020 sensory study prepared a nonalcoholic beer model with different bitter stimuli and matched their perceived intensity before running discrimination and preference tasks. The experiments examined whether beer drinkers can distinguish bitterness quality rather than only its magnitude. Their participant samples and simplified model beverage limit the generalization.[^v15-bitter-lab]

## IBU is not a sensation meter

A dry-hopping review explains that laboratory bitterness measures and human perception can diverge as different hop-derived substances contribute differently. Measuring absorbance associated with bittering compounds does not capture aroma, astringency, sweetness and context. Two beers with similar reported IBU may taste different.[^v15-dryhop-review]

## An interpretive checklist

When comparing beers, note analytical IBU (if available), hop addition stage, malt/body, polyphenol extraction, temperature and serving context. Separate a taster’s report from a laboratory concentration. A sensory descriptor is not proof that one particular botanical ingredient was used.[^v15-bitter-lab][^v15-dryhop-review]
