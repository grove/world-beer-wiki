# Beer bubble counts: a model is not a universal sensory score

## What researchers counted and what they modeled

A 2021 ACS Omega study explored bubble dynamics using one commercial bottled lager. The researchers measured dissolved CO2 around the pour and modeled nucleation in a glass. Its analysis depends on the assumed radius and distribution of gas pockets, glass geometry, filling level, dissolved concentration and conditions of service.[^v115-bubbles]

That is a careful physical question, not a declaration that beer should produce one standard number of bubbles. Different glasses, dissolved gases, temperatures and beer matrices produce different observable patterns. A long cascade does not inherently mean a higher-quality beer than a quiet surface, and the model does not test an international panel of consumer preferences.

## Evaluation depends on its purpose

The BJCP describes appearance, aroma, flavor and mouthfeel as sensory elements within a style-specific judging exercise. It also warns that vital statistics and grouping are guidelines, not universal constraints on market products. A physical estimate of bubble formation should not be substituted for style judging or placed into a ranking of cultures.[^v115-bjcp]

The practical reading order is: identify the beverage, note whether gas was measured or merely observed, describe the glass and serving conditions, and only then consider a relevant sensory assessment. When an experiment covers a single lager, other styles remain hypotheses for further trials.
