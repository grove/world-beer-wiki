# Colour and label expectations can influence beer perception

## A description can become a cue

A consumer may approach a beer with expectations about sweetness, bitterness or refreshment before tasting it. In their published 2022 abstract, Blackmore, Hidrio and Yeomans report experiments about the influence of beer colour, alcohol-label information and sensory descriptors on expected or perceived attributes. These are conclusions from a particular research setting, not evidence that a colour makes a chemical change to the liquid.[^v116-expect]

## Name the task before the conclusion

The ISO vocabulary standard is concerned with defining sensory terms across industries. Its public summary does not say that all tasters use those terms identically without training or context; the entire vocabulary and amendment are not reproduced here.[^v116-vocab]

BJCP educational materials are a separate practice: judges evaluate an entry in a style framework and are asked to describe aromas, appearance and flavour while managing distractions and fatigue. A competition framework cannot substitute for an experimentally randomized consumer expectation study.[^v116-bjcpEval]

## Keep observations and interpretations apart

For a robust beer description, identify whether a statement was a blinded test result, a subjective tasting note, a label on a product, or a predicted perception. A person reporting more bitterness after reading the word “bitter” does not demonstrate that the beer gained bittering compounds or a higher IBU value.

The source's full participant records and methods were not independently reviewed, so no universal psychological effect size is claimed.
