# A 2025 unpasteurized ale experiment separates filtration and storage effects

## The observed batch comes first

In an open 2025 experiment, De Francesco, Sileoni and Marconi studied one unpasteurized top-fermented craft beer with different microfiltration, temperature and yeast-refermentation treatments. Their trained panel and chemical analyses found that refrigerated samples retained stronger fruit and hop qualities after the studied period than samples held at room temperature. Some microfiltered beers showed changes in foam and sensory quality. The findings should not be extrapolated to every beer without matching the underlying process and matrix.[^v118-storage25]

The work also distinguishes actual yeast activity from the vague belief that all unfiltered products improve simply because yeast remains. A finding for a selected yeast and fermentation process is not microbiological safety certification and does not demonstrate that unplanned refermentation is harmless.

## A study is not a product label

The 2024 review by Klimczak and colleagues synthesizes several mechanisms of flavor change, including aldehydes and loss or transformation of esters, terpenes and hop compounds. It provides context for the case study without replacing its specific statistical design or extending its timepoint to all commercial packages.[^v118-aging24]

An editorial profile should preserve beer style and batch, filtration, inoculation choice, storage conditions, study duration, analytical endpoints and panel characteristics. Avoid claiming that any consumer can assess pressure safety or contamination through taste or temperature history.
