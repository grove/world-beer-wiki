# Cold fermentation history and served-beer flavor are separate evidence claims

## A historical refrigeration technology is not a tasting panel

The Swiss National Museum's 2026 account of beer cooling describes ice ponds, hillside cellars and later mechanical refrigeration as ways to support brewing and storage across seasons. It also describes how refrigeration affected the commercial availability of bottom-fermented beers. That historical narrative is not an experiment quantifying aroma intensity at a particular drinking temperature or proof that one modern style must always be served cold.[^v119-swissice26]

The University of Chicago Library's exhibit separately describes natural ice and Carl von Linde's refrigeration machinery as expanding storage and shipping possibilities. The source documents technology history, not consumer preference, a controlled tasting comparison or an exact product microbiological profile.[^v119-chicagoice]

## Name the temperature-related question

Fermentation temperature, lagering or maturation temperature, warehouse temperature and temperature in a serving glass are four different observations. A brewery's cooling infrastructure may influence some of them without recording any actual beer's sensory state. A modern tasting study would require identified beer, serving conditions, participant sample and appropriate control; archival history alone cannot substitute.

This page uses historical sources for the material setting, not for unmeasured sensory effects.
