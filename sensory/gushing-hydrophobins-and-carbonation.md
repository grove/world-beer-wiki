# Primary beer gushing and normal carbonation are different explanations

## An unexpected foam eruption is a clue, not a diagnosis

Some beers can foam abruptly and extensively when opened even when a simple excess-carbonation explanation is not established. The 2024 Jayathissa and colleagues paper on Fusarium in barley describes hydrophobins—fungal surface-active proteins—in the context of **primary gushing**. The authors studied host cultivars, fungal strains and crop years; the paper does not determine the cause of any specific retail bottle or drinking-glass observation.[^v117-fungal-strains]

A 2022 review of fungal mycotoxins in malting and beer adds an important distinction: the presence of a fungal-origin metabolite in a particular cereal sample is a different observation from identifying a hydrophobin-related foaming mechanism. It is therefore wrong to infer a specific toxin concentration from excessive foam, or to conclude a gushing incident is itself a quantitative contaminant test.[^v117-fusarium-review]

## Multiple routes to one appearance

Visible bubbles and foam can also vary with dissolved gas, package opening, glass surface condition and serving procedure. Evidence about CO2 nucleation or poor glass cleaning should not automatically replace evidence of a grain-associated mechanism; the appropriate cause requires independent examination of the actual product.

A responsible description says whether a claim comes from a fungal protein study, a brewery-quality investigation, a laboratory gas measurement or an observer's report. It should not turn a single video or visual appearance into a diagnosis of a production fault or food safety.
