# A beer can change between brewery and glass

Two servings drawn from the same product need not be experienced identically. Brewers Association material treats cleaning, line design, gas balance, beer temperature and pouring as part of delivered beer quality. Those variables can alter head formation, carbonation and perceived freshness independently of the original ingredient list.[^v114-draught]

A technical manual is not a blinded consumer experiment. It tells retailers what dimensions of equipment condition and care deserve attention, but does not establish how every person scores flavor at every serving temperature. Nor does it follow that a common service fault identifies a particular unwanted microorganism without sampling and analysis.[^v114-draught-retail]

This is especially useful when comparing cultures of service. A Czech foam pour, an English cask pint and a nitrogenated stout may seek intentionally different levels of dissolved gas, head or texture. These are not evidence that one style is more authentic than another; they are examples of how **serving choices form part of beer experience**.[^v114-draught]

Continue to [dispensing equipment](../objects/draught-lines-and-gas-balance.md), [line maintenance](../processes/draught-quality-from-keg-to-glass.md) and [oxidative aging](oxidative-aging.md). The service chain is a distinct causal layer between brewery and drinker, and its effects should be investigated rather than assumed from a style name.[^v114-draught]
