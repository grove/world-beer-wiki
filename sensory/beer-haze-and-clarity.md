# Beer haze: intentional appearance or instability?

A cloudy beer can be stylistically appropriate, but the word haze does not identify what is suspended. A scientific review describes protein-polyphenol interactions, non-starch polysaccharides and microorganisms as separate potential causes.[^v14-haze]

## Particles and storage

Cold conditions and time can shift aggregation and visibility. The malt-house research connects higher-molecular-weight beta-glucans with viscosity and filtration, showing another route from raw material to physical behavior.[^v14-haze][^v14-malt]

## Do not guess the organism

A cloudy sample is not proof of infection; conversely, a clear beer is not a safety certificate. Compare [clarification processes](../processes/beer-clarification-and-stability.md), [hazy IPA](../styles/hazy-ipa.md) and [barley beta-glucans](../ingredients/barley-beta-glucans.md).

## Research extension 1.5: Dry-hop astringency is not visual haze

A review of dry-hopping chemistry distinguishes volatile aroma extraction from polyphenol transfer and changes in measured or perceived bitterness. Some hop polyphenol effects are tactile, while visible haze is a separate physical characteristic. A cloudy appearance alone cannot identify the compounds or processes driving bitterness and mouthfeel.[^v15-dryhop-review]
