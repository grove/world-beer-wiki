# Boza: sweet, sour and the limits of sensory guessing

Descriptions of boza often emphasize sweetness, sourness and a dense cereal body. These descriptors do not yield a precise ethanol percentage. A 1997 fermentation experiment measured rising acidity and ethanol at the same time; the final figure of **0.79% ethanol** belongs to that preparation, not to a tongue-based test for boza in general.[^v112-boza-1997]

## Different signals, different sensory pathways

Sweetness may reflect remaining sugars, sourness organic acids, and texture cereal solids or processing. Those components can vary independently as raw ingredients and microbes change. A technical review describes the variability of Turkish fermented cereal beverages with the raw materials and fermentation process; it does not establish one universally perceived 'authentic' boza taste.[^v112-boza-review]

## A sensory descriptor cannot regulate a label

Readers should not infer commercial alcohol compliance, microbial safety or functional-health properties from 'slightly sweet', 'tart' or 'refreshing'. Instrumental chemical measurement and a documented recipe answer different questions from a drinker's impression. For a fair comparison, follow both the Turkish 24-hour study and the Bulgarian selected-isolate study, while keeping their research questions separate.[^v112-boza-1997]
