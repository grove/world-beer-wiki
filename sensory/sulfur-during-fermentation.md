# Sulfur character during brewing fermentation

Beer can carry sulfur-associated aromas that span egg-like, struck-match and cooked-cereal impressions. Those impressions do not all come from the same compound or process. A yeast review separates hydrogen sulfide and sulfur dioxide, both influenced by yeast metabolism, from dimethyl sulfide and other sulfur chemistry associated with wort and malt handling.[^v111-yeast-sulfur]

## Sulfur is a nutritional pathway too

Yeast uses sulfur in amino acids and other cellular components. The same metabolic network can generate volatile molecules that affect beer aroma, depending on strain, nutrients and conditions. Sulfite may also bind some flavor-active aldehydes, so it is too simplistic to call every sulfur-containing molecule only a spoilage defect or always a welcome signature.[^v111-yeast-sulfur]

## Do not diagnose from one smell

Sensory descriptors are not analytical identifications. 'Skunky' lightstruck aroma, for example, is associated with distinct photochemistry treated elsewhere in the wiki. Aging, malt processing and fermentation can produce overlapping impressions. Without a chemical assay and source context, this entry explains possible routes rather than naming a culprit strain or guaranteeing how to remove an odor.[^v111-metals]
