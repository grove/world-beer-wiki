# Beer aldehyde aging cannot be attributed only to package oxygen

## More than a single reaction after filling

A 2025 brewing review by Ditrych, Aerts and Andersen surveys staling aldehydes arising through multiple paths, including Strecker degradation, Maillard chemistry and oxidation involving ingredients, wort, yeast, oxygen and metal ions. Its overview makes an important distinction: packaging can influence subsequent change, but a beer can already contain precursors or relevant reaction products before it reaches a can or bottle.[^v118-aldehyde25]

A 2024 storage review describes changes to aldehydes, esters, hop terpenes, bitterness and colloidal stability over time. Different beers and storage conditions may respond differently; it is not a universal countdown to the same cardboard aroma.[^v118-aging24]

## A measured number answers a narrower question

The publicly indexed Analytica EBC description of total package oxygen concerns dissolved and headspace oxygen in a sealed beer package. That combined quantity is useful analytical context; it is not a direct measurement of every aldehyde or a guaranteed prediction of human perception.[^v118-ebc-tpo]

For a good scientific account, record the brewing stage, sample matrix, oxygen measurement, analytical method for any aldehydes, sensory design and elapsed time. Comparing two aged beers with equal headspace oxygen does not prove they began with identical precursor chemistry. This article does not prescribe chemical additives or process controls.
